# Findings: Ch 1–8 Figurative Language Audit

## Chapter 1: Purpose, Principles, And How To Use This Book

### Findings

1. **Quote:** "I have carried this house around for about ten years." (Line 3)
   **Type:** Literal-truth violation / Broken conceit
   **Severity:** High
   **Why:** A house cannot be carried. The author means "held the idea" or "thought about," but the literal claim is physically impossible.

2. **Quote:** "This book organizes that idea as information that can be checked, revised, permitted, built, inspected, and maintained." (Line 9)
   **Type:** Low — borderline
   **Severity:** Low
   **Why:** Mild abstraction. "Information" cannot literally be "permitted" or "built" — the *house* can be, not the information.

3. **Quote:** "Explain what the house is trying to be." (Line 17)
   **Type:** Personification (residual from prior pass — giving intention to inanimate object)
   **Severity:** Medium
   **Why:** A house cannot "try" to be anything.

4. **Quote:** "A house is not one big decision. It is thousands of small decisions stacked together." (Line 22)
   **Type:** Decorative language / Aphoristic phrasing
   **Severity:** Medium
   **Why:** Shaped to feel quotable. "Stacked together" adds no information.

5. **Quote:** "Building a house is more than paperwork and warnings. It is land, tools, concrete, weather, math, comfort, code, systems, and imagination brought into one build." (Line 24)
   **Type:** False poeticism / Sensory flourish
   **Severity:** Medium
   **Why:** A list dressed up as poetry. "Imagination brought into one build" is decorative.

6. **Quote:** "If a cheap choice creates hidden future damage, it is not cheap." (Line 63)
   **Type:** Aphoristic phrasing
   **Severity:** Medium
   **Why:** Shaped like a proverb. The plain statement is that low initial cost can produce higher total cost.

7. **Quote:** "If an expensive choice protects the structure for decades, it may be the cheaper choice over time." (Line 63)
   **Type:** Aphoristic phrasing
   **Severity:** Medium
   **Why:** Another line shaped to feel quotable. Reverses "cheap/expensive" for rhetorical effect.

8. **Quote:** "Those levels are not supposed to repeat the same idea four times." (Line 84)
   **Type:** Low — conversational, borderline personification
   **Severity:** Low
   **Why:** "Repeat" attributes action to abstract levels.

9. **Quote:** "Use a level only when it adds something new." (Line 84)
   **Type:** Low
   **Severity:** Low
   **Why:** Minor personification of "level."

10. **Quote:** "Some chapters will be plain. Some will be technical. Some will be full of numbers. The style should stay the same: clear, useful, direct, and readable." (Line 101)
    **Type:** Aphoristic phrasing
    **Severity:** Medium
    **Why:** Short declarative sentences shaped for rhythm and feel. Four-beat closure phrase.

### Summary

The chapter has isolated but clear figurative drift. The opening line is a broken conceit (High). There is a cluster of aphoristic phrasing in the principles section — sentences shaped to be quotable rather than to inform (Medium). The "imagination brought into one build" line is decorative (Medium). Personification lingers in "what the house is trying to be." Overall, mostly clean with specific problem spots concentrated in the opening and the principles table area.
