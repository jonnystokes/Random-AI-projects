## Chapter 1: Purpose, Principles, And How To Use This Book

### Findings (document order)
1. **Quote**: "I have carried this house around for about ten years."  
   **Type**: Literal-truth violation / Broken conceit  
   **Severity**: High  
   **Explanation**: A physical house cannot be carried; the author means they have held the *idea* of the house for ten years, but the literal claim is impossible and requires reader translation.

2. **Quote**: "The plan brings many pieces together: concrete walls, local smart-home control, service routes, Oregon code, owner-builder rules, plumbing, electrical, soil, inspection, testing, future repair, and all the small decisions that make or break a house."  
   **Type**: Idiom / Broken conceit  
   **Severity**: Medium  
   **Explanation**: "Make or break" is a cliché idiom; small decisions affect the house's success but do not literally make or break a physical structure.

3. **Quote**: "That does not make the goal smaller. It makes it buildable."  
   **Type**: Figurative language (metaphor)  
   **Severity**: Low  
   **Explanation**: Goals are abstract and do not have physical size; "smaller" uses size metaphor for scope reduction.

4. **Quote**: "These principles are not decoration. They make decisions."  
   **Type**: Literal-truth violation  
   **Severity**: Medium  
   **Explanation**: Inanimate principles cannot make decisions; they guide decisions, but the literal statement is false.

5. **Quote**: "Good design is not about making everything complicated. It is about putting the right effort in the right place."  
   **Type**: Idiom / Cliché  
   **Severity**: Low  
   **Explanation**: "Putting the right effort in the right place" is a common idiomatic phrase for prioritizing work, not literal placement.

### Chapter 1 Summary
The chapter has a few high/medium severity literal truth violations clustered in the opening and principle sections. The majority of the technical content (reading levels, design principles table) is clean plain language. The figurative drift is isolated slips rather than pervasive.
