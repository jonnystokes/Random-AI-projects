# Chapter 18: Inspection And Approval Sequence

This chapter covers the inspection and approval process: the statutory basis for inspections, the full list of inspections a build faces, which authority handles each one, how to schedule and be ready, the rule that work stays exposed until it is approved, what each major inspection verifies, how corrections and appeals work, and the records the process produces. It is the inspection-readiness reference; the technical detail of what is being inspected lives in the trade chapters, and the build-order coordination lives in the build-sequence chapter.

The Oregon inspection system has one statutory backbone but several parallel inspecting authorities: the building department, separate plumbing and electrical inspectors, county environmental health for septic, the water resources department for wells, the road authority for the driveway, the serving utility for the meter, the fire authority for fire access, and approved third-party testers for the energy tests. The build has to be scripted around all of them, and the closeout package assembles the inspection records into one audit-ready binder before the certificate of occupancy can issue.

## 18.1 What Inspections Are And The Statutory Framework

An inspection is a third-party code check at a defined moment, before the work is covered. The law requires the work to stay exposed until it is approved and puts the duty to provide access on the permit holder.

**Simple**

An inspection is a pause point where an inspector checks the work before it gets covered, buried, energized, or occupied, so a problem is caught while it is still reachable. The inspector is not the project's quality control; the owner-builder still owns daily quality, sequencing, and correction. The inspection is a code check at specific gates.

**Applied**

- Keep every element of the work accessible and exposed until it is approved; do not pour, backfill, insulate, drywall, energize, or occupy ahead of the required inspection.
- Provide the inspector with access, the approved documents, and any test setup.
- Plan the schedule so a failed inspection is a correction, not a crisis; do not stack the next phase so tightly that a re-work blows the schedule.

**Professional**

ORSC R109.1 requires that work for which a permit is required stay accessible and exposed until approved; work covered, backfilled, energized, or occupied before approval is presumptively non-compliant, and the building official may require it uncovered at the permit holder's expense. R109.3 puts the duty to provide access on the permit holder, and R109.2 lets the building official accept reports from approved agencies (third-party testers, ICC-ES evaluation reports, and special inspectors), which is how the blower-door and duct-leakage results and engineering-intensive items enter the record without the official witnessing each test. Which jurisdiction administers the program is set by ORS 455.148 and 455.150; most populated Oregon jurisdictions run their own programs while the Building Codes Division provides direct service in some counties and runs the statewide programs.

**Code**

```
ORSC R109.1: work stays accessible and exposed until approved; the building official may require uncovering of work covered before approval.
ORSC R109.3: permit holder provides access and means for inspection.
ORSC R109.2: the building official may accept reports from approved agencies (third-party testers, ICC-ES, special inspectors).
ORS 455.148 and 455.150: which jurisdiction administers the inspection program; ORS 455.690: appeals.
```

## 18.2 The Inspection List

The statutory minimum inspection list is short, but a real build faces a much longer list across several authorities. The project's specific list is confirmed with the controlling jurisdiction at the design pass.

**Applied**

Expect these inspection groups:

- Site and pre-construction (before the building permit issues): land-use sign-off, septic site-evaluation approval, well log on file, driveway approach permit, erosion-control approval, fire-access verification, and address posting.
- Foundation and below-grade: setback and staking, footing excavation, foundation reinforcement, under-slab, foundation drainage, waterproofing, and pre-backfill.
- Masonry and structural: masonry reinforcement and grout, bond beam and lintel, floor and wall and roof framing and sheathing, shear-wall nailing, and engineered-item documentation.
- Rough trades: rough plumbing with its pressure tests, rough mechanical, rough electrical, firestopping, and the combined frame inspection after the rough trades pass.
- Pre-cover and energy: insulation, air sealing or the blower-door result, vapor retarder, duct leakage, and the energy-compliance documentation.
- Final and occupancy: plumbing, electrical, and mechanical finals, the building final, and the temporary or full certificate of occupancy.

**Professional**

The statutory minimum under ORSC R109.1 is foundation, rough plumbing/electrical/mechanical, frame and masonry, insulation and vapor retarder, and final. The practical list adds the site and utility approvals, the separate energy tests, and the several authorities' inspections. The exact grouping (which inspections are combined or split) is a local-practice matter, so the project builds a specific inspection list at the design pass rather than assuming a standard sequence.

**Code**

```
ORSC R109.1: minimum inspections (foundation, rough plumbing/electrical/mechanical, frame and masonry, insulation/vapor retarder, final).
The practical list adds site/pre-construction, energy tests, and the several parallel authorities' inspections; confirm the project-specific list at design pass.
```

## 18.3 The Jurisdictional Matrix

Which authority handles which inspection depends on the jurisdiction, and the arrangement varies across Oregon. The project maps every inspection to its agency, contact, and scheduling method at the design pass.

**Applied**

Build a project matrix naming the authority for each inspection:

- Building department (city, county, or the Building Codes Division where it administers): structural, framing, foundation, energy, fire-rated assemblies, final building, and occupancy.
- Plumbing inspector and electrical inspector: their own trade inspections, often on separate staff; the electrical program is run directly by the Building Codes Division in many parts of Oregon.
- County environmental health: septic site evaluation, install, and operation, under delegated DEQ authority.
- Water resources department: the well log and water rights (a documentation gate, not an on-site inspection).
- Road authority or ODOT: the driveway approach.
- Serving utility: the meter set and energization, after the electrical inspector signs off the service equipment.
- Fire authority: fire-access verification and any sprinkler work.
- Third-party tester: the blower-door and duct-leakage tests, and any special inspection.

**Professional**

The building department employs Oregon-certified building inspectors, and the plumbing, electrical, and mechanical disciplines may be on the same staff (cross-certified) or separate. The electrical program is frequently run by the Building Codes Division's regional offices in counties without their own program, so a build can have county plumbing and electrical inspectors and a state building inspector, or the reverse. County environmental health carries delegated DEQ authority for septic, and the serving utility energizes only after the electrical inspector's approval. Because the arrangement varies, the project matrix names the agency, contact, and scheduling channel for each inspection and is verified at the design pass.

**Code**

```
Jurisdictional matrix (verify at design pass): building department, plumbing and electrical inspectors (electrical often Building Codes Division), county environmental health (septic, delegated DEQ), water resources department (well), road authority/ODOT (driveway), serving utility (meter), fire authority, third-party tester (energy tests).
```

## 18.4 Scheduling And Being Ready

An inspection is requested through the jurisdiction's channel and passes only when the work is complete, accessible, and backed by the documents the inspector needs on site. Being ready avoids a reinspection fee and a schedule stall.

**Applied**

- Request the inspection through the jurisdiction's channel (the statewide ePermitting system, a local portal, or phone), at least a day ahead unless same-day is reliably offered.
- Have on site: the approved construction documents, the posted permit placard, the inspection card, any test setup (gauges holding for a pressure test, ladder access to elevated work), and the documentation the item requires (an engineer's letter, an ICC-ES report, or the manufacturer's instructions where the listing controls).
- Be present or have someone competent to answer questions; inspectors defer or fail when no one can speak to the work.
- Confirm in writing what the inspection is for, photograph the signed inspection record after a pass, and read the inspector's notes after a failure for the specific correction.
- Do not call for an inspection until the work is complete and accessible.

**Professional**

Most Oregon jurisdictions accept requests through the Building Codes Division's statewide ePermitting system or a local portal, with same-day requests typically due by early morning and next-day requests by late afternoon the prior business day; rural inspectors cover large areas on route schedules that compress availability, so the route schedule for the build's area is confirmed rather than any-day availability assumed. ORSC R106.3.1 and R105.7 require the approved documents and the permit placard on site, and R109.4 the signed inspection card where issued. A reinspection fee is charged when the work is not ready, not accessible, or a failed item is not corrected, so the discipline is to call only when the work is complete.

**Code**

```
Scheduling: statewide ePermitting, a local portal, or phone; same-day typically by early morning, next-day by the prior afternoon; confirm rural route schedules.
ORSC R106.3.1 and R105.7: approved documents and permit placard on site; R109.4: signed inspection card where issued.
On site: test setups, access, and documentation (engineer letter, ICC-ES report, manufacturer instructions where the listing controls).
```

## 18.5 The Cover-And-Conceal Rule

The single rule that governs the build flow is that no work is covered before it is approved. Where a temporary cover is unavoidable, it is documented with photographs, but structurally critical work is never covered without the inspector's approval.

**Applied**

Do not, before the relevant inspection passes:

- Pour concrete over reinforcement, anchor bolts, holdowns, the vapor retarder, in-slab piping and conduit, or radiant tubing.
- Backfill against foundation walls until the foundation, drainage, and waterproofing inspections pass and the walls are braced.
- Insulate until the rough trades and framing inspections pass.
- Drywall until the insulation and any pre-cover inspections pass.
- Energize the electrical service until the electrical inspector signs off the service equipment.
- Pressurize plumbing or hydronic systems for service until the pressure test passes.
- Occupy any portion of the building until at least a temporary certificate of occupancy is granted.

Where a temporary cover is unavoidable (an overnight trench closure against weather), photograph the work from several angles with a date stamp and tell the inspector when scheduling; acceptance of photos in lieu of uncovering is the inspector's discretion, and rebar and anchor-bolt placement are never covered without approval.

**Professional**

ORSC R109.1 is the legal hook for the entire build flow, and the sequence in the build-sequence chapter exists to respect it. Most inspectors will accept clear, date-stamped photographic evidence for routine items covered out of necessity, but this is discretionary and not a right, and anything structurally critical stays exposed until approved. Temporary construction power is limited to 180 days and does not become permanent without the trade inspector's specific sign-off.

**Code**

```
ORSC R109.1: no work covered before approval; the binding build-flow rule.
Temporary covering out of necessity: date-stamped photographs from multiple angles, disclosed at scheduling; acceptance is discretionary; structurally critical work never covered without approval.
```

## 18.6 Foundation And Structural Inspection Readiness

The foundation inspection happens after the reinforcement and embeds are set and before the pour, and it verifies the excavation, forms, reinforcement, and every embedded item at once. The structural inspections verify the framing or masonry against the approved plans.

**Applied**

Have ready for the foundation inspection:

- The excavation to undisturbed bearing soil at the required depth, and the forms set square, level, and braced.
- The reinforcement (bar size, spacing, cover, splices, chairs), anchor bolts, and holdowns per the structural plan.
- The concrete-encased electrode (the grounding tail) in place per the electrical chapter.
- The under-slab vapor retarder, insulation, radon provisions where required, and every plumbing, electrical, and mechanical sleeve.
- The foundation drainage and the waterproofing, and the wall braced for the pre-backfill inspection.

Have ready for the structural inspections the framing or masonry per the approved plans: member sizes, spacing, sheathing and its fastening pattern, wall bracing, anchorage, and the engineered-item documentation.

**Professional**

ORSC R109.1.1 sets the foundation inspection after excavation, forms, and reinforcement and before concrete. The verified items (excavation depth to undisturbed soil, footing dimensions per Table R403.1, reinforcement cover, anchor bolts per R403.1.6, holdowns with their ICC-ES report, the concrete-encased electrode per NEC 250.52(A)(3), the vapor retarder, slab-edge insulation, sleeves, waterproofing per R406, and drainage per R405) are detailed in the foundation, structural, and electrical chapters. The framing inspection under R109.1.4 verifies the wall bracing per R602.10, the sheathing fastening (the most common framing-inspection failure is fasteners too far apart), and the anchorage, plus a moisture-content notice that framing is at or below 19 percent before interior finishes. For masonry, the reinforcement is inspected before grouting and the grout pour is observed, with cleanouts verified for high-lift pours.

**Code**

```
ORSC R109.1.1: foundation inspection after reinforcement and embeds, before concrete (excavation, forms, reinforcement/cover, anchor bolts per R403.1.6, holdowns, concrete-encased electrode per NEC 250.52(A)(3), vapor retarder, sleeves, waterproofing per R406, drainage per R405).
ORSC R109.1.4: frame and masonry inspection after rough trades; wall bracing per R602.10, sheathing fastening, anchorage; moisture content at or below 19 percent (R109.1.4.1).
Masonry: reinforcement before grouting, grout pour observed, cleanouts for high-lift pours (TMS 602).
```

## 18.7 Rough-Trade Inspection Readiness

The rough plumbing, mechanical, and electrical inspections happen after the systems are installed and before they are concealed, with the plumbing and hydronic systems held under a pressure test. The frame inspection cannot pass until all three rough trades do.

**Applied**

- Rough plumbing: hold the drain-waste-vent system under a water or air test and the water supply under a pressure test with the gauges holding when the inspector arrives; verify cleanout locations, gas-piping test where applicable, water-heater seismic strapping, and backflow devices.
- Rough mechanical: have the ducts, exhaust runs, refrigerant line-set (in place, not brazed), condensate routing, and makeup-air paths in and sealed.
- Rough electrical: have the service, panel, branch circuits, boxes, grounding and bonding, GFCI and AFCI circuit identification, smoke and CO alarm rough, and low-voltage separation in per the electrical chapters.
- Call the rough-trade inspections before the frame inspection, which is gated on them.

**Professional**

Rough plumbing under ORSC R109.1.2 and OPSC 103 tests the drain-waste-vent system (a 10-foot water head or a 5 psi air test, held 15 minutes) and the water supply (1.5 times working pressure, not less than 50 psi, held 15 minutes), with gas piping tested per OPSC 1213; the seismic strapping of the water heater is required (OPSC 507.3). The rough electrical verifies the service and panel (working space per NEC 110.26), the conductor-to-breaker match, grounding and bonding, the GFCI and AFCI protection (per the Oregon-amended list in the branch-circuit chapter), and the alarm and low-voltage rough. The mechanical rough verifies duct sealing (mastic, not fabric tape), duct and dryer-exhaust runs, and the refrigerant line-set before brazing. The technical detail for each is in the plumbing, electrical, and HVAC chapters; here the concern is that the work is complete, tested, and accessible.

**Code**

```
ORSC R109.1.2 and OPSC 712/609/1213: rough plumbing; DWV water/air test, supply pressure test, gas test; water-heater seismic strapping (OPSC 507.3).
OESC (NEC 90.4): rough electrical; service, panel (NEC 110.26 working space), conductor-to-breaker match, grounding/bonding, GFCI/AFCI, alarms, low-voltage separation.
Mechanical rough: duct sealing (mastic), duct and dryer-exhaust runs, refrigerant line-set before brazing.
Frame inspection gated on all rough-trade sign-offs.
```

## 18.8 Insulation, Energy, And Blower-Door Inspection

The insulation and air-sealing are inspected before cover, and the energy tests (blower door and, where ducts are outside the envelope, duct leakage) are run by an approved third-party tester and submitted before the certificate of occupancy. The target depends on the compliance path chosen.

**Applied**

- Pass the insulation inspection with the insulation and any vapor retarder installed to the energy documentation, before drywall.
- Have the blower-door test performed by an approved third-party tester, before final and ideally before drywall so a failure can be remediated, and submit the result on the state form.
- Have the duct-leakage test performed where ducts run outside the conditioned envelope.
- Have the crawl-space ground cover, eave baffles, and firestopping in for the pre-cover inspection.

**Professional**

The insulation inspection under ORSC R109.1.5.2 verifies the type, thickness, and R-value against the compliance documentation and the installation quality (full cavity fill, no gaps or compression), plus the vapor retarder where the climate zone requires it. Air sealing under N1104.8 is met by the air-barrier criteria or a blower-door result at or below the path target; the blower-door test (ASTM E779 or E1827, by an approved BPI or RESNET tester) is reported on the state form and the target depends on the path (a lower ACH50 for the Reach Code than the base path). Duct leakage under N1105.3 applies to ducts in unconditioned spaces. The building official's acceptance of the specific tester is confirmed before scheduling, since the code names approved agencies rather than specific credentials. The envelope and HVAC chapters carry the detailed values.

**Code**

```
ORSC R109.1.5.2: insulation inspection (type, thickness, R-value, install quality, vapor retarder) before interior finishes.
ORSC N1104.8: air sealing by criteria or blower-door result; test per ASTM E779/E1827 by an approved third-party tester, on the state form, before the certificate of occupancy.
ORSC N1105.3: duct-leakage test for ducts in unconditioned spaces.
Confirm the building official accepts the specific tester (R109.2 approved agency).
```

## 18.9 Final Inspection, Temporary Occupancy, And Occupancy

Each trade has its own final, the building final confirms the life-safety and energy items, and the certificate of occupancy is the legal gate to moving in. A temporary certificate is available at the building official's discretion when the life-safety items are complete.

**Applied**

- Complete the trade finals: plumbing (fixtures connected, water heater commissioned, pressure under load), electrical (devices live, panel labeled, GFCI and AFCI and alarms tested, service energized), and mechanical (equipment installed, startup documented, controls operational).
- Complete the building final: all trade finals signed off, energy documentation filed, life-safety items operational, required testing complete (well water, septic startup, radon where applicable), address numbers in place, and conditions of approval satisfied.
- Where a temporary certificate is used, physically separate and label any unfinished area as not for occupancy, and complete the written remaining-work list by the deadline.

**Professional**

The building final under ORSC R109.1.6 and R110 confirms the trade finals, the energy-compliance documentation (blower door, duct leakage, AHRI certificates, NFRC labels, and the lighting form), the life-safety items, and the required testing. A temporary certificate of occupancy under R110.4 is discretionary and requires the life-safety items complete, at least one functional bathroom and a kitchen with a sink and counter and one bedroom with egress, permanent power, water, waste, and heat, and a written remaining-work list with a deadline; it can be denied even when the minimum items are met if a condition of approval is unmet. Where finish work continues after move-in, a clean lockable separation between the occupied and in-progress areas is designed in. Selling before the certificate of occupancy is a disclosure risk covered in the legal chapter.

**Code**

```
ORSC R109.1.6 and R110: building final and certificate of occupancy; trade finals, energy documentation, life-safety items, required testing, conditions satisfied.
ORSC R110.4: temporary certificate of occupancy, discretionary; life-safety items, minimum habitable spaces, permanent utilities, and a written remaining-work list with a deadline; unfinished areas separated and labeled.
```

## 18.10 Corrections, Appeals, And Revisions

When work fails, the inspector issues a correction notice that is addressed before reinspection; disputes escalate through a local or state appeal; and a change to the approved design goes through a field revision, an engineer's letter, or a formal plan revision depending on its significance.

**Applied**

- Address each item on a correction notice, document the correction with photos and a written response, and request reinspection through the same channel.
- For a change during construction: a minor field revision that does not affect life safety can be approved in the field and documented on the field set with the inspector's initials; a structural deviation from stamped engineering needs a PE-stamped engineer's letter; a substantive change to structure, egress, or energy compliance needs a formal plan revision.
- For a dispute over a code interpretation, use the local board of appeals first (typically within 30 days of the decision), and the state appeal or a statewide alternate-method ruling only for items that genuinely matter.
- Stop work immediately on a posted stop-work order; continuing is a separate violation.

**Professional**

A correction notice identifies the failed item and code section, and reinspection carries a fee (commonly $50 to $200). The building official may issue a stop-work order under ORSC R114 for work in violation, without permit, or creating a hazard, with daily civil penalties under ORS 455.770 for continuing. Appeals run through the local board of appeals under R112 or a state appeal under ORS 455.690 and OAR 918-008-0120; a statewide alternate-method ruling under OAR 918-008-0095 is the alternate path that binds every jurisdiction. A field revision is documented on the field set; an engineer's letter clears a structural deviation and is retained in closeout; and a plan revision (added or removed structural elements, a changed egress or energy-affecting schedule, or an owner-driven design change) is resubmitted, re-reviewed, and can take weeks, so anticipated revisions are scheduled early.

**Code**

```
ORSC R114 and ORS 455.770: stop-work order and daily civil penalties for continuing.
ORSC R112 (local board of appeals) and ORS 455.690 / OAR 918-008-0120 (state appeal); OAR 918-008-0095 statewide alternate-method ruling.
Field revision documented on the field set; PE-stamped engineer's letter for structural deviations; formal plan revision for substantive changes.
```

## 18.11 Inspection Records

The inspection process produces a legal record and a larger project record. The legally required items are kept on site and filed; the recommended closeout binder assembles the full audit-ready history that the documentation and legal chapters rely on.

**Applied**

Keep the legally required records:

- The approved construction documents on site until completion, the posted permit placard, and the signed inspection card.
- The energy-compliance forms (blower-door and duct-leakage results, AHRI certificates, NFRC labels, and the lighting form) before the certificate of occupancy.
- The manufacturer's installation instructions on site for any product where the listing controls, and the contractor information form for any contractor work.
- Any special-inspection reports and floodplain elevation certificate where applicable.

Assemble the recommended closeout binder: every inspection sign-off and correction notice with its correction, the concrete batch tickets, the engineer's letters, the product cut sheets and label photographs, the ICC-ES reports, the AHRI and NFRC labels, the equipment startup and commissioning reports, the warranty registrations, the test results, the pre-cover photographs of every concealed condition, the plan revisions, and the permits and final certificate.

**Professional**

The legally required records (ORSC R106.3.1 approved documents, R105.7 placard, R109.4 inspection card, and the energy forms before occupancy) are the minimum. The closeout binder built in the materials chapter and referenced by the documentation and legal chapters is the substantive as-built record and the evidence behind the seller-property-disclosure form if a defect claim arises after a sale. It is kept as a paper copy in a fireproof location and as digital copies in two independent locations, including one off-site.

**Code**

```
Legally required: approved documents on site (R106.3.1), permit placard (R105.7), inspection card (R109.4), energy-compliance forms before occupancy, manufacturer instructions on site where the listing controls, contractor information form.
Recommended closeout binder: inspection sign-offs and corrections, batch tickets, engineer letters, product data and label photos, ICC-ES reports, AHRI/NFRC labels, startup/commissioning reports, warranties, test results, pre-cover photographs, plan revisions, permits, and the final certificate; paper (fireproof) plus digital in two independent locations.
```
