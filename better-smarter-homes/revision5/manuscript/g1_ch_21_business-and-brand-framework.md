# Chapter 21: Business And Brand Framework

This chapter covers how a public brand can grow around an owner-builder project without turning the house into a speculative development business. It describes the boundary between the person who builds and owns the house and the separate entity that owns the brand and content, the design and plan library, monetization and disclosure, intellectual property, privacy and site security, and honest expectations for a construction channel.

This chapter is a planning framework, not legal, tax, insurance, or business advice. It describes structures and routes every decision to a licensed professional. The legal chapter establishes the owner-builder exemption this framework depends on, and the documentation chapter establishes the sale-record obligations; this chapter separates the personal build from the business so neither compromises the other. Several statutes named here were amended or are being amended, and the tax and insurance points turn on facts only a CPA or agent can supply, so the specific choices are confirmed with the relevant professional before action.

## 21.1 The Activity-Based Boundary

Oregon draws the contractor-licensing line by activity, not by entity: offering, advertising, bidding, or arranging construction work for compensation is itself the licensable act. The owner-builder exemption protects the person building their own residence, so the person builds and owns the house and the brand entity owns only intangibles and never offers construction.

**Simple**

The house comes first, and the brand exists to share the learning, not to run the build. The person builds and owns the house under the owner-builder rules. A separate brand entity owns the videos, writing, plans, and courses. The two stay separate, because the moment a business offers to build for other people, it needs a contractor license, before any work is done.

**Applied**

Keep three lanes separate:

| Lane | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Personal house | Land, permits, construction, occupancy, maintenance, and sale decisions |
| Public brand | Videos, writing, photos, diagrams, lessons, and community |
| Business operations | Website, accounts, platform revenue, sponsorships, products, taxes, and insurance |

Keep the house and its permit in the person's name, never the business entity. Keep the brand entity to intangible property and never let it advertise, offer, arrange, or bid construction work for others.

**Professional**

Contractor licensure under ORS 701.021 attaches to anyone who undertakes, offers to undertake, or bids to do work as a contractor for compensation, and the licensing board's guidance sweeps in any business that advertises, offers, bids, or arranges construction. The owner-builder exemption (ORS 701.010) protects the person building their own residence with no intent to sell, so the residence stays personally owned and personally permitted, and the brand entity owns only the brand, the content, the site and channel, the design library, and the courses. Owning those creates no contractor issue because none is work as a contractor, but the offering itself is the licensable act, so the brand never crosses into offering construction. ORS 701.010 was amended by 2025 legislation with provisions operative in 2027, so the current text is verified before publication and the manuscript is reviewed by an Oregon attorney.

**Code**

```
ORS 701.021: contractor licensure attaches to undertaking, offering, bidding, or arranging construction work for compensation; the offering is itself the licensable act.
ORS 701.010: owner-builder exemption protects the person building their own residence with no intent to sell (see the legal chapter); amended by 2025 legislation, operative 2027; verify current text.
The house and permit stay in the person's name; the brand entity owns only intangible property.
```

## 21.2 What The Brand Entity Owns And What It Must Never Do

The brand entity owns intangible property and sells information as information; it never owns the house, takes construction money, or offers construction services. The prohibited list is drawn directly from the licensing statutes.

**Applied**

The brand entity may own the brand name and marks, the website, channel, newsletter, and social accounts, the book rights, the course and template products, the photo and video archive (assigned in writing from the person), the design library sold as reference plans, and the affiliate and sponsorship contracts.

The brand entity must not:

- Contract, offer, advertise, bid, or arrange construction work for others, including "we will manage your build" consulting that shades into arranging.
- Take construction deposits or progress payments of any kind.
- Own the house or the building permit, or build on another party's land or on entity-owned land for sale.
- Sell plan sets represented as stamped, approved, code-verified, or site-adapted construction documents.
- Provide engineering services or advice for compensation, or perform paid inspections that resemble home inspection.
- Use "architect," "architecture," "architectural," "engineer" as a service, "licensed," or "certified" in its marketing.
- Give individualized professional advice ("your specific footing should be...") in comments or consults.

**Professional**

Putting the residence into the business entity would destroy the owner-builder exemption logic and complicate title, insurance, lending, and the eventual sale, so the house stays personal. Developing a structure on entity-owned land for sale is developer activity, itself a licensed category. Selling plans as stamped or approved documents, or offering engineering answers for a third party's specific project, crosses into the architecture and engineering practice acts. The consumer-protection posture is to sell information products as information with the industry-standard disclaimers, because both the Oregon Unlawful Trade Practices Act and the Federal Trade Commission punish misrepresenting what a product is, which is why the "not construction documents" framing carries weight. The bright-line consulting list and the negative claim that content and courses need no contractor license are reviewed by an Oregon attorney before publication.

**Code**

```
Brand entity owns intangibles only; sells information as information with disclaimers (Oregon UTPA and FTC Section 5 punish misrepresenting a product).
Prohibited: offering/advertising/arranging/bidding construction (ORS 701.021); taking construction deposits; owning the house or permit; developing entity-owned land for sale (developer licensing, ORS 701.042); selling plans as stamped/approved; providing engineering (ORS 672); using architect/engineer/licensed/certified language (ORS 671.020).
The consulting bright-line list and the "no CCB license for content/courses" claim are reviewed by an Oregon attorney.
```

## 21.3 Intent To Sell And The Portfolio As Philosophy

The owner-builder exemption rests on building for one's own occupancy, so building with intent to sell converts an exempt owner-builder into an unlicensed developer. A build-occupy-sell-repeat portfolio is presented as philosophy, not as a repeatable business method, and sale timing is routed to an attorney.

**Professional**

Oregon case law treats a property owner who develops a structure with intent to sell, rather than for owner occupancy, as acting as a contractor or developer, and developer is itself a licensed category. A portfolio that schedules builds, occupancies, and sales drifts from exempt owner-builder into unlicensed development, so the manuscript presents the portfolio vision as a life philosophy rather than a method: build the home the owner intends to occupy, let life rather than a schedule determine any sale, understand that intent to sell changes the legal posture, and repeat only as genuine life chapters with attorney review at each transition. The framing strips out build counts, holding periods, sale-timing rules of thumb, and financial targets, because each is either evidence of intent to sell, an unverifiable projection, or legal advice the book cannot give. What remains is the documented-craft thesis: a meticulously documented, code-anchored build creates durable value in the asset, the records, and the brand at once, which is true and needs no legal promise. The first-sale permit-statement mechanics and the deed-recording obligation are in the documentation chapter.

**Code**

```
Intent to sell converts an exempt owner-builder into an unlicensed developer (Tandem Properties; ORS 701.042); the exemption rests on owner occupancy.
Portfolio presented as philosophy, not method: no build counts, holding periods, sale-timing rules, or financial targets.
Sale timing routed to an Oregon attorney; first-sale record and deed-recording obligations in the documentation chapter.
```

## 21.4 The Design And Plan Library

Oregon's single-family design exemption makes a plan library lawful, and the established stock-plan industry supplies the exact compliant template: copyrighted plans sold under a single-build license, unstamped, with code compliance and any required local stamp the buyer's responsibility. The title rules are strict.

**Applied**

- Sell plans as reference documents under a written single-build license (one dwelling, no resale or redistribution, defined modification rights).
- State plainly that the documents are unstamped reference material, not verified against any site, soil, climate, or local code, and that a jurisdiction requiring a stamp will require the buyer to hire a local licensed professional.
- State that omitted mechanical, electrical, and plumbing layouts are the buyer's responsibility and that local professional review is recommended before construction.
- Tell out-of-state buyers to check their own state's design-professional and permit requirements.
- Never use "architect," "architecture," or "architectural" in the brand, titles, or site copy, and never offer engineering for a specific third-party project.

**Professional**

ORS 671.030 lets a non-architect prepare technical submissions and provide construction-phase services for single-family dwellings and other buildings within the size and height limits, so publishing concepts, dimensioned reference drawings, technical guides, and templates for single-family dwellings is lawful non-architect activity. Two hard rules apply: the exemption covers the activity, not the title, so the architecture titles are never used even in website copy (a point Oregon case law makes directly), and engineering is a separate practice act with no equivalent blanket dwelling exemption, so structural calculations and load advice for a third party are off-limits and the safe lane is describing what the project's own engineer designed on the project's own build and telling readers to hire their own. The plan library's value concentrates in the distinctive documented system, because copyright protection excludes standard features and code-driven elements. Selling "plan concepts and reference documents, licensed for one build, not stamped, not verified against your site or codes" is an established, lawful product category; selling "approved construction documents" is not something anyone can promise.

**Code**

```
ORS 671.030: single-family design exemption; non-architects may prepare technical submissions for single-family dwellings within the size/height limits.
ORS 671.020: never use architect/architecture/architectural, even in website copy (Twist Architecture).
ORS 672: engineering is a separate practice act with no blanket dwelling exemption; no engineering services or advice for a third party's project.
Plan library: single-build license, unstamped reference material, buyer responsible for code compliance and any required local stamp; recommend local professional review.
```

## 21.5 Entity, Tax, And Insurance At The Framework Level

The business is organized as a separate limited-liability entity, but the entity mechanics, the tax treatment, and the insurance coverage are framework questions whose specifics belong to a professional. The one firm rule is that the house construction is never a business expense.

**Professional**

The brand operates through a separate limited-liability entity registered with the state, with a registered agent, an annual report, and an operating agreement that documents the intellectual-property boundary between the person and the entity; the current fees and filing mechanics are confirmed against the state's schedule at formation. The tax structure is described and every number is routed to a CPA: a single-member entity is a federal disregarded entity reported on the owner's return by default, either form can elect corporate treatment, pass-through profit generally carries self-employment tax, Oregon taxes the pass-through income on the personal return and offers an elective entity tax, and the state's commercial-activity tax applies above a threshold. The income types (affiliate, sponsorship, course, consulting, and book income) are ordinary business income, the hobby-loss rule is the real risk for a channel that runs losses against other income so profit-motive documentation matters, and the media-business expenses (home office, camera equipment, depreciation) follow normal rules, but the house construction itself is never an entity expense, which is the activity boundary restated in tax terms. Insurance is a coverage conversation for a licensed Oregon agent: a homeowner or builder's-risk policy typically excludes business activity, so filming, visitors, and running a business from the property are disclosed; the entity needs commercial general liability once it has revenue or visitors; media liability covers the content risks general liability does not; and volunteers or paid helpers on the personal build raise workers'-compensation questions covered in the build-sequence and legal chapters. The book supplies the structure and the questions; the CPA and the agent supply the numbers and the coverage.

**Code**

```
Entity: a separate limited-liability entity with a registered agent, annual report, and an operating agreement documenting the person-to-entity IP boundary; fees and mechanics verified at formation.
Tax (structure only; route every number to a CPA): disregarded-entity default, optional corporate election, self-employment tax on pass-through, Oregon PTE elective tax and commercial-activity tax above threshold, hobby-loss risk (IRC §183). House construction is never an entity expense.
Insurance (coverage conversation with a licensed agent): homeowner/builder's-risk business-activity exclusions, commercial general liability, media liability, and volunteer/helper workers'-compensation.
```

## 21.6 Monetization And Disclosure

Every material connection behind a recommendation is disclosed clearly in the content itself, per the federal endorsement rules, and a sponsor's product never overrides a documented engineering or specification decision. The disclosure rule is federal and current; the revenue mechanisms are described without dollar projections.

**Applied**

- Disclose every material connection (payment, free product, discount, affiliate commission, or family or employment tie) whenever part of the audience would not expect it.
- Make the disclosure clear and conspicuous and unavoidable, in the content itself (spoken and on-screen for video, and in the description), not buried in hashtags, a "more" fold, or a profile page, and repeat it periodically in a livestream.
- Use plain language ("paid link" or "I earn commissions from links in this post") rather than jargon tags.
- Recommend only products that were used, and do not present atypical results as typical.
- Keep review integrity: never gate, cherry-pick, or incentivize only positive reviews.
- State in the sponsorship policy that no sponsor's product overrides a documented engineering or specification decision.

**Professional**

The Federal Trade Commission's endorsement guides require clear-and-conspicuous disclosure of every material connection, per post, in the content, with platform disclosure tools alone insufficient and substantial per-violation civil penalties. Each post is its own advertisement requiring its own disclosure. For a construction channel the sponsorship-ethics rule is also a credibility asset: the documented engineering and specification decisions are the brand's core value, so a sponsorship that would override them is declined and the policy says so, which is both substantiation hygiene and brand protection. The revenue mechanisms (ad revenue, sponsorship, affiliate, courses, plans, book, and education-only consulting) are presented as a mechanism table with effort and risk characteristics rather than dollar figures, because no honest income baseline exists for this niche and any specific number would be fabricated precision.

**Code**

```
FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255): disclose every material connection, per post, clear and conspicuous in the content; plain language; each post its own ad; honest-experience and review-integrity rules.
Sponsorship policy: no sponsor product overrides a documented engineering or specification decision.
Revenue presented as a mechanism table (effort/risk), not dollar projections; no earnings claims.
```

## 21.7 Intellectual Property

The content, plans, and templates are protected by copyright from the moment they are fixed, and the book and plan library are registered so the strongest remedies are available. The brand name is cleared and the plan library is licensed per asset class, with the pre-formation content assigned in writing to the entity.

**Professional**

Copyright protects the content, photos, videos, plans, and templates on fixation, but timely registration is what enables statutory damages and attorney fees, so the book and the plan library are registered. Plans carry two protections, the drawings as graphic works and the built design as an architectural work, though protection excludes standard features and code-driven elements, which is why the library's value sits in the distinctive documented system. The brand name and logo are cleared through both the state registry and the federal trademark office before commitment, because state name availability is not trademark clearance, and federal registration follows when revenue justifies it. The licensing is chosen per asset class and documented: the plan library under the written single-build license, and educational documents or code repositories under a deliberate open or proprietary license so a paid asset is never open-sourced by an accidental repository license. The pre-formation content is assigned in writing from the person to the entity, and the registration and assignment mechanics are handled with an attorney.

**Code**

```
Copyright: content, plans, and templates protected on fixation; register the book and plan library for statutory damages and fees (17 USC; confirm registration-benefit specifics).
Plans protected as graphic works and as architectural works; protection excludes standard and code-driven features.
Trademark: clear the name through the state registry and the federal trademark office; federal registration when revenue justifies.
License per asset class (single-build license for plans; deliberate open/proprietary for other assets); written IP assignment person-to-entity, with an attorney.
```

## 21.8 Privacy, Filming, And Site Security

Filming a build creates privacy, consent, and security exposures, so releases, careful editing, and delayed posting protect the people on site and the site itself. The recording-consent rule is a legal point verified before it is stated as law.

**Applied**

- Get written releases from every recognizable visitor, worker, and volunteer, and do not film inspectors, utility workers, or delivery drivers without asking; never present an inspector's on-camera comment as an approval.
- Inform all participants before recording an in-person conversation on the jobsite.
- Blur or omit house numbers, road names, permit-card close-ups, and geolocatable landmarks, and strip location metadata from published photos.
- Delay-post site content (publish weeks behind reality) so the channel never advertises an unattended site full of tools, and never show security-system details, key locations, or family schedules.
- Notify neighbors rather than featuring them without releases, and require explicit opt-in for children and family members.

**Professional**

Oregon's recording statute treats in-person oral conversations differently from other communications, and informing the participants before recording an in-person conversation is the safe jobsite rule; the exact terms and exceptions are verified before the point is published as law. The remaining protocols are standard media practice published as recommendations: releases for recognizable people, careful treatment of officials and workers, removal of geolocation cues, and a delay-post rule that also serves site security, because theft of tools, copper, and appliances spikes at a known active project address. The delay-post rule, a locked container, and cameras are the practical security answer, cross-referenced to the equipment-security material in the build-sequence chapter.

**Code**

```
ORS 165.540: inform all participants before recording an in-person jobsite conversation (verify exact terms and exceptions before stating as law).
Practice guidance: written releases from recognizable visitors/workers/volunteers; do not present an inspector's comment as approval; omit geolocation cues and strip metadata; delay-post site content; protect security details; explicit opt-in for children and family.
```

## 21.9 Honest Expectations And Community Practice

The channel is presented with honest expectations rather than income projections, and the community is managed with the same cite-everything discipline as the book, including no individualized safety-critical advice in comments. The build is the product, and the channel is built to survive the build's variance.

**Professional**

Only a small share of channels ever reaches even the basic monetization thresholds, and income varies so widely that any specific example would be fabricated precision, so the chapter gives the revenue-mechanism table with effort and risk rather than dollar figures. Consistency matters more than a fixed cadence, and a construction project supplies a natural episodic structure, but any specific cadence promise is reality-checked against the schedule-contingency findings in the build-sequence and documentation chapters, because the build is the product and the channel must survive the build's variance. Platform terms, reach, and account access are revocable at the platform's discretion, so the mitigation is the owned-audience stack (a website and email newsletter as the canonical home, with platforms as distribution) plus backups of the subscriber list and the content masters. Community management follows a moderation policy, redirects safety-critical questions to "hire your local professional" as policy rather than answering them individually, and applies a documented correction practice when published content turns out wrong, which extends the book's cite-everything discipline to the channel. Every legal, tax, and insurance passage in this chapter carries the professional-referral frame: the book describes the process and the triggers and routes the outcomes to the attorney, the CPA, and the agent.

**Code**

```
Honest expectations: revenue-mechanism table (effort/risk), no income projections or earnings claims.
Owned-audience stack (website + newsletter canonical, platforms as distribution) with subscriber-list and content-master backups.
Community: moderation policy; no individualized safety-critical advice (redirect to a local professional); documented correction practice.
Every legal/tax/insurance passage routes outcomes to the attorney, CPA, or agent.
```
