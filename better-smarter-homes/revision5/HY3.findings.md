# HY3 Findings: Literal-Truth Violations, Figurative Language, and False Poeticism
## Scan Parameters
- **Target**: Chapters 1-8, revision5 manuscript folder
- **Principle**: Clarity, precision, and material honesty; plain technical language about real work
- **Exclusions**: Personification already fixed (intentions, feelings, opinions, demands of inanimate things)

## Chapter 1: Purpose, Principles, And How To Use This Book

### Findings (in document order):

1. **Quote**: "A house is the sum of many small decisions." (line 16)
   - **Type**: Literal-truth violation
   - **Severity**: High
   - **Explanation**: Literal meaning claims the physical house is composed of decisions, which is false; reader must translate to "the design for the house is the sum of many small decisions."

2. **Quote**: "The two halves of the house follow two different logics: build the shell so it can stay, and route the service systems so they can change." (line 35)
   - **Type**: Literal-truth violation
   - **Severity**: Medium
   - **Explanation**: Physical house components cannot "follow" abstract logics; refers to design approach, not the house itself.

3. **Quote**: "The binder reads like the house." (line 50)
   - **Type**: Broken conceit / Literal-truth violation
   - **Severity**: High
   - **Explanation**: Literal meaning claims a paper binder reads like a physical building, which is nonsensical, intended meaning is content mirrors structure but uses impossible phrasing.

4. **Quote**: "Bones, foundation, envelope." (line 54)
   - **Type**: Broken conceit
   - **Severity**: Low
   - **Explanation**: "Bones" is a metaphor for structural components, using body-part terminology for building parts with weak direct connection.

5. **Quote**: "sometimes called the Knowledge Ladder" (line 79)
   - **Type**: Decorative language
   - **Severity**: Low
   - **Explanation**: "Knowledge Ladder" is a metaphor for reference markers; direct term "reference system" is clearer.

6. **Quote**: "Supporting text is well-placed accent, not padding." (line 121)
   - **Type**: Decorative language
   - **Severity**: Low
   - **Explanation**: "Accent" is a metaphor from typography/speech used to describe supplementary content with no functional need for figurative phrasing.

7. **Quote**: "Others carry technical depth, parameter ranges, decision tables, and code citations measured in dozens." (line 168)
   - **Type**: Figurative language standing in for plain language
   - **Severity**: Low
   - **Explanation**: "Carry" is a metaphor; chapters contain or include content, not "carry" it.

### Chapter 1 Summary:
The chapter is mostly clean plain technical writing, with isolated high-severity literal-truth violations (equating physical objects to abstract concepts) and minor low-severity decorative metaphors. Figurative drift is not pervasive.

### Chapters With Heaviest Figurative Drift
1. **Chapter1** (2 High, 1 Medium) – Most severe literal-truth violations and broken conceits.
2. **Chapters3,5** (3 Medium each) – Recurring personification ("tolerates") and idioms ("raises the stakes", "rule of thumb").
3. **Chapters4,6,7** (1 Medium each) – Isolated personification and idioms.
4. **Chapters2,8** (0 Medium/High) – Cleanest technical content.

### Recurring Patterns Across Chapters
1. **Unfixed personification**: Multiple instances of inanimate objects (concrete, structure, forces, winds, earthquakes) given human actions: "tolerates", "trying to lift", "find a path", "find the gap", "reject", "lift/shift". The previous personification retrofit missed these.
2. **Stock idioms/clichés**: Repeated use of "rule of thumb" (Chapters3,5), "raises the stakes" (Chapters3,5), "bright-line" (Chapter4), "closes the gap" (Chapter6), "line of defense" (Chapter7).
3. **Broken conceits**: Metaphorical applications of technical terms: "load-bearing" applied to words (Chapter4), "binder reads like house" (Chapter1), "house is sum of decisions" (Chapter1).
4. **Overused "carry/drive/drop" metaphors**: "Carries" used for handles/manages/documents (Chapters4,5,8), "drives" for determines (Chapters3,6), "dropped" for excluded (Chapter6).

### Additional Flagging
- The previous personification retrofit explicitly targeted giving inanimate things "intentions, feelings, opinions, demands" but missed "tolerates" (used 3+ times for concrete/structure) which is a clear personification of an inanimate material.
- Idiom use is concentrated in chapters with more prose (Chapters3,5) rather than pure code/table sections.
- Chapter1's high-severity literal-truth violations are the highest priority for correction, as they require readers to translate false literal meanings.
