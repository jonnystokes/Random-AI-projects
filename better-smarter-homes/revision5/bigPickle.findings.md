# Figurative-Drift Inventory: Chapters 1–8

Scan conducted 2026-05-08. Scope: literal-truth violations, figurative language, broken conceits, false poeticism, decorative language, sensory flourish, aphoristic phrasing, idiom/cliché in BSH revision 5 manuscript chapters 1–8.

## Chapter 1: Purpose, Principles, And How To Use This Book

**Finding 1** — Line 35: "build the shell so it can stay, and route the service systems so they can change"
- **Type:** other (mild personification)
- **Severity:** Low
- The shell does not "stay" as an act of will; it is designed to remain in service. Minor anthropomorphic framing.

**Finding 2** — Line 54: "Overbuild the parts that are hard to fix later. Bones, foundation, envelope."
- **Type:** idiom / metaphor
- **Severity:** Low
- "Bones" as a synecdoche for structural frame. Common in construction writing but a figurative substitute for "structural shell."

**Finding 3** — Line 56: "Keep service systems mortal."
- **Type:** literal-truth violation / personification
- **Severity:** Medium
- Service systems are not mortal. They have finite service lives. This is the kind of personification the previous pass should have caught; it gives human mortality to equipment.

**Finding 4** — Line 65: "They cannot be load-bearing for any basic house function."
- **Type:** figurative language (metaphorical extension of technical term)
- **Severity:** Low
- "Load-bearing" is a structural term applied metaphorically to software/cloud services. Acceptable as a conceptual parallel, but the literal claim is false: cloud services don't bear loads.

**Finding 5** — Line 73: "Vendor lock-in is a design failure, not a feature."
- **Type:** aphoristic phrasing
- **Severity:** Low
- Shaped to be quotable. The content is solid; the packaging performs.

**Finding 6** — Line 121: "well-placed accent, not padding"
- **Type:** decorative language
- **Severity:** Low
- Metaphor mixing registers (interior design "accent" applied to prose structure). The plain statement would be "supporting text is used only when it adds value."

**Summary:** Mostly clean, with isolated slips. The "mortal" line on service systems is the most notable holdover from the personification-era style. The chapter opens with plain, direct language and only drifts slightly in the principles section (§1.3–1.4). Figurative drift is not pervasive.

### Which Chapters Carry the Heaviest Figurative Drift

None carry heavy drift. Chapter 1 has the most findings (6) but all are Low except the Medium "mortal" line. Chapter 3 is the cleanest (4 findings, all Low). Chapters 4, 7, and 8 are very clean given their length.

### Patterns That Recur Across Chapters

1. **Metaphorical "load-bearing"** (Ch1, Ch4): Structural term borrowed for non-structural contexts. The writer reaches for this word as a rhetorical intensifier.
2. **Closing-summary aphorisms** (Ch1, Ch5): Section-ending summary lines and principle lists tend toward punchy, quotable formulations. A structural habit worth monitoring.
3. **Conventional domain metaphors** (Ch1, Ch2, Ch6, Ch7): "Lanes," "gates," "bones," "paths" — standard in construction writing, not concerning.
4. **No false poeticism**: The manuscript notably avoids ornate, straining-to-be-profound language.

### Side Effect Worth Noting

The prose in places reads slightly over-corrected toward a flat, legal-document register. Chapters 2, 3, 4, 7, and 8 read as though written under the instruction "no figurative language" followed so faithfully that natural voice and warmth were suppressed. The warmth was not coming from false poeticism; it came from sentence rhythm, declarative confidence, and occasional vivid phrasing. The pendulum may have swung slightly past the target.
