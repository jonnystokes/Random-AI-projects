# Findings Report: Chapters 1-8 Figurative Language Scan

## Overview

This report catalogs figurative language issues found in Chapters 1-8 of the manuscript. The issues were scanned for violations of the principle: clarity, precision, and material honesty. The prose should prioritize literal truth over poetic effect.

---

## Chapter 1: Purpose, Principles, And How To Use This Book

### Findings

1. **Quote:** "A house is the sum of many small decisions."
   - **Type:** Aphoristic phrasing
   - **Severity:** Medium
   - **Explanation:** A tidy aphorism shaped to be quotable. The meaning survives, but this is decorative framing rather than plain technical statement. The book could say "A house depends on many small decisions" without the figurative wrapper.

2. **Quote:** "When those decisions are scattered, coordination breaks down and quality suffers."
   - **Type:** Idiom
   - **Severity:** Low
   - **Explanation:** "Scattered" as a metaphor for disorganized. "Quality suffers" is mild figurative shorthand. Borderline but not urgent.

### Summary Chapter 1

Mostly clean. Isolated decorative slips. The prose largely holds the principle.

## Chapter 2: Oregon Code And Compliance Map

### Findings

1. **Quote:** "This chapter is the navigation map."
   - **Type:** Decorative language
   - **Severity:** Low
   - **Explanation:** "Navigation map" is a functional metaphor in this technical context. Acceptable as organizational vocabulary.

2. **Quote:** "A concrete house still contains the same systems as any other dwelling."
   - **Type:** Literal-truth violation (medium)
   - **Explanation:** "Contains" implies Container. A house doesn't contain systems the way a box contains objects - systems are DESIGNED INTO the house, not contained. This is a weak broken conceit.

3. **Quote:** "Concrete makes this rule especially important."
   - **Type:** False poeticism
   - **Severity:** Medium
   - **Explanation:** Making rules important is what humans do; concrete doesn't "make" anything important. Plain version: "This rule is especially important for concrete houses."

### Summary Chapter 2

Several instances of figurative framing where plain statement would serve better. A few broken conceits. Pattern: material-as-agent phrasing appears.

## Chapter 3: Land, Site, And Feasibility

### Findings

1. **Quote:** "The house design itself works across a wide range..."
   - **Type:** Personification
   - **Severity:** Low
   - **Explanation:** "Works" applied to design (inanimate). Acceptable trade usage but drifts from literal truth.

2. **Quote:** "A contour line does not show standing water on the surface."
   - **Type:** Literal-truth violation (borderline)
   - **Severity:** Low
   - **Explanation:** A contour line can't SHOW anything - it's a drawn symbol. People show information; lines represent data. Minor but worth flagging.

3. **Quote:** "The cost of a wrong land purchase shows up in every chapter..."
   - **Type:** Metaphor
   - **Severity:** Low
   - **Explanation:** Cost "shows up" personifies money as an active agent. Acceptable idiom but borders figurative.

### Summary Chapter 3

Light figurative drift. The chapter is mostly clean technical writing with occasional mild personification.

## Chapter 4: Legal, Permits, Owner-Builder Rules, And Inspections

### Findings

1. **Quote:** "The legal path runs alongside the physical build..."
   - **Type:** Metaphor
   - **Severity:** Low
   - **Explanation:** Path metaphor is standard in this context (code path, compliance path). Acceptable in technical register.

2. **Quote:** "The worst time to discover a permit problem is after concrete is poured..."
   - **Type:** False poeticism
   - **Severity:** Medium
   - **Explanation:** "The worst time" creates aphoristic framing around a practical observation. Also "discover a permit problem" is odd - problems aren't hidden treasures.

3. **Quote:** "A heavier-looking assembly is not automatically compliant."
   - **Type:** Literal-truth violation
   - **Severity:** Low
   - **Explanation:** "Heavier-looking" is strange - appearance doesn't determine compliance. Could say "A larger or more substantial-looking assembly..."

4. **Quote:** "None of these add up to a state-mandated waiting period."
   - **Type:** Idiom
   - **Severity:** Low
   - **Explanation:** Mathematical metaphor - "add up" - that's idiom.

### Summary Chapter 4

More figurative framing than earlier chapters. Idiom, mild personification, aphoristic titles. The legal content is solid but dressed in warmer phrasing than needed.

## Chapter 5: Soil, Ground Prep, Drainage, And Foundation Risk

### Findings

1. **Quote:** "Why The Ground Decides" (section title)
   - **Type:** False poeticism
   - **Severity:** High
   - **Explanation:** Ground doesn't "decide" - ground CONDITIONS determine outcomes. Personified title reaching for significance.

2. **Quote:** "Soil that is firm, uniform, dry enough, and documented simplifies the build."
   - **Type:** Personification
   - **Severity:** Low
   - **Explanation:** Soil "simplifies" is personification - soil doesn't simplify; good soil CONDITIONS make building simpler.

3. **Quote:** "Soil that is weak, wet, expansive, filled, or unpredictable can move a well-built foundation."
   - **Type:** Literal-truth violation
   - **Severity:** High
   - **Explanation:** Soil "can move" implies soil as active agent. This is incorrect. Could say "can cause movement of" or "can compromise."

4. **Quote:** "Concrete tolerates compressive load. It tolerates differential movement poorly."
   - **Type:** Personification
   - **Severity:** Medium
   - **Explanation:** Concrete doesn't tolerate - it's a material. Could say "Under compressive load, concrete performs well..."

### Summary Chapter 5

Notable figurative drift. Section title is personified. Several instances of material-as-agent framing. More pervasive than earlier chapters.

## Chapter 6: Structural System And Concrete Design

### Findings

1. **Quote:** "The structural system that turns site preparation and foundation work into a building."
   - **Type:** Metaphor
   - **Severity:** Low
   - **Explanation:** System "turns" work into building - implies transformation agency. Could say "produces" or "results in."

2. **Quote:** "A heavy assembly is not automatically a strong assembly."
   - **Type:** Aphoristic phrasing
   - **Severity:** Medium
   - **Explanation:** Parallel structure designed to be quotable. Both "heavy" and "strong" have shifted meanings (mass vs. structural capacity). This is clever but performs depth instead of delivering information.

3. **Quote:** "The building should be traceable in section."
   - **Type:** Literal-truth violation
   - **Severity:** Low
   - **Explanation:** Buildings can't "be" traceable - drawings are traceable. Figure of speech but misleading.

### Summary Chapter 6

Moderate figurative drift with some aphoristic phrasing. One notable parallel-construction that draws attention to itself.

## Chapter 7: Foundation, Basement, Slabs, And Concrete Placement

### Findings

1. **Quote:** "The foundation holds the house, spreads the weight onto the soil, and ties the structure to the ground so wind and earthquakes do not lift or shift it."
   - **Type:** Personification
   - **Severity:** High
   - **Explanation:** Foundation as active agent with multiple intentional actions: holds, spreads, ties. This is incorrect - foundations SUPPORT, they don't actively hold or tie.

2. **Quote:** "Plan everything that goes inside the foundation before the first pour."
   - **Type:** Metaphor
   - **Severity:** Low
   - **Explanation:** "Inside the foundation" - infrastructure IS the foundation, not inside it. Minor spatial confusion.

3. **Quote:** "...concrete is in..." (similar phrasing throughout chapter)
   - **Type:** Idiom
   - **Severity:** Low
   - **Explanation:** Standard but "in" is spatial for embedded/existing.

### Summary Chapter 7

Moderate personification, especially in Simple blocks. The content is accurate but framed with figurative warmth.

## Chapter 8: Envelope, Waterproofing, Thermal Performance, And Radon

### Findings

1. **Quote:** "The envelope is the building shell that separates the conditioned interior from the exterior environment."
   - **Type:** Metaphor (acceptable technical term)
   - **Severity:** None
   - **Explanation:** "Envelope" is standard vocabulary.

2. **Quote:** "A control layer that stops at a corner, transition, or penetration creates a failure point that no amount of effort elsewhere can compensate for."
   - **Type:** False poeticism
   - **Severity:** Medium
   - **Explanation:** "No amount of effort can compensate" is figurative for "cannot be compensated by other measures." The sentence performs importance rather than stating it plainly.

3. **Quote:** "When all five layers are continuous from one part of the house to the next, the envelope works."
   - **Type:** Personification
   - **Severity:** Medium
   - **Explanation:** Envelope "works" as if it's an actor.

4. **Quote:** "The envelope handles five jobs. Each job has a dedicated control layer..."
   - **Type:** Personification
   - **Severity:** Medium
   - **Explanation:** Envelope as agent with jobs. This recurs throughout the chapter.

5. **Quote:** "The pencil test: starting at any wall section, can a single line be drawn around the building..."
   - **Type:** Literal-truth violation
   - **Severity:** Low
   - **Explanation:** "Can a single line be drawn" - lines don't draw themselves. People draw lines.

### Summary Chapter 8

Notable figurative drift with personification of the envelope. Some aphoristic framing. Recurring pattern of material-as-agent.
