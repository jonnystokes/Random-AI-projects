9. What are Amazon KDP's and IngramSpark's current royalty structures, fees, and print-cost tables, verified directly against kdp.amazon.com and ingramspark.com rather than third-party summaries?
Lands in: Business and Brand Framework. [COST]

**Answered (2026-07-14):** Verified directly against KDP's own help pages. **KDP paperback/hardcover royalty:** formula is (royalty rate x list price) - printing cost = royalty. Two royalty-rate tiers apply by list price on Amazon.com: 50% royalty for titles priced at $9.98 or below, 60% royalty for titles priced at $9.99 or above (thresholds vary by Amazon marketplace/currency). If "expanded distribution" (non-Amazon retail/library channels) is enabled, the rate drops to 40% of list price minus printing cost. Printing cost itself depends on marketplace, page count, and ink type (color vs. black-and-white), calculated via KDP's own printing-cost calculator rather than a single flat published formula on the page fetched. Minimum list price is set by KDP so that royalty (at the applicable rate) covers the printing cost — a heavily illustrated technical book with high page count and color interior will therefore have a materially higher minimum price than a plain-text paperback. **KDP ebook royalty:** two options — 35% of list price (all territories, including public-domain works, which are restricted to this option) or 70% of list price less a delivery cost (KDP states average delivery cost is $0.06/unit but varies by file size, so a large illustrated ebook with many images would carry a materially higher-than-average delivery cost), with the 70% rate available only in designated "70% territories" and outside those, sales revert to 35%. **IngramSpark (verified against ingramspark.com and its current price sheet):** print cost for black-and-white paperback interior is approximately $0.90 fixed plus roughly $0.013 per page (so a 300-page B&W paperback costs roughly $4.80 to print) — color interior (relevant for a heavily illustrated technical manual) costs substantially more per page, though the exact current color per-page rate was not itself re-derived in this pass and should be pulled from IngramSpark's live price sheet before quoting a number. Wholesale/bookstore discount is typically set at 55% (required for real bookstore/library shelf placement and returns eligibility) or as low as 50-35% if only using IngramSpark for online/non-Amazon distribution; IngramSpark additionally charges a 1.5% "market access fee" on routed (wholesale-channel) sales, deducted from the author's compensation. IngramSpark's compensation formula is: list price minus wholesale discount minus print cost minus the market-access fee (where applicable) = author payment per copy.

11. Is print-on-demand adequate for a heavily illustrated technical manual with diagrams, tables, and code citations, or does the format require offset printing and a different cost/distribution model?
Lands in: Business and Brand Framework. [MATERIALS/INGREDIENTS] [COST]

**Answered (2026-07-14):** Both models are viable, but they suit different scales and both have real technical-content tradeoffs, per print-industry sources compared in this pass. **Print-on-demand (KDP, IngramSpark):** no setup cost and no minimum order, so it is the right model for uncertain or low initial demand (the realistic case for a first-time technical/reference title before sales are proven) — but color-interior POD printing is markedly more expensive per unit than offset at any real volume, and POD's per-unit cost never drops with volume, since each copy is produced individually regardless of total quantity ordered. **Offset printing:** requires a substantial print run to be economical — sources place the offset break-even point anywhere from roughly 400 to 1,000 copies depending on the source and the specific job's complexity, with per-book cost for a 500-copy run starting around $5.00/copy and dropping to as low as $1.50/copy at 5,000+ copies — a real cost advantage at volume, but it requires committing capital to inventory before sales are proven, plus warehousing and fulfillment logistics POD does not require, and offset lead time runs 2-3 weeks versus POD's on-demand turnaround. Offset also supports print-quality options POD generally cannot match at any price — specialty paper stocks, custom bindings, foil stamping, embossing, and higher-fidelity color/diagram reproduction — which matters for a book whose value partly depends on diagram legibility. **The practical answer for this project:** launch on POD (KDP + IngramSpark) to validate demand and avoid unsold inventory risk, since the book's actual sales volume is unproven and any offset print run is a sunk cost if it does not sell; migrate some or all print volume to a short-run offset printer only once a specific title's ongoing unit volume is established at a level (the roughly 400-1,000-copy range cited above) where the per-unit savings and quality upgrade justify committing to inventory.
