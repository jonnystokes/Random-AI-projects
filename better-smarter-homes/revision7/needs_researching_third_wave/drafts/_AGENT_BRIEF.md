## The expansion philosophy (apply it)

- Only real information. No fluff. Every question must lead to a fact, a method, a material, a tool, a cost, a problem, or a solution the builder can actually use on THIS house.
- Go wide and worldwide: owner-builder and trade forums, homesteading and off-grid practice, international and vernacular building, university research, manufacturer product categories, historic DIY literature, and code where it genuinely governs. Real experience is a first-class source here, alongside code.
- The heaviest expansion is NON-CODE: creative-but-real building ideas, options laid out in full, the exact tools, the exact ingredients, the exact methods, and the real problems people hit with the solutions they found. Add missing code too, but the growth is on the non-code side.
- Ways to expand a topic: find the missing detail; zoom into the exact tool, ingredient, method, or failure that bites people; lay out every option side by side; and think outside the box with creative-but-real ideas that suit a long-life, independent, owner-built concrete home.

## Writing rules for your research text (enforced)

Write your notes and questions plainly, with the same discipline the book uses, so the next stage never has to strip poetry out of your text:

- State facts and questions. Nothing else. No metaphor, no analogy (no comparison to any object outside the thing under discussion), no personification of materials or documents, no aphorisms or wisdom lines, no marketing or blurb language, no rhetorical questions for effect, no balanced-clause performance, no em dashes.
- No invented citations, values, or engineering numbers. Any number you have not verified is a `Leads:` vein, labeled unverified.
- Reference chapters by topic name, never by number.
- Do not answer the questions. Do not draft manuscript sentences. If you record a candidate anchor fact, it must obey the Catalog and be marked unverified.

The point of these rules: the research you hand back must be clean, plain, and usable, not something a writer has to de-poeticize later.
