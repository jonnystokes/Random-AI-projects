# Appendix B: Glossary

This glossary defines the main terms used in the book. It is grouped by topic so the reader can scan it like a field reference.

This glossary keeps the important vocabulary but avoids turning definitions into unverified code, engineering, legal, product, or warranty instructions.

## How To Use This Glossary

**Simple**

Use this glossary when a word slows you down.

A definition should help you keep reading. It should not replace the chapter that explains the work.

**Applied**

If a term affects a real build decision, do not stop at the glossary. Go back to the chapter, approved plans, code source, product instructions, or professional review.

Examples:

- Use the glossary to learn what `DCP` means.
- Use the soil chapter and final research before relying on a DCP result.
- Use the glossary to learn what `GFCI` means.
- Use the electrical code path before choosing where protection is required.

**Professional**

The glossary is intentionally plain. It gives working meanings, not full standards-language definitions.

Where a term has multiple trade meanings, use the meaning that fits the chapter context.

**Code**

Glossary definitions do not create requirements. The current code, approved plans, product listings, manufacturer instructions, engineer, building official, and required inspections control the final answer.

## Site, Soil, And Ground Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Active layer | Soil depth range that changes moisture and volume seasonally. Important for expansive clay risk. |
| Alluvium | Soil or sediment deposited by flowing water. Often variable across a site. |
| Atterberg limits | Lab measures that describe fine-grained soil behavior at different moisture contents. Used to understand clay plasticity. |
| Backfill | Soil or aggregate placed back into an excavation after foundation, drain, or utility work. |
| Bearing | The ability of soil or rock to support load without unacceptable movement. Final bearing values need proper verification. |
| Bedrock | Rock below soil. Competent bedrock may support high loads, but actual conditions must be verified. |
| Capillary break | Layer or material that interrupts water wicking upward through pores. Common below slabs and foundations. |
| Cohesive soil | Soil, usually clay-rich, whose particles stick together. It can stand briefly in cuts but can also shrink, swell, or hold water. |
| Collapsible soil | Soil that loses volume when wetted or loaded. Needs professional attention before foundation design. |
| Compaction | Mechanical densification of soil or aggregate. Used to improve support and reduce settlement. |
| DCP | Dynamic cone penetrometer. A field tool used for soil screening and comparison. Do not convert results into design values without accepted support. |
| Differential settlement | Uneven foundation movement. Small uneven movement can damage a building more than larger uniform movement. |
| Engineered fill | Fill placed, compacted, tested, and documented to meet a design requirement. |
| Expansive clay | Clay soil that swells when wet and shrinks when dry. It can damage foundations if not handled correctly. |
| Geotechnical engineer | Licensed professional who evaluates soil, groundwater, slope, settlement, bearing, and foundation risk. |
| Groundwater | Water below the ground surface. It affects excavation, drainage, waterproofing, slabs, basements, and foundation design. |
| Liquefaction | Loss of soil strength during shaking in certain saturated loose soils. Requires site-specific evaluation where risk exists. |
| Percolation test | Test related to soil absorption for septic design. The septic authority controls the accepted method. |
| Proctor test | Lab test used to define moisture-density behavior for compacted soil. Field compaction targets depend on the specified method. |
| Slope stability | Whether a slope can stay stable under natural and building loads. Landslide-prone sites need careful review. |
| Water table | Level where soil or rock is saturated. It can change seasonally. |

## Concrete, Masonry, And Structure Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Admixture | Material added to concrete to change workability, set, durability, corrosion resistance, or other behavior. |
| Aggregate | Sand, gravel, crushed stone, or similar material in concrete or base layers. |
| Beam | Horizontal structural member that carries load to supports. |
| Bond beam | Reinforced horizontal masonry or concrete element that ties wall sections together. |
| Cantilever | Structural member supported at one end and extending beyond that support. |
| Cement | Binder ingredient in concrete and mortar. Cement is not the same thing as concrete. |
| CMU | Concrete masonry unit. Commonly called concrete block. |
| Concrete | Mixture of cementitious binder, water, aggregate, and possible admixtures that hardens by hydration. |
| Consolidation | In concrete, removal of trapped air during placement. In soil, slow volume change under load as pore water moves. Context matters. |
| Control joint | Planned weak line or movement joint used to manage cracking or movement. |
| Cover | Distance from concrete surface to reinforcement. Cover protects steel and affects durability. |
| Curing | Maintaining proper moisture and temperature so concrete can gain strength and durability. |
| Cylinder test | Concrete strength test made from field samples and tested by accepted methods. Exact procedures belong to standards and lab requirements. |
| Formwork | Temporary structure that shapes and supports concrete until it can support itself. |
| Grout | In masonry, a flowable cementitious fill placed in block cores or cavities. In tile, the material filling joints between tiles. |
| Lintel | Structural member over an opening, such as a door or window. |
| Masonry | Construction using units such as block, brick, or stone, often with mortar and reinforcement. |
| Mockup | Small or full-scale sample built before the main work to test fit, sequence, appearance, or performance. |
| Mortar | Material used to bed and bond masonry units. |
| Parapet | Wall extension above a roof edge. It needs careful cap, flashing, drainage, and structural detailing. |
| Rebar | Reinforcing bar embedded in concrete or masonry to resist tension and control cracking. |
| Reinforced concrete | Concrete with embedded reinforcement designed to work with it structurally. |
| Slump | Field measure of fresh concrete consistency. It is not a complete measure of concrete quality. |
| Topping slab | Thin slab layer placed over another structural or service layer. |
| Wythe | One vertical layer of masonry in a wall. A cavity wall can have multiple wythes. |

## Envelope, Water, Energy, And Indoor-Air Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ACH50 | Air changes per hour at the standard blower-door pressure difference. Used to compare envelope leakage. |
| Air barrier | Continuous layer or assembly that controls air movement through the building envelope. |
| Blower door | Fan-based diagnostic test used to measure and find envelope air leakage. |
| Building envelope | Boundary between conditioned interior space and exterior or unconditioned conditions. |
| Capillary action | Water movement through small pores or gaps. Concrete, masonry, soil, and some materials can wick water. |
| Condensation | Water forming when humid air meets a surface below dew point. |
| Control layer | Layer that manages water, air, vapor, heat, or soil gas. Good assemblies have continuous control layers. |
| Dew point | Temperature where air becomes saturated and water begins to condense. |
| Drainage plane | Layer or path that lets water drain out of an assembly. |
| Envelope | Short form for building envelope. |
| Flashing | Material and detailing that directs water out and away from openings, joints, and transitions. |
| Insulation | Material that slows heat flow. Type, location, continuity, moisture behavior, and fire protection matter. |
| R-value | Resistance to heat flow. Higher means more resistance, but assembly performance depends on continuity and detailing. |
| Radon | Radioactive soil gas that can enter buildings. Testing and mitigation requirements depend on current official sources and site conditions. |
| Rainscreen | Cladding approach with a drainage and drying gap behind the exterior surface. |
| Thermal bridge | Material path that bypasses insulation and moves heat faster than surrounding assembly. |
| Thermal mass | Material capacity to store heat. Concrete has high thermal mass, but comfort depends on the whole system. |
| U-factor | Heat transfer rate through an assembly or product. Lower means less heat transfer. |
| Vapor barrier | Material that strongly limits vapor diffusion. Use must fit the climate and assembly. |
| Vapor retarder | Material that slows vapor diffusion. The term is often more accurate than vapor barrier. |
| Weather barrier | Layer that resists bulk water and wind-driven moisture while allowing the assembly to work as designed. |
| Waterproofing | System intended to stop liquid water under more demanding conditions than damp-proofing. |

## Plumbing, Water, And Wastewater Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| AAV | Air admittance valve. A mechanical venting device. Acceptance depends on code and local interpretation. |
| Backflow preventer | Device or assembly that helps prevent non-potable water from flowing into potable water. |
| Cleanout | Access point for clearing a drain line. Location and clearance are code-controlled. |
| Cross-connection | Connection that can let non-potable water contaminate potable water. |
| DWV | Drain, waste, and vent. The plumbing system that removes wastewater and protects trap seals. |
| Fixture | Plumbing device such as sink, toilet, shower, tub, hose bib, or floor drain. |
| Fixture unit | Code method for estimating plumbing load. Exact use belongs to the current plumbing code. |
| Graywater | Wastewater from selected fixtures that may be reused or disposed under specific rules. Definitions and rules vary by jurisdiction. |
| Hose bib | Exterior or utility faucet. It may need frost protection, backflow protection, and drainage planning. |
| Manifold | Distribution point with multiple branches, often used for water supply or hydronic systems. |
| PEX | Cross-linked polyethylene tubing used for water distribution or radiant systems. Product type and listing matter. |
| PRV | Pressure reducing valve or pressure regulator, depending on context. Used to control excessive water pressure. |
| Potable water | Water intended to be safe for drinking and domestic use. |
| Sump pump | Pump used to remove collected water from a sump basin. Backup and discharge planning matter. |
| Trap | Water-seal fitting that blocks sewer gas from entering the building. |
| Trap arm | Pipe between a trap and its vented drain connection. Limits are code-controlled. |
| Vent | Plumbing pipe path that protects trap seals and drainage flow by admitting air. |
| Water heater | Equipment that heats domestic water. Installation depends on code, energy source, product listing, and safety devices. |
| Well | Private water source drilled or constructed under water-resource rules. Records and water testing matter. |

## Electrical, Power, And Low-Voltage Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| AC | Alternating current. Utility power is AC. |
| AFCI | Arc fault circuit interrupter. Protection device intended to reduce fire risk from arcing faults. |
| AWG | American Wire Gauge. Wire size system where smaller gauge numbers generally mean larger conductors. |
| Bonding | Connecting conductive parts so dangerous voltage differences do not develop between them. |
| Branch circuit | Wiring from a panel breaker or overcurrent device to outlets, lights, or equipment. |
| Breaker | Overcurrent protection device that opens a circuit during overload or fault conditions. |
| Conductor | Wire or cable element that carries electrical current. |
| Conduit | Raceway that protects and routes conductors or cables. |
| DC | Direct current. Batteries, solar modules, and many electronics use DC internally. |
| Disconnect | Device that allows equipment or circuits to be isolated from power. |
| Feeder | Conductors that carry power from one panel or distribution point to another. |
| GEC | Grounding electrode conductor. Connects grounding electrode system to service equipment. |
| GFCI | Ground fault circuit interrupter. Protection device intended to reduce shock risk. |
| Grounding | Connection of electrical system to earth and grounding electrodes as required by code. |
| Low-voltage | Wiring and systems operating below line-voltage power levels, such as data, controls, cameras, alarms, or sensors. |
| NEC | National Electrical Code, used as the model base for Oregon's electrical code. |
| Panel | Electrical distribution cabinet containing breakers or other protective devices. |
| PoE | Power over Ethernet. Method for carrying power and data over network cable to compatible devices. |
| Receptacle | Outlet device where cord-connected equipment plugs in. |
| Service | Utility power connection and service equipment serving the building. |
| SPD | Surge protective device. Equipment intended to reduce damaging voltage surges. |
| Subpanel | Panel fed from another panel rather than directly from utility service. |
| Ufer ground | Common name for a concrete-encased grounding electrode. |
| UPS | Uninterruptible power supply. Battery-backed equipment that supports loads during short power interruptions. |
| Voltage drop | Reduction in voltage along conductors due to resistance and load. It affects circuit design and equipment performance. |

## HVAC, Ventilation, And Indoor-Air Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Air handler | Equipment that moves air through ductwork or coils. This house may use less air distribution than a conventional forced-air house. |
| BTU | British thermal unit. Unit of heat energy used in HVAC capacity discussions. |
| CFM | Cubic feet per minute. Airflow measurement. |
| Commissioning | Testing and adjustment process that proves systems work as intended together. |
| Condensate | Water produced when humid air is cooled below dew point or by certain equipment. It needs drainage. |
| COP | Coefficient of performance. Heat-pump efficiency ratio. Exact performance depends on conditions. |
| Dehumidifier | Equipment that removes moisture from indoor air. |
| ERV | Energy recovery ventilator. Ventilation equipment that transfers heat and some moisture between outgoing and incoming air streams. |
| Exhaust | Air removed from a space, such as bathroom, kitchen, dryer, or equipment exhaust. |
| Heat pump | Equipment that moves heat rather than making heat directly. Can heat and sometimes cool. |
| HRV | Heat recovery ventilator. Ventilation equipment that transfers heat between outgoing and incoming air streams. |
| HVAC | Heating, ventilation, and air conditioning. In this book it also includes humidity, filtration, exhaust, controls, and comfort. |
| Hydronic | System using water or water-based fluid to move heat. |
| IAQ | Indoor air quality. Covers pollutants, humidity, ventilation, filtration, combustion risk, and occupant health. |
| Makeup air | Replacement air supplied when exhaust equipment removes air from the house. |
| Radiant heating or cooling | Comfort system that uses warm or cool surfaces to exchange heat with occupants and spaces. |
| Refrigerant | Fluid used in heat pumps and cooling systems. Work with refrigerants is controlled and not a casual DIY task. |
| Register or diffuser | Air terminal device that supplies or returns air. |
| Setpoint | Target value for a control system, such as temperature or humidity. |
| Thermostat | Control device that senses temperature and calls for heating or cooling. |
| Zone | Area controlled separately for heating, cooling, ventilation, or hydronic flow. |

## Deferred Glossary Expansion

The full source glossary contains many additional product names, brand names, exact values, standards, code references, and niche trade terms. Those should be reviewed during the research pass before they are added back into the final reader glossary.

Add a term to the final glossary only when it is:

- Used in this manuscript.
- Important for reader understanding.
- Defined without unsupported technical claims.
- Not better handled inside a chapter.
- Not a stale product or brand reference.
- Not a copyrighted code or standards excerpt.

The glossary stays last.
