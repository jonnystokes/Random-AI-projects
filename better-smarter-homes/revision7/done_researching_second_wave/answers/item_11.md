**Answered (2026-07-09):**

**Reconciled statement (use this wording and these anchors in every chapter).** An Oregon owner may personally perform the plumbing, electrical, and mechanical work on the owner's own dwelling without holding any trade license, under three separate authorities: plumbing under ORS 693.020(1)(a) together with ORS 447.060(1)(a); electrical under ORS 479.540(1), which adds the conditions that the property be residential or farm property owned by the person or a member of the person's immediate family and "not intended for sale, exchange, lease or rent"; and mechanical, for which Oregon statute creates no trade license at all, so the only gate is the mechanical permit at ORSC Section R105.1, with contractor (CCB) licensing inapplicable to owners under ORS 701.010(7). None of the three waives permits or inspections: ORS 693.020(4) and ORS 447.020(1) keep plumbing permits and inspections in force, ORS 479.540(1)(a) with ORS 479.550(1) keeps electrical permits in force, and ORSC R105.1 is itself the mechanical permit requirement.

**(a) Plumbing: the license exemption is ORS 693.020(1)(a), with ORS 447.060 as the companion.** ORS 693.020 (Persons and activities not regulated under chapter), subsection (1)(a): this chapter does not apply to "A person working on a building or premises owned by the person, regardless of whether the person holds a license under this chapter, if the person complies with all the rules adopted under this chapter and ORS 447.010 to 447.156 and ORS chapter 455." (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors693.html; https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors693.html). Conditions: the person must own the building or premises (occupancy is not required and is not mentioned), and the work must comply with the plumbing rules, the plumbing permit and inspection statutes (ORS 447.010 to 447.156), and the state building code statutes (ORS chapter 455). There is no for-sale or for-rent condition on a residence in the plumbing statute. The only sale-related restriction is ORS 693.020(2), and it reaches commercial and industrial buildings only: "Subsection (1)(a) to (d) of this section does not allow a person other than a journeyman plumber or apprentice plumber to install, remodel or alter plumbing in a commercial or industrial building being constructed or offered for sale, exchange, rent or lease," where "install, remodel or alter" means work inside a wall, floor, crawl space or ceiling, or a change in the configuration of a plumbing system (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors693.html). Permits and inspections survive the exemption by the statute's own terms: ORS 693.020(4): "Except as provided in subsection (1)(d) of this section, nothing in this section exempts a person from the plumbing inspection requirements of ORS 447.010 to 447.156." A secondary repair-only exemption is ORS 693.020(1)(e) (a person who owns, leases or operates residential property may repair, or use regular employees to repair, existing plumbing; "repair" defined at (1)(e)(A) as restoring the existing system to safe and sanitary operating condition).

**The exemption partly lives in ORS 447 as well.** ORS 447.060(1)(a): "ORS 447.010 to 447.156 do not apply to a person: (a) Engaging in plumbing work when not so engaged for hire," and (1)(b) extends this to work by the person's regular employees on property the person owns, leases or operates (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors447.html; https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors447.html). The business-level license the owner is being excused from is the plumbing contractor license at ORS 447.040(1); the trade-level license is the journeyman plumber license at ORS 693.030(1). The installation itself must still conform regardless of who performs it: ORS 447.020(1) requires all installations of plumbing and drainage in buildings in this state to be made in accordance with ORS 447.010 to 447.156, 447.992 and ORS chapter 455 (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors447.html).

**(b) Electrical: the license exemption is ORS 479.540(1).** Quote: "Except as otherwise provided in this subsection, a person is not required to obtain a license to make an electrical installation on residential or farm property that is owned by the person or a member of the person's immediate family if the property is not intended for sale, exchange, lease or rent." (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors479.html; https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors479.html). Conditions: (1) residential or farm property; (2) owned by the person or a member of the person's immediate family (ownership, not occupancy; "immediate family" is used but not defined in the ORS 479.530 definitions list); (3) the property is not intended for sale, exchange, lease or rent at the time of the work. Two subsections qualify it: ORS 479.540(1)(a): the exemption "does not exempt the work performed by the person from having to comply with the requirements for such work under ORS chapter 455 or this chapter and rules adopted thereunder," which keeps permits, inspections and the electrical code in force; and ORS 479.540(1)(b): if the residence is for rent, lease, sale or exchange, the exemption shrinks to maintenance work on existing installations and does not cover "new electrical installations or substantial alterations" (with a narrow landlord carve-out for replacing an existing garbage disposal, dishwasher or electric water heater of 30 amps or less, single phase). The permit chain for the owner: ORS 479.550(1): "Except as provided in ORS 479.540, no person shall work on any new electrical installation for which a permit has not been issued," and ORS 479.560(1)(a) directs the Department of Consumer and Business Services or its designated agent to issue a permit to "Any applicant who has complied with ORS 479.510 to 479.945 and the rules issued thereunder, covering an electrical installation to be made on residential or farm property owned by the applicant or a member of the applicant's immediate family as allowed under ORS 479.540 (1)" (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors479.html). A separate permit-side exemption, ORS 479.540(15), waives the permit for maintenance-only work on existing electrical installations on residential property owned by the person or immediate family, but expressly not for new installations or substantial alterations.

**(c) Mechanical: no trade license exists; the owner takes the permit directly under the ORSC.** No Oregon statute creates a mechanical trade license parallel to ORS chapter 693/447 (plumbing) or ORS 479.630 (electrical); the mechanical side is a permit-and-inspection program only (ORS 455.058(1) lists "electrical, gas, mechanical, elevator, boiler, plumbing or other systems" as the permit programs; the statute is reprinted after Section R105.4 in the ORSC chapter 1 PDF, C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf). For a dwelling, the governing permit text is 2023 ORSC Section R105.1: "Any owner or owner's authorized agent who intends to construct, enlarge, alter, repair, move or change the occupancy of a building or structure, or to erect, install, enlarge, alter, repair, remove, convert or replace any gas or mechanical system, the installation of which is regulated by this code, or to cause any such work to be performed, shall first make application to the building official and obtain the required permit" (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf). The owner is therefore an eligible mechanical and gas permit applicant by the code's own wording. Permit-exempt mechanical work is listed in ORSC R105.2 under "Mechanical:" items 1 through 8 (portable heating, cooking, clothes-drying and ventilation appliances; portable cooling units; steam, hot- or chilled-water piping within regulated equipment; replacement of any minor part that does not alter approval or make equipment unsafe; portable evaporative coolers; self-contained refrigeration systems with 10 pounds or less of refrigerant or motors of 1 horsepower or less; portable fuel-cell appliances), and ORSC R105.2.1 allows emergency mechanical replacements and repairs with the permit application submitted within 5 working business days (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf). On the rule side, OAR chapter 918 division 440 (Mechanical Specialty Code) contains no licensing or homeowner restriction: the division adopts the Oregon Mechanical Specialty Code (OAR 918-440-0010), amends it (918-440-0012) and sets fees (918-440-0050), and its only certification rule, OAR 918-440-0015 (Refrigeration Installer Certification, brazing and welding on refrigeration piping), states "Refrigeration systems installed in dwelling units regulated under the Oregon Residential Specialty Code are exempt from this rule" (https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_918-440-0015; division index https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_chapter_918_division_440; SOS source https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/displayDivisionRules.action?selectedDivision=4168; local index C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/oar/oar-918-440.html). Business-level licensing does not reach the owner either: ORS 701.021(1) requires a CCB license only of a person who undertakes work "as a contractor," and ORS 701.010(7) exempts "A person performing work on a property that person owns or performing work as the owner's employee, whether the property is occupied by the owner or not, or a person performing work on that person's residence, whether or not that person owns the residence," except where "the work is performed in the pursuit of an independent business with the intent of offering the structure for sale before, upon or after completion" (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors701.html; https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors701.html).

**Boiler caution for hydronic systems.** A boiler or pressure vessel in a private residence is exempt from the Boiler and Pressure Vessel Law "except as to all provisions relating to construction, installation, alteration or repair and to installation permits" (ORS 480.525(3)(c)), and the boiler contractor license attaches only to "A person engaging in the business of installing, repairing or altering boilers or pressure vessels" (ORS 480.630(1)); ORS 480.525(5) further lets the department assign single-family boiler inspections to specialty-code inspectors and exempts those units from periodic inspection (ORS 480.560) and operating permits (ORS 480.585) (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors480.html). Chapters discussing hydronic heat should flag the installation permit as still required.

**The asymmetry chapters must not blur.** Only the electrical exemption carries the intent test ("not intended for sale, exchange, lease or rent," ORS 479.540(1)); the plumbing exemption has no residential for-sale condition (its sale language, ORS 693.020(2), reaches commercial and industrial buildings only); the mechanical side has no license to be exempted from. All three trades still require permits and inspections for an owner-builder's new dwelling work.
