### 6 — Electrical Materials

#### 6.1 Service Equipment (Meter Base, Main Disconnect)

**What's specified:** Meter base sized for 320–400A service, main disconnect within sight of the meter (NEC 230.85), interconnect box housing the assembly. See the service entry discussion in the Electrical Power and Lighting chapter.

**Brands:**
- **Square D / Schneider Electric** — broad lineup, dominant US brand
- **Eaton** — alternative full-line manufacturer
- **Siemens** — alternative
- **Milbank** — meter sockets and combo units (premium)

**Suppliers:**
- **Electrical supply houses** — Platt Electric Supply (largest in Pacific NW), Rexel, CED, Border States
- **Big-box stores** — limited selection but stock common items
- **Online** — for specialty configurations

**Pricing (2026, rough):**
- 320–400A meter-main combo (outdoor): $1,500–$3,500
- Separate meter base: $300–$600
- Main breaker: $300–$1,000 (sized to service)

#### 6.2 Main and Sub Panels

**What's specified:** Main distribution panel with 40+ breaker spaces, plus subpanels per floor. See the panel and distribution discussion in the Electrical Power and Lighting chapter.

**Brands:**
- **Square D QO** — premium residential, the gold standard for quality and breaker availability
- **Square D Homeline** — mid-tier residential, less expensive than QO
- **Eaton BR** — mid-tier alternative to Homeline
- **Eaton CH** — premium alternative to QO
- **Siemens** — strong alternative across tiers
- **Leviton** (smart panels) — newer, integrated breaker monitoring

**Selection note:** **Pick a brand and stick with it throughout the project.** Breakers are not cross-compatible between brands. Square D QO and Eaton CH are the highest-quality options.

**Suppliers:**
- **Electrical supply houses** — best pricing for full panels
- **Big-box stores** — common panels in stock

**Pricing (2026, rough):**
- 200A main panel (40 spaces): $200–$500
- 200A subpanel: $100–$300
- Smart panels (Span, Leviton): $3,000–$8,000

#### 6.3 Circuit Breakers

**What's specified:** Per design — 15A and 20A general; 30A, 40A, 50A as needed for appliances; AFCI/GFCI combination for habitable spaces. See the GFCI and AFCI protection discussion in the Electrical Power and Lighting chapter.

**Important:** Match breakers to panel brand. Square D QO breakers don't fit Eaton CH panels and vice versa.

**Pricing (2026, rough):**
- Standard 20A breaker: $5–$15
- AFCI 20A: $40–$70
- GFCI 20A: $40–$70
- Combination AFCI/GFCI 20A: $50–$80
- Larger amperage (30A–60A): $20–$80
- Tandem breakers (where allowed): $20–$40

#### 6.4 Conduit (EMT, PVC, RMC)

**What's specified:** Conduit for most wiring runs, as described in the wiring-route discussion in the Electrical Power and Lighting chapter.

**Materials:**
- **EMT (Electrical Metallic Tubing)** — thin-wall steel, indoor and protected outdoor; stocked in 1/2 inch through 4 inch
- **RMC (Rigid Metal Conduit)** — heavy-wall steel, demanding applications; stocked in similar sizes
- **PVC Schedule 40 / Schedule 80** — for outdoor, underground, corrosive areas; standard residential is Schedule 40

**Brands:**
- **Allied Tube & Conduit** — major EMT producer
- **Wheatland Tube** — alternative
- **Carlon (Lamson & Sessions)** — primary PVC conduit brand

**Suppliers:** Electrical supply houses, big-box stores.

**Pricing (2026, rough):**
- 1/2-inch EMT 10 ft: $4–$8
- 3/4-inch EMT 10 ft: $6–$11
- 1-inch EMT 10 ft: $9–$15
- 2-inch PVC Schedule 40 10 ft: $8–$15

#### 6.5 Conductors / Wire

### 7 — HVAC Equipment and Materials

#### 7.1 Ground-Source Heat Pump (GSHP)

**What's specified:** Black-box heat exchanger — final selection at build time. See the ground-loop heat exchanger discussion in the HVAC, Ventilation, and Air Quality chapter.

**Brands (for reference; specifics deferred):**
- **WaterFurnace** — strong residential lineup, broad dealer network
- **ClimateMaster** — broad lineup, Oklahoma-based

- **Carrier Aquazone** — Carrier's GSHP line
- **Trane Envirowise** — Trane's GSHP line
- **Hydron Module** — premium

- COP ≥ 3.5 (heating); EER ≥ 15 (cooling)

- **Mountain Plumbing & Heating** (Pacific NW area, GSHP specialist)
- Local HVAC contractors with IGSHPA certification — limited number in Oregon, search by ZIP code on IGSHPA's website

**Pricing (2026, rough):**
- Equipment only (3-ton): $5,000–$10,000

This section covers the tools the owner-builder needs to acquire (or rent) for the build. The "tools paid forward" — meaning many of these tools last for multiple builds and many years of homeownership — is part of the case for owner-building.

#### 10.1 Power Tools — Essential

**Cordless platform:** Pick one and stick with it (DeWalt 20V Max, Milwaukee M18, Makita LXT, Ryobi One+ all work). Battery sharing across tools matters.

**Must-have power tools:**
- **Drill/driver + impact driver** combo
- **Circular saw** (for cutting wood, light metal)
- **Reciprocating saw** (Sawzall) for demolition and rough cuts
- **Angle grinder** (4.5 inch) for cutting metal, masonry, rebar
- **Oscillating multi-tool** for fine cuts and detail work
- **Random orbital sander**
- **Shop vacuum**
- **Miter saw / chop saw** (10 inch or 12 inch)

**Pricing (2026, rough) for a starter cordless kit:**
- Mid-tier brand combo kit: $300–$600
- Building out to full toolset: $2,000–$5,000

#### 10.2 Power Tools — Specialized for Masonry

- **Masonry / wet saw** for cutting CMU and tile
- **Hammer drill** with masonry bits for anchoring
- **Diamond cutoff saw** for clean CMU cuts (pricier than a standard masonry saw but worth it for visible cuts)
- **Mortar mixer** (gas or electric)
- **Power trowel** for slab finishing (can rent)

#### 10.3 Concrete Tools

- **Bull float** for screeding wet concrete
- **Magnesium float**
- **Wood float**
- **Steel trowel** (multiple sizes)
- **Edger and groover**
- **Concrete vibrator** (for consolidation)
- **Slump cone** (for testing described in the Testing, Commissioning, and Quality Control chapter)
- **Wheelbarrow**

#### 10.4 Hand Tools — Plumbing Specific

- **PEX expansion tool** (manual or battery powered)
- **PEX crimp tool** (less critical with PEX-A)
- **Pipe cutters** (copper tubing cutter; PVC cutter; PEX cutter)
- **Soldering torch and supplies** (propane or MAPP gas)
- **Pipe threading tool** (for steel pipe — rarely needed)
- **Channel-lock pliers** (multiple sizes)

#### 10.5 Hand Tools — Electrical Specific

- **Wire strippers** (range of sizes)
- **Wire crimpers**
- **Cable cutters**
- **Voltage tester** (non-contact)
- **Multimeter** (Klein, Fluke)
- **Conduit benders** (1/2 inch and 3/4 inch — manual)
- **Fish tape** (for pulling wire through conduit)
- **Pliers — needle-nose, lineman's, side-cutters**

#### 10.6 Measuring and Leveling

- **Tape measures** (16 ft, 25 ft, 100 ft)
- **Laser level** (rotary or line, depending on application)
- **Spirit levels** (multiple sizes — 6 in, 24 in, 48 in)
- **Plumb bob**
- **Combination square**
- **Framing square**
- **Speed square**
- **Chalk lines**

#### 10.7 Safety Equipment

Essential for ALL construction work:
- **Hard hat**
- **Safety glasses** (multiple pairs)
- **Hearing protection** (foam plugs and over-ear muffs)
- **Work gloves** (multiple types — leather, nitrile, cotton)
- **Steel-toed boots**
- **Knee pads**
- **Respirator** (N95 disposable for everyday; half-face with cartridges for paint/chemical/silica work)
- **Fall protection harness** for any work above 6 feet
- **First aid kit**

#### 10.8 Specialty Diagnostic Tools

For testing and verification described in the Testing, Commissioning, and Quality Control chapter:
- **Thermal imaging camera** (the owner already has one — FLIR ONE or similar)
- **Laser thermometer / pyrometer** (for IR temperature spot measurements)
- **Manometer** (for blower door testing — typically rented)
- **Blower door fan + frame** (rent rather than buy unless multiple builds planned)
- **Air quality monitors** — CO, CO2, VOC, PM2.5
- **Moisture meter** (for wood, drywall, or surface measurements)

#### 10.9 Big Equipment (Rent)

- **Excavator** — rent with operator for foundation; rent without operator for landscaping
- **Concrete pumper truck** — rent with operator (specialty rental)
- **Crane** — rent with operator for roof and large lifts
- **Scissor lift or scaffold** — rent for elevated work
- **Plate compactor** — rent for small jobs
- **Skid-steer** — rent for site cleanup
- **Air compressor** (large for nail guns, small for pneumatic tasks)

**Rental sources (Oregon):**
- **Sunbelt Rentals** — broad equipment, multiple locations
- **United Rentals** — alternative
- **Local rental yards** — often best service for owner-builders
