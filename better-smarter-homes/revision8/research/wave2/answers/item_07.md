## (a) Where the residential fuel-gas rules live: ORSC Chapter 24, not the OPSC

1. **ORSC Chapter 24 (Part VI, Fuel Gas) is the governing text for a single-family dwelling.** Adopted scope, ORSC G2401.1 (101.2): "This chapter covers those fuel gas piping systems, fuel-gas appliances and related accessories, venting systems and combustion air configurations most commonly encountered in the construction of one- and two-family dwellings and structures regulated by this code. Coverage of piping systems shall extend from the point of delivery to the outlet of the appliance shutoff valves ... Piping systems requirements shall include design, materials, components, fabrication, assembly, installation, testing and inspection." Anything the chapter omits falls to "the applicable provisions of the Mechanical Code" (Oregon wording; the model IRC says International Fuel Gas Code here). Full Oregon-adopted chapter text, fetchable: https://up.codes/viewer/oregon/irc-2021/chapter/24/fuel-gas
2. **The OPSC explicitly does not contain fuel gas.** OAR 918-750-0110(3): "Chapter 12, and Appendices F, G, H, I, J, L, and N of the 2021 Uniform Plumbing Code are not adopted as part of the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code." (UPC Chapter 12 is the model's Fuel Gas Piping chapter.) Rule text: https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_918-750-0110 (mirror of secure.sos.state.or.us OARD, browser-only). The 2023 OPSC carries the 2021 UPC base forward ("The 2023 OPSC is based on the 2021 OPSC": https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Documents/codechangesummary.pdf), and that 2023 amendment summary contains no Chapter 12 item, so the exclusion stands in the current edition.
3. **Oregon's statutory definition of plumbing contains no fuel gas.** ORS 447.010(6) defines plumbing as water supply, fixtures, soil/waste/vent pipes, house drain and sewer, storm drainage, medical gas and vacuum, and solar heating and cooling; fuel gas is absent. 4. **Permit split, stated in the adopted code.** ORSC R105.1 requires a permit under the ORSC itself to "erect, install, enlarge, alter, repair, remove, convert or replace any gas or mechanical system, the installation of which is regulated by this code." ORSC R105.2 sends the other trades out: "Electrical: See the Electrical Code. ... Plumbing: See the Plumbing Code." Local file: C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf (pages 6-7).
5. **BCD program statements.** BCD's residential permits page puts gas piping under the mechanical permit: "Install, alter, or repair gas piping between the meter or LPG tank and an appliance, both indoors and outdoors" (mechanical permit list), and notes that homeowners installing, extending, altering, or repairing LP-gas appliances must be licensed through the State Fire Marshal (consistent with the already-settled LPG installer item): https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/lbdd/pages/oregon-permits.aspx. BCD's manufactured-dwelling installer study guide states the residential anchor directly: "Gas supply requirements not specifically covered in this code must be in accordance with the Oregon Residential Specialty Code, Chapter 24," and repeats the Chapter 24 test values: https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/licensing/Documents/md-study-guides/mdisc-studyguide-chapter8.pdf
6. **How the split works overall.** Dwellings inside ORSC scope: gas piping and gas appliances under ORSC Chapter 24 (mechanical/gas permit); water and drainage plumbing under the OPSC even in dwellings (OAR 918-750-0100(3): the OPSC "applies to all plumbing installations ... including one- and two-family dwellings of three stories or less within the scope of the Oregon Residential Specialty Code," https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_918-750-0100). Buildings beyond ORSC scope: fuel gas under the OMSC, which incorporates the 2024 IFGC (https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Pages/mechanical.aspx). ORSC G2402.3 (Oregon amendment) ties the chapters together by borrowing undefined terms from "applicable provisions of the Oregon Mechanical Specialty Code or applicable provisions of the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code" (https://up.codes/viewer/oregon/irc-2021/chapter/24/fuel-gas).
7. **Appendix AA (Sizing and Capacities of Gas Piping) is not adopted.** ORSC R101.2.3.2 lists Appendix AA first among matters that "are not adopted, are preempted by the state building code and may not be adopted by a local municipality"; the adopted-appendix list in R101.2.4 (AE, AF, AH, AK, AR, AS, AU, AW) does not include it. Gas pipe sizing therefore runs on the Chapter 24 body tables (G2413). Local file: C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf (pages 2-3).

## (c) Pressure-test acceptance criteria in the adopted text

Oregon replaced the model IRC criteria. The model's G2417.4.1 (1-1/2 times working pressure, 3 psig minimum) and G2417.4.2 (10-minute minimum duration) do not apply in Oregon; the model duration/purging sections G2417.4.2 through G2417.7.3.1 are deleted (26orsc-matrix.pdf entry quoted above; for the model values, see for example the model-code explainer at https://www.jaspector.com/codes/irc-2024/ch24-fuel-gas/gas-leak-test-irc-2024/). The adopted 2023 ORSC text (all quotes from https://up.codes/viewer/oregon/irc-2021/chapter/24/fuel-gas, fetched 2026-07-09):

- **G2417.4.1 Test Pressure (Oregon amendment):** "Gas piping systems under 14 inches water column (3.48 kPa) pressure shall be tested at a pressure of not less than 10 pounds per square inch (69 kPa). Test pressures shall be held for not less than 15 minutes with no perceptible drop in pressure. For welded piping and for piping carrying gas at pressures exceeding 14 inches water column (3.48 kPa) pressure, the test pressure shall be at least 60 pounds per square inch (414 kPa) for not less than 30 minutes. Exception: Testing, inspection and purging of gas piping systems performed in accordance with NFPA 58." A standard residential system (supply pressure 7 to 11 inches water column, below the 14-inch threshold) therefore tests at not less than 10 psi held not less than 15 minutes with no perceptible drop.
- **G2417.4 (406.4) Test Pressure Measurement (gauge requirements, Oregon-amended):** "Test pressure shall be measured with a manometer or with a pressure-measuring device designed and calibrated to read, record, or indicate a pressure loss caused by leakage during the pressure test period. The source of pressure shall be isolated before the pressure tests are made. Mechanical gauges used to measure test pressures shall use a 15-pounds-per-square-inch (103 kPa) gauge or a 30-pounds-per-square-inch (206 kPa) gauge for testing 14 inches water column (3.48 kPa) systems and a 100-pounds-per-square-inch (689 kPa) gauge for testing systems exceeding 14 inches water column (3.48 kPa) operating pressure. The gauges shall not exceed the manufacturer's listing and labeling." (The model rule, a gauge scale no greater than five times the test pressure, is replaced by these fixed gauge sizes.)
- **G2417.2 (406.2) Test Medium:** "The test medium shall be air, nitrogen, carbon dioxide or an inert gas. Oxygen shall not be used as a test medium."
- **G2417.1 (406.1) General:** all piping installations must be visually inspected and pressure tested before acceptance and initial operation. **G2417.3 (406.3):** pipe joints, including welds, left exposed during the test (exception for previously tested concealed end joints). **G2417.3.3/G2417.3.4:** appliances rated below the test pressure must be disconnected and outlets capped; appliances rated at or above it may be isolated by their shutoff valves.
- **G2417.1.1.1 Rough Piping Inspection and G2417.1.1.2 Final Piping Inspection (Oregon-added):** rough inspection after piping is installed and before it is covered, concealed, or connected to a fixture or appliance; final inspection after concealment and attachment of fixtures, appliances, or shutoff valves. **G2401.2.1 (Oregon-added):** "A serving gas supplier or person furnishing gas shall not turn on, or cause to be turned on, fuel gas or gas meters until the approval of the rough piping inspection is in compliance with Section G2417.1.1.1."
- **G2417.1.2 Repairs and Additions:** work done after the pressure test requires retesting the affected piping; minor repairs and additions may instead be inspected and leak-checked with noncorrosive leak-detecting fluid or other approved methods.

Corroboration of the Oregon values: BCD study guide, "Gas piping systems under 14 inches water column pressure, must be tested at a pressure of not less than 10 PSI. Test pressure must be held for not less than 15 minutes with no drop in pressure" (https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/licensing/Documents/md-study-guides/mdisc-studyguide-chapter8.pdf). The identical amendment appears in the prior edition at 2021 ORSC G2417.4.1 (https://codes.iccsafe.org/s/ORRSC2021P1/part-vi-fuel-gas/ORRSC2021P1-ChPanel-SecG2417.4.1, browser-only; returns 403 to tools), so the 10 psi/15 minute and 60 psi/30 minute criteria are stable Oregon policy, not a 2023 novelty.
