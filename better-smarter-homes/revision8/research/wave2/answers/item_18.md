## (a) ORS 672 engineering exemptions, with the ORS 671 architecture parallel

Source: C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors672.html (ORS chapter 672, 2025 Edition).

**The "for others" threshold.** ORS 672.005(1) defines the regulated activity as service work done for other people: "'Practice of engineering' or 'practice of professional engineering' means any of the following when done for others: (a) Performing a service or creating an original work requiring engineering education, training and experience. (b) In connection with utilities, structures, buildings ... applying special knowledge of the mathematical, physical and engineering sciences to services or original works such as: (A) Consultation; (B) Investigation; (C) Evaluation; (D) Planning; (E) Design; and (F) Services during construction ..." (ors672.html). Designing and building one's own home, and describing that work afterward, is not "done for others" as a service.

**What counts as practicing or offering to practice.** ORS 672.007(1) (ors672.html): "A person is practicing or offering to practice engineering if the person: (a) By verbal claim, sign, advertisement, letterhead or card or in any other way implies that the person is or purports to be a professional engineer or registered professional engineer; (b) Through the use of some other title implies that the person is a professional engineer or a registered professional engineer; or (c) Bids to perform or performs commercial or professional service or work that is defined by ORS 672.005 as the practice of engineering." A non-engineer publishing technical documentation about the author's own home stays outside this section only by (i) never claiming or implying engineer status in any title, byline, or marketing, and (ii) never bidding or performing engineering services for others.

**The exemptions.** ORS 672.060 (ors672.html): "Activities not requiring registration. Registration under ORS 672.002 to 672.325 is not required for the following:"

- ORS 672.060(2), own-property exemption: "The performance of engineering work by an employee, sole proprietorship, firm, partnership or corporation: (a) On property owned or leased by the [person] ... or on property in which the [person] has an interest, estate or possessory right; and (b) That affects exclusively the property or interests of the [person] ..., unless the performance affects the health or safety of the public or an employee."
- ORS 672.060(8), single-family dwelling exemption: "The making of drawings or specifications for, or the supervision of the erection, enlargement or alteration of, a building, or an appurtenance thereto, if the building is to be used for a single family residential dwelling or farm building or is a structure used in connection with or auxiliary to a single family residential dwelling or farm building, including but not limited to a three-car garage, barn or shed or a shelter used for the housing of domestic animals or livestock. The exemption in this subsection does not apply to a registered professional engineer."
- ORS 672.060(7), small-building exemption: same "making of drawings or specifications for" language for any building "of 4,000 square feet or less" in ground area "and ... not more than 20 feet in height from the top surface of lowest flooring to the highest interior overhead finish of the structure"; also inapplicable to a registered professional engineer.
- ORS 672.060(12) and (13) give the land-surveying parallels (surveying on one's own property affecting exclusively one's own interests; a landowner surveying within the landowner's own boundaries).

**Architecture parallel.** Source: C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors671.html (ORS chapter 671, 2025 Edition). ORS 671.030(2)(b): "ORS 671.010 to 671.220 do not prohibit ... (b) A person that is not an architect from preparing technical submissions or providing construction phase services related to erecting, enlarging or altering a building or an appurtenance to a building if the building or the appurtenance: (A) Is a single family residential dwelling or farm building; (B) Is a structure used in connection with or auxiliary to a single family residential dwelling or farm building, including but not limited to a three-car garage, barn or shed ...; or (C) Has a ground area of 4,000 square feet or less and is not more than 20 feet in height from the top surface of lowest flooring to the highest interior overhead finish." ORS 671.030(2)(c) separately exempts planning, designing, specifying, or observing alterations or repairs when no structural part is involved and neither the occupancy nor construction-type classification changes. Title protection is separate and has no exemption: ORS 671.020(1) (ors671.html) bars an unregistered person from the practice or the title, so the exempt person may prepare the dwelling documents but may not be called an architect.

## (b) ORS 165.540 recording-consent terms and exceptions

Source: C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors165.html (ORS chapter 165, 2025 Edition; section history through 2023 c.234).

**Operative terms.** ORS 165.540(1) opens: "Except as otherwise provided in ORS 133.724 or 133.726 or subsections (2) to (8) of this section, a person may not:" and then splits electronic from in-person:

- ORS 165.540(1)(a), telecommunication or radio communication (phone calls): may not obtain "unless consent is given by at least one participant." Oregon phone recording is one-party consent.
- ORS 165.540(1)(c), in-person "conversation": may not obtain by "any device, contrivance, machine or apparatus ... if not all participants in the conversation are specifically informed that their conversation is being obtained." In-person audio recording requires that every participant be specifically informed (notice to all, not merely one party's consent).
- ORS 165.540(1)(d) and (e) extend the ban to obtaining a communication known to have been illegally recorded and to using or divulging anything obtained in violation of the section.

The dividing definition is ORS 165.535(1) (same file): "'Conversation' means the transmission between two or more persons of an oral communication which is not a telecommunication or a radio communication, and includes a communication occurring through a video conferencing program." The section regulates obtaining oral communications; it does not address silent video.

**Main exceptions relevant to jobsite filming.**

- ORS 165.540(6)(a): the (1)(c) all-party-notice rule "do[es] not apply to persons who intercept or attempt to intercept oral communications ... if the person uses an unconcealed recording device" at "(A) Public or semipublic meetings such as hearings before governmental or quasi-governmental bodies, trials, press conferences, public speeches, rallies and sporting or other events; (B) Regularly scheduled classes ...; or (C) Private meetings or conferences if all others involved knew or reasonably should have known that the recording was being made." Subsection (6)(a)(C) is the operative carve-out for open, visible camera work among people who can see they are being filmed; announced recording under (1)(c) remains the clean practice.
- ORS 165.540(5)(a), felony carve-out: (1)(c) does not apply to "[a] person who records a conversation during a felony that endangers human life."
- ORS 165.540(5)(b), law-enforcement carve-out: (1)(c) does not apply to recording "a conversation in which a law enforcement officer is a participant" if made while the officer performs official duties, "openly and in plain view of the participants," the conversation is "audible to the person by normal unaided hearing," and the person "is in a place where the person lawfully may be." Subsection (10) adds that this does not authorize criminal trespass or interfering with a peace officer.
- ORS 165.540(3): the prohibitions of (1)(a) to (c) do not apply to "subscribers or members of their family who perform the acts prohibited in subsection (1) of this section in their homes."
- ORS 165.540(4) and (7): public broadcasts, amateur and citizens-band radio, and readily accessible public-safety radio traffic are outside the ban.
- ORS 165.540(8): the use-and-divulge bans of (1)(d) and (e) do not apply to a person who did not participate in the original recording "if the conversation ... is regarding a matter of public concern."

Penalty: ORS 165.540(9): violation of subsection (1) is a Class A misdemeanor. ORS 165.543 (same file) separately makes willful interception of a wire or oral communication without any party's consent a Class A misdemeanor.

## (c) Oregon UTPA provisions covering claims about information products

Source: ORS 646.608, https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_646.608 (accessed 2026-07-09; that page states currency through the 2023 ORS edition plus 2024 regular-session changes; the paragraphs quoted below are longstanding text).

**Coverage.** The Unlawful Trade Practices Act reaches consumer information products through its definitions at ORS 646.605, https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_646.605: "real estate, goods or services" means those "obtained primarily for personal, family or household purposes" (ORS 646.605(6)(a)), and "trade" and "commerce" mean "advertising, offering or distributing, whether by sale, rental or otherwise, any real estate, goods or services" (ORS 646.605(8)). A book, plan set, video course, or membership sold to homeowners is a good or service obtained for personal, family, or household purposes.

**Operative unlawful-practice provisions, ORS 646.608(1), most relevant to representations about an information product:**

- (1)(e), the central one for product claims and author credentials: a person engages in an unlawful practice if the person "[r]epresents that real estate, goods or services have sponsorship, approval, characteristics, ingredients, uses, benefits, quantities or qualities that the real estate, goods or services do not have or that a person has a sponsorship, approval, status, qualification, affiliation, or connection that the person does not have." Overstating what the product does, or overstating the author's own status or qualifications (for example implying engineer, architect, or inspector status), sits here.
- (1)(b): "[c]auses likelihood of confusion or of misunderstanding as to the source, sponsorship, approval, or certification of real estate, goods or services."
- (1)(c): "[c]auses likelihood of confusion or of misunderstanding as to affiliation, connection, or association with, or certification by, another."
- (1)(g): "[r]epresents that real estate, goods or services are of a particular standard, quality, or grade ... if the real estate, goods or services are of another."
- (1)(i): "[a]dvertises real estate, goods or services with intent not to provide the real estate, goods or services as advertised."
- (1)(s): "[m]akes false or misleading representations of fact concerning the offering price of, or the person's cost for real estate, goods or services."
- (1)(u), catch-all: "[e]ngages in any other unfair or deceptive conduct in trade or commerce." Its reach is limited by ORS 646.608(4): "An action or suit may not be brought under subsection (1)(u) of this section unless the Attorney General has first established a rule in accordance with the provisions of ORS chapter 183 declaring the conduct to be unfair or deceptive in trade or commerce."

ORS 646.608(2) makes omissions actionable: "A representation under subsection (1) of this section or ORS 646.607 may be any manifestation of any assertion by words or conduct, including, but not limited to, a failure to disclose a fact."

## (d) CCB home-inspector certification rule (title and advertising)

**Statute.** ORS 701.445(1), C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors701.html (2025 Edition): "An individual may not undertake, offer to undertake or submit a bid to do work as a home inspector unless the individual is certified as a home inspector under this section by the Construction Contractors Board and is an owner of, or employed by, a business that is licensed by the board." ORS 701.445(2) applies the same bar to businesses. (History note in the same file: ORS 701.445 was formerly ORS 701.350, which is why older rule authority lines cite 701.350.)

**Rule.** OAR 812-008-0030, Certification and License Required, in OAR chapter 812 division 8 (Certification of Individuals and Licensing of Businesses Engaged in Home Inspections). Official text: https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/displayDivisionRules.action?selectedDivision=3593; also https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_812-008-0030. Key provisions:

- 812-008-0030(1): "no individual shall undertake, offer to undertake or submit a bid to do work as an Oregon certified home inspector without first receiving certification to do same from the agency and without being an owner or employee of a business that is licensed with the agency."
- 812-008-0030(2): the parallel prohibition for businesses (licensed as residential general contractor, residential specialty contractor, or home inspection services contractor, with a certified owner or employee).
- 812-008-0030(4), the title rule: "No person, including persons covered by section (3) of this rule, shall use the title Oregon certified home inspector without receiving such certification from the agency."
- 812-008-0030(3)(b) to (e) exempt persons performing energy audits, forensic evaluations, home performance testing, and home energy performance scores.
- Authority line cites ORS 670.310, 701.235, 701.350 and 701.355 (the pre-renumbering anchors for today's ORS 701.445 and 701.450).

**Advertising anchor.** OAR 812-008-0201(4), Standards of Behavior (same division): "A business shall not advertise home inspection services unless the business is properly licensed," and all such advertisements "shall not be misleading or deceptive." https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_812-008-0201; also https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/oregon/Or-Admin-Code-SS-812-008-0201. Net rule for the book: an author who is not CCB-certified may not advertise or hold out as an "Oregon certified home inspector" or advertise home-inspection services; describing one's own construction project is outside these rules so long as no inspection services are offered.

## (e) FTC fake-reviews trade rule, 16 CFR Part 465

**Location and dates.** Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, codified at 16 CFR Part 465; current text location https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465 (direct eCFR fetch was bot-blocked on the check date; text verified through the Cornell CFR mirror, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/part-465, which shows Authority: 15 U.S.C. 57a and Source: 89 FR 68077, Aug. 22, 2024). Final rule published August 22, 2024, effective October 21, 2024: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/08/22/2024-18519/trade-regulation-rule-on-the-use-of-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials. Sections: 465.1 definitions; 465.2 fake or false consumer reviews, consumer testimonials, or celebrity testimonials; 465.3 reserved; 465.4 buying positive or negative consumer reviews; 465.5 insider consumer reviews and consumer testimonials; 465.6 company-controlled review websites or entities; 465.7 review suppression; 465.8 misuse of fake indicators of social media influence; 465.9 severability.

**The three prohibitions most relevant to a content creator:**

- 16 CFR 465.2, fake or false reviews: a business may not write, create, or sell a consumer review, consumer testimonial, or celebrity testimonial that "materially misrepresents" that the reviewer exists, that the reviewer used or had experience with the product, service, or business, or the reviewer's actual experience; and may not purchase, procure, or disseminate such a review when it "knew or should have known" of the material misrepresentation (constructive-knowledge standard). https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.2
- 16 CFR 465.4, buying reviews: a business may not provide compensation or other incentives conditioned, "expressly or by implication, on the writing or creation of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment, whether positive or negative." The condition on sentiment is the trigger; the ban covers buying negative reviews of competitors as well as positive reviews of oneself. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.4
- 16 CFR 465.5, insider reviews: an officer or manager writing a consumer review or testimonial about the business or its products must give a "clear and conspicuous disclosure" of the material relationship; officers and managers also may not solicit reviews from their immediate relatives or from employees or agents where the resulting reviews omit that disclosure and the business knew or should have known of the relationship, and must instruct disclosure and take remedial steps on discovery. Generalized review solicitations to purchasers are excepted. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/465.5

Adjacent sections a creator should know exist: 465.7 (review suppression, including unfounded legal threats to remove negative reviews) and 465.8 (buying or selling fake social-media followers or views).

## (f) Copyright registration timing, 17 U.S.C. 412

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/412 (accessed 2026-07-09). Section 412 bars the two premium remedies unless registration is timely: no award of statutory damages or attorney's fees (17 U.S.C. 504 and 505) shall be made for "(1) any infringement of copyright in an unpublished work commenced before the effective date of its registration; or (2) any infringement of copyright commenced after first publication of the work and before the effective date of its registration, unless such registration is made within three months after the first publication of the work."

The two preserved windows: register before the infringement begins, or, for a published work, register any time within three months after first publication (the grace period, which preserves statutory damages and fees even against infringement that began right after publication). Miss both and only actual damages, infringer profits, and injunctive relief remain for that infringement. Companion prerequisite: 17 U.S.C. 411(a), "no civil action for infringement of the copyright in any United States work shall be instituted until preregistration or registration of the copyright claim has been made" (a proper application that is refused also satisfies the prerequisite with notice to the Register): https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/411. Practical consequence for the book: register within three months of the publication date as a fixed launch-checklist item.

## (g) USPTO trademark filing basics and the 2025 fee structure

Sources: https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/fees-payment-information/summary-2025-trademark-fee-changes and the live schedule at https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/trademark-fee-information (fees effective January 18, 2025 for USPTO filings and February 18, 2025 for WIPO/Madrid filings; checked 2026-07-09).

**Application options and base fee.** Effective January 18, 2025, the USPTO eliminated the TEAS Plus ($250) and TEAS Standard ($350) tiers and replaced them with a single base application for Trademark Act Section 1 and Section 44 filings at $350 per class of goods or services (also stated at https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/how-much-does-it-cost). Per-class surcharges attach to noncompliant or free-form applications: $100 per class for an application with insufficient information (missing base-application requirements); $200 per class for entering the identification of goods or services in the free-form text box instead of selecting from the Trademark ID Manual; and $200 per class for each additional 1,000 characters of free-form identification beyond the first 1,000.

**Use in commerce vs intent to use.** Section 1(a), use in commerce, is for applicants "already using your trademark in interstate commerce"; Section 1(b), intent to use, is for a "bona fide intention to use" the mark and adds later filings and fees before registration can issue: https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/how-much-does-it-cost. An intent-to-use applicant must later prove use by filing an Amendment to Allege Use (before approval for publication) or a Statement of Use (after the Notice of Allowance), each $150 per class under the 2025 schedule (raised from $100): https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/trademark-fee-information. The Statement of Use is due within six months of the Notice of Allowance; the deadline can be extended in six-month increments at $125 per class, up to five extensions, for a maximum of 36 months from the Notice of Allowance: https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/apply/intent-use-itu-applications and https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/trademark-timelines/section-1b-timeline-application-based-intent-use. For the book's brand plan this means: filing intent-to-use before launch costs the $350 base plus at least one $150 use filing per class, and the mark does not register until use in commerce is proven.
