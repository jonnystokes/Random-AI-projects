## Part 1: ORSC Section R327 as amended (current text, local library PDF)

Source read in full: C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-r327-amend.pdf (identical to https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Documents/23orsc-r327-amend.pdf). Header: "2023 Oregon Residential Specialty Code Amendments," "Effective Aug. 5, 2025 (Ref.: ORS 455.610)." Summary text: "In accordance with Senate Bill 83 (2025) this amendment makes Section R327, Wildfire Hazard Mitigation, available for local adoption and applicable to new construction of new ORSC governed buildings. This code amendment was adopted by temporary rule effective Aug. 5, 2025 through Jan. 2, 2026 and subsequently adopted by permanent rule effective Jan. 1, 2026."

### Trigger: local opt-in, not a state map and not automatic WUI status

- R327.1 General: "Where adopted by the local municipality, the provisions of this section shall apply to new dwellings and their accessory structures required by a local municipality via local ordinance to be protected against wildfire." (local PDF above, p. 1)
- R327.1.1 Local adoption: a municipality may adopt R327 in whole by local ordinance without the normal local-amendment process (ORS 455.040 / OAR 918-020-0370). The ordinance must include: (1) identification of the areas subject to R327, (2) a transition plan for subdivisions already under development, (3) a local appeal process. Municipalities that adopted R327 under prior code editions may keep their existing ordinance unchanged. (local PDF, p. 1)
- R327.1.2 Notification: the adopting municipality must notify the Building Codes Division and identify the areas where R327 is required. (local PDF, p. 1)
- R327.1.3 Application: applies to newly constructed dwellings and their accessory structures in the areas designated by the municipality. Exceptions: nonhabitable detached accessory structures not greater than 400 sq ft located at least 50 ft from all other structures on the lot containing habitable space; structures exempted by ORS 455.315; detached accessory membrane-covered frame structures. (local PDF, p. 2)
- Statutory ceiling: ORS 455.433 (created by SB 83 §7, Oregon Laws 2025 ch. 590) pins the standards to the 2023 ORSC version of R327, limits application to "new construction of new buildings," requires municipalities to notify DCBS, and forbids the state from requiring local adoption. BCD's local-adoption guidance confirms: adoption must be in whole, provisions cannot be modified, only municipalities that run a building inspection program may adopt, and R327 does NOT apply to alterations, additions, or repairs of existing buildings (e.g., re-roofing or re-siding an existing house is out of scope; a detached new ADU is in scope as a new dwelling, an attached ADU addition is not). Session law: https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/lawsstatutes/2025orlaw0590.pdf (Sections 6-7). Guidance: https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Documents/23orsc-r327-localadoption.pdf
- Related: R302.2.12 exception 2 sends townhouse roof coverings in municipally determined "Wildfire Hazard Zones" to R327. (local PDF, p. 1)
- Note on the PDF markup: the file is a redline (added vs. struck text); plain-text extraction loses the strikethrough, but the struck language ("Where required," additions, and whole-element replacement provisions) is confirmed removed by the PDF's own summary line and by ORS 455.433(3)'s new-construction-only limit.

### Technical requirements where R327 applies (all from the local PDF, pp. 2-5)

- R327.3 Roofing: minimum Class B rated assembly or better; permitted systems are asphalt shingles (R905.2), slate (R905.6), metal (R905.4), tile/clay/concrete shingles (R905.3), or approved Class B equivalent. Wood shingle and shake roofs are prohibited. Open spaces between roof covering and decking (e.g., tile profiles) must be fireblocked or covered with one layer of 72-lb mineral-surfaced nonperforated cap sheet (ASTM D3909). Valley flashing: minimum 0.019 in. (No. 26 gage) galvanized corrosion-resistant metal over a 36-in.-wide 72-lb cap sheet.
- R327.3.1 Gutters and downspouts: noncombustible, with an approved means to prevent leaf/debris accumulation.
- R327.3.2 Ventilation: attic/soffit/rafter/underfloor vent openings sized per R408 and R806, covered with noncombustible corrosion-resistant metal mesh (openings min 1/16 in., max 1/8 in.) or ember-and-flame-resistant vent devices. R327.3.2.1: no vents on the underside of eaves, soffits, or cornices, except listed ember/flame-resistant vents approved by the building official or openings at least 12 ft above grade.
- R327.3.3 Exterior walls: one of (1) noncombustible material, (2) ignition-resistant material (defined in R327.2 by extended 30-minute ASTM E84/UL 723 test, flame spread index not more than 25), (3) heavy timber, (4) log wall, (5) assembly passing ASTM E2707 direct-flame test per R327.3.3.2. Deemed-to-comply alternatives: 5/8-in. Type X exterior gypsum sheathing behind the cladding, or the exterior portion of a 1-hour fire-resistance-rated exterior wall assembly. R327.3.3.1: the covering runs from top of foundation to the roof, terminating at 2-in. nominal solid blocking at overhangs. (A concrete or ICF wall system satisfies item 1 directly.)
- R327.3.4 Overhanging projections: undersides of eaves/soffits below 12 ft (R327.3.4.1), patio/porch ceilings over 200 sq ft and below 12 ft (R327.3.4.2), cantilevered floors below 12 ft (R327.3.4.3), and exposed underfloor of elevated structures (R327.3.4.4) must be noncombustible, ignition-resistant, 5/8-in. Type X protected, 1-hour-rated exterior side, or ASTM E2957 tested per R327.3.4.5.
- R327.3.5 Walking surfaces: deck/porch/balcony surfaces between 30 in. and 12 ft above grade must be tested materials (ASTM E2632 + E2726 per R327.3.5.1), ignition-resistant, exterior fire-retardant-treated wood, or noncombustible; exception for decks not greater than 200 sq ft built of nominal 2-in. lumber.
- R327.3.6 Glazing: exterior windows, glazing in exterior doors, and skylights must be tempered glass, multilayered glazed panels, glass block, or minimum 20-minute fire-rated.

## Part 2: The statewide wildfire map — SB 762 framework, rise and repeal

- SB 762 (2021), Oregon Laws 2021 ch. 592, created the statewide map-driven framework: a statewide wildfire risk map under former ORS 477.490, mandatory State Fire Marshal defensible space standards under former ORS 476.392, and mandatory home-hardening building code standards under former ORS 455.612, which required DCBS to apply R327 to new dwellings and accessory structures "for the high wildfire hazard zone in the wildland-urban interface" identified on the statewide map (former text at https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_455.612).
- Map v1 (risk map, 5 classes) was released June 30, 2022 by ODF/OSU and withdrawn Aug 4, 2022 after roughly 750 appeals and insurance-cancellation backlash: https://www.opb.org/article/2022/08/05/oregon-wildfire-prevention-map-risk-forest-fire-insurance/
- SB 80 (2023) renamed it a "hazard" map and cut five risk classes to three hazard zones (high/moderate/low); SB 82 (2023) separately barred insurers from using any state map for rating/underwriting: https://oregonpropertyowners.org/guest-post-the-oregon-wildfire-hazard-map-is-back-and-it-cant-affect-your-insurance/
- Map v2 (final Statewide Wildfire Hazard Map) was adopted Jan 7, 2025; ODF sent certified-mail notices to owners of tax lots designated high hazard inside the WUI: https://news.oregonstate.edu/news/updated-wildfire-hazard-map-provides-landowners-policymakers-objective-data-inform-wildfire
- SB 83 (2025) repealed the entire statewide scheme: passed the Senate unanimously (April 2025) and the House 50-1 (June 2025), approved by Governor Kotek July 24, 2025, filed July 28, 2025, effective on passage July 24, 2025 (emergency clause). Enacted as Oregon Laws 2025 ch. 590: https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/lawsstatutes/2025orlaw0590.pdf ; OLIS overview: https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2025R1/Measures/Overview/SB83 . Key operative parts: Section 1 repeals ORS 477.490 (the map), ORS 455.612 and 455.614 (map-triggered home hardening), and ORS 476.390/476.394 (map-triggered defensible space); Section 8 declares every ODF order assigning property to a wildfire hazard zone "null and void"; Section 7 (codified as ORS 455.433) creates the local-adoption R327 pathway described in Part 1; Section 2 (amending ORS 476.392) converts the State Fire Marshal's role to a voluntary model defensible space code consistent with the 2024 International Wildland-Urban Interface Code §§603-604 that the state "may not require a local government to adopt"; the seller's property disclosure WUI question was struck (ORS 105.464 amendment). Law-firm summary: https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2025/07/oregon-repeals-statewide-wildfire-hazard-map-and-mitigation-requirements
- What replaced the map: nothing statewide. BCD re-issued R327 as a local-adoption section (temporary rule Aug 5, 2025; permanent rule Jan 1, 2026, per the local PDF), and the State Fire Marshal finalized the voluntary model defensible space code in May 2026: https://apps.oregon.gov/oregon-newsroom/OR/OSFM/Posts/Post/oregon-adopts-defensible-space-model-code and https://www.oregon.gov/osfm/wildfire/pages/defensiblespace.aspx . The 2026 short session (Feb 2 - Mar 6, 2026) did not reinstate a statewide map.

## Part 3: What a builder must do at design time (July 2026) — jurisdiction-dependent

There is no statewide wildfire map and no statewide R327 mandate. Whether wildfire-hardening construction rules apply to a rural parcel depends entirely on which municipality operates the building inspection program for that parcel and whether it has adopted R327 by ordinance for an area that includes the parcel (the ordinance may define the area by map or by written description — BCD guidance, https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Documents/23orsc-r327-localadoption.pdf).

Steps:
1. Check BCD's "Home hardening and wildfire hazard mitigation" page for the current list of adopting municipalities: https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/pages/wildfire-hazard-mitigation.aspx . As of 2026-07-09 it lists five: Ashland, Bend, Deschutes County, Grants Pass, and Sisters. Known effective dates: unincorporated Deschutes County and the City of Sisters, all new dwellings and new accessory structures permitted on or after April 1, 2026 (https://www.deschutes.org/cd/page/fire-hardening-requirements-r327); City of Bend, ordinance adopted April 15, 2026, effective May 15, 2026, new detached residential construction (https://www.bendsource.com/business/businessnews/city-of-bend-adopts-residential-wildfire-mitigation-building-code-effective-may-15/). This list will grow; do not treat it as static.
2. Ask the building department that serves the parcel (the city department if the city runs its own program, otherwise the county building department) two questions in writing: has ORSC R327 been locally adopted, and is this tax lot inside the designated area. Only the local building department can enforce R327 (delegated authority under ORS 455.148, per the BCD guidance), so the local building official is the party who determines and states parcel applicability in writing (permit pre-application response, code interpretation letter, or the plan-review record). BCD in Salem confirms which jurisdictions have adopted but does not make parcel determinations. The adopting ordinance must contain a local appeal process (R327.1.1 item 3) if the owner disputes the designation, and the building official may approve alternate materials/methods under ORSC R104.11.
3. Separately ask the county/city planning department about land-use wildfire overlays and any locally adopted defensible space code (the OSFM model code is voluntary and adopted jurisdiction by jurisdiction), since those are independent of the building code.

County/site dependency, stated plainly: identical rural parcels in different counties face different rules today — a new house outside Sisters (Deschutes County) must comply with R327 in full; a comparable parcel in most other counties currently has no wildfire-hardening construction requirement at all unless and until that jurisdiction adopts R327. For a concrete-walled house, R327's exterior-wall requirement (R327.3.3 item 1, noncombustible material) is inherently satisfied; the cost-relevant items become roofing class, ember-resistant venting, protected eaves/projections, deck surfaces, and glazing.

Requirements are statutorily pinned to R327 of the 2023 ORSC (ORS 455.433); a future ORSC edition or legislative session can change this framework, and the list of adopting jurisdictions changes over time.
