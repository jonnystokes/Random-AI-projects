## 1. Smoke alarms when a dwelling is SOLD: ORS 479.260, 479.265, 479.297

**ORS 479.260(1) (sale/transfer prohibition).** Quoted from the local library (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors479.html; same text at https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors479.html and https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_479.260):

> "(1) A person may not convey fee title to any real property that includes a dwelling unit or lodging house, or transfer possession of any dwelling unit or lodging house pursuant to a land sale contract, unless there is installed in the dwelling unit or lodging house a smoke detector or the required number of approved smoke alarms, installed in accordance with the state building code and rules of the State Fire Marshal adopted under ORS 479.295. The smoke alarms required by this subsection must meet the requirements of ORS 479.297."

**ORS 479.260(2)(a)-(b) (manufactured dwellings).** Same prohibition for conveying ownership or transferring possession of a manufactured dwelling (as defined in ORS 446.003); alarms per the state building code or federal manufactured dwelling standards under ORS 446.155, and "(b) A smoke alarm installed in a manufactured dwelling that is resold by a person other than the manufacturer or authorized dealer must meet the requirements of ORS 479.297." Section history: [1979 c.642 §3; 1997 c.647 §5; 1999 c.307 §§3,4; 2003 c.655 §78] (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors479.html).

**Duty holder.** The statute is phrased as a prohibition on the transferor: the person conveying fee title or transferring possession. In practice this is the seller's obligation to have compliant alarms installed at conveyance.

**Buyer remedy, ORS 479.265.** A purchaser or transferee aggrieved by a violation of ORS 479.260 "may bring an individual action in an appropriate court to recover actual damages or $50, whichever is greater," plus discretionary attorney fees and costs; the action "must be commenced within one year of the date of sale or transfer"; and "violation of ORS 479.260 does not affect the transfer of the title, ownership or possession of the dwelling unit." History: [1979 c.642 §4; 1981 c.897 §56; 1995 c.618 §77; 2003 c.655 §79] (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors479.html).

**Ionization and battery rules, ORS 479.297.** (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors479.html):

> "(1) All ionization smoke alarms sold in this state that are solely battery-operated shall be packaged with a 10-year battery.
> (2) All ionization smoke alarms sold in this state shall include a 'hush' mechanism that allows a person to temporarily disengage the alarm for a period of not more than 15 minutes."

Subsection (3) exempts alarms designed for persons who are hard of hearing, alarms sold for shipment out of state, and alarms for RVs, commercial vehicles, railroad equipment, aircraft, marine vessels, or manufactured dwellings. History: [1997 c.647 §2,3; 1999 c.307 §12; 2007 c.70 §274]. Note for the manuscript: the word "photoelectric" does not appear anywhere in ORS chapter 479 (grep of C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors479.html, 2026-07-09). The technology-specific statutory rules attach only to ionization alarms; there is no statutory photoelectric mandate. Installation and placement standards are the State Fire Marshal's rules adopted under ORS 479.295, codified at OAR chapter 837, division 45 (Smoke Alarms and Smoke Detectors), https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/displayDivisionRules.action?selectedDivision=3810.

## 2. Smoke alarms when a dwelling is RENTED: ORS 479.255, 479.270, 479.275, 479.280

**ORS 479.255(1) (baseline requirement).** "Every dwelling unit regulated under ORS chapter 90, every lodging house and every hotel guest room shall contain an approved and properly functioning smoke alarm or smoke detector, installed in accordance with the state building code and rules of the State Fire Marshal." (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors479.html). Under ORS 479.257(1), alarms in these units installed after January 1, 2002 must contain the features described in ORS 479.297 (1) and (2).

**ORS 479.270 (landlord duties).** Subsection (1): the owner of any rental dwelling unit or the owner's authorized agent "shall be responsible for supplying, installing and maintaining the required smoke alarms or smoke detectors and shall provide a written notice containing instructions for testing," given "at the time the tenant first takes possession." Subsection (2): the maintenance duty (including working batteries) arises "(a) Prior to the beginning of every new tenancy when the tenant first takes possession of the premises; and (b) During the tenancy upon written notice from the tenant of any deficiency, not including replacing dead batteries." Subsection (3): supplying and maintaining the alarm "shall be considered a habitable condition under ORS 90.320." History: [1979 c.642 §5; 1993 c.369 §19; 1999 c.307 §6] (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors479.html).

**ORS 479.275 (tenant duties).** The tenant must test alarms per manufacturer instructions at intervals not exceeding six months, notify the owner in writing of deficiencies, and replace dead batteries during the tenancy. History: [1979 c.642 §6; 1981 c.309 §2; 1993 c.369 §20; 1999 c.307 §7] (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors479.html).

**ORS 479.280(1) (enforcement).** If the owner has not installed a properly operating alarm "within 10 days after receiving written notice from the tenant of the deficiency," the tenant may file a complaint with the State Fire Marshal or the local fire official, who may investigate and issue a citation (ORS 479.280(2)). Penalty: ORS 479.990(2) makes violation of ORS 479.255, 479.260, 479.270, 479.280, 479.297 or 479.300 "punishable by a civil penalty imposed by the State Fire Marshal in an amount not to exceed $250." (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors479.html).

**Tampering, ORS 479.300.** No person shall remove or tamper with a properly functioning smoke alarm or smoke detector, including "removal of working batteries." (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors479.html).

## 3. Carbon monoxide alarms at sale and rental

**Note on the statute map:** ORS 476.725 is the rulemaking and preemption section, not the sale trigger. The sale-transfer prohibition is ORS 105.838; the rental duties are ORS 90.316 and 90.317. All are parts of the Lofgren and Zander Memorial Act: per ORS 105.844, "ORS 90.316, 90.317, 105.836 to 105.842, 455.360 and 476.725 shall be known and may be cited as the Lofgren and Zander Memorial Act. [2009 c.591 §15]" (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors105.html).

**ORS 476.725 (State Fire Marshal CO alarm standards; statewide preemption).** Subsection (1): the State Fire Marshal shall adopt rules establishing minimum standards for CO alarms in one and two family dwellings and multifamily housing (design, inspection, testing, maintenance). Subsection (2): rules for placement and location in dwellings "that were not subject to state building code requirements for carbon monoxide alarm placement or location at the time of construction." Subsection (4): "A governmental subdivision, as defined in ORS 476.005 may not enact or enforce any local ordinance, rule or regulation regarding the design, inspection, testing, maintenance, placement or location of carbon monoxide alarms." History: [2009 c.591 §4] (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors476.html).

**ORS 105.838(1) (sale/transfer prohibition).** Quoted from C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors105.html:

> "(1) A person may not convey fee title to a one and two family dwelling or multifamily housing that contains a carbon monoxide source, or transfer possession under a land sale contract of a one and two family dwelling or multifamily housing that contains a carbon monoxide source, unless one or more properly functioning carbon monoxide alarms are installed in the dwelling or housing at locations that provide carbon monoxide detection for all sleeping areas of the dwelling or housing."

Subsection (2): installation must conform to State Fire Marshal rules and the state building code. Subsection (3): violation "does not invalidate any sale or transfer of possession." History: [2009 c.591 §2].

**"Carbon monoxide source" trigger, ORS 105.836(2).** "(a) A heater, fireplace, appliance or cooking source that uses coal, kerosene, petroleum products, wood or other fuels that emit carbon monoxide as a by-product of combustion; or (b) An attached garage with an opening that communicates directly with a living space." (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors105.html). This matters for the book: an all-electric concrete home with no attached garage opening into living space contains no statutory carbon monoxide source, so ORS 105.838 does not require a CO alarm at sale, although the state building code required CO alarms in new dwellings with a source or attached garage at construction.

**Buyer remedy, ORS 105.840.** A purchaser aggrieved by a violation of ORS 105.838 or a rule under ORS 476.725 may sue "to recover the greater of actual damages or $250 per residential unit," plus discretionary attorney fees and costs; one-year limitation from date of sale or transfer. History: [2009 c.591 §3] (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors105.html). Tampering, including removing working batteries, is prohibited by ORS 105.842 [2009 c.591 §5].

**Rental CO duties, ORS 90.317** (chapter 90 is not in the local library; verified at https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_90.317, publication current through the 2024 session): landlord must install CO alarms if the unit contains or is connected to a carbon monoxide source, provide the tenant written testing instructions, repair or replace on written notice of a deficiency other than dead batteries, and alarm maintenance is a habitability requirement under ORS 90.320. The 2025 and 2026 session amended-and-repealed tables show no change to ORS 90.316 or 90.317 (https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/lawsstatutes/2025OrLawAR.pdf and https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/lawsstatutes/2026OrLawAR.pdf, both checked 2026-07-09).

## 4. Interaction with the seller's property disclosure form, ORS 105.464

The statutory disclosure form in ORS 105.464 covers both alarms in Section 5, DWELLING STRUCTURE (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors105.html):

> "5. DWELLING STRUCTURE ...
> C. Are there smoke alarms or detectors? [ ]Yes [ ]No [ ]Unknown
> D. Are there carbon monoxide alarms? [ ]Yes [ ]No [ ]Unknown"

Items 5.C and 5.D are not asterisked items; the form's instruction "*If you mark yes on items with *, attach a copy or explain on an attached sheet" therefore does not attach to them, so they are plain yes/no/unknown disclosures with no required attachment. Delivery duty: ORS 105.465(2) requires the seller to "complete, sign and deliver a seller's property disclosure statement as set forth in ORS 105.464 to each buyer who makes a written offer to purchase real property in this state," subject to the ORS 105.475(4) refusal/revocation mechanism and the ORS 105.470 exclusions (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors105.html). The disclosure items are representations about presence of alarms; they do not replace the independent transfer prohibitions in ORS 479.260 and ORS 105.838, which apply regardless of what is disclosed.
