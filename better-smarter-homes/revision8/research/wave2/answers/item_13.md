**Answered (2026-07-09):**

**Source and edition.** 2023 Oregon Residential Specialty Code (ORSC), Chapter 1, Scope and Administration, official Oregon PDF (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf). Rule-side check: OAR chapter 918 division 480, One- and Two-Family Dwelling Specialty Code (local index C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/oar/oar-918-480.html; https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_chapter_918_division_480; SOS source https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/displayDivisionRules.action?selectedDivision=4173).

**The expiration rule: yes, 180 days.** 2023 ORSC Section R105.5 (Expiration): "Every permit issued shall become invalid unless the work authorized by such permit is commenced within 180 days after its issuance or if the work authorized by such permit is suspended or abandoned for a period of 180 days after the time the work is commenced. The building official is authorized to grant, in writing, one or more extensions of time, for periods not more than 180 days each. The extension shall be requested in writing and justifiable cause demonstrated." (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf). Two distinct triggers: failure to commence within 180 days of issuance, and any 180-day suspension or abandonment after commencement. The code measures suspension of "the work," not of inspections; treating a passed inspection as proof the work has not been suspended is common local administration, not code text.

**Extension mechanics.** Under R105.5 the building official may grant "one or more" extensions, so the number is not capped at state level; each extension runs not more than 180 days; the request must be in writing with justifiable cause demonstrated, and the grant must be in writing. No state fee attaches to an extension: OAR 918-480-0020 (One- and Two-Family Dwelling Permit Fees) sets fee methodology only and says nothing about expiration, reinstatement or extension fees (https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_918-480-0020). The permit application itself has a parallel clock: ORSC R105.3.2 (Time limitation of application) deems an application abandoned 180 days after filing "unless such application has been pursued in good faith or a permit has been issued," again with written 180-day extensions for justifiable cause available from the building official (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf).

**Reinstatement: no state provision exists.** The 2023 ORSC Chapter 1 contains no reinstatement section for an expired permit; a text search of the chapter PDF finds no occurrence of reinstatement in any form (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf). Once a permit is invalid under R105.5, what happens next (reactivation for a fee, a new permit at full or reduced fee, re-review under a newer code edition) is set by each building department's local policy, so the manuscript should tell the reader to ask their jurisdiction before letting 180 days run, and should present the written extension request under R105.5 as the safe path that avoids the question entirely. Related statewide backstop worth citing: ORS 455.058, reprinted in the chapter PDF after Section R105.4, lets the department or municipality assess an investigation fee when work subject to a permit "is commenced before the permit required for the work is obtained," with an emergency-repair exception if the permit is obtained within five business days (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf).

**Oregon amendments visible in the PDF.** Section R105.5 itself carries the standard 180-day framework with no Oregon-specific insertion. The Oregon-marked changes in the permit part of the chapter are: R105.1.1 (a building permit is required to create new habitable spaces, new toilet rooms or new bathrooms); R105.2 (Oregon exempt-work list, including the 200-square-foot accessory structure exemption with a 400-square-foot exception on parcels of 2.0 acres or more); R105.2.1 and R105.2.1.1 (emergency mechanical repairs may proceed with the permit application submitted within 5 working business days; emergency temporary structural repairs require 72-hour notice and application within 5 business days); R105.3.1.1 (the flood-hazard substantial-improvement determination section "is not adopted by the State of Oregon"); and the reprint of ORS 455.058 following R105.4 (all at C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf).

**OAR 918-480 has no permit-duration provision.** The division's full rule list is 918-480-0002 (name change), 918-480-0005 (adopts the ORSC), 918-480-0010 (amendments), 918-480-0020 (permit fees), 918-480-0030 (phased project and deferred submittal fees), 918-480-0125 (uniform alternate construction standard), 918-480-0130 (conventional light frame construction), 918-480-0140 (certificates of occupancy, residential) and 918-480-0150 (low volume window label program); none addresses permit expiration, duration or reinstatement (C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/oar/oar-918-480.html; https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_chapter_918_division_480). Permit duration for dwellings therefore rests entirely on ORSC R105.5 as adopted through OAR 918-480-0005. Note when citing the rule text online: the oregon.public.law snapshot of 918-480-0005 is stale (it still describes the 2021 ORSC adoption); the operative edition is the 2023 ORSC per C:/Users/jon/Projects/theBook/revision6_code_library/README.md, so verify rule text against the SOS OARD source when the edition matters.
