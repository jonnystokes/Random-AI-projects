## 2026-10-27  What does Oregon's own silica rule say, and where is its Table 1?
Finding: Oregon's silica rule is OAR 437, Division 2, Subdivision Z (Administrative Order 3-2019), and it covers construction as well as general industry. 437-002-1053: applies to all occupational exposures in general industry and construction, except sorptive clays and "Operations where objective data demonstrates that employee exposures to respirable crystalline silica will remain below 25 micrograms per cubic meter of air (25 ug/m3) as an 8-hour time-weighted average (TWA) under any foreseeable conditions." 437-002-1055: PEL 50 ug/m3, 8-hour TWA. 437-002-1057: "This rule lists specific tasks and control measures that do not require an exposure assessment and is only applicable to construction and construction like-activities"; (1) fully and properly implement the Table 1 controls unless exposure is assessed under 437-002-1056; (2) wet methods at flow rates sufficient to minimize visible dust; (4) "Where an employee performs more than one task in Table 1 during the course of a shift, and the total duration of all tasks combined is more than four hours, the required respiratory protection for each task is the respiratory protection specified for more than four hours per shift." Table 1 rows and values match the federal table (below). 437-002-1061 Housekeeping: (1) no dry sweeping or dry brushing where it could contribute to exposure unless wet sweeping, HEPA vacuuming or other methods are not feasible; (2) "Do not allow compressed air to be used to clean clothing or surfaces where such activity could contribute to employee exposure to respirable crystalline silica unless: (a) The compressed air is used in conjunction with a ventilation system that effectively captures the dust cloud created by the compressed air; or (b) No alternative method is feasible." 437-002-1062(1)(a): medical surveillance for each employee engaged in construction who will be required to use a respirator for 30 or more days per year; the note keeps the 1910.134 medical evaluation for anyone wearing a respirator. Oregon OSHA fact sheet FS-67 (4/22) confirms Table 1 at 437-002-1057, PEL 50, action level 25, adoption in September 2016 and compliance from July 1, 2018.
Source: https://osha.oregon.gov/OSHARules/div2/div2Z-437-002-1053-1065-silica.pdf (AO 3-2019, retrieved 2026-10-27); https://osha.oregon.gov/OSHAPubs/factsheets/fs67.pdf (retrieved 2026-10-27).
Confidence: high (the rule text).
Used in: 27.11 Code and Professional.

## 2026-10-27  Federal Table 1 text, checked row by row
Finding: 29 CFR 1926.1153(a) scope: "This section applies to all occupational exposures to respirable crystalline silica in construction work, except where employee exposure will remain below 25 micrograms per cubic meter of air (25 ug/m3) as an 8-hour time-weighted average (TWA) under any foreseeable conditions." Table 1 rows used: (i) stationary masonry saws, integrated water delivery, none/none; (ii) handheld power saws, water delivery, outdoors none / APF 10 over 4 hours, indoors APF 10 / APF 10; (iv) walk-behind saws, water delivery, outdoors none/none, indoors APF 10 / APF 10; (vi) rig-mounted core saws or drills, integrated water delivery to the cutting surface, none/none, no indoor-outdoor split; (vii) handheld and stand-mounted drills including impact and rotary hammer drills, shroud or cowling with dust collection at the manufacturer's airflow, 99 percent filter with a filter-cleaning mechanism, HEPA vacuum to clean holes, none/none; (viii) dowel drilling rigs, outdoors only, APF 10/APF 10; (x) jackhammers and handheld chipping tools, water or shroud, outdoors none / APF 10, indoors APF 10 / APF 10; (xi) handheld grinders for mortar removal, shroud and collector at 25 cfm per inch of wheel diameter, APF 10 / APF 25; (xii) handheld grinders for other uses: the water option is outdoors only (none/none); the shroud option is none/none outdoors and none / APF 10 indoors; (xiii) walk-behind milling machines and floor grinders, water or the manufacturer's dust collection, none/none, HEPA vacuum between passes indoors; (xviii) heavy equipment for grading and excavating, water or dust suppressants, or an enclosed cab when the operator is the only employee in the task, none/none. 1926.1153(c)(3) is the combined-task rule; (f)(2) is the compressed-air rule. ## 2026-10-27  Does Oregon's safety law reach a solo owner-builder with no employees?
Finding: ORS 654.005(5): "'Employer' includes: (a) Any person who has one or more employees. (b) Any sole proprietor or member of a partnership who elects workers' compensation coverage as a subject worker pursuant to ORS 656.128. (c) Any successor or assignee of an employer." ORS 654.005(4)(a): an employee is any individual who furnishes services for remuneration subject to the direction and control of an employer. Consistent with the federal interpretation in research/answers/ch30_body-and-mind.md items 14 and 70 (OSHA standard interpretation 2001-05-23-3).

## 2026-10-27  What does the federal powder-actuated tool rule require?
Finding: 29 CFR 1926.302(e)(1) only employees trained on the particular tool may operate it; (e)(2) the tool is tested each day before loading, by the manufacturer's procedure; (e)(5) not loaded until just before firing, never pointed at anyone; (e)(6) loaded tools not left unattended; (e)(7) "Fasteners shall not be driven into very hard or brittle materials including, but not limited to, cast iron, glazed tile, surface-hardened steel, glass block, live rock, face brick, or hollow tile."; (e)(8) easily penetrated materials only when backed; (e)(9) no fastener into a spalled area; (e)(12) tools meet ANSI A10.3-1970.
Source: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.302 (retrieved 2026-10-27).
Confidence: high.
Used in: 27.10 Code and Applied.

## 2026-10-27  What notice does Oregon require before digging with a rented machine?
Finding: OAR 952-001-0050(1): "at least two full business days but not more than 10 full business days before beginning an excavation the excavator must notify the Oregon Utility Notification Center of the date and location of the proposed excavation, and the type of work to be performed." (2): the notice does not apply in an emergency, or if all of these apply: the excavator is a tenant or owner of private property, the excavation is on that property, it is less than 12 inches deep, and it is not within an established easement. (4): work at multiple sites or over a large area calls for reasonable steps with facility operators, including preconstruction meetings.
Source: https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_952-001-0050 (retrieved 2026-10-27).
Confidence: high.
Used in: 27.4 Code and Applied.
