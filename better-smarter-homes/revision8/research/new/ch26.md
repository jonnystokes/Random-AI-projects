## 2026-10-29  What does the Oregon Fire Code say about gates across a fire apparatus access road?
Finding: Oregon Fire Code (2022 edition, 2021 IFC base) Appendix D, D103.5 Fire apparatus access road gates: "Gates securing the fire apparatus access roads shall comply with all of the following criteria: 1. Where a single gate is provided, the gate width shall be not less than 20 feet (6096 mm). Where a fire apparatus road consists of a divided roadway, the gate width shall be not less than 12 feet (3658 mm). 2. Gates shall be of the horizontal swing, horizontal slide, vertical lift or vertical pivot type. 3. Construction of gates shall be of materials that allow manual operation by one person. 4. Gate components shall be maintained in an operative condition at all times and replaced or repaired when defective. 5. Electric gates shall be equipped with a means of opening the gate by fire department personnel for emergency access. Emergency opening devices shall be approved by the fire code official. 6. Methods of locking shall be submitted for approval by the fire code official. 7. Electric gate operators, where provided, shall be listed in accordance with UL 325. 8. Gates intended for automatic operation shall be designed, constructed and installed to comply with the requirements of ASTM F2200." Section 503.6 Security gates: installation across a fire apparatus access road approved by the fire code official, an approved means of emergency operation, gates and emergency operation maintained operational at all times, electric operators listed to UL 325, automatic gates to ASTM F2200. Section 503.2.1 gives the 20-foot unobstructed width "except for approved security gates in accordance with Section 503.6". Section 503.1.1 exception 1 lets the fire code official modify Sections 503.1 and 503.2 where (1.3) "There are not more than two non-sprinklered one- and two-family dwellings or Group R-3 occupancies, or Group U occupancies." Table D103.4 as the answer file read it: 0 to 150 feet no turnaround, 151 to 500 feet 20 feet wide with a 120-foot hammerhead, 60-foot Y or 96-foot cul-de-sac, 501 to 750 feet 26 feet wide, over 750 feet special approval. The page shows no Oregon amendment markers. The UpCodes viewer has no page for a 2025 Oregon Fire Code (404).
Source: https://up.codes/viewer/oregon/or-fire-code-2022/chapter/D/fire-apparatus-access-roads and https://up.codes/viewer/oregon/or-fire-code-2022/chapter/5/fire-service-features (retrieved 2026-10-29).
Confidence: high for the 2022 text as served; the 2025 edition (in force since October 1, 2025) is unread.
Used in: 26.4 Code and Professional; open item "2025 fire code gate text".

## 2026-10-29  Does Oregon's residential code require fire separation for an outbuilding near the lot line, and at what distance?
Finding: ORSC R302.1 (2023 edition, 2021 IRC base, as served by the UpCodes Oregon viewer): "Construction, projections, openings and penetrations of exterior walls of dwellings and accessory structures" comply with Table R302.1(1). The table's "Fire separation distance provided" column reads: walls fire-resistance rated (1 hour, tested from both sides) at "< 3 feet"; walls not rated at ">= 3 feet"; projections not allowed at "< 2 feet"; projections fire-resistance rated at ">= 2 feet to < 3 feet"; projections not rated at ">= 3 feet"; openings not allowed at "< 3 feet"; openings unlimited at ">= 3 feet"; penetrations per R302.4 at "< 3 feet", none required at ">= 3 feet". The model 2021 IRC table uses 5 feet for walls and a 25-percent-openings row at 3 feet; the Oregon table has neither, so the 3-foot distance is Oregon's amendment. Exception 3: "Detached nonhabitable accessory structures exempted from permits are not required to provide wall protection based on location on the lot. Projections beyond the exterior wall shall not extend over the lot line." Exception 4: detached garages within 2 feet of a lot line may have eave projections up to 4 inches.
Source: https://up.codes/viewer/oregon/irc-2021/chapter/3/building-planning (retrieved 2026-10-29, two reads, the second asking for the distance column verbatim).
Confidence: high for the text as served; the Oregon amendment PDFs in the code library do not reproduce R302.1, so the official PDF was not cross-checked.

## 2026-10-29  What does ODFW's deer and elk fence guide specify, read directly?
Finding: "Deer Fences / Elk Fences" (Oregon Department of Fish and Wildlife, 5 pages). Woven wire deer fence: total height at least 76 inches on flat ground, a few inches added on the uphill side on slopes; "Adult deer in good condition can actually jump a 76 inch fence but generally do so only when chased. This height has been used by hundreds of gardeners with great success." Corner posts: wooden, "at least 9 1/2 feet in length and at least 5 inches in diameter at the top"; line posts 3-inch top-diameter wood or 8-foot #133 studded T steel posts, every seventh post wood where steel is used; wood posts treated for at least the bottom three feet except yew, "do not use posts treated with copper-type wood preservatives"; posts sunk about 2-1/2 feet, about 15 feet apart, braced at corners, angles and intervals. Woven wire from the ground to at least 58 inches, 14-1/2 gauge filler, 11 gauge top and bottom wires, vertical stays no more than 6 inches apart, spliced with hog rings; barbed strands no more than 5 inches apart above to 76 inches; bottom at ground level, "Deer can go under gaps as small as 6 inches"; 1-1/2-inch staples, at least 10 per post. Electric deer fence (New Zealand design, from Pennsylvania State University): at least 60 inches, six strands of 12-1/2 gauge class III high-tensile wire at 10-inch intervals, bottom wire 10 inches up, tension 250 pounds, wires two and four groundable in dry summers, a New Zealand-style energizer (220 V, 110 V, 12 V or solar) of at least 4,000 volts output, low impedance, pulse 1/30,000 second or less, 8-foot ground rod and lightning arrestor; corner posts 6 inches by 10 feet with 4 feet buried, line posts up to 50 feet apart. Polywire version: 5 feet, six strands at 10 inches, line posts about 30 feet. Elk fence: at least 84 inches, corner posts at least 10 feet long and 5 inches at the top, two 39-inch widths of woven wire (10 gauge top and bottom, 12-1/2 gauge filler, stays 6 inches in the lower width and 12 inches in the upper), 1-1/2-inch staples at least 12 per post, hog rings every 18 inches joining the widths, at least one barbed strand above. Correction to research/answers/ch26_developing-site.md item 51: the 10-foot corner post is the elk fence's; the deer fence's corner post is 9-1/2 feet. The electric-fence detail item 51 said it lacked is in the document.
Source: https://www.dfw.state.or.us/wildlife/living_with/docs/deerelkfences.pdf (downloaded and text extracted 2026-10-29).

## 2026-10-29  Does Oregon law decide who fences against livestock, and what is a partition fence worth?
Finding: ORS 607.005: "Livestock district" means an area wherein it is unlawful for livestock or a class of livestock to run at large; "Open range" means an area wherein livestock may lawfully be permitted to run at large; livestock means cattle, horses, mules, asses, sheep, goats and swine. ORS 607.007(1): "Adequate fence" means a continuous barrier consisting of natural barriers, structures, masonry, rails, poles, planks, wire or the combination thereof, installed and maintained in a condition so as to form a continuous guard and defense against the ingress or egress of cattle or equines; (2)(b) estray animals include cattle or equines "found to be trespassing on land enclosed by an adequate fence". ORS 607.018: a landowner applies to the county governing body to create or annex to a livestock district. ORS 607.044: a person who permits livestock to run at large on land in a district closed to that class is liable to the landowner. ORS 607.045(1): no owner or custodian may permit livestock to run at large, or be herded or pastured, on another's land in such a district. ORS 607.300: when cattle or equines break through a fence on the open range, the Department of Agriculture determines the fence's adequacy. ORS 607.311(1): a person who finds an estray animal on premises in their control may take it up. ORS 96.010: where a fence "such as a good husbandman ought to keep" stands on a line and the adjoining owner or long-term lessee encloses against it, that person "shall pay the owner of such fence already erected, one-half of the value of so much thereof as serves as a partition fence", recoverable in a civil action with costs and attorney fees. ORS 96.020 and 96.030: a justice of the peace may direct repair of a neglected partition fence, and the complainant may repair and recover. ORS 96.040: six months' written notice before removing one's share of a partition fence. ORS 96.060: a fence built on another's land by mistake and in good faith may be removed by its builder within one year of discovering the mistake.
Source: https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors607.html and ors096.html (downloaded 2026-10-29).
Confidence: high.
Used in: 26.5 Code and Professional; 26.19 table (the mistaken fence).
