## 2026-10-27  How did the 2026 NEC reorganize the low-voltage and communications rules?
Finding: EC&M's "Top Changes to the 2026 National Electrical Code" (November 2025): new Article 720 "General Requirements for Limited-Energy Systems Wiring Methods and Materials" ("created to consolidate the general rules for limited-energy systems, including Class 2, Class 4, power-limited fire alarm (PLFA), and communication systems"); Article 721 power sources; Article 722 "Limited-Energy Cables for Power-Limited Circuits, Fault-Managed-Power Circuits, Optical Fiber Circuits, and Communications Circuits"; Article 723 raceways, cable routing assemblies and cable trays; Article 742 overvoltage protection; Article 750 grounding and bonding of limited-energy systems; deleted Articles 770 (optical fiber cables) and 805 (communications circuits); Article 220 moved to Article 120; energy management systems moved from Article 750 to Article 130; small switch-type wiring devices moved from Article 404 into Article 406. EC&M's code-basics article adds: "Articles 770, 805, and 840 were deleted"; "The scope of Chapter 8 was changed to apply only to outdoor installations"; 90.3 revised to end Chapter 8's independence. The 2026 Oregon Table 1-E amends 720.6(F), 720.8(A)(1) (strikes "Sealants shall be identified for use with cable insulation, conductor insulation, a bare conductor, a shield, or other components"; keeps "The raceway or sleeve shall be sealed to prevent the circulation of warm air to a colder section of the raceway or sleeve"), 720.11 and 720.30(A)(3), and 800.48.

## 2026-10-27  What do OAR 918-261-0039 and 918-271-0030 actually say?
Finding: OAR 918-261-0039 "Board Exemption for Products Located on the Load Side of a Listed Class 2 Transformer": "(1) An exemption from product certification is granted under ORS 479.540 for installation of Class 2 products, other than wiring, located on the load side of a listed Class 2 transformer. The exemption applies only to products that are not: (a) In a damp or wet location; (b) Located in a plenum, duct or other air-handling space; (c) Located in any area from chapter 5 of the adopted Oregon Electrical Specialty Code; or (d) Part of a protective signaling system. (2) An exemption from permitting is created for installation of Class 2 wiring located on the load side of a listed garage door controller. The exemption applies only to one- and two-family dwellings when the wiring is not part of the original wiring of the dwelling." OAR 918-271-0030 is "Correction of Defects" in Division 271 "Electrical Inspections": "(1) Defects in electrical installations noted by the electrical inspector shall be corrected and an inspection request made within 20 calendar days of the date of actual notice of deficiency." It has nothing to do with CCTV or intercoms. The licensing exemption the Revision 7 text meant is OAR 918-282-0110(4): "electrical licenses are not required to ... (4) Install limited energy systems not exceeding 100 voltampere ('VA') in Class 2 and 3 systems limited to: (a) Single station smoke or ionization detectors installed in buildings three stories or less in height; (b) Closed circuit television systems installed in buildings three stories or less in height; (c) Master Antenna Television ('MATV') systems installed in buildings three stories or less in height; or (d) Intercom and audio systems installed in one- and two-family dwellings." It is a licensing exemption, not a permit exemption.

## 2026-10-27  Which Oregon statutes govern recording intimate images, and at what grade?
Finding: ORS 163.700 (invasion of personal privacy in the second degree) covers knowingly recording "another person's intimate area without the consent of the other person" where the person "has a reasonable expectation of privacy concerning the intimate area"; "(3) Invasion of personal privacy in the second degree is a Class A misdemeanor." (2)(d): places with a reasonable expectation of personal privacy include "a bathroom, dressing room, locker room that includes an enclosed area for dressing or showering, tanning booth and any area where a person undresses in an enclosed space that is not open to public view." ORS 163.701 (first degree): knowingly recording "another person in a state of nudity without the consent of the other person" in such a place; "(2)(a) Invasion of personal privacy in the first degree is a Class C felony."

## 2026-10-27  Does ORS 166.065 (harassment) reach a camera aimed at a neighbor?
Finding: No. ORS 166.065(1) covers offensive physical contact, false reports of death or serious injury, and threats. The stalking definitions do reach electronic monitoring: ORS 163.730(3)(o), "Contact" includes "The use of an electronic service, application, device or other electronic means to obtain, monitor or interfere with the location, communication or activities of the other person, without the consent of the other person" (2024 c.90). ORS 163.732(1): stalking is knowingly alarming or coercing another "by engaging in repeated and unwanted contact"; it must be objectively reasonable to be alarmed, and the contact must cause "reasonable apprehension regarding the personal safety of the victim"; (2)(a) Class A misdemeanor. ORS 163.730(9): "Repeated" means two or more times.
Source: oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors166.html and ors163.html (downloaded 2026-10-27).
Confidence: high for the text.
Used in: 24.13 Professional and Code. Replaces the Revision 7 Code entry sourced to ORS 166.065; corrects wave 1 research line 958.

## 2026-10-27  Does DEQ require a high-water alarm on a pumped septic system?
