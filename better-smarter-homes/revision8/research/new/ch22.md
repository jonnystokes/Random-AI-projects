## 2026-10-27  What changed in the 2026 NEC for PV and storage (base text, not Oregon amendments)?
Finding: Mayfield Renewables (Portland): "Article 220 is now Article 120" and "Article 750 is now Article 130"; 706.16 "was deleted in its entirety from the 2023 to the 2026 versions"; 706.15 directs users to 705.20 for disconnect requirements; 702.4 now governs ESS backup sizing and most ESS fall under optional standby rather than Article 710; 705.11(C)(1) reinstates conductor-length limits for supply-side connections; 690.13 references 705.20. GreenLancer: "Section 705.13 is also refocused on PCS in 2026, with informational notes referencing UL 3141 and UL 1741"; "Section 706.16 was deleted, and most interconnected ESS used for backup now follow the optional standby system rules in Article 702"; a new 702.4 option lets inverter-based backup systems in one- and two-family dwellings use a listed PCS. Search summaries from other installers (heavendesigns.in, portlandiaelectric.supply) state that 690.12's initiation-device language was reorganized under 690.12(C), labeling sits in 690.12(D), and the performance targets did not change. The Oregon 2026 amendments cite 120 Parts I to IV, 130.30 and 130.70, consistent with the renumbering.
Source: mayfield.energy/technical-articles/2026-nec-updates-for-solar-and-energy-storage-systems/; greenlancer.com/post/2026-nec-solar-changes (both retrieved 2026-10-27); 26oesc-table1-E.pdf page 20.

## 2026-10-27  Correction to the same answer file, Solar item 37: is the protected-membrane roof "a poured concrete structural deck rather than insulation-over-deck"?
Finding: The answer argues that ballast compressing insulation "is a materially smaller concern on this project's actual roof, which is a poured concrete structural deck rather than insulation-over-deck". In a protected-membrane roof (the roof answer file's PMR, ch15 items 5, 13 and 15) the insulation and ballast lie on top of the membrane, so a ballasted rack's feet bear on pavers over the insulation. The foot loads are checked against the insulation board's compressive strength, and the rack layout is coordinated with the roof drains and the ballast. The manuscript states the check without a number.
Source: research/answers/ch15_roof.md items 13 and 15 (the PMR assembly: ballast and insulation over the membrane); the answer text quoted above.
Confidence: high that the statement does not describe a PMR; the compressive-strength figures belong to the roof design.
Used in: 22.5 Applied; cross-chapter flag for the roof chapter.

- **2026-10-27. Chapter 15, Roof System, and research/answers/ch21-24_electrical-solar-hvac-smart.md Solar item 37.** Item 37 says ballast compression of insulation is "a materially smaller concern on this project's actual roof, which is a poured concrete structural deck rather than insulation-over-deck".
