# Cross-chapter flags (append only)

A writer who finds a problem in another chapter files it here and does not fix the other
chapter. The orchestrator carries each flag to the chapter's writer or to the whole-book pass.
Format: date, the chapter and location, what is wrong, the evidence.

## 2026-10-26, from the chapter 2 writer

- **Chapter 31, Inspection and Approval Sequence, section 31.10 and its Code block (Revision 7 text).** Cites "ORSC R114 (2023 edition)" as the stop-work authority. R114 is "Not adopted by the State of Oregon" in both the 2023 and 2026 ORSC Chapter 1; stop-work authority is municipal ordinance preserved by ORS 455.020(4). Evidence: revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf page 14; ors455.html lines 2332 to 2345; research/new/ch02.md.
- **research/answers/ch03_land_part4.md, item 134.** States that the Local Building Department Directory lists each department "including its administration type". The Directory page shows contact information, building official and permit services only. Evidence: oregon.gov/bcd/lbdd (retrieved 2026-10-26); ch02 answer file item 5 agrees. The land chapter's writer follows item 5.
- **Every chapter citing ORSC R104.11, R104.11.1 or R104.4.** The 2026 ORSC Chapter 1 (effective and mandatory October 1, 2026) renumbers the alternative-materials provision to R104.2.2 (with sub-sections R104.2.2.1 to R104.2.2.6.2), changes the equivalency criteria list, and moves the inspection-report acceptance to R109.2. Chapters citing the 2023 numbers must add or switch to the 2026 numbers. Evidence: research/new/ch02.md, entry "Does the 2026 ORSC Chapter 1 keep the 2023 numbering".
- **Structural System and Concrete Design chapter.** Statewide Alternate Method 26-01 (January 20, 2026) lets a 2023 ORSC nonprescriptive design keep referencing the 2022 OSSC (ASCE 7-16 snow maps) until the 2026 ORSC is mandatory; the 2025 OSSC adopts ASCE 7-22 snow maps, which raise design snow loads in some parts of the state and lower them in others. The structural chapter states which OSSC edition the drawings reference. Evidence: revision6_code_library/sam-26-01-23orscref22ossc.pdf.

## 2026-10-27, from the chapter 4 writer (merged by the orchestrator from research/new/ch04.md)


- **Chapter 2, Oregon Code and Compliance Map, section 2.7 Code entry "A sale soon after completion requires a lien-protection notice", and the "Post-completion lien notice" row of the 2.7 Professional table.** The entry states that the seller serves the Homebuyer Protection Act notice "when a residential structure is sold within about 75 days of substantial completion". ORS 87.007 applies to the sale of any new single-family residence (and to a sale within three months of $50,000 or more of improvements), whenever it sells; the seller protects the buyer by one of five methods, one of which is closing after the 75-day lien-perfection deadline of ORS 87.035(1), and delivers a CCB form by closing day (87.007(3)). The 75-day period in ORS 87.093(2)(a) is the original contractor's notice duty to a first purchaser. The value line also hedges a number ("about 75 days"). Chapter 4, section 4.4, now carries the corrected entries. Evidence: ors087.html (87.007, 87.035, 87.093); research/new/ch04.md.
- **Chapter 2, section 2.7 Code entry "Federal tax sets its own occupancy clock".** The Source line reads "IRC section 121"; brief v2 and v3 require "26 U.S.C. 121", since IRC means the International Residential Code in this book. Chapter 4 cites 26 U.S.C. 121(a) and (b).
- **Chapter 2, section 2.7 Code entry "An owner may install their own septic system under their own permit".** The Source is OAR 340-071-0600, which as amended effective January 1, 2026 contains no property-owner exception (it requires an installer license "for any person to construct or install onsite systems"). The permittee exception from installer certification is OAR 340-071-0650(1)(a), and DEQ's fact sheet "Procedures and Requirements for Installing a New Septic System" states that a system is constructed by "either the property owner or a DEQ-licensed installer". Evidence: revision6_code_library/oar/oar-340-071-full.html; research/new/ch04.md.
- **Chapter 3, Land, Site, and Feasibility, section 3.19 Professional ("Lien law, the partition statutes and the owner-builder exemption are in the legal chapter").** Chapter 4 covers construction liens only at contracting (the contractor's Information Notice to Owner, ORS 87.093) and at the sale of a new house (ORS 87.007 and 87.035). Lien law during construction (notices of right to a lien from suppliers and subcontractors, lien waivers against draws) has no home in either chapter; the project finance chapter, which owns the draw schedule, is the likely place.
- **Chapter 31, Inspection and Approval Sequence, section 31.10 (Revision 7 text), Code entry "Disputes have a two-level appeal path".** The value line reads "Local board of appeals (R112, typically within 30 days)". OAR 918-008-0120(1) lets the aggrieved person appeal to either the local appeals board or the state specialty code chief, "The appeals process selected may not change once initiated", and (3) "An appeal must be filed within 30 calendar days of the building official's decision"; the chief decides in 14 days, 30 at most (5). The rewrite can cite the rule and drop "typically". Chapter 4, section 4.8, states these as Code entries. Evidence: oregon.public.law/rules/oar_918-008-0120.
- **Chapter 36, Business and Brand Framework (Revision 7 text), the occupancy presumption passage.** It states ORS 701.031(1)(b) (two or more newly built structures offered for sale within 36 months) and OAR 812-002-0520 (occupancy events) in prose. Chapter 4, section 4.4, now carries both as verified Code entries, so the business chapter can name the legal chapter at that overlap and keep its own portfolio discussion.
