## 2026-10-29  What does Oregon's construction lien law require of suppliers, subcontractors and owners during construction?
Finding: ORS 87.021(1): "Except when material, equipment, services or labor ... is furnished at the request of the owner, a person furnishing any materials, equipment, services or labor ... for which a lien may be perfected under ORS 87.035 shall give a notice of right to a lien to the owner of the site. The notice of right to a lien may be given at any time during the progress of the improvement, but the notice only protects the right to perfect a lien for materials, equipment and labor or services provided after a date which is eight days, not including Saturdays, Sundays and other holidays ..., before the notice is delivered or mailed." 87.021(3)(a): a lien may be perfected only to the extent the notice is given, except for commercial improvements (3)(b). 87.023 prescribes the notice form ("THIS IS NOT A LIEN"). 87.025(3): no lien for materials has priority over a recorded mortgage or trust deed unless the supplier delivers a copy of the notice to the mortgagee within eight days (weekends and holidays excluded) of delivery; (4) the mortgagee may demand a list within 15 days; (5) "Upon payment and acceptance of the amount due to the supplier of materials or supplies, and upon demand of the person making payment, the supplier shall execute a waiver of all lien rights as to materials or supplies for which payment has been made"; (7) perfection relates back to commencement of the improvement. 87.027: an owner who receives a notice may demand in writing a list of materials or a statement of the contractual basis including percentage complete and charges to date, delivered within 15 days (weekends and holidays excluded), on pain of losing attorney fees and costs in a foreclosure suit. 87.035(1): perfection not later than 75 days after the claimant ceases to provide labor or materials or 75 days after completion, whichever is earlier; (2) by filing a claim of lien with the county recording officer; (3) and (4) contents and verification. 87.039(1): notice of filing mailed to the owner and the mortgagee within 20 days, with a copy of the claim; (2) no costs or attorney fees otherwise. 87.045(1): completion occurs at substantial completion, at a posted and recorded completion notice, or at abandonment; (2) when all original contractors have substantially performed, "any original contractor, the owner or mortgagee, or an agent of any of them may post and record a completion notice"; (5)(a) an improvement is abandoned on the 75th day after work ceases; (6) a nonabandonment notice posted and recorded not later than the 74th day, renewable at 150-day intervals. 87.055: no lien binds the improvement longer than 120 days after filing unless suit is brought, and no payment extension carries it past two years. 87.057(1): written notice of intent to foreclose to the owner and mortgagee not later than 10 days before suit; (2) a list of materials and charges within five days of the owner's demand. 87.076(1) and (2): a surety bond or a deposit with the county treasurer of 150 percent of the amount claimed or $1,000, whichever is greater, releases the lien; (3) at any time after the claim is filed. 87.093: the Information Notice to Owner (the legal chapter's entry). The chapter contains forms for the notice of right to a lien (87.023) and the Information Notice (87.093) and no waiver form; "waiver" appears in the construction-lien sections only at 87.007(2)(d) and 87.025(5).
Source: https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors087.html (downloaded, windows-1252, sections extracted by script).
Confidence: high (statute text).
Used in: 33.6 Code (ten entries), 33.6 Professional and Applied, 33.13.

## 2026-10-29  How does the Construction Contractors Board describe the owner's lien protections?
Finding: The CCB pamphlet "Construction Liens" (f:constructionlienspamphlet/7-16): the notice of right to a lien "may be given at any time during the construction, but it should be given within eight working days of the start date of the work or the delivery date of the materials or equipment"; "A lien holder has 75 days after completing the construction, or ceasing work on the construction, in which to file the lien"; the owner's protections are joint checks ("many banks will not accept checks made payable to multiple parties unless each party appears at the bank with government-issued identification") and lien waivers or releases ("Before you make any payments to your contractor, ask every person who gave you a Notice of Right to a Lien to provide you with a signed lien waiver or release"); an owner who receives a notice may demand a list, answered within 15 days; "Liens are invalid after 120 days if no lawsuit to foreclose on the lien has been filed"; suppliers must also notify the holder of a recorded mortgage or trust deed within eight working days to keep priority.
Source: https://www.oregon.gov/ccb/Documents/Construction%20Liens%20brochure.pdf (downloaded; text extracted with pdftotext).
Confidence: high (the agency's own pamphlet; it restates the statute).

## 2026-10-29  How does Oregon assess and bill a house under construction?
Finding: ORS 308.007(1): assessment year is the calendar year, tax year begins July 1; (2) the assessment year beginning January 1 corresponds to the tax year beginning July 1 of the same calendar year. ORS 308.210(1): the assessor records the assessment "as of January 1, at 1:00 a.m. of the assessment year". ORS 308.146(1): maximum assessed value "equals 103 percent of the property's assessed value from the prior year or 100 percent of the property's maximum assessed value from the prior year, whichever is greater"; (2) assessed value is the lesser of maximum assessed value and real market value; (3)(a) new property or new improvements are valued under 308.149 to 308.166. ORS 308.149(5)(a): "minor construction" means additions of real property improvements not exceeding $18,200 in any assessment year or $45,000 cumulative over five assessment years, (b) indexed to the CPI West from assessment years beginning on or after January 1, 2025; (6)(a) and (b): "new property or new improvements" means changes in value from new construction, reconstruction, major additions, remodeling and the rest, and excludes general ongoing maintenance and repair and minor construction. ORS 308.153(1): the maximum assessed value of property with new improvements is the prior maximum plus "the product of the value of the new property or new improvements ... multiplied by the ratio, not greater than 1.00, of the average maximum assessed value over the average real market value for the assessment year"; (2)(a) the value of new improvements equals their real market value less retirements. ORS 311.250(1): tax statements delivered or mailed on or before October 25 for taxes payable the following November 15. ORS 311.505(1): one-third on or before November 15, February 15 and May 15; (2) interest at one and one-third percent per month on a late installment; (3) discounts of two percent on two-thirds paid by November 15 and three percent where all is paid by then. ORS 309.100(1): the owner may petition "the property value appeals board"; (2) petitions are filed with the board's clerk "during the period following the date the tax statements are mailed or otherwise delivered for the current tax year and ending December 31"; (3) in writing, stating facts and grounds, signed and verified.
Source: https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors308.html, ors311.html, ors309.html (downloaded).

## 2026-10-29  What do Measures 5 and 50 say, in the constitution's own words?
Finding: Article XI, section 11(1)(b): "For tax years beginning after July 1, 1997, the property's maximum assessed value shall not increase by more than three percent from the previous tax year." Section 11(1)(c): new property or new improvements (among other triggers) are valued "at the ratio of average maximum assessed value to average real market value" of comparable property in the same area and class. Section 11b(1): the table of maximum taxes per $1,000 of real market value gives $5.00 for the school system and $10.00 for other government for fiscal year 1995-1996 "and thereafter". Section 11b(3): "The limitations of subsection (1) of this section apply to all taxes imposed on property or property ownership except" the listed exceptions, which include taxes to pay bonds authorized by specific constitutional provisions and voter-approved general-obligation bonds for capital construction or improvements.

## 2026-10-29  How does an Oregon county assessor value a partly built house?
Finding: Clackamas County Assessment and Taxation FAQ: "The assessment date for all property is January 1. This means new construction is valued at the percent completed on January 1. If 50% complete, then a house is valued at half its market value ... If construction on your house was started after January 1, then you will pay tax only on the land until the following year." Also: "New construction, additions and remodeled properties do receive a Measure 50 benefit similar to existing property. The market value of the new construction is reduced by the ratio of the maximum assessed value to real market value ... Once a new assessed value is established, it then continues to grow by the annual 3% increase required by Measure 50." Washington County's assessment page gives the $18,200 minor-construction figure and the "greater of the prior year's MAV or the prior year's Assessed Value increased by three (3) percent" rule.
Source: https://www.clackamas.us/at/faq; http://www.washingtoncountyor.gov/at/appraisal/property-assessment.

## 2026-10-29  Which federal tax rules reach construction interest and the basis of a self-built house?
Finding: 26 CFR 1.163-10T(p)(5)(i): "A taxpayer may treat a residence under construction as a qualified residence for a period of up to 24 months, but only if the residence becomes a qualified residence, without regard to this paragraph (p)(5)(i), as of the time that the residence is ready for occupancy." 26 U.S.C. 163(h)(3)(B)(ii): $1,000,000 ($500,000 married filing separately); (F)(i), "In the case of taxable years beginning after December 31, 2017", subclause (II): "Subparagraph (B)(ii) shall be applied by substituting '$750,000 ($375,000' for '$1,000,000 ($500,000'", with (III) carving out indebtedness incurred on or before December 15, 2017; the Cornell text on 2026-10-29 contained no "before January 1, 2026" sunset in (F)(i). 26 U.S.C. 1012(a): "The basis of property shall be the cost of such property." IRS Publication 523 (2025), "Construction": a house built on land you own has a basis of the land's cost plus what it cost to complete the house, including labor and materials, contractor payments, architect fees, building permit charges, utility meter and connection charges and legal fees directly connected with building; "If you built all or part of your house yourself, its basis is the total amount it cost you to complete it"; "Don't include in the cost of the house: The value of your own labor" or "The value of any other labor for which you didn't pay"; keep basis records "until 3 years after the due date for your tax return for the year in which you sold your home". IRS Publication 936 (2025): "You can treat a home under construction as a qualified home for a period of up to 24 months" if it "becomes your qualified home at the time it is ready for occupancy"; the acquisition-debt limit for debt secured after December 15, 2017 is "$750,000 ($375,000 if married filing separately)"; the publication does not say whether the limit is permanent. Secondary tax commentary (CPA Practice Advisor, Journal of Accountancy, July 2025) reports that Public Law 119-21 removed the 2025 sunset; not verified against the public law's text.
Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.163-10T; https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/163; https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1012; https://www.irs.gov/publications/p523; https://www.irs.gov/publications/p936.
Confidence: high for the regulation, statute and publication texts; medium for the permanence of the $750,000 limit (the current statute text shows no sunset, the amending act was not read).
