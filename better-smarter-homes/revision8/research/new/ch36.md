## 2026-10-29  What does ORS 701.021(1) say, current text?
Finding: "Except as provided in ORS 701.010, a person or joint venture that undertakes, offers to undertake or submits a bid to do work as a contractor must have a current license issued by the Construction Contractors Board and possess an appropriate endorsement as provided in this section." (2) lists the residential endorsements, among them residential developer and home inspector services contractor. History: 2007 c.836, amended through 2015 c.498.
Source: oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_701.021; the library copy revision6_code_library/ors/ors701.html holds the same text (its heading breaks "701.021" and the title across a line, which is why a space-delimited search misses it).
Confidence: high.
Used in: 36.1 Code.

## 2026-10-29  Who is a "contractor" by owning land and building to sell?
Finding: ORS 701.005(5)(b): "A person that purchases or owns property and constructs or for compensation arranges for the construction of one or more residential structures or small commercial structures with the intent of selling the structures." (6): "Developer" means a contractor that owns property or an interest in property and engages in the business of arranging for construction work or performing other activities associated with the improvement of real property, with the intent to sell the property. The 2027-operative version (Oregon Laws 2025, chapter 78, section 17) restates both.
Source: revision6_code_library/ors/ors701.html (2025 edition), read 2026-10-29.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 36.1 Code (replaces the Revision 7 pointer entry "ORS 701.005; ORS 701.021").

## 2026-10-29  Which ORS 701.010 exemptions bear on a materials seller and on a small paid job that is advertised?
Finding: ORS 701.010(3) exempts "A person who furnishes materials, supplies, equipment or finished product and does not fabricate them into, or consume them, in the performance of the work of a contractor." (4) exempts a person whose contracts on one structure total less than $1,000 for casual, minor or inconsequential work, and "This subsection does not apply to a person who advertises or puts out any sign or card or other device that might indicate to the public that the person is a contractor." (5) and (7) read as the legal chapter states them, with the non-occupancy presumption in (5). The note in the library text: the amendments by section 18, chapter 78, Oregon Laws 2025 become operative July 1, 2027, and the 2027 text of (3), (4), (5) and (7) is identical.
Source: revision6_code_library/ors/ors701.html (2025 edition with the 2027 text set out), read 2026-10-29.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 36.1 Code ((4)), 36.16 Code ((3)), 36.3 Professional (the amendment).

## 2026-10-29  What is ORS 701.031's wording?
Finding: "(1) It is prima facie evidence that a person is doing business as a contractor if: (a) The person for that person's own use performs, employs others to perform, or for compensation and with the intent to sell the structure arranges to have performed, work described in ORS 701.005 (5); and (b) Within any 36-month period the person offers for sale two or more newly built structures on which work described in paragraph (a) of this subsection was performed. (2) Licensure under this chapter is prima facie evidence that the licensee conducts a separate, independent business." History: 2007 c.114 §3.
Source: oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_701.031.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 36.3 Professional (named to the legal chapter, whose 4.4 carries the entry).

## 2026-10-29  What do the home-inspection statute and rules require?
Finding: ORS 701.445(1): an individual may not undertake, offer to undertake or submit a bid to do work as a home inspector unless certified by the board and an owner of, or employed by, a business licensed by the board. (2): the same for a business, which must have a certified owner or employee. (6): fees capped at $50 application, $50 testing, $150 initial two-year certificate, $150 renewal. ORS 701.005(9)(a): a home inspector is a person who, for a fee, inspects and provides written reports on the overall physical condition of a residential structure. OAR 812-008-0030(4): anyone, including those exempt under section (3), must receive certification before using the title "Oregon certified home inspector". OAR 812-008-0201(4): advertising must not be misleading or deceptive, and a business may not advertise inspection services unless properly licensed.
Source: revision6_code_library/ors/ors701.html; oregon.public.law/rules/oar_812-008-0030 and oar_812-008-0201 (the pages give no effective-date history; the SOS record holds it).
Confidence: high for the statute, medium-high for the rule paraphrases (the summarizer paraphrased (4) of each rule rather than quoting).

## 2026-10-29  Which Unlawful Trade Practices Act provisions reach an information product?
Finding: ORS 646.608(1): "A person engages in an unlawful practice if in the course of the person's business, vocation or occupation the person does any of the following:" (b) "Causes likelihood of confusion or of misunderstanding as to the source, sponsorship, approval, or certification of real estate, goods or services." (c) "Causes likelihood of confusion or of misunderstanding as to affiliation, connection, or association with, or certification by, another." (e) "Represents that real estate, goods or services have sponsorship, approval, characteristics, ingredients, uses, benefits, quantities or qualities that the real estate, goods or services do not have or that a person has a sponsorship, approval, status, qualification, affiliation, or connection that the person does not have." ORS 646.605(6)(a): "Real estate, goods or services" means those obtained primarily for personal, family or household purposes (and any purpose where obtained through a telephone solicitation). (8): "Trade" and "commerce" mean advertising, offering or distributing any real estate, goods or services. The 646.608 text shown is the version operative January 1, 2025 (Oregon Laws 2023, chapter 410).
Source: oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_646.608 and ors_646.605.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 36.2 Code.

## 2026-10-29  What does the engineering act define as practice, and which exemptions reach plans for a house?
Finding: ORS 672.005(1): "'Practice of engineering' or 'practice of professional engineering' means any of the following when done for others:" (the list follows). ## 2026-10-29  What are the LLC registered-agent and annual-report rules, and the state fees?
Finding: ORS 63.111(1): an LLC must continuously maintain a registered agent and registered office in Oregon, at a physical street address where the agent can be served in person; mail-receiving agencies, mail-forwarding businesses and virtual offices do not qualify. (2): the agent is an Oregon-resident individual whose business office is the registered office, or a domestic or authorized foreign entity with its business office there. ORS 63.787(1): the annual report is due by the company's anniversary and states the name and state of organization, the registered office and agent, the principal office, the managers or at least one member, and the primary business activity. (2): the information is current as of 30 days before the anniversary. The Secretary of State's Business Registry fee schedule PDF (br-fee-schedule.pdf) lists the Articles of Organization and the annual report for a domestic LLC; the figures and the sheet's revision date are read from the converted text and recorded in the next entry.

## 2026-10-29  Corporate activity tax thresholds
Finding: ORS 317A.125(1): the tax equals $250 plus 0.57 percent of taxable commercial activity in excess of $1 million; (2): "A tax is not owed under this section if the person's taxable commercial activity does not exceed $1 million." ## 2026-10-29  Independent-contractor tests

## 2026-10-29  Copyright registration timing, fees and the scope of plan protection
Finding: 17 U.S.C. 412: no statutory damages or attorney's fees for (1) infringement of an unpublished work commenced before the effective date of its registration, or (2) infringement commenced after first publication and before registration, "unless such registration is made within three months after the first publication of the work." 17 U.S.C. 411(a): no civil action for infringement of a United States work until preregistration or registration has been made, and where registration has been refused the applicant may sue if notice and a copy of the complaint are served on the Register. 17 U.S.C. 101: an "architectural work" is "the design of a building as embodied in any tangible medium of expression, including a building, architectural plans, or drawings. The work includes the overall form as well as the arrangement and composition of spaces and elements in the design, but does not include individual standard features." "Pictorial, graphic, and sculptural works" include "maps, globes, charts, diagrams, models, and technical drawings, including architectural plans." 37 CFR 201.3(c): $45 for an electronic application by a single author, same claimant, one work, not for hire ((c)(1)(i)(A)); $65 for all other electronic filings ((c)(1)(i)(B)); $85 for a group of unpublished works ((c)(11)). The copyright.gov fee page shows the same three figures.
Source: law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/412, /411, /101; law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/201.3; copyright.gov/about/fees.html.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 36.8 Code.

## 2026-10-29  Takedown notice, counter-notice and misrepresentation
Finding: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(3)(A): six elements, a physical or electronic signature of an authorized person, identification of the copyrighted work, identification of the infringing material with information sufficient to locate it, the complainant's contact information, a statement of good-faith belief, and a statement under penalty of perjury that the information is accurate and the complainant is authorized. 512(g)(2)(C): after a counter notification the provider replaces the material "not less than 10, nor more than 14, business days following receipt of the counter notice" unless its agent first receives notice that the complainant has filed a court action. 512(f): a person who "knowingly materially misrepresents" that material is infringing, or was removed by mistake, is liable for the resulting damages, costs and attorney's fees.
Source: law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 36.9 Code.

## 2026-10-29  The endorsement guides and the consumer-review rule, current text
Finding: 16 CFR 255.5(a): a connection between an endorser and the seller that might materially affect the weight or credibility of the endorsement, and that the audience would not reasonably expect, must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously; such connections include family or personal ties, payments, free or discounted products and other benefits. 255.2(b): an endorsement of a central attribute may be read as typical, and without substantiation for that the advertisement "should clearly and conspicuously disclose the generally expected performance." 16 CFR part 465 (89 FR 68077, August 22, 2024): 465.2 bans writing, creating or selling a consumer review that materially misrepresents that the reviewer exists or used the product, buying such a review, or procuring reviews from officers, managers, employees, agents or their relatives known to be misleading; 465.4: "It is an unfair or deceptive act or practice and a violation of this part for a business to provide compensation or other incentives in exchange for, or conditioned expressly or by implication on, the writing or creation of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment, whether positive or negative"; 465.5: an officer's or manager's review carries a clear and conspicuous disclosure of the material relationship, and solicited insider reviews without disclosure violate the rule; 465.7: an unfounded or groundless legal threat, intimidation or a knowing false accusation to prevent or remove a review, and misrepresenting that displayed reviews are all or most of those submitted while hiding negative ones; 465.8: selling or buying fake indicators of social media influence. 16 CFR 1.98(d) and (e): the civil penalty for a violation of Section 5(m)(1)(A) or (B) of the FTC Act is $53,088 per violation for penalties assessed after January 17, 2025 (90 FR 5581).
Source: law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.5, /255.2, /465.2, /465.4, /465.5, /465.7, /465.8, /1.98.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 36.10 Code. The part's effective date (October 21, 2024) is from the Revision 7 text and the answer file, not re-read today.

## 2026-10-29  The silica standard's employer trigger and Oregon's rule
Finding: 29 CFR 1926.1153(a): applies to all occupational exposures to respirable crystalline silica in construction work except where employee exposure stays below 25 micrograms per cubic meter as an 8-hour time-weighted average under any foreseeable conditions. (b): "Employee exposure means the exposure to airborne respirable crystalline silica that would occur if the employee were not using a respirator." (c)(1): for each employee engaged in a Table 1 task the employer implements the controls the table specifies, unless the employer assesses and limits exposure under (d). (g)(1): "The employer shall establish and implement a written exposure control plan" describing the tasks, the controls per task, housekeeping, and access restriction. Oregon's own rule, OAR 437-002-1053 to 437-002-1065 (Division 2, Subdivision Z), "applies to all occupational exposures to respirable crystalline silica in general industry and construction activities", with the same 25-microgram exception; the September 23, 2016 adoption order placed construction under the Division 2 rule and amended 437-003-1000. OSHA's May 23, 2001 interpretation letter (standard 1975.3(d)), per J. J. Keller's and other summaries, states that a self-employed person with no employees is outside the Act's construction standards; osha.gov returned 403 to this session, so the letter itself was not read today.
Source: law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/1926.1153; oregon.public.law/rules/oar_437-002-1053; osha.oregon.gov/OSHARules/adopted/2016/ao5-2016-ltr.pdf (search result); jjkellercompliancenetwork.com summary of the 2001 letter.
Confidence: high for the rule texts; medium for the letter's wording.
