## 2026-10-26  Which subsections of ORS 701.010 hold the owner exemptions, and where does the "under the direction of a residential general contractor" condition sit?
Finding: Three subsections. (5): "An owner who contracts for work to be performed by a licensed contractor. This subsection does not apply to a person who, in the pursuit of an independent business, constructs, remodels, repairs or for compensation and with the intent to sell the structure, arranges to have constructed, remodeled or repaired a structure with the intent of offering the structure for sale before, upon or after completion. It is prima facie evidence that there was an intent of offering the structure for sale if the person who constructed, remodeled or repaired the structure or arranged to have the structure constructed, remodeled or repaired does not occupy the structure after its completion." (6): "An owner who contracts for one or more licensed contractors to perform work wholly or partially within the same calendar year on not more than three existing residential structures of the owner. This subsection does not apply to an owner contracting for work that requires a building permit unless the work that requires a permit is performed by, or under the direction of, a residential general contractor." (7): "A person performing work on a property that person owns or performing work as the owner's employee, whether the property is occupied by the owner or not, or a person performing work on that person's residence, whether or not that person owns the residence. This subsection does not apply to a person performing work on a structure owned by that person or the owner's employee, if the work is performed in the pursuit of an independent business with the intent of offering the structure for sale before, upon or after completion." The Revision 7 chapter's statement that "An owner who directly hires subcontractors for permit-required work loses the homeowner-GC exemption unless the work is performed under the direction of a licensed general contractor" (cited as "ORS 701.010 (2008 amendment)") misplaces the condition: it belongs to subsection (6), which covers an owner's existing residential structures (up to three a year), not an owner building a new dwelling to occupy under (5) and (7). The statutory homeowner statement itself (ORS 701.325(2)(d)) reads "I will be my own contractor and, if I hire contractors, I will hire only contractors licensed with the Construction Contractors Board and properly endorsed for the work to be performed."
Source: revision6_code_library/ors/ors701.html, sections 701.010 and 701.325, read 2026-10-26.
Confidence: high for the text; the Revision 7 "2008 amendment" reading is not supported by it.
Used in: 4.3, 4.4 and 4.6 (the condition is dropped from the owner-builder path and the Dropped list names it).

## 2026-10-26  What do ORS 701.005 and the CCB rules say about an owner who builds to sell?

## 2026-10-26  What are the penalties for unlicensed contracting and for an uninsured employer, and what is OAR 812-005-0800 (OBLIGATIONS: "pairing with the workers'-comp penalty schedule reads oddly")?

(7) $1,000 per offense for hiring a unlicensed subcontractor; ... (12) Failing to use a written contract as required by ORS 701.305 ...: $500 for the first offense; $1,000 for the second offense; and $5,000 for subsequent offenses." The Revision 7 chapter paired OAR 812-005-0800 with the workers'-compensation penalty; the rule is the CCB schedule and the statute is ORS 656.735.

## 2026-10-26  What does the Homebuyer Protection Act require, and when (the Revision 7 entry said "a sale within about 75 days of substantial completion requires a lien-protection notice")?
Finding: ORS 87.007(1): the section applies to a sale of "A new single family residence or a single family residence where the sales price is $50,000 or more for original construction or the contract price is $50,000 or more for improvements to the residence completed within three months before the date the property is sold" (and the same for condominium units and buildings of up to four units). (2): the owner of record who sells "shall protect the purchaser from claims of lien that arise before the date on which the sale is complete but that may become perfected under ORS 87.035 after the date on which the sale is complete by one of the following methods: (a) ... title insurance ... (A) Without exception for filed and unfiled claims of construction lien ...; (b) Retain in escrow ... not less than 25 percent of the sale price ...; (c) Maintain a bond or letter of credit in an amount that is not less than 25 percent of the sale price ...; (d) Obtain written waivers from every person that claims or perfects a lien or liens ... that, in an aggregate amount, exceed $5,000 ...; (e) Complete the sale of the residential property after the deadline for perfecting a claim of lien under ORS 87.035". (3): by the day the sale completes the seller delivers a CCB-designated form stating the method chosen or that the section does not apply. (5): violation of (3) is a Class A violation; (6): a purchaser may recover up to twice actual damages for a violation of (2), within two years. ORS 87.093(2)(a): an original contractor delivers the Information Notice to Owner to "The first purchaser of residential property constructed by the contractor and sold before or within the 75-day period immediately following the completion of construction". So the Act covers the sale of any new residence, at any time; closing after the 75-day lien-perfection window is one of the five ways to comply, and the 75-day period in 87.093 is a contractor's notice duty.
Source: oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors087.html (87.007, 87.045, 87.093), downloaded and read 2026-10-26.
Confidence: high.

## 2026-10-26  What does ORS 455.202 require of a municipality using a contract building official?
Finding: (1)(c): "'Discretionary decision' means: (A) Waiving a plan review, an inspection or a provision of the state building code; or (B) Allowing an alternative material, design or method of construction." (1)(d): the qualified employee is a municipal employee who completed the director's basic training before exercising oversight and any advanced training within 180 days of designation. (2)(a): a city that procured a contract official between January 1, 2018 and January 1, 2022 may continue; a city that did not may procure one only for not more than 180 days while recruiting a building official, plus not more than 180 additional days with the director's approval. (2)(b): "A county may procure services from a contract building official after January 1, 2022, only if the county complies with the provisions of ORS 455.202 to 455.208 and only if the county's procurement occurs for the periods and under the circumstances described for cities in paragraph (a)(A) and (B)". (3): the contract official notifies the qualified employee in writing of each discretionary decision and notifies the permit applicant of each decision relating to the application, listing hearing and appeal opportunities; the qualified employee "must review and ratify or disapprove a contract building official's discretionary decision within 30 days after receiving notice". (4): a local board for appeals of discretionary decisions, which may be the county's or an adjacent county's; a county board includes a building official from an adjacent county; no contract official or inspection-company person sits on it. (5)(a): those appeal rights are "in addition to and not in lieu of" other appeal rights; the appeal is submitted within 30 days after the notice and decided within 30 days; (5)(b): the applicant "must establish by a preponderance of the evidence that overturning the discretionary decision of the contract building official will not create a dangerous or unsafe condition or decrease the minimum fire and life safety standards". (6): a city has an independent audit of the program's finances at least every two years, a county an audit covering the contract period, results posted on the municipality's website.
Source: revision6_code_library/ors/ors455.html (455.202), read 2026-10-26.
Confidence: high for the text; medium for the reading that long-term private operation of a county program runs through the state's delegated program under ORS 455.148(6) rather than the county's own procurement (an inference from (2)(b) and 455.148(6) as stated in research/new/ch02.md).

## 2026-10-26  Does the residential code itself allow a phased permit or a deferred submittal for a dwelling (the Revision 7 text called residential deferral "local, non-statutory accommodation")?
Finding: ORSC R106.3.3 Phased approval (same text in the 2023 and 2026 editions): "The building official is authorized to issue a permit for the construction of foundations or any other part of a building or structure before the construction documents for the whole building or structure have been submitted, provided that adequate information and detailed statements have been filed complying with pertinent requirements of this code. The holder of such permit for the foundation or other parts of a building or structure shall proceed at the holder's own risk with the building operation and without assurance that a permit for the entire structure will be granted." R106.3.4 Design professional in responsible charge (both editions): where documents must be prepared by a registered design professional, the official "shall be authorized to require the owner to engage and designate on the building permit application a registered design professional who shall act as the registered design professional in responsible charge", who "shall be responsible for reviewing and coordinating submittal documents prepared by others, including phased and deferred submittal items, for compatibility with the design of the building." OAR 918-480-0030 Phased Project and Deferred Submittal Fees: "When requested to review and approve plans for phased construction or deferred plan submittals under the Oregon Residential Specialty Code ... (1) Phased Permits. There is an application fee of $50 for each separate phase of the project. In addition, the plan review fee is increased in an amount equal to 10 percent of the building permit fee ... using the value of the particular phase of the project, not to exceed an additional $1,500 for each phase. (2) Deferred Submittals. The fee for processing and reviewing deferred plan submittals is an amount equal to 65 percent of the building permit fee ... using the value of the particular deferred portion or portions of the project. This fee is in addition to the project plan review fee based on total project value." So a residential phased permit and deferred submittals exist in the code and in state fee rule, at the official's discretion; the statute's mandatory process is commercial-only.

## 2026-10-27  Does federal law give a partial capital-gains exclusion when an owner sells inside the two-year window for a job change?

## Cross-chapter flags

- **Chapter 2, Oregon Code and Compliance Map, section 2.7 Code entry "A sale soon after completion requires a lien-protection notice", and the "Post-completion lien notice" row of the 2.7 Professional table.** The entry states that the seller serves the Homebuyer Protection Act notice "when a residential structure is sold within about 75 days of substantial completion". ORS 87.007 applies to the sale of any new single-family residence (and to a sale within three months of $50,000 or more of improvements), whenever it sells; the seller protects the buyer by one of five methods, one of which is closing after the 75-day lien-perfection deadline of ORS 87.035(1), and delivers a CCB form by closing day (87.007(3)). The 75-day period in ORS 87.093(2)(a) is the original contractor's notice duty to a first purchaser. The value line also hedges a number ("about 75 days"). Chapter 4, section 4.4, now carries the corrected entries. Evidence: ors087.html (87.007, 87.035, 87.093); research/new/ch04.md.
- **Chapter 2, section 2.7 Code entry "Federal tax sets its own occupancy clock".** The Source line reads "IRC section 121"; brief v2 and v3 require "26 U.S.C. 121", since IRC means the International Residential Code in this book. Chapter 4 cites 26 U.S.C. 121(a) and (b).
- **Chapter 2, section 2.7 Code entry "An owner may install their own septic system under their own permit".** The Source is OAR 340-071-0600, which as amended effective January 1, 2026 contains no property-owner exception (it requires an installer license "for any person to construct or install onsite systems").
