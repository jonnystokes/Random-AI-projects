## 2026-10-26  When does ORSC R327 (wildfire hazard mitigation) apply, and what does it require?
Finding: The Building Codes Division amendment headed "Effective Aug. 5, 2025 (Ref.: ORS 455.610)" states: "In accordance with Senate Bill 83 (2025) this amendment makes Section R327, Wildfire Hazard Mitigation, available for local adoption and applicable to new construction of new ORSC governed buildings. This code amendment was adopted by temporary rule effective Aug. 5, 2025 through Jan. 2, 2026 and subsequently adopted by permanent rule effective Jan. 1, 2026." R327.1: "Where adopted by the local municipality, the provisions of this section shall apply to new dwellings and their accessory structures required by a local municipality via local ordinance to be protected against wildfire." R327.1.1: a municipality may adopt the section "in whole ... via local ordinance" and the ordinance must identify the areas subject to the standards, include a transition plan for subdivisions under development, and provide a local appeal process. R327.1.2: the municipality notifies the division and provides the adopted map. R327.1.3 exceptions: "Nonhabitable detached accessory structures with a floor area of not greater than 400 square feet (37.2 m2) located not less than 50 feet (15 240 mm) from all other structures on the lot that contain habitable space"; "Structures exempted by ORS 455.315"; detached accessory membrane-covered frame structures. R327.3 Roofing: asphalt, slate, metal, tile, clay or concrete shingles under Chapter 9, "or other approved roofing that is deemed to be equivalent to a minimum Class B-rated roof assembly. Wood shingle and shake roofs are not permitted." R327.3.1: gutters and downspouts of noncombustible materials with a means to prevent debris accumulation. R327.3.2: every ventilation opening covered with noncombustible corrosion-resistant metal mesh "a minimum of 1/16-inch (1.6 mm) and a maximum of 1/8-inch (3.2 mm) in any dimension" or ember-resisting vents; no vents on the underside of eaves, soffits or cornices, except approved ember-resistant vents or openings 12 feet or more above grade. R327.3.3 Exterior walls: "1. Noncombustible material. 2. Ignition-resistant material. 3. Heavy timber assembly. 4. Log wall construction assembly. 5. Wall assemblies that have been tested ... ASTM E2707". R327.3.4: eaves, soffits, porch ceilings over 200 square feet, floor projections and underfloor areas less than 12 feet above grade protected by noncombustible or ignition-resistant material or the listed alternatives. R327.3.5: deck, porch and balcony walking surfaces more than 30 inches and less than 12 feet above grade from tested, ignition-resistant, exterior fire-retardant-treated or noncombustible material, with an exception for surfaces not greater than 200 square feet of nominal 2-inch lumber. R327.3.6: "Exterior windows, windows within exterior doors, and skylights shall be tempered glass, multilayered glazed panels, glass block or have a fire-resistance rating of not less than 20 minutes."
Source: revision6_code_library/23orsc-r327-amend.pdf (BCD, 2023 ORSC Amendments, Section R327), text extracted and read in full 2026-10-26.
Confidence: high for the text. The session-law chapter the Revision 7 text attached to SB 83 ("Oregon Laws 2025 chapter 590") was not verified and is not used.
Used in: 3.3 Professional and Code, 3.12 Applied.

## 2026-10-26  Did the 2026 ORSC Chapter 1 change the accessory-structure permit exemption (2023 R105.2, Building, item 1)?
Finding: Yes, in three ways, with the section number unchanged. The 2026 Chapter 1 (header: "Effective Oct. 1, 2026 (ref. ORS 455.610, OAR 918-480-0005) ... became effective and mandatory Oct. 1, 2026") R105.2, Building, item 1: "One-story detached accessory structures, not intended to be used for sleeping or containing permanent cooking provisions, provided that the building area, or floor area where a roof is not present, does not exceed 200 square feet (18.58 m2) and does not exceed a height of 15 feet (4572 mm) measured from grade plane to the average height of the highest roof surface, or where a roof is not present to the highest point of the structure." Its exceptions: "1. Where the structure is located on a parcel not less than 2 acres in area, the building area or floor area limit may be increased to 400 square feet (37.16 m2). 2. Detached platforms and decks that are located not more than 30 inches (762 mm) above adjacent grade measured to any point within 3 feet (914 mm) horizontally of the walking surface may be of any size." The official 2023 Chapter 1 reads: "1. Nonhabitable one-story detached accessory structures, provided that the building area does not exceed 200 square feet (18.58 m2) and does not exceed a height of 15 feet (4572 mm) measured from grade plane to the average height of the highest roof surface. Exception: Where the structure is located on a parcel of 2.0 acres or greater in area, and the structure is located a minimum of 20 feet (6096 mm) from all property lines and regulated structures, the building area may be increased to 400 square feet". So the 2026 edition replaces "nonhabitable" with the sleeping-and-cooking test, extends the exemption to roofless structures by floor area, and its 400-square-foot exception no longer carries the 20-foot setback condition. The 2026 document's change marking (red strikethrough, blue underline) shows the first two changes on page 7 and does not mark the missing 20-foot clause on page 8; the printed operative text has no such clause. Item 16 of the 2026 list (item 15 in 2023) exempts "Membrane-covered frame structures that are nonhabitable, not more than 500 square feet (46.45 m2) in floor area, one story in height and not closer than 3 feet (914 mm) to a lot line." The list closes: "Unless otherwise exempted, separate plumbing, electrical and mechanical permits may be required for the previously listed exempted items."
Source: oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Documents/26orsc-ch01.pdf (2026 ORSC Chapter 1, 17 pages), downloaded 2026-10-26, text extracted, pages 7 and 8 read as rendered images; revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf (2023 ORSC Chapter 1), text extracted 2026-10-26.
Confidence: high for both printed texts. Medium on whether the dropped 20-foot clause was intended, because the change marking omits it; the chapter states the 2026 printed text and carries the question as an open item.
Used in: 3.14 Professional; cross-chapter flag for the legal chapter (section 4.8) and the site-development chapter.

## 2026-10-26  Did the 2026 ORSC Chapter 1 keep the retaining-wall permit floor (2023 R101.2.2, item 2)?
Finding: Yes, same number and substance. 2026 R101.2.2 Optional local adoption to require a building permit, item 2: "Retaining walls. Statewide, retaining walls that provide safeguards for the users of the building, support a regulated building or retain material that, if not restrained, could impact a regulated building shall require a building permit. A municipality may adopt an ordinance to regulate other retaining walls, provided that the threshold established for requiring a permit does not include retaining walls 4 feet (1219 mm) or less in height, when measured from the bottom of the footing to the top of the wall, except where the retaining wall supports ascending slopes exceeding 3:1 or where the retaining wall supports a nonsoil surcharge. A local height threshold greater than 4 feet (1219 mm) is allowed." The 2023 text contains the same statewide first sentence and the same 4-foot floor with the same two exceptions. Item 4 of the same 2026 list exempts ground-mounted photovoltaic panel systems 10 feet or less in height with no public access beneath unless a municipality adopts an ordinance requiring a permit.
Source: 26orsc-ch01.pdf page 2 and 23orsc-ch1.pdf page 1 (as above), read 2026-10-26.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 3.13 Code.

## 2026-10-26  What decision deadline binds a county on a land-use application (the Revision 7 text cited only the city statute, ORS 227.178)?
Finding: ORS 215.427(1): "Except as provided in subsections (3), (5) and (10) of this section, the governing body of a county or its designee shall take final action on an application, including resolution of all appeals under ORS 215.422, within the shortest applicable period of the following periods, all of which begin on the date that the application is deemed complete: (a) 150 days; (b) 120 days, for land within an urban growth boundary or for applications for mineral aggregate extraction; (c) 100 days, for an application for the development of affordable housing as provided in ORS 197A.470; or (d) 63 days, for an expedited land division under ORS 197A.140." Subsection (2): for an incomplete application the county notifies the applicant in writing of exactly what is missing within 30 days of receipt. Subsection (3)(a)(A): a complete application is approved or denied on the standards and criteria applicable when it was first submitted.
Source: revision6_code_library/ors/ors215.html (Oregon Legislature official chapter HTML), read 2026-10-26.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 3.20 Code.

## 2026-10-26  Which edition of the Oregon Fire Code is in force (the Revision 7 access figures were quoted from a 2018-IFC-based district guide)?
Finding: The Office of State Fire Marshal permanent rule filing OSFM 6-2025, "FILING CAPTION: Adoption of the 2024 International Fire Code with Oregon amendments for 2025", "EFFECTIVE DATE: 10/01/2025", amends OAR 837-040-0010, 837-040-0015, 837-040-0020 and 837-040-0140. OAR 837-040-0010(2): "Effective October 1, 2025, the 2025 Oregon Fire Code which is the 2024 edition of the International Fire Code, as" amended. (3)(a): "For the purposes of implementing a phase-in period for the 2025 Oregon Fire Code in new construction plan review only, the 2022 Oregon Fire Code is adopted for a period of 180-days beginning October 1, 2025 and ending" March 31, 2026; (3)(b): during the phase-in "new construction plan reviews will be reviewed to either the 2025 Oregon Fire Code or the 2022 Oregon Fire Code as directed by the local building official." ## 2026-10-26  Where is SB 391 (2021), the rural accessory dwelling option, codified, and what does it require beyond the size, acreage and distance limits?
Finding: ORS 215.495 Accessory dwelling units. (2): "Consistent with a county's comprehensive plan, a county may allow an owner of a lot or parcel within an area zoned for rural residential use to construct one accessory dwelling unit on the lot or parcel, provided: (a) The lot or parcel is not located within an area designated as an urban reserve as defined in ORS 197A.230; (b) The lot or parcel is at least two acres in size; (c) One single-unit dwelling is sited on the lot or parcel; (d) The existing single-unit dwelling property on the lot or parcel is not subject to an order declaring it a nuisance or subject to any pending action under ORS 105.550 to 105.600; (e) The accessory dwelling unit will comply with all applicable laws and regulations relating to sanitation and wastewater disposal and treatment; (f) The accessory dwelling unit will not include more than 900 square feet of usable floor area; (g) The accessory dwelling unit will be located no farther than 100 feet from the existing single-unit dwelling; (h) If the water supply source for the accessory dwelling unit or associated lands or gardens will be a well using water under ORS 537.545 (1)(b) or (d), no portion of the lot or parcel is within an area in which new or existing ground water uses under ORS 537.545 (1)(b) or (d) have been restricted by the Water Resources Commission; (i) No portion of the lot or parcel is within a designated area of critical state concern; (j) The lot or parcel is served by a fire protection service provider with professionals who have received training or certification described in ORS 181A.410; and (k) The county has adopted land use regulations that ensure that: (A) The accessory dwelling unit has adequate setbacks from adjacent lands zoned for resource use; and (B) The accessory dwelling unit has adequate access for firefighting equipment, safe evacuation and staged evacuation areas. (3) A county may not allow an accessory dwelling unit allowed under this section to be used for vacation occupancy".

## 2026-10-26  Is "ORS 105.700 to 105.795" the recreational-immunity statute the Revision 7 Code entry cited?
Finding: No. ORS 105.672 "Definitions for ORS 105.672 to 105.696" opens the recreational-use provisions; ORS 105.682 "Liabilities of owner of land used by public for recreational purposes, gardening, woodcutting or harvest of special forest products" states: "(1) Except as provided by subsection (2) of this section, and subject to the provisions of ORS 105.688, an owner of land is not liable in contract or tort for any personal injury, death or property damage that arises out of the use of the land for recreational purposes, gardening, woodcutting or the harvest of special forest products when the owner of land" permits the use. ORS 105.700 is "Prohibiting public access to private land; notice requirements; damages": "(1) In addition to and not in lieu of any other damages that may be claimed, a plaintiff who is a landowner shall receive liquidated damages in an amount not to exceed $1,000 in any action in which the plaintiff establishes that: (a) The plaintiff closed the land of the plaintiff as provided in subsection (2) of this section; and (b) The defendant entered and remained upon the land of the plaintiff" without permission. No section 105.795 exists in the chapter file.
Source: revision6_code_library/ors/ors105.html (Oregon Legislature official chapter HTML), read 2026-10-26.
Confidence: high for the section numbers and quoted text; the exceptions in 105.682(2) and 105.688 were not read in full.

## 2026-10-26  What does the ORSC footings-on-slopes section say, and what is its Oregon number (the Revision 7 entry cited "ORSC R403.1.7" with a 45-degree plane)?
Finding: The UpCodes Oregon residential viewer shows the section with a double heading, "R403.1.7 R403.1.9", and its text cross-references the Oregon numbers: "The placement of buildings and structures on or adjacent to slopes steeper than 1 unit vertical in 3 units horizontal (33.3-percent slope) shall conform to Sections R403.1.9.1 through R403.1.9.4." R403.1.9.1 (ascending slopes): "In general, buildings below slopes shall be set a sufficient distance from the slope to provide protection from slope drainage, erosion and shallow failures. ... Where the existing slope is steeper than 1 unit vertical in 1 unit horizontal (100-percent slope), the toe of the slope shall be assumed to be at the intersection of a horizontal plane drawn from the top of the foundation and a plane drawn tangent to the slope at an angle of 45 degrees (0.79 rad) to the horizontal. Where a retaining wall is constructed at the toe of the slope, the height of the slope shall be measured from the top of the wall to the top of the slope." R403.1.9.2 (descending slopes): "Footings on or adjacent to slope surfaces shall be founded in material with an embedment and setback from the slope surface sufficient to provide vertical and lateral support for the footing without detrimental settlement. Except as provided for in Section R403.1.9.4 and Figure R403.1.9.1, the following setback is deemed adequate to meet the criteria. Where the slope is steeper than 1 unit vertical in 1 unit horizontal (100-percent slope), the required setback shall be measured from an imaginary plane 45 degrees (0.79 rad) to the horizontal, projected upward from the toe of the slope." R403.1.9.4: "Alternate setbacks and clearances are permitted, subject to the approval of the building official. The building official is permitted to require an investigation and approved documentation demonstrating that the intent of this section has been satisfied. Such an investigation shall include consideration of material, height of slope, slope gradient, load intensity and erosion characteristics of slope material." The setback distances themselves (the IRC figure's H/3 and 40-foot and H/2 and 15-foot values) are in Figure R403.1.9.1, which the fetch could not read as text. The 2023 ORSC stacked-amendments PDF in the code library contains no R403.1 text.
Source: up.codes/viewer/oregon/irc-2021/chapter/4/foundations (retrieved 2026-10-26, first 100,000 characters); revision6_code_library/23orsc-stacked-amend.pdf (searched 2026-10-26).
Confidence: high for the quoted text; medium for the Oregon section number (inferred from the viewer's double heading and the text's own cross-references, not from a BCD document).
Used in: 3.13 Code; Open items (figure values).
