## 2026-10-26  Which edition of the Oregon Residential Specialty Code is in force at permit application?
Finding: OAR 918-480-0005, as amended by BCD 14-2026 (filed 09/30/2026, effective 10/01/2026), states: "Effective October 1, 2026, the 2026 Oregon Residential Specialty Code consists of the following:" "(a) The 2024 edition of the International Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings" as amended by the division; "Chapter 1 of the 2026 Oregon Residential Specialty Code is effective October 1, 2026 with no phase-in period"; "The 2023 Oregon Residential Specialty Code, with the exception of Chapter 1, is adopted for a period of six months beginning October 1, 2026 and ending March 31, 2027"; "all building departments in the state are required to accept plans for low-rise residential dwellings designed to either the 2026 Oregon Residential Specialty Code or to the 2023 Oregon Residential Specialty Code"; "Code requirements in effect at the time a plan review or permit application is filed controls the construction under the application unless the applicant agrees to be controlled by subsequent changes." BCD's residential adoption page: Chapter 1 "Effective and mandatory Oct. 1, 2026"; Chapters 2-44 and appendices "Effective Oct. 1, 2026 with six-month phase-in period", "Mandatory April 1, 2027"; "During the phase-in period, except for Chapter 1, use of the 2023 ORSC or the 2026 ORSC is permitted"; "ORSC Section R109.1.7 applies in conjunction with the use and application of Chapter 11, so will not be mandatory until Chapter 11 is mandatory, April 1, 2027"; the integrated 2026 codebook "is currently being published with ICC" and is "anticipated to be available for purchase before the end of this year". Chapter 1 of the 2026 ORSC is published as 26orsc-ch01.pdf.
Source: secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/view.action?ruleNumber=918-480-0005 (retrieved 2026-10-26); oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Pages/orsc-adoption.aspx (retrieved 2026-10-26); oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Pages/adopted-codes.aspx (retrieved 2026-10-26); oregon.gov/bcd/laws-rules/Documents/20261001-2026orsc-adoption-pr.pdf (permanent rule notice, found by search, not read).
Confidence: high (rule text and the agency's own page agree).

## 2026-10-26  What does Statewide Alternate Method 26-01 say?
Finding: "STATEWIDE ALTERNATE METHOD NO. 26-01, 2023 ORSC: continued reference to the 2022 OSSC for nonprescriptive design", Code reference "2023 Oregon Residential Specialty Code (ORSC); 2022 Oregon Structural Specialty Code (OSSC)", "Issued: Jan. 20, 2026". Overview: "Using the structural design provisions from the Oregon Structural Specialty Code (OSSC) for nonprescriptive designs of portions of structures governed by the Oregon Residential Specialty Code (ORSC) is a well-established practice. A substantive change occurred between the 2022 OSSC and 2025 OSSC regarding snow load design." Discussion: "Chapter 2 of the 2023 ORSC defines 'Building Code' as the OSSC adopted by Oregon Administrative Rule (OAR) 918-460-0010"; "the 2023 ORSC will reference the 2022 OSSC until March 31, 2026, then reference the 2025 OSSC through March 31, 2027"; "The 2025 OSSC is based on the 2024 International Building Code, which references the American Society of Engineer's (ASCE) 7-22 ... for design snow load provisions. ASCE 7-22 introduces different mapping techniques and design methodologies, resulting in increased design snow loads in some areas of the state and decreased in others." Conclusion: "For application of the 2023 ORSC, customers may choose to continue referencing the 2022 OSSC for nonprescriptive design until the 2026 ORSC is adopted and becomes mandatory statewide under OAR 918-480-0005." Boilerplate: "Statewide Alternate Methods are approved by the division administrator in consultation with the appropriate advisory board"; "Building officials shall approve the use of any material, design or method of construction addressed in a statewide alternate method"; "The decision to use a statewide alternate method is at the discretion of the applicant"; "Statewide alternate methods do not limit the authority of the building official to consider other proposed alternate methods encompassing the same subject matter."
Source: revision6_code_library/sam-26-01-23orscref22ossc.pdf (BCD document, linked from the adopted-codes page as sam-26-01-23orscref22ossc.pdf), read in full 2026-10-26.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 2.4 Professional, 2.5 Professional and Code, Who decides what (the engineer picks the OSSC edition the nonprescriptive design references). Routed: the snow-load design-basis consequence belongs to the Structural System and Concrete Design chapter.

## 2026-10-26  Does the 2023 ORSC adopt the model code's stop-work-order section (R114)?
Finding: The official Chapter 1 PDF, page 14: "SECTION R114 STOP WORK ORDER. Not adopted by the State of Oregon, Building Codes Division, as part of this code. A municipality may have authority outside of this code to regulate these matters locally, where not preempted." The same wording appears under SECTION R111 SERVICE UTILITIES. R104.5, R104.6 and R112.4 are each "not adopted by the State of Oregon, Building Codes Division, as part of this code." R110.5 ends: "Nothing in this code limits a local municipality's ability to require application of its own ordinances or to enforce its own ordinances." R112.1: "the municipality shall establish an appeals procedure. ORS 455.475 provides an alternative appeal process to that set forth by the local municipality."
Source: revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf (BCD, 2023 ORSC Chapter 1), text extracted and read 2026-10-26.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 2.6 Professional and Code. Flag for the orchestrator: the Inspection and Approval Sequence chapter (Revision 6 carryover text, section 31.10 and its Code block) cites "ORSC R114 (2023 edition)" as the stop-work authority. That citation is wrong for Oregon; the authority is municipal ordinance preserved by ORS 455.020(4).

## 2026-10-26  Does ORS 455.020(4) preserve municipal stop-work authority?
Finding: ORS 455.020(4): "This chapter and any specialty code does not limit the authority of a municipality to enact regulations providing for local administration of the state building code; local appeal boards; fees and other charges; abatement of nuisances and dangerous buildings; enforcement through penalties, stop-work orders or other means; or minimum health, sanitation and safety standards for governing the use of structures for housing, except where the power of municipalities to enact any such regulations is expressly withheld or otherwise provided for by statute."
Source: revision6_code_library/ors/ors455.html (lines 2332-2345), read 2026-10-26.
Confidence: high.

## 2026-10-26  What does ORS 455.060(1) say about state rulings on materials and methods?
Finding: "(1) Any person who desires to use or furnish any material, design or method of construction or installation in the state, or any building official, may request the Director of the Department of Consumer and Business Services to issue a ruling with respect to the acceptability of any material, design or method of construction about which there is a question under any provision of the state building code. Requests shall be in writing and, if made by anyone other than a building official, shall be made and the ruling issued prior to the use or attempted use of such questioned material, design or method." ## 2026-10-26  Official text of R104.4, R104.11 and R104.11.1 (2023 ORSC Chapter 1)
Finding: R104.4 matches the answer file's UpCodes reading word for word. R104.11: "The provisions of this code are not intended to prevent the installation of any material or to prohibit any design or method of construction not specifically prescribed by this code, provided that any such alternative has been approved. An alternative material, design or method of construction shall be approved where the building official finds that the proposed alternative meets all of the following: 1. ... is satisfactory and complies with the intent of the provisions of this code. 2. Where the alternative material, design or method of construction is not approved, the building official shall respond in writing, stating the reasons why the alternative was not approved." Test methods shall be as specified in this code or by other recognized test standards. In the absence of recognized and accepted test methods, the building official shall approve the testing procedures. Tests shall be performed by an approved agency. Reports of such tests shall be retained by the building official for the period required for retention of public records." R104.9: "Materials, equipment and devices approved by the building official shall be constructed and installed in accordance with such approval."

## 2026-10-26  What does the DEQ onsite rule require for operation-and-maintenance contracts?

## 2026-10-26  Does the 2026 ORSC Chapter 1 keep the 2023 numbering and text for alternative materials (R104.11) and the stop-work section (R114)?
Finding: No for R104.11, yes for R114. The 2026 Chapter 1 ("became effective and mandatory Oct. 1, 2026"; purple text marks new 2024 IRC language, blue Oregon additions) moves the alternative-materials provision to R104.2.2 under R104.2 Determination of compliance. R104.2.2: "The provisions of this code are not intended to prevent the installation of any material or to prohibit any design or method of construction not specifically prescribed by this code, provided that any such alternative has been approved." R104.2.2.1 Approval authority: approved "where the building official finds that the proposed alternative is satisfactory and complies with Sections R104.2.2 through R104.2.2.6.2, as applicable." R104.2.2.2 Application and disposition: "Where required, a request to use an alternative material, design or method of construction shall be submitted in writing to the building official for approval. Where the alternative material, design or method of construction is not approved, the building official shall respond in writing, stating the reasons the alternative was not approved." R104.2.2.3 Compliance with code intent. R104.2.2.4 Equivalency criteria: "not less than the equivalent of that prescribed in this code with respect to all the following, as applicable: 1. Quality. 2. Strength. 3. Effectiveness. 4. Durability. 5. Safety, other than fire safety. 6. Fire safety." R104.2.2.5 Tests: "Tests conducted to demonstrate equivalency ... shall be of a scale that is sufficient to predict performance of the end use configuration. Such tests shall be performed by a party acceptable to the building official." R104.2.2.6.1 Evaluation reports: "Evaluation reports shall be issued by an approved agency and use of the evaluation report shall require approval by the building official for the installation. The alternate material, design or method of construction and product evaluated shall be within the scope of the building official's recognition of the approved agency. Criteria used for the evaluation shall be identified within the report and, where required, provided to the building official." R104.2.2.6.2 Other reports: "shall describe criteria, including but not limited to any referenced testing or analysis, used to determine compliance with code intent and justify code equivalence. The report shall be prepared by a qualified specialist, laboratory or specialty organization acceptable to the building official." R104.2 item 2 (Oregon language): interpretations, policies and procedures "Shall not have the effect of waiving safeguard requirements specifically provided for in this code, statewide alternate methods or statewide code interpretations." R104.2.1 Listed compliance: "Installation shall be in accordance with the listing and the manufacturer's instructions, and where required to verify compliance, the listing standard and manufacturer's instructions shall be made available to the building official." R106.1.2 (manufacturer's instructions on the job site at inspection), R109.1 (work remains accessible and exposed until approved), R109.1.5.3 (reinforced masonry, ICF and conventionally formed concrete walls in SDC D0, D1, D2 and E inspected after embedded systems and reinforcing are in place and before grout or concrete; size, location, spacing and lapping verified; grout cleanouts and grout space for masonry), R110.1, R112.1 and R114 ("Not adopted by the State of Oregon") keep their 2023 numbering and substance. R109.1.7 (durable energy compliance summary posted near the furnace or service panel) is new and not mandatory until April 1, 2027.
Source: oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Documents/26orsc-ch01.pdf (2026 ORSC Chapter 1, 17 pages), retrieved and read 2026-10-26.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 2.3 Code, 2.4 Professional and Code, 2.6 Code, 2.8 Code. Flag for the orchestrator: every chapter that cites ORSC R104.11 or R104.4 needs the 2026 number beside it (R104.2.2 and its subsections); ch02 answer-file items 1 and 2 are rechecked against the 2026 text by this entry (the edition trigger they named).
