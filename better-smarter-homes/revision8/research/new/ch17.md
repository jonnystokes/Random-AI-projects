## 2026-10-27  Does Oregon exempt pressure regulators of 1 1/2 inches and larger from the strainer?
Finding: No. The 2026 adoption matrix, section 608.2, lists the existing Oregon amendment "Deletes AWWA C530 and Deletes that pressure regulators equal to or exceeding 1 1/2 inches shall not require a strainer", outcome "Retained OR A". The change summary (page 38) prints 608.2 with "or AWWA C530. Pressure regulator(s) equal to or exceeding 1 1/2 inches (40 mm) shall not require a strainer." in red strikethrough (existing Oregon deleted language). The Oregon text therefore reads: "an approved-type pressure regulator preceded by an adequate strainer shall be installed and the static pressure reduced to 80 psi (552 kPa) or less. Pressure regulators for potable water distribution systems shall comply with ASSE 1003." The Revision 7 text and research/wave1 (Chapter 9 item 1) state the reverse (that Oregon created the exemption).
Source: oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Documents/26opsc-matrix.pdf page 3; oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Documents/26opsc-change-summary.pdf page 38 (rendered and read 2026-10-27).
Confidence: high (both BCD documents agree; the strikethrough was read on the rendered page).
Used in: 17.9 Professional and Code; Dropped list (the old statement).

## 2026-10-27  What do OPSC 608.2 and 608.3 require of expansion tanks in the 2026 edition?
Finding: 608.2 (Oregon text): "Each such regulator and strainer shall be accessibly located aboveground or in a vault equipped with adequate means to provide drainage and shall be protected from freezing, and shall have the strainer readily accessible for cleaning without removing the regulator or strainer body or disconnecting the supply piping. Pipe size determinations shall be based on 80 percent of the reduced pressure where using Table 610.4. An approved expansion tank shall be installed in the cold water distribution piping downstream of each such regulator to prevent pressure exceeding 80 psi from developing due to thermal expansion. Expansion tanks used in potable water systems intended to supply drinking water shall comply with NSF/ANSI/CAN 61. The expansion tank shall be properly sized, securely fastened to the structure, and installed in accordance with the manufacturer's installation instructions and listing. Systems designed by a certified plumbing contractor or registered design professionals shall be permitted to use approved pressure relief valves in lieu of expansion tanks provided such relief valves have a maximum pressure relief setting of 100 psi (689 kPa) or less." ("certified" is new 2026 Oregon language replacing "licensed".) 608.3: "A water system provided with a check valve, backflow preventer, or other normally closed device that prevents dissipation of building pressure back into the water main, independent of the type of water heater used, shall be provided with an approved, listed, and adequately sized expansion tank or other approved device having a similar function to control thermal expansion. Prepressurized water expansion tanks shall comply with IAPMO/ANSI Z1088. Such expansion tank or other approved device shall be installed on the building side of the check valve, backflow preventer, or other device and shall be sized, securely fastened to the structure, and installed in accordance with the manufacturer's installation instructions." Storage water heating equipment takes a listed combination temperature and pressure-relief valve. "Exception: An expansion tank shall not be required for an instantaneous on-demand water heater." The commentary: piping alone may not support the tank unless the building official approves. Neither section contains a prohibition on a valve between the expansion tank and the system; the Revision 7 text stated one as an Oregon rule.
Source: 26opsc-change-summary.pdf pages 38 (rendered); 26opsc-matrix.pdf pages 3 and 4.
Confidence: high for the text; medium that the valve prohibition never existed (it is absent from the 2026 text with all existing Oregon amendments marked).
Used in: 17.9 Applied, Professional, Code; Dropped list.

## 2026-10-27  How often must a backflow assembly be tested under the OPSC in Oregon?
Finding: At installation only, under the plumbing code. 603.2 keeps "Such devices or assemblies shall be tested at the time of installation" and strikes (existing Oregon deletion) the model code's "repair, or relocation and not less than on an annual schedule thereafter" and the certified-tester paragraph. 603.4.2 (2026): "The premise owner or responsible person shall have the backflow prevention assembly tested at the time of installation by a backflow assembly tester certified by the Oregon Health Authority"; the model annual schedule, the Series 5000 procedures and the ASSE 1064 test kit sentence are struck. Annual testing on a public water connection comes from OAR 333-061-0070(6), which applies to public water systems (OAR 333-061-0010), per research/wave2/answers/item_10.md. A private well serving one household carries no annual-test duty under either.
Source: 26opsc-change-summary.pdf page 36 (rendered); 26opsc-matrix.pdf page 3; research/wave2/answers/item_10.md.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 17.18 Applied, Professional, Code; Who decides what; Open items (whether a county requires annual tests on private systems).

## 2026-10-27  Where may a backflow preventer not be installed?

Exception: A double check valve assembly (DC) shall be permitted to be installed below grade." (The exception is new 2026 Oregon language; the Oregon amendment that had deleted the first sentence is rescinded.)

## 2026-10-27  Irrigation devices, nonpotable labeling and yard hydrants in the 2026 OPSC
Finding: 603.5.6: potable supplies to irrigation systems with no pumps or chemical injection are protected by an AVB, PVB, SVB, RP, DC (Oregon addition) or a valve complying with IAPMO PS 72. 603.5.6.2: where a backflow device is downstream of a potable supply pump, the device is an AVB, PVB, SVB, RP or DC. 603.5.11: a nonpotable water line's exposed portions are identified "in accordance with this code", and each outlet on it is posted "CAUTION: NONPOTABLE WATER, DO NOT DRINK." 603.5.17: "Potable water outlets, freeze-proof yard hydrants, combination stop-and-waste valves, or other fixtures that incorporate a stop and waste feature that drains into the ground shall not be installed underground. Exception: External vertical yard hydrants shall be of the self-draining sanitary type or be provided with a dual check valve backflow preventer in accordance with ASSE 1024."

## 2026-10-27  What does the 2026 OPSC say about dead legs?
Finding: 309.5 (2026): "Where a dead leg exceeds 1.5 times the diameter of the pipe in length, an accessible method of flushing shall be provided." The struck 2023 wording read "Dead legs shall have an accessible method of flushing and shall not exceed 1.5 times the diameter of the pipe." BCD commentary: "A dead leg is a section of pipe at least 1.5 times its diameter in length, that has no flow or does not circulate. A segment of pipe less than 1.5 times its diameter is not a dead leg, and no method of flushing is required. Where a dead leg exists, an accessible method of flushing is required. This can be accomplished in many ways such as by extending that pipe to a fixture or other regularly used outlet such as a hose bibb, or by providing access to openable valves, angle stops, hammer arrestors, or even expansion tanks." Statewide Code Interpretation 22-01 is retained.
Source: 26opsc-change-summary.pdf (309.5 commentary and text); 26opsc-matrix.pdf page 1.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 17.17 Professional and Code.

## 2026-10-27  Water heater rules in the 2026 OPSC (Chapter 5 and 608.5)
Finding: 501.1: storage water heater minimum capacity follows the first-hour rating in Table 501.1(2); final installation approved by the building official. Table 501.1(1) new note: "Dual purpose water heaters shall be installed in accordance with this code, the Boiler Code and the manufacturer's installation instructions"; new definition "Water Heater, Dual Purpose. An appliance intended to be a heat source for both space heating and domestic hot water applications." 503.2: "No appliance or part thereof shall be covered or concealed until the same has been inspected and approved." 504.6: no shutoff valve between the relief valve and the water heater or on its discharge. 507.2: "Storage tank water heaters shall be anchored or strapped to resist horizontal displacement due to earthquake motion. Strapping shall be at points within the upper one-third and lower one-third of its vertical dimensions. At the lower point, a distance of not less than 4 inches (102 mm) shall be maintained from the controls with the strapping. Exception: Water heaters in one- and two-family dwellings in Seismic Design Category B and C and townhouses in Seismic Design Category B are not required to be anchored or strapped." 507.4 drainage pan: where the heater is in an attic, in or on an attic ceiling, floor-ceiling or floor-subfloor assembly, or where damage results from a leak, a watertight corrosion-resistant pan not less than 1 1/2 inches deep with a drain not less than 3/4 inch to an approved location, the terminating end readily visible, pan piping rated for the heater's temperature, no relief-valve discharge into the pan; exceptions for replacements and instantaneous heaters. 608.5 relief-valve discharge: no valves or obstructions; full size; rated material; "discharge independently by gravity through an air gap into the drainage system, onto an on-grade concrete garage floor, or outside of the building with the end of the pipe not exceeding 2 feet (610 mm) and not less than 6 inches (152 mm) above the ground and pointing downwards"; not trapped or subject to freezing; terminal end not threaded; no discharge into a water heater pan; termination readily observable. 604.13: flexible connectors comply with ASME A112.18.6/CSA B125.6; copper, copper alloy or stainless flexible connectors not over 24 inches; no PEX, PEX-AL-PEX, PE-AL-PE or PE-RT within the first 18 inches of piping connected to a water heater; exception for CPVC, PE-RT, PEX and PP at an instantaneous on-demand tankless heater (Oregon exception).
Source: 26opsc-change-summary.pdf pages 30 to 35, 37, 38 and 39.
Confidence: high for text; the Table 501.1(2) values were not reprinted.
Used in: 17.12 Code; 17.14 Applied, Professional, Code; 17.16 Code; 17.21 Code; Open items (Table 501.1(2) values).

## 2026-10-27  Disinfection, water hammer, pipe insulation and pressure loss in the 2026 OPSC
Finding: 609.10: "New or repaired potable water systems shall be disinfected prior to use where required. The method shall comply with the following: (1) The pipe system shall be flushed with clean, potable water until potable water appears at the points of the outlet. (2) The system or parts thereof shall be filled with a water-chlorine solution containing not less than 50 parts per million of chlorine, and the system or part thereof shall be valved-off and allowed to stand for 24 hours; or, the system or part thereof shall be filled with a water-chlorine solution containing not less than 200 parts per million of chlorine and allowed to stand for 3 hours. (3) Following the allowed standing time, the system shall be flushed with clean, potable water until the chlorine residual in the water coming from the system does not exceed the chlorine residual in the flushing water. (4) The procedure shall be repeated where it is shown by a bacteriological examination made by an approved agency that contamination persists in the system." 609.11: arresters complying with ASSE 1010 or PDI-WH 201 as close as possible to quick-acting valves; "Exception: This section shall not apply to one- and two-family dwellings or townhouses." 609.12: "Insulation of domestic water piping shall be in accordance with the Building Code." 610.2: pressure loss through a water filter, softener, backflow preventer, tankless heater or similar device is included in the system calculations and the pipe and meter sized for it; no such device may produce an excessive pressure drop; "In the absence of specific pressure drop information, the diameter of the inlet or outlet of such device or its connecting piping shall be not less than the diameter of such water distribution piping to the fixtures served by the device." Table 610.3 (reprinted rows): water closet, 1.28 gpf gravity tank, 1/2-inch minimum branch, 2.5 WSFU private. 610.13(6): irrigation piping outside a building, separated by an air gap or backflow device, is not regulated, but the potable piping supplying it is sized for the full connected domestic and irrigation demand. 408.3 is now "Water Consumption": showerheads and body sprays at not more than 1.8 gpm at 80 psi (Oregon). 408.4.3: temperature-actuated flow-reduction devices comply with ASSE 1062 "and shall not be used alone as a substitute for the balanced pressure, thermostatic or combination shower valves requirements or as a substitute for bathtub or whirlpool tub water temperature-limiting valves requirements." 312.6 and 313.1 read as in 2023 (freeze protection; seismic restraint per the Building Code except 507.2).
Source: 26opsc-change-summary.pdf pages 23, 27, 40 and 41 (page 40 rendered).
Confidence: high.
Used in: 17.8 Code; 17.7 Code; 17.11 Professional and Code; 17.13 Code; 17.15 Professional and Code; 17.17 Professional; 17.20 Code; 17.5 Code.

## 2026-10-27  What changed in Chapter 16 (nonpotable rainwater) and the appendices?
Finding: 1601.2 (2026): "Rainwater catchment systems shall be designed in accordance with this chapter by a licensed plumbing contractor, certified rainwater system designer or other registered design professional. Components, piping, and fittings used in a rainwater catchment system shall be listed. Exceptions: (1) A person registered or licensed to perform plumbing design work is not required to design rainwater catchment systems used for irrigation with a maximum storage capacity of 360 gallons (1363 L). (2) ... is not required to design rainwater catchment systems for single family dwellings where outlets, piping, and system components are located on the exterior of the building." BCD commentary: "The existing Oregon amendments in Section 1601.2 deleting the exceptions are rescinded." The matrix also rescinds the Oregon amendment deleting the 1601.3 permit exceptions (the restored text was not reprinted). 1601.5: no treatment for aboveground irrigation at 360 gallons or less, or for subsurface or drip irrigation. 1601.9.2: an abandoned underground tank is drained and filled with earth, sand, gravel, concrete or other approved material. 1602.1: uses approved by the building official; rooftop systems "shall comply with ARCSA/ASPE/ANSI 63." 1602.2: no permit until complete plumbing plans are approved by the building official. 1603.4: water quality per Table 1603.4, IAPMO IGC 324 or NSF/ANSI 350. 1603.15: treatment devices listed or labeled. 1605.3 (heading's "Annual" struck): "A test in accordance with Section 1602.5 shall be performed on both the potable and rainwater catchment water systems. The potable and rainwater catchment water systems shall be isolated from each other and independently inspected and tested to ensure there is no cross-connection"; UPC 1605.3.4 Annual Inspection not adopted. 102.7.1 lists the adopted appendices: A, B, C, D, E, K (Potable Rainwater Catchment Systems) and M (Peak Water Demand Calculator). 102.7.2.1: Appendix L and Appendix N ("Impact of Water Temperature on the Potential for Scalding and Legionella Growth") may be adopted by local ordinance and are not adopted by the state. Appendix K (2026): applies to potable rainwater catchment in single-family dwellings; designed by a registered design professional or a person the authority deems competent; water quality as the authority determines, otherwise EPA/600/R-12/618; "It is the sole responsibility of the owner of the system to maintain potable water in a safe and responsible manner, therefore frequent testing is advised." Chapter 2 defines Potable Water as water "that meets the requirements of the Oregon Health Authority and the Building Official." The standards list on page 82 includes IAPMO PS 59 and IAPMO IGC 352 among standards "not adopted".
Source: 26opsc-change-summary.pdf pages 4, 19, 80, 81, 82, 93 (80 and 81 rendered); 26opsc-matrix.pdf pages 6 and 7.
Confidence: high for the reprinted text; the 1601.3 permit exceptions and the status of the diverter-valve standards are open.
Used in: 17.11 Code; 17.15 Professional; 17.19 Professional and Code; Open items.

## 2026-10-27  Does the 2026 ORSC require a heat pump water heater?
Finding: The 2026 ORSC heat-pump provision is N1105.8, "Heat pump for split-system air-conditioning. In new dwellings where split-system air-conditioning is installed, the outdoor condensing unit and indoor evaporator coil shall have heat pump operation that provides both heating and cooling. Heat pump operation for heating shall be first stage heating." BCD: it "Does not dictate the heating system fuel for the home" and has alternate pathways (N1102.3 additional measures, N1109 ERI, N1110 simulated performance). It says nothing about water heaters.
Source: oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Documents/26orsc-heatpumpprovision-summary.pdf (BCD technical summary, read 2026-10-27).
Confidence: high.
Used in: 17.14 Professional. Cross-chapter flag for the HVAC chapter.

## 2026-10-27  What is OAR 330-070-0073?
Finding: Title "Energy-Efficient Appliances", OAR chapter 330 (Oregon Department of Energy), division 70. Heat pump water heaters "must meet the 'Northern Climate' specifications" of the Northwest Energy Efficiency Alliance: Tier 1 with configuration options for semi-conditioned or unconditioned spaces, "rated at a minimum 1.8 Energy Factor"; Tier 2 or greater for semi-conditioned, unconditioned and conditioned spaces, "rated at a minimum 2.0 Energy Factor". The page does not say whether the rule is current; it sets appliance eligibility, not a construction requirement.
Source: oregon.public.law/rules/oar_330-070-0073 (retrieved 2026-10-27; source snapshot May 26, 2025).
Confidence: medium (eligibility purpose inferred from the title and division).

## 2026-10-27  What does ORS 448.271 require at a sale?
Finding: In a sale or exchange of real estate that includes a well supplying groundwater for domestic purposes, the seller, upon accepting an offer, has the well tested for arsenic, nitrates and total coliform bacteria; the Oregon Health Authority may add tests by rule for areas of public-health concern; the seller submits the results to the Authority and the buyer within 90 days of receiving them; failure does not invalidate the conveyance.
Source: oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors448.html (retrieved 2026-10-27).
Confidence: high.
Used in: 17.3 Code. The Revision 7 citation "OAR 333-061-0307" was not checked; Open items.

## 2026-10-27  What does Oregon's well-construction rule say about pitless adapters?
Finding: OAR 690-210-0270, "Pitless Well Adapters and Units": the casing seal requirements apply; "The seal shall cover that interval occupied by the pitless case from the point of casing connection to land surface"; "A cement grout seal shall not be allowed within the pitless unit or pitless adaptor sealing interval"; the interval is sealed with unhydrated bentonite (OAR 690-210-0330 and -0340); the adapter or unit, cap, case and attachments are watertight; "Pitless units shall be vented to the atmosphere." No burial depth or frost requirement.
Source: oregon.public.law/rules/oar_690-210-0270 (retrieved 2026-10-27).
Confidence: high.
Used in: 17.20 Professional and Code.

## 2026-10-27  What doses and times does OHA give for shock chlorination?
Finding: OHA's water-system operations page: "the desired chlorination dose is either 10 or 50 mg/L (unless the 'Method' is spray application)"; "There are three exposure time-frames that can be used: 3-hour, 6-hour, and 24-hour"; flush until "the chlorine residual is <4 mg/L"; take special coliform samples and repeat if coliform is found; tank volume by length times width times height times 7.48 (rectangular) or radius squared times pi times height times 7.48 (circular); an xls calculator. The page does not pair each dose with each time.
Source: oregon.gov/oha/ph/healthyenvironments/drinkingwater/operations/pages/shockchlorination.aspx (retrieved 2026-10-27).
Confidence: high for the quoted values; the pairing is open.
Used in: 17.8 Professional and the disinfection table; Open items.

## 2026-10-27  Plastic drainage air testing in 2023 and 2026
Finding: The 2026 matrix rescinds the existing Oregon amendments to 712.1 ("Deletes 'except that plastic pipe shall not be tested with air'") and 723.1 ("Deletes 'Plastic DWV piping systems shall not be tested by the air test method'"). ## Cross-chapter flags

- **2026-10-27. Water Independence chapter (skeleton), sections 18.4 and 18.6, and Resilience and Disaster Independence (skeleton).** Nine answered items in research/answers/ch17_plumbing-supply.md belong to those chapters and are not integrated in chapter 17, which carries one sentence naming each chapter by topic: items 15 (autonomy-day cistern sizing and the cost curve), 21 (roof harvest volume), 39 (shortfall buffer against the autonomy tank), 20 (cistern material, the owner-poured cistern) and 40 and 41 (hydrofracturing a marginal well, Oregon basalt) go to Water Independence; items 14 (NFPA 1142 fire-water reserve, delivery options), 19 (fire and domestic takeoffs at different heights in one tank; no statewide residential minimum) and 16 (gravity filters for an extended emergency) go to Resilience. Chapter 23, HVAC, Ventilation, Humidity, and Air Quality (Revision 6 carryover).** The 2026 ORSC heat-pump rule is N1105.8 (first-stage heat-pump heating wherever split-system air conditioning is installed in a new dwelling, with three alternate pathways); it belongs in the HVAC chapter, and the Revision 7 water-supply text had presented it as a water-heating direction. Chapter 23 gives heat-pump water heater noise as 45 to 50 dB; chapter 17 uses the answer file's 45 to 55 dB in heat-pump mode. Evidence: 26orsc-heatpumpprovision-summary.pdf; research/answers/ch17_plumbing-supply.md item 9.
- **2026-10-27. Every chapter citing OPSC sections (19, 25, 31, 32, 35).** The 2026 OPSC renumbered within Chapter 4 (408.3 is now "Water Consumption"; the temperature-actuated flow-reduction devices are 408.4.3), Chapter 5 (water heaters on roofs and in attics: UPC 508.3 and 508.4 become 508.1 and 508.2) and Chapter 16 (1601.7 becomes 1601.5, 1601.11 becomes 1601.9).
