## 2026-10-27  What are Oregon's stair, landing and hallway values in the 2023 ORSC?
Finding: R311.7.5.1: "The riser height shall be not more than 8 inches (203 mm)... The greatest riser height within any flight of stairs shall not exceed the smallest by more than 3/8 inch." No minimum riser height. R311.7.5.2: "The tread depth shall be not less than 9 inches (229 mm)", same 3/8-inch rule. R311.7.5.3: nosing projection 3/4 to 1-1/4 inches, radius not over 9/16 inch or bevel not over 1/2 inch, not required where the tread is at least 10 inches. R311.7.8.1: handrail height "not less than 30 inches (762 mm) and not more than 38 inches (965 mm)"; where the handrail tops a guard, 34 to 38 inches. R311.7.8 handrails on at least one side of each flight with four or more risers; R311.7.8.2 projection 4-1/2 inches; R311.7.8.3 1-1/2 inches clear of a wall; R311.7.8.5 Type I (1-1/4 to 2 inch round; noncircular perimeter 4 to 6-1/4 inches, cross section 2-1/4 inches) and Type II. R311.7.1 width 36 inches above the handrail, 31-1/2 at and below with one handrail, 27 with two. R311.7.2 headroom 6 feet 8 inches. R311.7.3 rise per flight 12 feet 7 inches. R311.7.6 landing at top and bottom, 36 inches in the direction of travel on a straight run, exception at the top of an interior flight where no door swings over the stairs. R311.7.7 walking surface slope 1:48. R311.3 landing each side of each exterior door, 36 inches deep, exterior slope 1/4 in 12. R311.3.1 required egress door: landing not more than 1-1/2 inches below the top of the threshold; exterior side not more than 8 inches below where the door does not swing over it. R311.3.2 other exterior doors 8 inches. R311.6 hallway width not less than 3 feet. R311.2 egress door side-hinged, 32 inches clear at 90 degrees, 78 inches clear height, openable from inside without a key. R303.7 interior stairs lit to 1 footcandle at treads and landings; a wall switch at each floor level where the stair has six or more risers. The model 2021 IRC values (7-3/4 riser, 10 tread, 34 to 38 handrail, 7-3/4 exterior landing) are not Oregon's.
Source: UpCodes Oregon, chapter 3 (up.codes/viewer/oregon/irc-2021/chapter/3/building-planning), retrieved 2026-10-27. Corroboration for 8 and 9: City of Portland appeal record efiles.portlandoregon.gov/record/15338848 (found by search; the record states "a maximum riser height of 8 inches... The minimum tread depth shall be 9 inches").
Confidence: high for the text as published on the designated route; medium-high that UpCodes carries every Oregon amendment (the official ICC viewer refuses tools).
Used in: 25.4, 25.5 (Code; the R7 model values kept in Applied as the design, Jon to confirm).

## 2026-10-27  Oregon guard and window-fall rules (2023 ORSC R312)
Finding: R312.1.1 guards where a walking surface is more than 30 inches above the floor or grade within 36 inches horizontally. R312.1.2 at least 36 inches; on the open sides of stairs at least 34 inches from the nosing line; where the guard top serves as the handrail, 34 to 38 inches. R312.1.3 openings reject a 4-inch sphere; the stair triangle formed by riser, tread and bottom rail rejects a 6-inch sphere; "Guards on the open side of stairs shall not have openings that allow passage of a sphere 5 inches (127 mm) in diameter. Opening limitations for required guards on open sides of stairways are applicable above the second riser of the stair." R312.2.1: an operable window whose clear opening bottom is less than 24 inches above the finished floor and more than 72 inches above the finished grade (or a flat surface at least 36 inches wide) outside either passes no 4-inch sphere when fully open or has ASTM F2090 opening control or fall prevention devices. R312.2.2: on an escape window the released device does not reduce the opening below R310.2.1 and R310.2.2. "Section R313 Automatic Fire Sprinkler Systems. This section is not adopted by the State of Oregon."
Source: UpCodes Oregon, chapter 3, retrieved 2026-10-27.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 25.3, 25.5.

## 2026-10-27  Garage separation and under-stair protection in Oregon's text; is the garage door required to self-close?
Finding: R302.5.1 "Openings from a private garage directly into a room used for sleeping purposes shall not be permitted." R302.5.1.1 "Other openings between the garage and residence shall be equipped with solid wood doors not less than 1-3/8 inches (35 mm) in thickness, solid or honeycomb-core steel doors not less than 1-3/8 inches (35 mm) thick, or 20-minute fire-rated doors. Exception: Compliance with this section shall not be required where both the dwelling unit and garage are protected by an automatic fire sprinkler system installed in accordance with NFPA 13D or other approved automatic fire sprinkler system." No self-closing, automatic-closing or self-latching requirement appears in the Oregon text (the model 2021 IRC requires a self-closing or automatic-closing device). R302.5.2 ducts minimum No. 26 gage sheet steel with no openings into the garage; a vibration isolator in the garage duct at least 18 inches from the penetration. R302.5.3 other penetrations per R302.11 item 4. R302.6 and Table R302.6: 1/2-inch gypsum on the garage side (from the residence and attics); 5/8-inch Type X under habitable rooms above; 1/2-inch gypsum on walls and structure supporting the separation floor/ceiling; 1/2-inch gypsum on the interior side of exterior walls of a garage less than 3 feet from a dwelling on the same lot; walls perpendicular to the dwelling wall exempt; gypsum attached per Table R702.3.5 (Type X at a garage ceiling beneath habitable rooms: fasteners at 6 inches, 1-7/8-inch 0.099-inch galvanized nails or equivalent screws, framing at 24 inches maximum, perpendicular). R302.7 enclosed space under stairs reached by a door or access panel: walls, under-stair surface and soffits protected on the enclosed side with 1/2-inch gypsum, with an NFPA 13D exception.
Source: UpCodes Oregon, chapters 3 and 7, retrieved 2026-10-27. The wave 1 research (research_questions_ch13_ch16.md line 464) cited a self-latching and self-closing requirement from an UpCodes page that was evidently the model IRC, not Oregon's.
Confidence: high for the published text; the R7 self-closing claim is not Oregon law.
Used in: 25.6, 25.5 (under-stair), 25.24.

## 2026-10-27  Light, ventilation, room size, ceiling height, fixture spacing (2023 ORSC R303 to R307)

R303.3.1 any room with a bathtub, shower or spa gets mechanical ventilation per M1505. R303.3.2 a toilet room without bathing: 3 square feet of glazing, half openable, or artificial light plus mechanical ventilation at Table M1505.5 rates. R304.1 70 square feet, R304.2 7 feet in any horizontal dimension, kitchens excepted from both. R304.3 portions under a sloped ceiling below 5 feet, or a furred ceiling below 7 feet, do not count. R305.1 7 feet for habitable space and hallways; 6 feet 8 inches for bathrooms, toilet rooms and laundry rooms; sloped ceilings: the required floor area at least 5 feet and half of it at least 7 feet; a bathroom may have up to 75 percent of its floor under a sloped ceiling below 6 feet 8 inches if a 21 by 24 inch area in front of toilets and lavatories and a 24 by 30 inch area in front of and inside a tub or shower keep 6 feet 4 inches; basement beams and ducts to 6 feet 4 inches; beams spaced at least 4 feet apart may project 6 inches below the required height. R307.1 fixtures spaced per Figure R307.1 "and in accordance with the requirements of the Plumbing Code"; the figure note reads "(See the Plumbing Code for shower clearances.)". R307.2 tub and shower floors and walls above tubs with showerheads and in shower compartments nonabsorbent "to a height of not less than 6 feet (1829 mm) above the floor." R303.10 heating to 68°F (not used).

## 2026-10-27  Fixture clearance, shower size, curb, lining and bidet temperature in the UPC (the base of the 2023 OPSC)
Finding: UPC 402.5 (text as carried in the 2025 California Plumbing Code, 2024 UPC base): water closets and bidets at least 15 inches from center to a side wall or obstruction and 30 inches center to center; "clear space in front of a water closet, lavatory, or bidet" at least 24 inches; grab bars and paper dispensers are not obstructions. Shower compartments (CPC 408.7, the 2021 UPC's 408.6): at least 1,024 square inches of finished interior, able to encompass a 30-inch circle, maintained to 70 inches above the drain outlet; a receptor at least 30 by 60 inches is exempt from the area and dimension minimums; fold-down seats in accessible showers may protrude. Lining (CPC 408.8, the 2021 UPC's 408.7): subfloor and rough walls lined to "not less than 3 inches (76 mm) above the top of the finished dam or threshold"; pitched 1/4 inch per foot to the weep holes; not nailed or perforated less than 1 inch above the finished dam; PVC (ASTM D4551), CPE (ASTM D4068), sheet lead, sheet copper, or built-up felt; flood test to 2 inches for 15 minutes, with a temporary 2-inch barrier where there is no threshold. Bidets (410.3, same number): hot water limited to 110°F (43°C) by an ASSE 1070/ASME A112.1070/CSA B125.70 or CSA B125.3 device, or an ASSE 1084 water heater. UPC 408.5 Finished Curb or Threshold (2021 UPC, as reproduced from the IAPMO 2021 UPC Illustrated Training Manual): a finished dam, curb or threshold at least 1 inch lower than the receptor's sides and back, dam depth 2 to 9 inches, finished floor sloped "not less than 1/8 inch per foot" and not more than 1/2 inch per foot to the drain, thresholds wide enough for a 22-inch door, shower doors keeping a 22-inch unobstructed opening.
Source: gocodebook.com 2025 CPC pages for 402.5, 408.7, 408.8 and 410.3 (retrieved 2026-10-27); tileletter.com/?p=32535 (the 2021 UPC Illustrated Training Manual excerpt, retrieved 2026-10-27). The numbering shift is consistent: the 2024 UPC added 408.2 Tileable Shower Receptors, so CPC 408.5 to 408.8 are the 2021 UPC's 408.4 to 408.7, and BCD's 2021 OPSC change summary names 408.5 "Finished Curb or Threshold" and 408.6 "Shower Compartments".
Confidence: medium-high. Base UPC text from two independent reproductions; Oregon amendments to 402.5, 408.6 and 408.7 not read (the OPSC text on UpCodes needs an account).
Used in: 25.10, 25.14. Corrects R7: front clearance 21 to 24 inches, shower 30 by 30 to 1,024 square inches and a 30-inch circle, lining 2 to 3 inches, "OPSC 417.5.2" (an IPC number) to OPSC 408.7.

## 2026-10-27  Oregon's own plumbing amendments that touch interiors
Finding: BCD's 2021 OPSC change summary: the model code added that a shower without a threshold makes the floor space in the same room a wet location; Oregon replaced "wet location" with "wet room", kept its own definition of wet room (which "points to a barrier free shower"), and deleted the cross-references to other codes (OPSC 408.5); the earlier Oregon wet-room exception in 408.6 was rescinded as unnecessary; the model code's ASSE 1084 water-heater allowance applies to 408.3, 409.4 and 410.3. BCD's 2023 OPSC summary: 408.2 and Table 401.3 showerheads "1.8 gpm at 80 psi" (Oregon amendment, HB 2062 (2021)); Statewide Code Interpretation 22-04 "clarifies that a finished curb or dam is not required and defines a threshold" (retained).
Source: oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Documents/21opsc-change-summary.pdf and codechangesummary.pdf, retrieved 2026-10-27.
Confidence: high for the summaries; the text of the wet-room definition and of SCI 22-04 was not found (open item).
Used in: 25.10, 25.14.

## 2026-10-27  What are the federal formaldehyde emission limits for composite wood?
Finding: 40 CFR 770.10(b): "For hardwood plywood made with a veneer core or a composite core, 0.05 parts per million (ppm) of formaldehyde"; "For medium-density fiberboard, 0.11 ppm"; "For thin medium-density fiberboard, 0.13 ppm"; "For particleboard, 0.09 ppm." 770.10(a): the standards apply to composite wood products "sold, supplied, offered for sale, or manufactured (including imported) on or after June 1, 2018 in the United States." The March 22, 2019 date in the Revision 7 text and the answer file is the end of the transition after which only TSCA Title VI third-party certification counted.
Source: law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/770.10 (retrieved 2026-10-27); the eCFR page redirected to a bot check and was not used.
Confidence: high for the limits and the 2018 date (regulation text); medium for the characterization of the 2019 date (from the answer file, not re-read).
Used in: 25.23 Code and Professional.

## Cross-chapter flags

Filed 2026-10-27 by the chapter 25 writer. Format: chapter and location, what is wrong, the evidence.

- **Chapter 21, Electrical: Branch Circuits, section 21.10 Applied step 1 and its first Code entry.** The list of rooms needing a wall-switched lighting outlet omits laundry areas. The 2023 NEC 210.70(A)(1) heading reads "Habitable Rooms, Kitchens, Laundry Areas, and Bathrooms" and adds the laundry area (new in 2023). Evidence: Leviton's 2023 code-change page (captaincode2023.leviton.com/node/315); research/new/ch25.md, entry "Does the 2023 NEC require a switched lighting outlet in the laundry?". The interiors chapter states the rule in one Professional sentence and points at the branch-circuit chapter.
- **Chapter 21, section 21.10 Professional and the closet-luminaire Code entry.** States a single 12-inch clearance for enclosed fixtures. NEC 410.16(C) sets 12 inches for surface-mounted incandescent or LED fixtures with an enclosed source, 6 inches for surface fluorescent and for recessed fixtures, and no clearance for fixtures identified for the storage space, with pendants and exposed lamps prohibited. Evidence: the Revision 7 interiors text (revision7 chapter, section 25.12, sourced to OESC/NEC 410.16), carried into the interiors chapter's Applied steps; the NEC text itself was not re-read by this writer.
- **Chapter 29, Build Sequence, section 29.12 Applied, Professional and Code.** Sends the foam thermal barrier and the radiant topping slab to the interiors chapter. The interiors chapter now carries neither: the thermal barrier is in the Envelope chapter (14.5, R316) and the topping slab in the HVAC chapter (23.9). The same section calls the garage-dwelling separation a "fire-rated" assembly installed "to its listed system"; Table R302.6 prescribes gypsum thickness and type, not a rated or listed assembly. Evidence: UpCodes Oregon R302.6 and Table R302.6; the Envelope and HVAC chapter texts.
- **Chapter 19, Plumbing DWV, section 19.14 Code.** The fixture-clearance entry cites "2023 OPSC Chapter 4" only (a chapter-level source) and states no value. The section is OPSC 402.5: 15 inches from a water closet or bidet center to a side wall or obstruction, 30 inches center to center, 24 inches clear in front of a water closet, lavatory or bidet. Evidence: research/new/ch25.md, entry "Fixture clearance, shower size, curb, lining and bidet temperature in the UPC".
- **research/answers/ch25_interiors.md, item 2.** Gives early-entry saw-cut timing as 4 to 12 hours and conventional wet-cut as up to 24 hours. The Foundation chapter (10.x, "why slabs crack") gives early-entry 1 to 4 hours and wet-cut 6 to 12 hours. The interiors chapter follows the Foundation chapter and states no timing. Evidence: the two texts; for the whole-book pass or the answer-file correction list.
- **research/answers/ch25_interiors.md, item 96.** States ANSI A326.3's recommended minimum wet DCOF as 0.43. The trade summaries read 0.42 for the Interior Wet class (consistent with the A137.1 figure the Revision 7 text used). Evidence: floridatile.com/productuseclassifications and crossville.com DCOF pages (retrieved 2026-10-27).
- **research/answers/ch25_interiors.md, items 3 and 61, and the Revision 7 interiors text.** Both give the shower-pan liner turn-up as 2 inches and cite "OPSC 417.5.2". The number belongs to the IPC's 417.5.2; the UPC-based OPSC's lining section (408.7 in the 2023 edition) requires 3 inches above the finished dam or threshold. Evidence: research/new/ch25.md, the UPC entry.
- **Every chapter stating stair geometry (the Structural, Foundation and Build Sequence chapters where they mention stairs).** Oregon's 2023 ORSC stair text differs from the model code: riser 8 inches maximum, tread 9 inches minimum, handrail 30 to 38 inches, guard openings on open stair sides 5 inches above the second riser. Any chapter quoting the model's 7-3/4, 10 and 34 as Oregon code is wrong. Evidence: research/new/ch25.md, first entry (UpCodes Oregon R311.7.5 and R311.7.8.1), corroborated by a Portland appeal record.
