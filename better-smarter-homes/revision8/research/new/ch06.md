## 2026-10-29  What does Oregon's R301.2.3 require for snow?
Finding: R301.2.3: wood, cold-formed steel, masonry and concrete, and SIP construction "in regions with ground snow loads 70 pounds per square foot (3.35 kPa) or less, shall be in accordance with Chapters 5, 6 and 8. Buildings in regions with ground snow loads greater than 70 pounds per square foot (3.35 kPa) shall be designed in accordance with Section R301.1.3." R301.2.3.1: "Site-specific ground snow loads, pg, shall be those set forth in the online lookup tool at Snowload.seao.org/lookup.html. ## 2026-10-29  What changed in Oregon's design snow loads, and what does Statewide Alternate Method 26-01 permit?
Finding: The Building Codes Division's technical bulletin "Comprehensive Design Snow Load Changes in the 2025 OSSC": the 2025 OSSC, effective October 1, 2025 and mandatory April 1, 2026, "adopts ASCE 7-22, replacing Oregon's legacy SEAO snow load map with a national, risk-targeted, digitally mapped ground snow load database"; the SEAO electronic map (2013) "has served as Oregon's codified snow load standard source through the 2022 OSSC"; ground snow loads "cannot be directly compared to prior editions", "national averages show roughly 12% increases, some regions of Oregon will experience significantly higher values"; values are now strength-level; the thermal factor "now depends on the roof insulation R-value and the ground snow load, potentially increasing the roof snow load for well-insulated buildings"; "Oregon's historic minimum roof snow load requirement (regardless of slope) has been removed"; "Do not mix old and new methods"; the lookup is "the ASCE 7 Hazard Tool (set to ASCE 7-22 version to match the 2025 OSSC) or the Oregon Design Criteria Hub (2025 OSSC application)"; SAM 26-01 "allows continued reference to the 2022 OSSC throughout the effective period of the 2023 ORSC (through March 31, 2027)". Resources: ascehazardtool.org and oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Pages/design-criteria.aspx. SAM 26-01 itself reads as research/new/ch02.md quotes it.
