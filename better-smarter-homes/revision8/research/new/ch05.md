## 2026-10-27  Which ORSC section sets the retaining-wall engineering trigger (Revision 7 and answer items 12 and 13 cite R404.1.1)?
Finding: R404.4 Retaining Walls: "Retaining walls that are not laterally supported at the top and that retain in excess of 48 inches (1219 mm) of unbalanced fill, or retaining walls exceeding 24 inches (610 mm) in height that resist lateral loads in addition to soil, shall be designed in accordance with Section R301.1.3 to ensure stability against overturning, sliding, excessive foundation pressure and water uplift. Retaining walls shall be designed for a safety factor of 1.5 against lateral sliding and overturning. This section shall not apply to foundation walls supporting buildings." R404.1.1 Design Required is the foundation-wall section: "Concrete or masonry foundation walls shall be designed in accordance with Section R301.1.3 where either of the following conditions exists: Walls are subject to hydrostatic pressure from ground water. Walls supporting more than 48 inches (1219 mm) of unbalanced backfill that do not have permanent lateral support at the top and bottom."
Source: up.codes/viewer/oregon/irc-2021/chapter/4/foundations (2023 ORSC), retrieved 2026-10-27.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 5.16 Code (R404.4), 5.13 and 5.22 Code (R404.1.1). Correction to research/answers/ch05_soil.md items 12 and 13 (the threshold values are right; the section number is R404.4).

## 2026-10-27  Does the ORSC require a sump where gravity discharge is not feasible (Revision 7 cited R405.2)?
Finding: R405.1 Concrete or Masonry Foundations: "Drains shall be provided around concrete or masonry foundations that retain earth and enclose habitable or usable spaces located below grade. Drainage tiles, gravel or crushed stone drains, perforated pipe or other approved systems or materials shall be installed at or below the top of the footing or below the bottom of the slab and shall discharge by gravity or mechanical means into an approved drainage system. Gravel or crushed stone drains shall extend not less than 1 foot (305 mm) beyond the outside edge of the footing and 6 inches (152 mm) above the top of the footing and be covered with an approved filter membrane material. The top of open joints of drain tiles shall be protected with strips of building paper. Except where otherwise recommended by the drain manufacturer, perforated drains shall be surrounded with an approved filter membrane or the filter membrane shall cover the washed gravel or crushed rock covering the drain. Drainage tiles or perforated pipe shall be placed on not less than 2 inches (51 mm) of washed gravel or crushed rock not less than one sieve size larger than the tile joint opening or perforation and covered with not less than 6 inches (152 mm) of the same material. Exception: A drainage system is not required where the foundation is installed on well-drained ground or sand-gravel mixture soils according to the Unified Soil Classification System, Group I soils, as detailed in Table R405.1." R405.2 is "Wood Foundations"; its R405.2.3 requires, "In other than Group I soils, a sump ... not less than 24 inches (610 mm) in diameter or 20 inches square ... not less than 24 inches (610 mm) below the bottom of the basement floor". Table R405.1: Group I GW, GP, SW, SP, GM, SM (drainage good); Group II GC, SC, ML, CL (medium); Group III CH, MH (poor); Group IV OL, OH, Pt (OL poor, OH and Pt unsatisfactory). Footnotes: "The percolation rate for good drainage is over 4 inches per hour, medium drainage is 2 inches to 4 inches per hour, and poor is less than 2 inches per hour. Soils with a low potential expansion typically have a plasticity index (PI) of 0 to 15, soils with a medium potential expansion have a PI of 10 to 35 and soils with a high potential expansion have a PI greater than 20."
Source: as above, 2023 ORSC chapter 4, retrieved 2026-10-27.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 5.14 Code and Professional, 5.5 Professional (USCS table group column), 5.9 Professional. The sump requirement and the 24-inch dimension belong to wood foundations only; flagged for the foundation chapter below.

## 2026-10-27  What triggers R406.2 waterproofing (Revision 7 and answer item 7: "seasonal high groundwater within 24 inches of the footing")?
Finding: R406.2: "In areas where a high water table or other severe soil-water conditions are known to exist, exterior foundation walls that retain earth and enclose interior spaces and floors below grade shall be waterproofed from the finished grade to the higher of the top of the footing or 6 inches (152 mm) below the top of the basement floor." Listed materials: "Two-ply hot-mopped felts. Fifty-five-pound (25 kg) roll roofing. Forty-mil (1 mm) polymer-modified asphalt. Sixty-mil (1.5 mm) flexible polymer cement. One-eighth-inch (3 mm) cement-based, fiber-reinforced, waterproof coating. Sixty-mil (1.5 mm) solvent-free liquid-applied synthetic rubber." with joints "lapped and sealed with an adhesive compatible with the membrane". R406.1 dampproofs walls "Except where required by Section R406.2 to be waterproofed", over the same height. No groundwater depth appears in either section.
Source: as above.
Confidence: high for the text; the origin of the 24-inch figure in the wave-1 research was not traced.
Used in: 5.13 Code and Professional; Open items (24-inch figure).

## 2026-10-27  Where does the ORSC define expansive soil (Revision 7 cited IBC 1803.5.3)?
Finding: The Oregon numbers are R403.1.10 and R403.1.10.1 (UpCodes marks the model numbers R403.1.8 and R403.1.8.1 as deleted; Oregon inserted R403.1.7 Isolated Footings, shifting the later numbers by two). R403.1.10: "Foundation and floor slabs for buildings located on expansive soils shall be designed in accordance with Section 1808.6 of the Building Code. Exception: Slab-on-ground and other foundation systems that have performed adequately in soil conditions similar to those encountered at the building site are permitted subject to the approval of the building official." R403.1.10.1: "Soils meeting all of the following provisions shall be considered to be expansive, except that tests to show compliance with Items 1, 2 and 3 shall not be required if the test prescribed in Item 4 is conducted: Plasticity Index (PI) of 15 or greater, determined in accordance with ASTM D4318. More than 10 percent of the soil particles pass a No. 200 sieve (75 µm), determined in accordance with ASTM D422. More than 10 percent of the soil particles are less than 5 micrometers in size, determined in accordance with ASTM D422. Expansion Index greater than 20, determined in accordance with ASTM D4829."
Source: as above.
Confidence: high for the text; medium for the Oregon numbers (from the viewer's markings, not a BCD document), the same basis as the land chapter's R403.1.9.
Used in: 5.9 Code.

## 2026-10-27  What do R401.2, R401.4, R401.4.1, R401.4.2, R403.1 and R403.1.4 say?
Finding: R401.2: "Foundation construction shall be capable of accommodating all loads in accordance with Section R301 and of transmitting the resulting loads to the supporting soil. ... Fill soils that support footings and foundations shall be designed, installed and tested in accordance with Section R301.1.3." R401.4: "Where data indicate expansive soils, compressible soils, shifting soils or other questionable soil characteristics are likely to be present, the building official shall determine whether to require a soil test to determine the soil's characteristics at a particular location. This test shall be done by an approved agency using an approved method." R401.4.1: "In lieu of a complete geotechnical evaluation, the load-bearing values in Table R401.4.1 shall be assumed." Table R401.4.1: crystalline bedrock 12,000 psf; "Sedimentary and foliated rock" 4,000; "Sandy gravel and/or gravel (GW and GP)" 3,000; sand, silty sand, clayey sand, silty gravel and clayey gravel (SW, SP, SM, SC, GM and GC) 2,000; clay, sandy clay, silty clay, clayey silt, silt and sandy silt (CL, ML, MH and CH) 1,500. Footnote a: "Where soil tests are required by Section R401.4, the allowable bearing capacities of the soil shall be part of the recommendations." Footnote b: "Where the building official determines that in-place soils with an allowable bearing capacity of less than 1,500 psf are likely to be present at the site, the allowable bearing capacity shall be determined by a soils investigation." R401.4.2: "Instead of a complete geotechnical evaluation, where top or subsoils are compressible or shifting, they shall be removed to a depth and width sufficient to ensure stable moisture content in each active zone and shall not be used as fill or stabilized within each active zone by chemical, dewatering or presaturation." R403.1: "... Footings shall be supported on undisturbed natural soils or fill [soils] in accordance with Section R401.2." R403.1.4: "Exterior footings shall be placed not less than 12 inches (305 mm) below finished grade on the undisturbed ground surface." R403.1.4.1 lists four frost methods (below the frost line in Table R301.2, R403.3, ASCE 32, solid rock) and states "Footings shall not bear on frozen soil unless the frozen condition is permanent." Table R403.1, 8-inch solid concrete or fully grouted masonry, footing width in inches at 1,000 / 1,500 / 2,000 / 3,000-plus psf: one story 24 / 16 / 12 / 12; two story 44 / 29 / 21 / 14; three story 63 / 42 / 32 / 21.
Source: as above.
Confidence: high. R401.4.2 reads two ways (stabilization as an alternative to removal, or barred inside the active zone); the Revision 7 text and the answer file took opposite readings.
Used in: 5.1, 5.8, 5.9, 5.11, 5.20 Code; Open items (R401.4.2 reading).

## 2026-10-27  What do the ORSC fill and slab-base sections say (Revision 7 stated R506.2.3 as a 10-mil Class A retarder lapped 6 inches)?
Finding: R506.2.1 Fill: "Fill material shall be free of vegetation and foreign material. The fill shall be compacted to ensure uniform support of the slab, and except where approved, the fill depths shall not exceed 24 inches (610 mm) for clean sand or gravel and 8 inches (203 mm) for earth." R506.2.2 Base: "A 4-inch-thick (102 mm) base course consisting of clean graded sand, gravel, crushed stone, crushed concrete or crushed blast-furnace slag passing a 2-inch (51 mm) sieve shall be placed on the prepared subgrade where the slab is below grade. Exception: A base course is not required where the concrete slab is installed on well-drained or sand-gravel mixture soils classified as Group I". R506.2.3, retitled by Oregon "Ground Cover": "A 6-mil (0.006 inch; 152 µm) polyethylene or approved Class I vapor retarder with joints lapped not less than 12 inches (305 mm) shall be placed between the concrete floor slab and the base course or the prepared subgrade where a base course does not exist", with exceptions for garages, unheated accessory structures, small unheated storage rooms and carports, flatwork, and "Where approved by the building official, based on local site conditions."
Source: up.codes/viewer/oregon/irc-2021/chapter/5/floors (2023 ORSC), retrieved 2026-10-27.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 5.20 Code (R506.2.1), 5.14 Professional (R506.2.2, R506.2.3). The 10-mil Class A membrane is a project specification in the foundation chapter, not the code value.

## 2026-10-27  What exactly does the 1200-C construction stormwater permit require, and when?
Finding: "Coverage under this General Permit is required for all construction activities that have the potential to discharge stormwater ... to surface waters of the state or to conveyance systems draining to such waters". Coverage required for (a) "Construction activity that will disturb one acre of land or more, including cumulative off-site disturbance acreage related to construction support activities, regardless of project phasing"; (c) "Construction activity disturbing less than one acre of land that is part of a common plan of development or sale that will ultimately disturb one acre or more of land"; (e) under one acre "where DEQ has documented water quality violations attributable to the activity or where DEQ determines there is significant potential to contribute pollutants". Construction activities include "Clearing, grading, excavating, grubbing, stumping, demolition, stockpiling" and support activities including "portable/temporary concrete or asphalt batch plants ... equipment and material staging and storage". Application: "The operator must submit complete application materials at least 30 days before the commencement of any construction activities", including the application form, an Erosion and Sediment Control Plan, a Land Use Compatibility Statement, current wetland or stream delineations, and the fees; coverage requires DEQ's written notification before work. "Small Lot Exception: The small lot permit is required for construction activities disturbing less than one acre if the activity is part of a 'common plan of development or sale' that disturbs one or more acres. Small lot permit applications require payment of a reduced application fee and no annual fee." Issued pursuant to ORS 468B.050; issuance and effective date December 15, 2025; expiration December 14, 2030.
Source: oregon.gov/deq/wq/Documents/1200CPermit.pdf, downloaded and text extracted 2026-10-27.

## 2026-10-27  Which excavation rules apply in Oregon, and what do they say?
Finding: OAR 437-003-0001(16) adopts by reference 29 CFR 1926.650, 1926.651, 1926.652 and Appendices A to F of Subpart P. 1926.652(a)(1): "Each employee in an excavation shall be protected from cave-ins by an adequate protective system ... except when: (i) Excavations are made entirely in stable rock; or (ii) Excavations are less than 5 feet (1.52 m) in depth and examination of the ground by a competent person provides no indication of a potential cave-in." Note in the Oregon text: "The tables contained in Appendices A, B, C, D, E, and F do not contain sloping or shoring information for excavations over 20 feet deep. The protective systems for excavations over 20 feet in depth must be designed by a registered professional engineer". 1926.652(b)(1)(i): without another option, "Excavations shall be sloped at an angle not steeper than one and one-half horizontal to one vertical (34 degrees measured from the horizontal)". Table B-1, excavations less than 20 feet deep: Stable Rock vertical (90 degrees), Type A 3/4:1 (53), Type B 1:1 (45), Type C 1 1/2:1 (34). 1926.651(h)(1): "Employees shall not work in excavations in which there is accumulated water, or in excavations in which water is accumulating, unless adequate precautions have been taken ... could include special support or shield systems to protect from cave-ins, water removal to control the level of accumulating water, or use of a safety harness and lifeline." (h)(2): water removal equipment "shall be monitored by a competent person". (h)(3): "If excavation work interrupts the natural drainage of surface water (such as streams), diversion ditches, dikes, or other suitable means shall be used to prevent surface water from entering the excavation".
Source: osha.oregon.gov/osharules/div3/div3p.pdf (Oregon OSHA Division 3, Subdivision P), downloaded and text extracted 2026-10-27.

## 2026-10-27  What does Oregon's fish-passage law require at a driveway culvert (answer item 14 gives a bed width of 1.2 times the active channel width plus 2 feet)?
Finding: ORS 509.580(1): "'Artificial obstruction' means any dam, diversion, culvert or other human-made device placed in the waters of this state that precludes or prevents the migration of native migratory fish." (2)(d): "Construction" means, "For purposes of culverts, installation or replacement of a roadbed or culvert." ORS 509.585(2): "a person owning or operating an artificial obstruction may not construct or maintain any artificial obstruction across any waters of this state that are inhabited, or historically inhabited, by native migratory fish without providing passage for native migratory fish." (4): "A person owning or operating an artificial obstruction shall, prior to construction, fundamental change in permit status or abandonment of the artificial obstruction in any waters of this state, obtain a determination from the department as to whether native migratory fish are or historically have been present in the waters. If the department determines that native migratory fish are or historically have been present in the waters, the person ... shall either submit a proposal for fish passage to the department or apply for a waiver ... Approval ... must be obtained from the department prior to construction, permit modification or abandonment". OAR 635-412-0035(3)(a), Stream Simulation Option: crossing structures "shall have beds under or within the structure that: (i) Are equal to or greater than the active channel width, as measured at sufficient locations outside the influence of any artificial or unique channel constrictions or tributaries both upstream and downstream of the site; (ii) Are equal to the slope of, and at elevations continuous with, the surrounding long-channel streambed profile ...; (iii) Have, for open-bottomed road-stream crossing structures, a minimum of 3 feet vertical clearance from the active channel width elevation to the inside top of the structure"; (v)(V) bed material in closed-bottom structures "at a minimum depth of 20 percent of the structure height and a maximum depth of 50 percent"; (3)(b) an alternative design may be approved on fish-performance and hydraulic justification. No 1.2 multiplier and no added 2 feet appear in the rule.
Source: oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_509.580 and ors_509.585; oregon.public.law/rules/oar_635-412-0035; retrieved 2026-10-27.
Confidence: high.
Used in: 5.17 Code and Professional. Correction to research/answers/ch05_soil.md item 14 (the bed-width formula).

## 2026-10-27  What does ACI say about standing water and debris before a footing pour?

## Cross-chapter flags

Filed here per the coordinator's instruction of 2026-10-27. Format as in research/new/_cross-chapter-flags.md: the chapter and location, what is wrong, the evidence.

- **Chapter 10, Foundation, Basement, Slabs, and Concrete Placement, sections 10.11.3 and 10.12 Code (Revision 7 text).** States "sump pit per R405.2: 24 inches minimum depth, tight-fitting cover" and cites "R405.2" for the sump. In the 2023 ORSC, R405.2 is Wood Foundations; the 24-inch sump is R405.2.3, for wood foundations in other than Group I soils.
