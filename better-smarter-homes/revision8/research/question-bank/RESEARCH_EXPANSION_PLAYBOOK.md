# Wave 3 Research-Building Playbook (the next AI's first job)

Created: 2026-07-12. Read this before touching `research_questions_revision7.md`. Your first job in the next session is not to answer research questions — it is to BUILD a huge, deep bank of high-quality research questions. Target: grow Wave 3 from its seed to **at least 100 questions, aiming higher**. Then, once the question bank is built and reviewed, the research phase answers them, and then the chapters get written.

## The owner's expansion philosophy (apply everywhere)

- **Only real information. No fluff.** Every added question must lead to a fact, a method, a material, a tool, a cost, a problem, or a solution the builder can use.
- **Make the book as big as it can honestly get** — double or triple the depth where real content exists. This is not only about concrete; every chapter has non-code material that can be expanded a lot.
- **The biggest expansion is in the NON-CODE / non-technical material.** The book has leaned on code. Now research and add: creative building ideas, options in detail, tools used, ingredients used, methods, and — across every chapter — the real-world PROBLEMS people hit and the SOLUTIONS they found. Add missing code too, but the major growth is the non-code side.
- **Research beyond code books.** Look at what people have done all around the world: owner-builder and trade forums, homesteading/off-grid communities, international and vernacular practice, university research, manufacturer categories, historic DIY literature. Real experience and worldwide practice are first-class sources here, alongside code.
- **Ways to expand a topic:** find the missing details; zoom into an interesting, useful thing (the exact tool, the exact ingredient, the exact method, the failure that bites people); lay out all the options; and think outside the box with creative-but-real ideas.

## Writing-structure rule for the coming chapters (owner directive, 2026-07-12)

The four-layer structure (`Simple` / `Applied` / `Professional` / `Code`) is **the default for code and regulated/technical content, not a requirement for every section.** A section that is primarily creative, procedural, comparative, or explanatory (a survey of ways to build a mold, a taxonomy of concretes, a problem-and-solution catalog, a tools rundown) may use **whatever freeform shape serves it** — prose, lists, comparison tables, or only the layers that actually help. `Code` content still uses the three-part sourced entry. Everything, layered or freeform, still obeys the Complete Catalog Of Writing Problems and the no-fluff rule in `../PROBLEMS_AND_GOALS.md` and `../writing_style_guide.md`.

## The owner's flagged research veins (make these deep)

1. **Make-your-own structural materials, outside the box.** Every material the owner could make and use IF it passes a lab compression test on a cylinder (ASTM C39) or block (C140/C90) under the alternative-materials path (ORSC R104.11): dry-cast / zero-slump concrete, compressed stabilized earth blocks (CSEB), cement-stabilized rammed earth (his named idea), geopolymer, lime-pozzolan, ferrocement, fiber-reinforced and recycled variants, and more.
2. **Fast-demold / high-throughput production.** Press or compact the unit so it demolds in minutes-to-an-hour, to make ~100 units/day without 100 molds. The scientific-method path to prove a novel mix passes the lab press.
3. **Block-making tools and molds.** DIY molds (materials, gang molds, release agents, tolerances), hand-press rigs, vibrating methods, the imported machine classes (~$1,000–$15,000), mixers (pan vs drum for zero-slump), curing for fast turnaround, and the 100/day logistics.
4. **Mortar, grout, and jointing.** How to make mortar, the ingredients and admixtures that change its properties, and every failure mode the owner fears (cracking, shrinkage/expansion, efflorescence, spalling/popping, peeling, bond failure/separation) with causes and prevention.
5. **Problems-and-solutions in every chapter,** from real builders worldwide — the core of the non-code expansion.

## Method: build the questions chapter by chapter

Walk all 26 chapters. For each, ask the expansion questions and write real research questions (do not answer them yet):

1. What creative or alternative approaches exist that the book does not yet cover? (methods, materials, designs used anywhere in the world)
2. What tools, ingredients, and exact methods does this work actually use? Zoom in.
3. What are all the options, laid out for the owner to choose?
4. What goes wrong here on real projects, and how do experienced people prevent or fix it? (problem-and-solution, from real experience)
5. What costs, in ranges with a year, are missing?
6. What code or standard is genuinely missing (add it), and where is code NOT needed and creative/practical detail belongs instead?

Tag every question: `Lands in: <chapter>`, plus one or more of `[CODE]` `[REAL-WORLD-EXPERIENCE]` `[CREATIVE-IDEA]` `[TOOLS]` `[MATERIALS/INGREDIENTS]` `[METHOD]` `[PROBLEM-SOLUTION]` `[TEST-TO-PROVE]` `[COST]`. Follow the eleven-rule Instructions and the fixed status tags in `research_questions_revision7.md` (they carry the `OWNER-DESIGN-CHOICE` tag for the owner's own decisions).

## The full pipeline (revised 2026-07-12 per the owner)

This playbook covers the question-building phases; `../AI_TODO.md` holds the master pipeline and the detailed **Expansion Areas To Pre-Research** (four new flagship ideas — Resilience and Disaster Independence, What Concrete Uniquely Enables, Aesthetics and Architectural Expression, Designing for Centuries — plus Water Independence, Developing the Site, Passive Design, and the Owner-Builder's Body and Mind). The order:

1. **Pre-research** each new area with agents online (dig deep into the whole topic; return gathered understanding AND candidate questions to `drafts/`).
2. **Shape the structure**: create/place new chapters or fold areas into existing ones; expand outlines; update `../STRUCTURE_PLAN.md`; add skeleton files.
3. **Finalize the question bank**: merge the drafts and build the full per-chapter set (100+ and beyond, non-code-heavy) here.
4. **Research and answer**: download authoritative material first, then read and answer each question with sources and status tags, recording answers per the Wave 2/3 pattern (with `answers/` files). Lots of agents, each writing to files.
5. **Write**: one calibration chapter, full quality checks, then the owner's external Reviewer AI; once it passes, write as much as fits until context or the 5-hour bar is used. Four layers for code; freeform for creative sections; only real information.
6. **Closeout** (deferred): front matter, appendices, table of contents.
