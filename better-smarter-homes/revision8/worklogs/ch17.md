## Corrections found against the 2026 code text (filed in research/new/ch17.md)

- The 2026 OPSC (2024 UPC) took effect October 1, 2026; the Revision 7 text called it anticipated.
- OPSC 608.2: Oregon's amendment DELETES the model code's 1 1/2-inch strainer exemption (Revision 7 said Oregon created it). Every regulator takes a strainer.
- OPSC 608.2 and 608.3 (2026): expansion tanks are securely fastened to the structure; a certified plumbing contractor or RDP may design relief valves at 100 psi or less in lieu of the tank. No "valve between tank and system" prohibition appears in the 2026 text (Revision 7 claimed one): to the Dropped list.
- OPSC 603.2 and 603.4.2: Oregon requires the backflow assembly test at installation, by an OHA-certified tester; the model code's annual schedule is struck. Annual tests on a public connection come from OAR 333-061-0070 (public water systems only). Revision 7 cited both for annual testing.
- OPSC 603.4.9 (2026): devices with atmospheric vents or ports never in pits, underground or submerged; a double check may go below grade.
- OPSC 309.5 (2026): a dead leg longer than 1.5 pipe diameters takes an accessible flushing method (Revision 7 read it as a length cap).
- OPSC 1601.2 (2026): exceptions restored (irrigation at 360 gallons or less; single-family systems entirely on the exterior need no licensed designer); certified rainwater system designer added. 1605.3: a one-time cross-connection test of both systems, annual inspection struck.
- OPSC 609.10 (2026): new or repaired potable systems disinfected, 50 ppm for 24 hours or 200 ppm for 3 hours. New Code entry.
- OPSC 507.2, 507.4, 608.5, 503.2, 604.13, 610.2, 609.11, 609.12, 312.6, 313.1 read in 2026 text. New entries: water-heater strapping, drain pan, relief-valve discharge, pressure loss through treatment devices.
- Appendix K and Appendix M adopted statewide; Appendix N (Legionella) printed but not adopted; the OPSC defines potable water by OHA's requirements.
- The 2026 ORSC heat-pump rule (N1105.8) covers split-system air conditioning, not water heaters. Revision 7's hot-water Code entry framed it as a water-heating direction. Rewritten; flagged for the HVAC chapter.
- OAR 330-070-0073 is an Oregon Department of Energy appliance-eligibility rule; it does not bind the house. Demoted to a Professional sentence.
- "OPSC 508.2" for a 22 by 30 inch tank manhole: 508.2 governs appliances (water heaters) in attics; the tank dimension came from Ohio's cistern rule by analogy. The dimension stays as a project specification; the citation goes to Open items.
