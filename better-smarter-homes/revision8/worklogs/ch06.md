## What the verification changed (details in research/new/ch06.md)

- Floor live loads: ORSC Table R301.5 gives stairs 40 psf uniform with a 300-pound concentrated load, not 50 psf as Revision 7 stated. Decks 40 psf. Corrected.
- Masonry story height: ORSC R301.3 allows masonry a story height of 13 feet 7 inches with a bearing-wall clear height of 12 feet. Revision 7's "wall height 10 ft (R301.3)" was the ICF limit. Corrected.
- The three-story masonry maximum cited to R301.2.2.7 is not in that section (it limits wood, cold-formed steel and SIP buildings). Dropped; the real masonry limit is Table R606.12.2.1 (NP for a masonry second story in D0 to D2 without design) and R606.12.3.1.
- Prescriptive masonry in D0 to D2: R606.12.3.1 requires TMS 402 design except one-story walls with 9 feet or less between lateral supports, and R606.12.1.1 assumes wood or cold-formed steel diaphragms (the Wall System chapter carries the entry). The "two design tracks" table is rewritten on that basis.
- Stack bond: Oregon's rule is R606.3.7.2 (0.0007 of the vertical section in bed joints or bond beams at 48 inches), plus R606.12.4.1 and R606.12.4.2 in D2 (0.0015 and 0.0025, 24 and 16 inches). The answer file's "R606.8.1" is a Florida number.
- Grout lifts: R606.3.5.1 (8-foot lifts, 64-inch lifts with special inspection above 8 feet) and R606.3.5.2 (cleanouts above 64 inches where the building official requires them, 32 inches apart). The Revision 7 5-foot threshold is replaced.
- Mortar: R606.2.8.3 reads "Type M or S Portland cement-lime or mortar cement", and R606.12.3.4 bars Type N and masonry cement from the lateral force-resisting system. Corrected.
- Anchor bolts: R403.1.6.1 sets the 4-foot spacing "for buildings over two stories in height", not for a two-story house in D2. The entry itself moves to the Foundation chapter, which already carries it (flag filed for chapter 10 and the sample page).
- Snow: R301.2.3.1 names snowload.seao.org, sets 36 psf as the minimum ground snow load for prescriptive design and 25 psf as the minimum roof snow load for nonprescriptive design, and sends ground snow over 70 psf to engineered design. The 2025 OSSC replaced the SEAO map with ASCE 7-22 (BCD bulletin), and SAM 26-01 lets a 2023 ORSC design keep the 2022 OSSC. The Oregon code map chapter's "pending engineer" decision on the OSSC edition is kept, not duplicated.
- Wind: Oregon's Table R301.2 prints a special-wind-region basic design wind speed by county (120 mph for the coast and the Coast Range counties, 98 for Clackamas and Multnomah). R802.11 exempts connections at 200 pounds or less and roofs at 115 mph Exposure B with 5:12 pitch, 32-foot span and 24-inch spacing.
- Irregular buildings: the seven R301.2.2.6 conditions read from the text, including "an opening in a floor or roof exceeding the lesser of 12 feet or 50 percent of the least floor or roof dimension between adjacent braced wall lines".
- R608: R608.2 limits the prescriptive concrete wall to SDC A and B (and detached dwellings in C), two stories, 10 psf floor dead load and 15 psf roof dead load, which excludes concrete floors and roofs.
- Referenced editions in the 2023 ORSC (Chapter 44): ACI 318-19, ACI 332-20, PCA 100-17, TMS 402-16, TMS 602-16, ASCE 7-16 with Supplement 1, ICC 500-2020.
- FEMA P-320 sixth edition (December 2024): $12,700 to $16,100 for an 8-by-8-foot safe room and $25,600 to $34,500 for 14 by 14, excluding the door, with doors at $2,500 to $3,200. The answer file's figures were an earlier edition's.
- Chile: Moroni, Astroza and Acevedo (2004) print damage bands by wall density (light above 1.15 percent, moderate 0.85 to 1.15, severe 0.50 to 0.85) and the 69 and 57 percent figures. The answer file's "code minimum 0.85 percent" is not in the paper.
- GFRP: the StructureMag article (Szoke, Tepke and Gold, April 2023) confirms ACI 440.11 bars GFRP from any structural member in SDC D, E and F.
- Grout cost: Terrapin's page is dated May 2026 and gives $2 to $4 per square foot over hollow and $22 to $26 in coastal high-seismic markets.
- The IPC is not an Oregon code (chapter 5's flag). Overflow drains now cite ORSC R903.4.1; primary drain sizing is a Professional sentence with the plumbing code named and an Open item for the OPSC section.
