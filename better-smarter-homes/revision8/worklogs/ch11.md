## What the verification changed (details in research/new/ch11.md)

- R703.8.4.1 reads 32 inches horizontal and "24 inches (635 mm)" vertical, 2.67 square feet per tie, and an exception of 2 square feet per tie in SDC D0 to D2. The seismic tie-area reduction now has a code source; the 24/25 discrepancy is the code's own inch/metric mismatch (635 mm is 25 inches, the TMS value). The 24 governs.
- R703.8.3.1 table runs 3 to 20 feet by angle and stories above, and R703.8.3.2 allows an 18-foot-3-inch detail. The R7 entry ("6 to 14 feet") was incomplete.
- R606.12 excludes anchored veneer built to R703.8 from the seismic masonry rules, including R606.12.3.4 (no Type N or masonry cement in the lateral-force-resisting system). R606.2.8.3 allows Type M or S portland cement-lime or mortar cement for that system in D0 to D2.
- R606.12.3.1: in D0 and D1, masonry is designed to TMS 402 or TMS 403 except one-story walls with 9 feet or less between lateral supports. R606.12.1.1 assumes wood or cold-formed steel diaphragms.
- R606.13: cavity walls, each wythe 3 inches nominal minimum, cavity 4 inches nominal maximum unless ties are calculated, backing not thinner than facing. R606.13.2.2: adjustable ties one per 2.67 square feet, 24 inches maximum each way, 1.25-inch offset, 1/16-inch clearance, two 3/16-inch pintle legs.
- R606.2.12 permits Type M or S mortar with water added to pouring consistency as grout. The R7 claim "mortar is not an ASTM C476 material, so an inspector has grounds to reject" is corrected: stiff mortar packed into cores fails R606.3.5.1, thinned M or S mortar is allowed.
- R606.3.5.1 requires grout placed within 1-1/2 hours of water, consolidated while placing and reconsolidated after excess water is absorbed and before plasticity is lost; work stopped an hour or more stops 1 inch below the top. R606.3.5.2 cleanouts where the building official requires them for pours over 64 inches, at 32 inches maximum in solid-grouted masonry. Table R606.3.5.1 gives the fine and coarse grout space minimums (the R7 "paywalled thresholds").
- R606.3.3 tie installation (5/8-inch cover, 1-1/2-inch embedment, no bending after embedment); Table R606.3.4.1 corrosion minimums (A153 B-2 hot-dip galvanized for exterior joint reinforcement and ties not fully embedded; A167 Type 304 stainless for any exposure); R606.5 corbels; R606.6.1 two-thirds bearing per wythe; R606.10 block-wythe lintels designed; R606.11 note: bolt cells grouted solid.
- R703.8.5: base flashing beneath the first course above finished ground, and at floors, shelf angles and lintels.
- The code text names no TEK document for control joints. CMHA CMU-TEC-009-26 (current) replaces TEK 10-2C: 25 ft 4 in or a 1.5-to-1 panel ratio with joint reinforcement at 16 in (or 0.025 sq in per ft of height); first joint within half the spacing of a corner; reinforced relief joints instead of control joints where horizontal steel exceeds 0.2 percent of the net vertical section.
- TN 18A primary: the formula and its 23-ft-2-in example confirmed; joints within 2 ft of corners preferred; parapet joints at 15 ft maximum; ASTM C920 Grade NS Use M sealant, Class 50 recommended; backer rod 25 percent oversize, sealant depth half the width (1/4 in minimum).
- Movement magnitudes now sourced: brick grows 0.0009 x length (TN 18A); block drying shrinkage 0.0002 to 0.00045, carbonation 0.00025 (CMU-TEC-009-26).
- TN 9B map: all of Oregon at weathering index 50 or more; Table 1 recommends Grade SW for every exposure there.
- TEK 03-01C (TMS 602 tables): the hot-weather 100/90-with-8-mph and 115/105 lines are preparation and construction requirements, and fog spraying keys to mean daily temperature; R7's "submittal is triggered" corrected.
- ASTM C1714 is the preblended mortar specification and C952 is withdrawn; bond testing is ASTM C1072.
- ACI's ferrocement documents are ACI 549R-18 (report) and ACI 549.1R-18 (design guide).
- The R7 "integral drip on the tie" check conflicts with the structural chapter's tie table (no drip bend); the chapter follows the structural chapter.
