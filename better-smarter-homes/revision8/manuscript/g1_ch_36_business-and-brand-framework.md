# Chapter 36: Business and Brand Framework

In Oregon a company that offers, advertises, bids or arranges construction for anyone else needs a contractor's license before the work starts, because the offer itself is the licensable act. So the house, its land and its permits stay in Jon's name under the owner-builder exemption, and Better Smarter Homes LLC owns only the intangible property: the plans, the writing, the recordings and the name. Had the company owned the land, or built to sell, it would be a contractor by definition. Jon's exemption itself, the resale clocks and the words the company may not use in public are the legal chapter's. Costs are 2026 figures unless a different year is given.

Better Smarter Homes owns the plans, the writing, the photographs and the name, and it will never own land, pull a permit or take a dollar to build for anyone else. I once pictured a run of houses, each built, lived in and sold in turn. ## 36.1 Where the licensing line falls

**Simple**

Oregon licenses contractors by what they offer to do. Once a business advertises, bids or arranges construction for someone else, it must hold a contractor's license before the first hour of work. You may build your own house without one, because the law exempts an owner working on their own property. That exemption covers a person, never a company. So the land, the permits and the house stay in your name. Everything the company owns is intangible: the plans, the recordings, the writing and the name. Under the statute's own definition, a company that owns land and builds on it to sell is a contractor.

**Applied**

| Lane | What it holds |
|---|---|
| The house | Land, permits, construction, occupancy, maintenance and any sale decision |
| The brand | Recordings, writing, photographs, drawings, lessons and the audience |
| The company | Website, accounts, platform income, sponsorships, products, taxes and insurance |

1. Hold the land and every permit in your own name, never the company's.
2. Keep the company to intangible property and to selling information.
3. Let the company advertise, offer, arrange or bid no construction work for anyone.
4. Take no construction deposit or progress payment through the company, under any label.
5. Treat any advertised paid work on someone else's house as licensable, whatever its size.

[FIGURE: three-lanes, the house, the brand and the company as three columns with what each owns]

**Professional**

Licensure follows the offer rather than the work. Exposure therefore begins with the company's website copy and its replies to comments, before any work exists. A monetized channel that also takes small paid jobs for neighbors stands worse than a silent hobbyist, because its advertising is on the record.

Building in order to sell puts an owner inside the contractor definition. For that business the CCB issues a residential developer endorsement, with a contractor's license, bond and insurance behind it. In its license-application sheet the CCB describes the covered business as one that advertises, offers, bids or arranges to do construction, the plain form of the statute's verbs.

No company can claim the owner exemption, because the exemption names a person at work on that person's own property. Land held in the company's name puts the company inside the definition the day it builds, and a house held that way complicates title, insurance, lending and the eventual sale. Intent to sell, its two presumptions and the four resale clocks are the legal chapter's.

**Code**

```
Offering to do contractor work requires a license before the work.
  A person or joint venture that undertakes, offers to undertake or submits a bid to do work as a contractor must hold a current CCB license with the endorsement for that work.
  Source: ORS 701.021(1).

An owner who builds to sell is a contractor by definition.
  A person that owns property and constructs, or for compensation arranges the construction of, one or more residential structures with the intent of selling them.
  Source: ORS 701.005(5)(b).

Advertising ends the small-job exemption.
  The under-$1,000 exemption for casual, minor or inconsequential work on one structure does not apply to a person who advertises or puts out a sign, card or device suggesting they are a contractor.
  Source: ORS 701.010(4).
```

## 36.2 What the company sells, and what it never says

**Simple**

This company sells knowledge and nothing built. It may sell a manual, a plan set, a course, a sponsorship or an hour of teaching. It may not sell construction, take a deposit toward one, inspect a house for a fee, or size a paying client's footing. That last line separates teaching from engineering. Engineering offered to others needs a license at any price. It also claims nothing it is not: no "architect", no "engineer", no "certified", no "approved". Oregon's consumer law treats a false claim about a product or a credential as an unlawful practice.

**Applied**

Assets the company may own:

- the brand name and marks, the website, the channel, the newsletter and the social accounts
- the rights in the manual, the courses and the templates
- the photograph and video archive, assigned in writing from the person
- the plan library, sold as reference drawings under a single-build license
- the affiliate and sponsorship contracts

What the company never does:

- contracts for, offers, advertises or arranges construction for anyone, or bids on it
- sells "we will manage your build" consulting
- takes a construction deposit or progress payment of any kind
- owns a house, a building permit or land to build on for sale
- sells a plan set described as stamped, approved, code-verified or site-adapted
- gives structural sizes, load answers or engineering advice for a specific third-party project
- performs, offers or advertises home inspection
- uses "architect", "architecture", "architectural", "engineer", "licensed" or "certified" in its name or copy
- answers a specific footing, beam or mix question, in a comment or a paid call

Write every consulting product on the teaching side of the line:

1. Describe principles, this build's documented decisions, and plan review for general code problems.
2. Direct no labor, material or sequence on a client's active jobsite.
3. Tie no fee to the outcome of a client's build.
4. Have an Oregon attorney read the offer's wording before it goes on sale. Put any unclear case to the CCB in writing.

**Professional**

Performing, offering and bidding construction work are the statute's verbs. General teaching sits outside them. Direction of a specific paying client's jobsite sits inside "arranging". On consultants the CCB publishes no rule and answers the question case by case through its licensing line, 503-934-2227. Freestone Built's coaching product runs on the teaching side as plan review, milestones and messaging, with no subcontractor direction and no construction money.

Home inspection is its own certified category in Oregon. Its trigger is a fee for a written report on a house's overall condition. Describing one's own construction on camera is outside it, and a paid walkthrough of a viewer's house is inside it.

Oregon's Unlawful Trade Practices Act reaches goods and services bought for personal, family or household purposes, which covers a plan set or a course sold to a household. Its provision for an information product governs the description of what the product is and who stands behind it. Section 5 of the FTC Act is the federal counterpart, and the disclosure rules apply it.

Both the reserved architecture words and the engineer-title rule, with a table of wording to use instead, are in the legal chapter. By the annotation to the architecture statute, a firm not licensed in Oregon violated the section by indicating on its website that it practiced architecture here (Twist Architecture v. Board of Architect Examiners, 361 Or 507, 2017).

**Code**

```
Home inspection for a fee requires certification and a licensed business.
  No individual may undertake, offer to undertake or bid to do work as a home inspector unless certified by the CCB and an owner of, or employed by, a board-licensed business.
  Source: ORS 701.445(1) and (2).

The title "Oregon certified home inspector" is reserved.
  No person may use the title before receiving the agency's certification, and a business may not advertise inspection services unless properly licensed.
  Source: OAR 812-008-0030(4); OAR 812-008-0201(4).

Misdescribing a product or a credential is an unlawful trade practice.
  Representing that goods or services have sponsorship, approval, characteristics, uses or benefits they do not have, or that a person has a status, qualification or affiliation the person does not have.
  Source: ORS 646.608(1)(e).

Confusion about source, approval, certification or affiliation is separately unlawful.
  Causing a likelihood of confusion as to the source, sponsorship, approval or certification of goods or services, or as to affiliation with or certification by another.
  Source: ORS 646.608(1)(b) and (c); ORS 646.605(6)(a) and (8).
```

## 36.3 Occupancy, a later sale and the repeat build

**Simple**

Living in the finished house is what makes you an owner-builder in the law's eyes. Under the statute, a builder who never moves in is presumed to have meant to sell. By a second rule, a person who offers two newly built houses within three years is presumed to be in the contracting business. Neither sets a waiting period, and no state office promises one. Building, living a while, selling and building again is therefore a sequence of separate decisions. Each is made when circumstances call for it, and each is reviewed by a lawyer first. Meanwhile the full record of the build raises what the house is worth at any sale, and it supplies the brand's material.

**Professional**

Occupancy is a dated event in the CCB's definitions, and the move-in record is what rebuts the presumption. That makes the sale question, with its four clocks, the legal chapter's. Scheduling a portfolio supplies the evidence the second presumption names: repeated offers of newly built structures within one short period. For that reason the plan records no build count, no holding period and no sale target.

Someone who wants the repeat build as a business takes the residential developer endorsement instead, and with it the license, the bond and the insurance of a contractor. House Bill 2800 (2025) amends the exemption statute from July 1, 2027. It changes the professional employer organization terminology in a later subsection and leaves the two owner exemptions and their presumption as they read.

## 36.4 The company's paperwork and its two sets of books

**Simple**

Better Smarter Homes is an Oregon limited liability company with its own bank account, its own card and its own ledger. Nothing the house costs ever runs through that account. No company dollar ever buys anything that ends up in the house. Tax auditors read the same line the licensing board does. Building a house for its owner is a personal expense, and a company that paid for it would look like a developer. So every receipt is coded to the house or to the company on the day it is paid. Money for the owner comes out as a draw on a fixed schedule. Mixed money, called commingling, is evidence against both the exemption and the business deduction.

**Applied**

1. File articles of organization with the Secretary of State ($100). Name a registered agent at a physical Oregon street address.
2. Calendar the $100 annual renewal on the company's anniversary.
3. Register a brand name that differs from the company's as an assumed business name ($50). Renew it every two years.
4. Sign an operating agreement that assigns the pre-formation content to the company. Have it disclaim any company interest in the land or the house.
5. Open a business checking account and card in the company's name. Pay no personal or house expense from them.
6. Code every receipt to the house ledger or the company's on the day of purchase.
7. Pay yourself by a scheduled owner's draw.
8. Log a helper's hours to each ledger the same day when they work on both.
9. Lease the shop or the yard to the company in writing, at a stated rate.
10. Keep the books yourself while they fit a spreadsheet. Then hire a bookkeeper at $99 to $400 a month.
11. Engage a CPA at the first employee, depreciation schedule, IRS notice or second income stream.
12. Ask the construction lender at application how the company's income and expenses enter its underwriting.
13. Register for the corporate activity tax in the year commercial activity first passes $750,000.

**Professional**

For federal tax a single-member company is a disregarded entity by default. Its income lands on the owner's Schedule C and carries self-employment tax, and it can elect corporate treatment later, when the profit justifies a salary. Oregon taxes the pass-through income on the personal return. Oregon's elective pass-through-entity tax lets a partnership or an S corporation deduct state tax at the entity level. By the Department of Revenue's eligibility list it is closed to a single-member company taxed as a proprietorship, so it enters the picture only with a second member or an S-corporation election.

Hobby-loss treatment is the live risk for a channel that runs losses against wage income. Deductions survive only for an activity carried on for profit. Nine factors in the Treasury regulation decide it, with the businesslike manner of the activity and its books first among them (26 CFR 1.183-2(b)). Affiliate, sponsorship, course, coaching and royalty income are all ordinary business income to the company. Camera equipment, a home office and depreciation follow the ordinary rules, for the media business alone.

That discipline comes from the Small Business Administration's guidance for a home-based business, and its reason is evidentiary: splits reconstructed from memory are the pattern an examiner reads as commingling. For a shared asset such as the shop, a written lease between the person and the company at a stated rate is the same discipline applied to space. It is drafted with the attorney and the CPA together, because it touches both the property and the return.

Relay's and OnPay's small-business guides are the source of the bookkeeper band, and they add $25 to $50 an hour for freelance help and 1 to 3 percent of revenue as the rule of thumb. Both place the end of do-it-yourself bookkeeping near $250,000 of revenue or the first hire. They tie the move to a CPA to complexity, with the conversation starting between $100,000 and $250,000. CPA-supervised bookkeeping costs 25 to 40 percent more than a bookkeeper alone (ProAxis CPA's 2026 comparison).

With course, coaching, plan and royalty income, this business reaches that complexity early in its revenue curve. Construction lenders underwrite the person's whole return, so the company's income and losses enter the debt-to-income calculation on the lender's terms. State filing charges come from the Secretary of State's Business Registry fee schedule, and the registered-agent and annual-report duties from the limited liability company act.

**Code**

```
The company keeps a registered agent at a physical Oregon address.
  A registered office at a street address where the agent can be served in person, and never a mail-receiving agency, mail-forwarding business or virtual office.
  Source: ORS 63.111(1).

The annual report is due by the company's anniversary.
  Name and state of organization, registered office and agent, principal office, the managers or at least one member, and the primary business activity, current as of 30 days before the anniversary.
  Source: ORS 63.787(1) and (2).

Commercial activity above $750,000 requires registration, and tax starts above $1 million.
  Register in the year commercial activity first exceeds $750,000, and pay $250 plus 0.57 percent of taxable commercial activity over $1 million.
  Source: ORS 317A.131(1)(a); ORS 317A.125(1) and (2).

Three profitable years in five presume a profit motive.
  An activity whose gross income exceeds its deductions in three or more of five consecutive tax years is presumed engaged in for profit unless the IRS shows otherwise.
  Source: 26 U.S.C. 183(d).
```

## 36.5 Insurance for the company and the property

**Simple**

Your homeowner's or builder's-risk policy covers a house and the people who live in it. Filming for money, hosting paying visitors or running a company from the property can fall outside it. That changes once the insurer is told and agrees. Once it has income or visitors, the company needs its own liability policy. It also needs a media policy for what a general policy leaves out. Those claims are that a video defamed someone, copied someone, or led a viewer to hurt themselves following it. Volunteers who help the company, as against the house, have no workers' compensation available at any price. Oregon's state fund covers volunteers only for public bodies and nonprofits.

**Applied**

1. Tell the property insurer in writing about the filming and the visitors. File the reply.
2. Buy commercial general liability before the company's first dollar of revenue or first visitor.
3. Add media liability for defamation, copyright and "I followed your video" claims.
4. Before selling any block, get a product-liability quote naming "concrete masonry units sold to third parties, structural use".
5. Ask for a second quote for a garden-wall and paver line under its own product description.
6. Price event liability and a participant-injury rider before the first paid workshop.
7. Describe the activity as "hands-on instruction with a hydraulic press and power tools". Ask whether a trade-instruction program fits better than an educator policy.
8. Assign unpaid help to the house side of the ledger, never to the company's.

**Professional**

Homeowner and builder's-risk forms carry exclusions for business activity, and the carrier's written answer to the disclosure is the record that a filmed, visited site stays inside the policy. Commercial general liability covers injury and property damage to people on the site who are not employees. Media liability, sold alone or as an errors-and-omissions endorsement, covers the content claims a general policy excludes.

For a small producer, product liability is written inside the general liability policy more often than as a separate line. Insureon's and TechInsurance's 2026 cost pages put basic small-producer coverage at $500 to $1,500 a year. It rises to $2,000 to $10,000 or more for a higher-risk product or larger revenue. Neither page prices a maker of structural masonry units, so the premium turns on how the underwriter classes "concrete masonry units sold for structural use" against a garden-wall or paver line.

Instructor coverage is priced for lower risks than a press and a saw. ACT Insurance's workshop add-on is $125 a year over a base policy from $24.25 a month, and its example injury is a hot glue gun. Educator liability programs (Insurance Canopy, biBERK) run $108 to $203 a year for $1 million of cover.

Both figures are floors for hands-on instruction with a hydraulic press. Single-event liability for a hands-on event runs $49 to $250 per event (Barter Insurance from $49, ACT Insurance, Events Guardian's 2025 cost guide). Participant-injury coverage adds $50 to $1,000, depending on what participants operate. Insurers require the signed waiver as a condition of cover and treat it as no substitute for it. Only a commercial-lines agent, with the equipment named, can give a bindable quote for a block press in a hands-on class.

SAIF, the state's workers' compensation insurer, writes volunteer coverage only as an opt-in endorsement, so a volunteer injured while working for the company is an ordinary negligence claim with no exclusive-remedy cap. Tool and equipment coverage, the inland marine floater, is the tools chapter's.

## 36.6 Choosing and clearing the name

**Simple**

Nothing is printed with a name until the name is checked. Finding it taken after a year of videos means retitling the year's work. Three checks run in order, and all three are free. First, the federal trademark database shows whether someone already holds the name for similar goods. Second, the state business registry shows whether an Oregon company already uses it. Third, a handle checker shows whether the same name is open on the platforms and as a web domain. Owning the trademark does not win a handle someone else registered first. Filing federally costs $350 per class of goods, and the mark registers only once the name is in use in trade.

**Applied**

1. Search the candidate name at tmsearch.uspto.gov, the USPTO's Trademark Search, with its Basic Search first.
2. Search the Oregon Secretary of State's business registry for the same name and near spellings.
3. Check the domain and the handles with Namechk, KnowEm or Namecheckr.
4. Reject any name whose clean handle or .com is taken, and start again.
5. Register the domain and the handles the day the name clears.
6. File the federal application at $350 per class. Describe the goods from the USPTO's ID Manual to avoid the $200 free-form surcharge.
7. On an intent-to-use filing, budget $150 per class for the statement of use. Budget $125 per class for each six-month extension.
8. Prove use in commerce within six months of the notice of allowance. Extend at most to 36 months in all.
9. Put the registration certificate, with its date and class numbers, in the binder.

**Professional**

On November 30, 2023 the USPTO retired its TESS search system. Its cloud-based replacement, Trademark Search, holds the same database of more than 15 million registered, pending and abandoned federal records. It covers federal filings only. Clearance therefore adds a state registry check, with a separate foreign search for sales abroad, and a state's name availability is not trademark clearance.

Two documented cases show the order of operations. Randy Cassingham, publisher of ThisIsTrue.com, held the trademark and still could not take the matching YouTube username, which an unrelated party had registered first (MarketingSherpa's account). Two founders writing on dev.to built a product for 14 months before learning that the name was another company's registered mark. They put the cost at $15,000, ten weeks and one lost contract, and in both cases the check came after the investment.

Since January 18, 2025 one base fee applies, after the fee rule ended the two-tier application system. Filed on intent to use, a mark registers only after the statement of use proves use in commerce, so the filing date is set against the launch timeline rather than the formation date.

**Code**

```
The federal application fee is set per class.
  $350 per class for an electronic base application, with $100 per class added for an application missing required information and $200 per class for a free-form goods description.
  Source: 37 CFR 2.6(a)(1)(iii), (iv) and (v).

A statement of use is due within six months of the notice of allowance.
  One six-month extension on request, then further extensions for good cause aggregating not more than 24 months, each with a verified statement of continued intent and the fee.
  Source: 15 U.S.C. 1051(d)(1) and (2).

Use filings carry their own fees.
  $150 per class for a statement of use or an amendment to allege use, and $125 per class for each six-month extension request.
  Source: 37 CFR 2.6(a)(2)(ii), (3)(ii) and (4)(ii).
```

## 36.7 Filming, releases and the site's security

**Simple**

Cameras on a construction site record people who did not come to be filmed. Every person who can be recognized signs a release. It says what will be done with the footage, whether it earns money and for how long. For a child, a parent signs. Inspectors, utility workers and delivery drivers are asked first, and nothing an inspector says on camera is presented as an approval. Oregon's recording law adds a separate rule for sound: everyone in an in-person conversation has to be told it is being recorded. Footage goes out weeks after it was shot, with house numbers, road names and permit cards blurred. No video shows an unattended site in real time.

**Applied**

1. Get a signed release from every recognizable person before their footage is published.
2. Write the release to name the subject and the signer. State the uses, the platforms, any commercial use and a duration.
3. For a minor, have a parent or legal guardian sign, both parents where practical. Include a line stating the signer's relationship and authority.
4. Keep the media release separate from any liability waiver.
5. Ask an inspector, utility worker or delivery driver before recording them, and accept a no.
6. Present no inspector's on-camera remark as an approval of anything.
7. Tell everyone present, in words, before recording any in-person conversation, each time.
8. Blur house numbers, road names, permit cards and landmarks. Strip location metadata from every published photograph.
9. Publish site footage weeks behind the work. Show no security-system details, key locations or family schedules.
10. Notify neighbors and film them only with releases. Require an explicit opt-in for children and family members.
11. Keep the raw footage and the signed release behind every published clip.

**Professional**

Oregon's recording statute is the smart-home chapter's, with its all-party notice for in-person conversations, the open-device exception, the one-party rule for phone calls and the camera practice the house uses. On a jobsite the other party in most conversations is a visitor, so an announcement each time replaces the posted sign. Silent video of the site is outside that statute. Audio is what comes off a clip before it leaves the house.

Releases are contracts, and the standard templates agree on their contents. Those templates (LegalZoom's, Wrapbook's and Jotform's forms) name the subject and the signer separately, state the exact uses and platforms and whether the use is commercial, give a duration, and carry a signature block. Minors cannot consent, so a parent or legal guardian signs with a stated relationship and authority, and both parents where practical. Consent to physical risk, the liability waiver, is a different document. Keeping the two documents apart lets a participant refuse the camera and keep their place.

Delay-posting changes when a clip is published and not how often. Whatever schedule the plan sets stays in place, and no engagement cost attaches to the delay itself. Video of a stocked site shows anyone watching what is there and when nobody is, which is the security reason for the delay.

Complaints arrive through a licensing board or an employer as well as a court. Arizona home inspector Cy Porter posted videos of defects found in new homes. Taylor Morrison, the builder, filed a complaint with the state's Board of Technical Registration, seeking discipline and deletion of the videos (2024, reported by 12News and KSDK). That board found no verifiable violation and issued a letter of concern over one clip that paired gas-leak footage with unrelated audio. Raw footage and the release behind each clip are the answer to such a complaint.

## 36.8 Copyright, registration and the licenses

**Simple**

Everything the company makes is copyrighted the moment it is saved, but the remedies that make a lawsuit affordable are not automatic. Registering a work before anyone copies it, or within three months of publication, earns set damages per work and attorney fees against the copier. After both windows the case shrinks to the losses you can prove, which for one plan set may be one sale. Drawings and the design of the house are protected separately. Standard features and anything the code dictates are free to everyone. Once published, a mix recipe cannot be a trade secret. Patenting it would mean clearing the prior art already filed on block presses and stabilizer mixes.

**Applied**

1. Register Stone and Wire and the plan library within three months of first publication. Register before any pre-publication sharing where you can.
2. Use the $45 single-author application for a one-author work, and the $65 standard application otherwise.
3. Register several unpublished drafts or detail sheets together in an $85 group application.
4. Assign every pre-formation photograph, video and text to the company in a written, dated assignment.
5. Keep the plan library under its written single-build license.
6. Give each educational document and code repository a deliberate license, open or proprietary. Never let a repository's default license cover a paid asset.
7. Keep worldwide and all-language rights out of any boilerplate grant-of-rights clause.
8. Before paying for a patent search, write down which refinements, if any, will stay unpublished. Guard only those with a nondisclosure agreement.

**Professional**

Statutory damages run from $750 to $30,000 per work infringed, and up to $150,000 where the infringement is willful (17 U.S.C. 504(c)). With attorney fees, they are what makes a suit over one copied plan set worth filing, which is why registration timing is a launch item.

Of a plan's two protections, the graphic-work copyright covers the drawing and the architectural-work copyright covers the built design. Value in the library therefore sits in the documented system: the block, the wall section and the details this house alone has. Generic sheets a buyer could draw from any code commentary add nothing a court would protect.

Self-published authors keep every subsidiary right, translation rights included. Translation licenses grant a foreign publisher one language and territory for a term of three to seven years, against an advance and royalties (Jane Friedman's foreign-rights guide, the Alliance of Independent Authors' guidance). Nonfiction draws foreign offers at lower domestic sales than fiction (Jane Friedman). Nothing in United States registration has to be structured to keep those rights. One drafting point remains, a grant-of-rights clause in a later contract that quietly covers all languages.

Trade secrecy ends the day a method is published. Once taught in a course or printed in a manual, a mix design is not a secret, and secrecy remains only for a refinement deliberately kept out of print and guarded with agreements (the USPTO's trade-secret policy page). Patents trade disclosure for 20 years of exclusion and need novelty against prior art. That prior art already includes dry-cast block machines (United States patents 8844228 and 9636842) and a fly-ash and sodium-silicate stabilizer dose (8519016). Prosecution takes two to three years and attorney fees, and enforcement depends on detecting and litigating infringement. Copyright in the specific text, drawings and photographs, with the single-use license, is the protection that remains.

**Code**

```
Statutory damages and attorney fees depend on registration timing.
  Neither is available for infringement that begins before registration, unless a published work is registered within three months of its first publication.
  Source: 17 U.S.C. 412.

Registration comes before any infringement suit.
  No civil action for infringement of a United States work until registration or preregistration is made, or until registration is refused and the Register is served with the complaint.
  Source: 17 U.S.C. 411(a).

Plans carry two protections, with standard features excluded.
  Technical drawings and architectural plans as pictorial or graphic works, and the design of the building as an architectural work, which excludes individual standard features.
  Source: 17 U.S.C. 101; 17 U.S.C. 102(a)(5) and (8).

Electronic registration fees are fixed by rule.
  $45 for a single author, same claimant, one work, not for hire, $65 for any other electronic application, and $85 for a group of unpublished works.
  Source: 37 CFR 201.3(c)(1) and (c)(11).
```

## 36.9 Scrapers, copies and takedowns

**Simple**

Two kinds of copying reach a public build channel. One is a person who reposts a video or resells a plan set. Machines are the other, reading the whole site to train a model. Against the person, federal law gives a fixed notice that any platform must act on, built from six set elements. Against the machine, United States law gives no right to opt out yet. So the site declares its refusal where crawlers look, and its hosting company blocks the crawlers that ignore it. Europe's rules bind the model makers, so the declaration is worth making from Oregon. Copyright exists in 182 countries automatically. Enforcing it in any one of them takes a lawyer licensed there.

**Applied**

1. Add robots.txt disallow lines for the AI training crawlers, GPTBot, Google-Extended, CCBot and ClaudeBot among them. Review the list quarterly.
2. Publish a /.well-known/tdmrep.json file or send the tdm-reservation header, with a tdm-policy link.
3. Behind Cloudflare, turn on its AI crawler blocking and managed robots.txt, which the free plan includes.
4. Check YouTube Studio for a third-party AI training setting, and set it for each channel.
5. For a reposted video, file YouTube's copyright-complaint form with the six elements.
6. For a copied plan file on another site, send the six-element notice to that host's agent. Find the agent in the Copyright Office's DMCA directory.
7. When a host will not act, file a Google Search removal request for the page.
8. Send a notice only on a good-faith belief that the material infringes, never over criticism.
9. If a bad-faith notice takes down your own content, file a counter-notice. Expect restoration in 10 to 14 business days.
10. For infringement abroad, start with the platform takedown. Weigh a local lawyer's fee against the sale lost.

**Professional**

Robots.txt directives bind no developer by statute, and whether training on copyrighted text is fair use is being decided case by case in the authors' and publishers' suits. Until then the declarations are voluntary infrastructure with evidentiary value rather than a legal opt-out.

W3C's TDM Reservation Protocol (final community group report, May 10, 2024) gives three forms. One is a /.well-known/tdmrep.json file with location rules. Another is a pair of response headers, tdm-reservation and tdm-policy, which the report prefers. Last is a pair of meta elements in the page head. Each carries a value of 1 for reserved.

That reservation is the machine-readable form Article 4(3) of the EU copyright directive (Directive (EU) 2019/790) recognizes. Article 53(1)(c) of the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) has applied since August 2, 2025, and it requires providers of general-purpose models to put in place a policy to identify and comply with such reservations. Cloudflare's managed robots.txt and AI-crawler blocking, on its free plan since July 2025, enforce at the network edge what the file only requests.

Takedowns work because the platform has an interest of its own. Hosts that act on a compliant notice keep their safe harbor, so YouTube's copyright form and a host's designated agent move faster than a letter from a lawyer. Google Search removal takes the infringing page out of results when the host will not act. Counter-notices carry the same perjury statement and a consent to federal jurisdiction. A notice therefore rests on infringement and never on criticism.

All 182 contracting parties to the Berne Convention (WIPO's treaty list) protect a work from the moment of fixation under national treatment, with no registration anywhere. No international court comes with it. Infringement in another country is litigated in that country's courts by counsel licensed there, at a cost that exceeds most single infringements. Platform takedowns and any United States-reachable point of enforcement are therefore the first tools abroad as well.

**Code**

```
A takedown notice has six required elements.
  A signature of an authorized person, identification of the work, identification and location of the infringing material, contact information, a good-faith statement, and a statement under penalty of perjury of accuracy and authority.
  Source: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(3)(A).

A counter-notice restores the material on a fixed clock.
  The provider replaces removed material not less than 10 nor more than 14 business days after receiving a compliant counter notification, unless the complainant has filed suit.
  Source: 17 U.S.C. 512(g)(2)(C).

A knowing false notice or counter-notice carries damages.
  Any person who knowingly and materially misrepresents that material is infringing, or was removed by mistake, is liable for the resulting damages, costs and attorney's fees.
  Source: 17 U.S.C. 512(f).
```

## 36.10 Disclosure, reviews and sponsors

**Simple**

If money, free product or a relationship stands behind a recommendation, the audience is told, in the video and in the description, in plain words. That is a federal rule. Each post is its own advertisement and needs its own disclosure. Fake reviews, paid-for opinions and bought followers are banned by a second federal rule, with a civil penalty above $53,000 per violation. For a construction channel one rule is the company's own: no sponsor's product replaces a decision the engineer or the specification already made. Those documented decisions are the channel's value to its audience.

**Applied**

1. Disclose every material connection wherever part of the audience would not expect it. Connections include payment, free product, discount, commission, and family or employment ties.
2. Put the disclosure in the content itself, spoken and on screen in video, and in the description.
3. Never rely on a hashtag, a "more" fold or the profile page alone.
4. In a livestream, repeat the disclosure at intervals.
5. Use plain words such as "paid link" instead of tags such as "#sp".
6. Recommend only products you used. Where a result is unusual, state what a typical user gets.
7. Write, buy or solicit no review that misstates who wrote it or what they used.
8. Pay for no review conditioned on its being positive or negative.
9. Disclose your tie on any review by you or your family of the company's products.
10. Buy no followers, views or likes. Send no legal threat over a review you merely dislike.
11. Publish the sponsorship policy that no sponsor product replaces a documented engineering or specification decision.
12. Decline a sponsorship that conflicts with the record before it is announced.

**Professional**

Under the endorsement guides a connection is material when it would change the weight a viewer gives the recommendation. Disclosures 101, the FTC's guidance for influencers, specifies placement where the viewer cannot miss it, because a platform's disclosure tool alone does not reach the viewer who never opens it. A disclosure in an earlier video covers nothing in the next.

On October 21, 2024 the consumer-review rule took effect as a trade regulation rule. That status is what makes its violations penalty-bearing under Section 5(m)(1)(A) of the FTC Act without a prior order. Fake reviews have little to do with an original construction channel until it sells a course or a plan set. Then the testimonials on the sales page, the owner's own review of a tool, and any review a family member posts all fall under it.

Sponsorship policy exists because the conflict cannot be managed after the fact. Pepsi's 1989 cancellation of a $5 million Madonna sponsorship, and the creator-brand splits since then (the James Charles and Morphe parting, in Phyllo's case collection), were handled after the conflict became public. So the policy is written and posted before the first sponsor signs, and a sponsor that would require a product the record rejected is declined at the proposal.

**Code**

```
A material connection behind an endorsement is disclosed.
  A connection that might materially affect the weight or credibility of the endorsement, and that the audience would not reasonably expect, is disclosed clearly and conspicuously.
  Source: 16 CFR 255.5(a).

An endorsement that is not typical discloses the generally expected result.
  Where an endorser's experience with a central attribute is not substantiated as typical, the advertisement clearly and conspicuously discloses the generally expected performance.
  Source: 16 CFR 255.2(b).

Fake reviews and bought sentiment are prohibited.
  No writing, selling or buying of a review that misrepresents the reviewer's existence or experience, and no compensation conditioned on a review's sentiment, positive or negative.
  Source: 16 CFR 465.2; 16 CFR 465.4.

Insider reviews are disclosed, and review suppression and fake influence are prohibited.
  An officer's or manager's review discloses the relationship, no groundless legal threat or false accusation to remove a review, and no selling or buying of fake followers, views or likes.
  Source: 16 CFR 465.5; 16 CFR 465.7; 16 CFR 465.8.

Each violation of a trade rule carries a civil penalty.
  Up to $53,088 per violation for penalties assessed after January 17, 2025.
  Source: 16 CFR 1.98(d) and (e); 15 U.S.C. 45(m)(1)(A).
```

## 36.11 Platforms and the revenue mechanisms

**Simple**

Channels earn nothing until they clear the platform's thresholds, which 3 in 100 reach, by RecurPost's count. On YouTube that means a thousand subscribers and either 4,000 hours watched in a year or ten million short-video views in 90 days. After that, advertising pays by the thousand views, at rates the platform sets and changes. Sponsors, affiliate links, memberships and products are the other mechanisms, and each costs a different amount of work for a different risk. Memberships need new content every month to justify the fee, which a single house cannot supply once it is finished. Pre-sales and one-time products fit a build that ends.

**Applied**

| Mechanism | What it needs first | Effort | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform advertising | 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days | Low per video once eligible | Demonetization, rate changes, policy strikes |
| Sponsorship | An audience a brand wants, and a published policy | Negotiation per deal | A conflict with the documented record |
| Affiliate links | Disclosure on every post | Low | Program terms change, small amounts |
| Membership or Patreon | A monthly reason to keep paying | Continuous | Ends with the build |
| Pre-sale or crowdfunding | An existing list and priced tiers | One campaign | Delivering what was promised |
| Plans, courses, the manual | A finished product | Front-loaded | Pricing and refunds |
| Education-only consulting | The licensing line held | Hours for money | Drifting into project direction |
| Magazine articles | A finished, photographed project | Per article | Modest pay |

1. Make the website and the email list the home, and each platform a distributor.
2. Export the subscriber list and back up the content masters on a schedule.
3. Before applying to the partner program, turn on 2-Step Verification and link an AdSense account. Clear any Community Guidelines strike.
4. Review YouTube's updated partner terms before January 31, 2027, when the program changes.
5. Price any paid tier against the eventual retail product. Put a file tier near the ebook price and a print tier near the paperback.
6. On a demonetization notice, file the appeal within 21 days. Change what the notice named, then reapply after the required interval.
7. Treat a membership as a product with an end date. Plan its close or its pivot at the house's completion.

**Professional**

YouTube's partner-program page sets the two thresholds and the account conditions. It announces program changes for early 2027, with updated terms to accept beforehand. In July 2026 the same page described review as about 30 days, with reapplication after 30 days, or 90 after a second rejection.

Creator-tool blogs (Lenostube, Miraflow, Outlierkit) converge on $4 to $15 CPM across all content, without publishing a method. RPM, the creator's take per thousand views after the platform's share and the unmonetized views, runs well below it. Graphtreon's aggregated Patreon statistics convert 50 patrons to about $460 a month, 200 to $1,800 and 500 to $4,600. Its maker category spreads from $25 to $120 a month at a growth stage to $180 to $860 at a professional one, and it has no construction category. Those figures price continuous content, and a single-house channel either pivots at completion or sells complete products instead.

In July 2026 Kickstarter's own statistics page put the share of launched projects reaching their goal at 42 percent, with wide variation by category. Most pledges come from a creator's existing audience. Pre-sale campaigns for a manual or a plan library are therefore sized to the first print run, the editing and the cover, with tiers priced near the eventual retail products, and launched to an existing list.

Demonetized channels appeal through YouTube's own tool within a deadline. Lenostube's guide puts the odds near 30 to 40 percent for prompt appeals. One channel owner's Medium account describes a 180,000-subscriber compilation channel suspended for reused content, with a first appeal refused. Reinstatement came about 90 days later, after the owner added original commentary and cut the weakest uploads, at 44 percent of the earlier revenue three months on. Original footage of a documented build is the opposite of the reused-content profile that triggered it.

Open Source Ecology's compilation counted about 50,000 permitted owner-built homes in 2014, against 1.5 million housing starts. It excludes unpermitted rural building and is a decade old, so it is a floor and not a market estimate.

## 36.12 The plan library

**Simple**

Oregon lets a person who is not an architect draw plans for a single-family house. Selling the drawings of this house as reference plans is therefore lawful. Two conditions hold the product inside that permission. First, the plans are sold unstamped, as reference material that no one has checked against the buyer's soil, climate or local code. Buyers are told so in plain words. And the company never offers to adapt them to a buyer's site, because that is engineering done for others. Value in the library lies in the documented system: the block, the wall section and every detail that is this house's own. Standard features, and whatever the code itself dictates, belong to no one.

**Applied**

1. Sell each plan set under a written single-build license: one dwelling, no resale or redistribution, modifications named.
2. State on the cover sheet that the documents are unstamped reference material, unverified against any site, soil, climate or code.
3. State that a stamp jurisdiction will require the buyer to hire a local licensed professional.
4. State which layouts are omitted, mechanical, electrical and plumbing, and that the buyer produces them.
5. Tell out-of-state buyers to check their own state's design-professional and permit rules.
6. Get the engineer of record's written license before selling any sheet derived from the engineer's drawings.
7. Offer three tiers, a single detail sheet, a topic bundle and the complete set.
8. Price the complete set against stock-plan sellers' current prices, read the same week.
9. Price a bundle at 15 to 20 percent below the sum of its sheets. After each change, check profit per order, not order size.
10. Test a tier with a landing page and real pre-orders before drawing the rest. Treat 10 to 20 pre-sales as demand.
11. Add a deviation clause to every sheet that states a mix, a press setting or a cure. Any deviation voids the tested-performance statements.

**Professional**

Reference sets drawn from this house stay outside the engineering act's registration, which turns on work done for others, as long as they are not adapted to a third party's site. Both the architecture exemption for single-family plans and the three reserved words are in the legal chapter.

Stock-plan sellers supply the packaging for the product. Donald A. Gardner's copyright and disclaimer page and HPA Design's license page state that a stock plan carries no professional stamp. They put code compliance and any required local stamp on the buyer, tell a buyer in a stamp jurisdiction to hire a local professional, and license one build per purchase with the modification rights defined. "Approved construction documents" is a promise no seller can make.

Those sellers price by square footage and set completeness. Full sets run from the low hundreds to the low thousands of dollars by house size and license scope, against $700 to $10,000 for a custom drafted plan (HomeGuide's 2026 cost guide).

None of them lists a single detail sheet as a product, so the first tier is this plan's own experiment and is priced by test. EightX, a direct-to-consumer pricing consultancy, sets the bundle discount where the saving feels real to the buyer and the margin survives. It puts bundle-driven order-value gains at 15 to 30 percent, with mix-and-match bundles at the top. Its worked example shows a deeper bundle cutting profit dollars per order by more than 12 points, so the measure after any price change is profit per order.

Van Westendorp's price sensitivity meter (1976) asks prospective buyers four prices: too expensive, expensive but acceptable, a bargain, and so cheap the quality is doubted. It plots the acceptable corridor, and it suits a product with no established comparable.

Creator-economy practice is a landing page with a real buy or reserve button. Matt Giaro's and LearnyBox's course-presale guides read a small run of real pre-sales as demand, and a weak response as a pricing or targeting fault. Whether narrow single-sheet PDFs sell in volume in a maker niche is untested, so that tier opens as a test. Every sheet that states a mix, a press pressure or a cure carries the deviation clause. Sold paper describes a physical process the buyer will carry out, and the clause is the paper half of the product-liability posture.

**Code**

```
Engineering is regulated when it is done for others.
  The practice of engineering or professional engineering means the listed services when done for others.
  Source: ORS 672.005(1).

Drawings for a single-family dwelling or farm building need no engineering registration.
  Drawings, specifications or supervision of construction for a single-family residential dwelling or farm building, with its accessory buildings, by a person who is not a registered engineer.
  Source: ORS 672.060(8).

Two more exemptions cover small buildings and one's own property.
  Buildings of 4,000 square feet of ground area or less, and work by a person or firm that affects exclusively its own property or interests, unless public or employee safety is affected.
  Source: ORS 672.060(7) and (2).
```

## 36.13 The book

**Simple**

Printing a technical manual this size happens one copy at a time until it has proved it sells. Print-on-demand costs more per copy and nothing up front. Offset runs cost a third as much per copy, paid in advance. Amazon's print service pays a share of the cover price minus the printing cost. Its catalog partner reaches bookstores and libraries at a deeper discount. Before any of that, an editor, a cover designer and a layout artist are paid by the word, the project and the page. Diagrams and code citations make a manual cost more to edit than the same number of words of story. Two magazines pay for construction stories from an owner's own build.

**Applied**

1. Launch on print-on-demand through KDP and IngramSpark, with no inventory.
2. Price the paperback at $9.99 or above on Amazon. Below that KDP's royalty rate drops from 60 percent of list price to 50.
3. Run KDP's printing-cost calculator at the real page count before setting the list price.
4. Set IngramSpark's wholesale discount at 55 percent with returns on for bookstore and library shelves. Set 35 to 50 percent for online-only distribution.
5. Move a title to offset only once a year's steady sales pass the break-even count.
6. Budget 3 to 4 cents a word for copyediting and 4 to 5 for developmental editing.
7. Budget 2 to 3 cents a word for proofreading and 2 to 2.5 for an index.
8. Budget $625 to $1,250 for the cover and $500 and up for an illustrated interior layout.
9. Before paying for layout, test the price with a landing page and pre-orders. A four-question price survey to the list is the alternative.
10. Pitch Fine Homebuilding with an outline, a project description and photographs, at $150 a published page.
11. Pitch Country Lore at Mother Earth News at $25 to $100 an item, print only.
12. Keep digital, print, translation and audio rights unassigned to any single distributor.

**Professional**

KDP's royalty formula makes the printing cost the lever. That cost is subtracted from the royalty, rises with page count and a color interior, and sets the minimum list price, so an illustrated technical manual prices higher than a text paperback of the same length. Expanded Distribution to other retailers and libraries pays a lower rate on the same formula. Ebook royalty is 35 percent anywhere, or 70 percent less a delivery cost that averages $0.06 a unit and rises with file size.

IngramSpark pays the list price less the wholesale discount, the print cost and a market access fee on wholesale-channel sales. Its March 10, 2020 price sheet put a black-and-white interior at 1.3 cents a page and premium color at 6.9 cents, and a February 1, 2026 sheet has since raised each.

PRC Book Printing's guide puts print-on-demand at $8 to $12 a copy for a 300-page 6-by-9 paperback at any quantity. Offset runs $3 to $4 a copy at 500 copies and $2.20 to $2.80 at 1,000. Break-even sits near 200 to 300 copies for that book and 300 to 400 for a full-color hardcover. Offset also offers the paper stocks, bindings and color fidelity that print-on-demand does not match, which matters for diagram legibility, and it takes 3 to 6 weeks from file approval against 2 to 7 business days. Where diagram density makes offset necessary rather than cheaper is a quality judgment priced per run.

Per-word medians come from the Editorial Freelancers Association's 2025 rate survey. At its copyediting median every hundred thousand words costs $3,000 to $4,000 before the citation checking that a code-heavy manual adds, a service the survey does not price. Reedsy's 2025 data put the average cover at $880 across about 9,600 projects, with illustrated or art-directed covers at $900 to $1,500 and up. Its self-publishing cost guide places half of interior-layout projects between $480 and $1,300.

Fine Homebuilding buys a submission as an outline, a project description and photographs. Mother Earth News pays only for print and assigns features to its contributing editors, with occasional freelance assignments. Grit and Off Grid Magazine take homestead and off-grid pieces without published rates. In The Freelance Writing Network's 2026 roundup, 37 home-and-garden markets pay up to $2 a word for design features, a ceiling for that market rather than a construction rate.

O'Reilly sells one subject at two depths as two named series. Head First (from Kathy Sierra and Bert Bates's Head First Java, 2003) serves the reader starting from nothing, In a Nutshell is the dense desk reference, and a buyer self-selects by series. Nothing in that catalog is a single manual with labeled layers for several readers. Its layering is therefore the claim its marketing makes, and the series precedent is the evidence that readers at different depths buy the same subject.

## 36.14 Courses and coaching

**Simple**

Courses sell the method of this build to someone building their own, and a coaching call sells an hour of it. Both stay on the teaching side of the licensing line, under the same limits as any consulting. Hands-on skills such as pressing block are taught best live. Cohort courses with a live session finish at 85 to 90 percent, and self-paced video at 10 to 15 percent. Platform rent runs from $39 to $499 a month. Moving platforms later costs more than any month's fee.

**Applied**

| Platform and plan | Monthly price (annual billing) | Platform fee | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teachable Starter | $39 ($29) | 7.5 percent | 5 products |
| Teachable Builder | $89 ($69) | None with Teachable Payments | 10 products |
| Teachable Growth | $189 ($139) | None with Teachable Payments | 50 products |
| Thinkific Start | $109 ($82) | None, 2.9 percent processing | 10,000 students |
| Thinkific Grow | $219 ($164) | None, 2.9 percent processing | 10,000 students |
| Kajabi Basic | $179 ($143) | 2.9 percent plus $0.30 processing | 5 products, 2,500 contacts |
| Kajabi Growth | $249 ($199) | 2.8 percent plus $0.30 processing | 50 products, 25,000 contacts |

1. Choose the platform after a trial, for the courses planned three years out. Expect never to switch.
2. Price a mini-course of under five hours at $47 to $197.
3. Price a five-to-fifteen-hour core course with community access at $297 to $997.
4. Run the block-pressing course as a cohort with a live session. Keep self-paced video for the theory.
5. Write every coaching offer as plan review, milestones and questions answered from this build. Direct no client's subcontractors and take no construction money.
6. Price coaching as a monthly subscription with a minimum term. Freestone Built charges $799 a month with a three-month minimum.
7. Ask the platform in writing whether it collects sales tax on your courses as a facilitator.
8. Test a coaching offer on the existing list before building its materials.

**Professional**

Freestone Built's 1-on-1 Build Coaching is the working model. It is a recurring subscription with a minimum term, built from plan review, a milestone roadmap with checklists and messaging through an app, and it touches no construction cost, subcontractor payment or financing. Tom Landis's Owner Builder Coach is a comparable single-coach service. UBuildIt runs the same idea as a franchise, with local construction consultants and prescreened subcontractors, and claims 15 to 30 percent savings against a general contractor. Neither specializes in concrete or masonry, so a block-and-concrete coaching product would be a new niche rather than a copy.

Among schools, the Auroville Earth Institute is the oldest precedent for selling the method itself. Its six-day CSEB Production course runs 9 AM to 6 PM. Two days of theory cover soil identification, stabilization, blockyard organization, quality control and cost analysis. Four days of hands-on work cover pressing, press maintenance and field testing, with the fee given on application. Its curriculum already includes the economic-feasibility module a block-making business course would need.

BuddyBoss's and FreshLearn's course-pricing guides supply the price bands and the completion figures. Both find that buyers who choose on price alone refund and abandon more often, which is the case against a discount launch. Within three months of 2026 the platforms changed their terms: Thinkific dropped its lowest plan, and Teachable removed its student caps. Kajabi's higher floor buys an integrated marketing, funnel and email suite that a single narrow course does not need. DreamGrow's and Jenna Kutcher's accounts of creators switching between Teachable, Thinkific and Kajabi describe re-uploading every video, rebuilding landing pages and automations, and a revenue dip from broken links. That is why the first choice is made for the three-year product line.

In The Creator Playbook's telling, a newsletter with 500 subscribers earned $6,000 from a coaching offer while one with 4,000 earned under $1,000 a month. It is a self-published, uncorroborated account by a seller of newsletter advice, so it is a reason to test a coaching offer on the existing list and not a forecast. Live and recorded delivery are taxed differently in many buyers' states.

## 36.15 Workshops on the property

**Simple**

Under the law, a paying visitor pressing block in your yard is a customer. Customers are covered by your liability insurance and a signed waiver, never by workers' compensation. Paid helpers on the company's side are employees unless they run a real independent business. Silica rules that only advised you as a solo builder bind you in full the day an employee breathes the same dust. On farm or forest land a workshop is a home occupation, with a cap on staff and no new building allowed for it. Before anyone is paid to teach, a one-day workshop costs an insurance premium and the cement, aggregate and protective gear for four to eight people.

**Applied**

1. Before the first workshop, get the county planning department's written confirmation that a paying-visitor home occupation is allowed, with its terms.
2. Bind the event policy and the participant-injury rider before setting the ticket price.
3. Have every participant sign an activity-specific liability waiver before touching a press or a saw.
4. Give a safety briefing and issue a P100 respirator and eye protection before any participant handles a tool. Run every cut wet.
5. Apply the silica standard's specified controls to every workshop task, participant or employee alike.
6. Budget $500 to $1,500 of cement, aggregate and protective equipment for four to eight people.
7. Set the ticket price against the 2022 residential cob-course rate, $100 a participant-day. That rate included meals and camping.
8. Pay any company-side helper only as an employee with workers' compensation in place. Otherwise require a contractor who meets the independent-business tests.
9. Keep volunteers off the company's side entirely.
10. Put up no new building for a workshop on farm or forest land.

**Professional**

Three relationships on a workshop day carry three different rules. Paying participants have no employment relationship with the host. Premises liability and negligence are therefore the host's duty, met by the safety briefing, guarded equipment, protective gear and the waiver. Courts do not enforce a waiver against gross negligence or a failure to warn of a known hazard (WRS Law's waiver guidance, and the waivers that operating workshops such as AR Workshop post).

Oregon's casual-labor exception is written for work outside the employer's trade or business, which a workshop is not, so it shelters no workshop helper, and the independent-contractor tests decide the rest. Volunteers for the company have no coverage to buy, as SAIF's rule states, which is why unpaid help stays on the house.

OSHA's authority runs entirely through the employer-employee relationship. Its May 23, 2001 interpretation letter states that a self-employed person with no employees cannot be cited for their own exposure. The first employee brings the whole standard, and no reduced tier exists for occasional or short work. Oregon enforces its own rule, OAR 437-002-1053 to 437-002-1065, which covers construction in the same rule as general industry.

Participants are outside the standard, since no employment relationship exists, and inside the host's duty of care. So the same controls apply to them on the premises-liability footing, and a waiver does not reduce the dust.

Costs come from workshop-hosting guides and natural-building schools. Milieu Studio's workshop guide is the source of the consumables band, and it puts insurance and permits at $300 to $700 and rented space at $50 to $500 an hour. On the owner's own site, parking, a restroom, weather cover and staging replace the rented space. Cob Hill Natural Building School's 10-day course of September 2022 cost $1,000, with three plant-based meals a day and tent camping. Auroville's course runs six days. With no lodging or meals, a one-day block workshop sits below those per-day rates. Once a date is set, the owner's real local figures for insurance, consumables and site preparation replace the proxies.

On farm or forest land the home-occupation statute caps the on-site staff, confines the business to the dwelling or the buildings that go with the zone's permitted uses, and bars a new building put up for it. That statute, the LUBA decision on a purpose-built events building and the local size caps are the land chapter's.

**Code**

```
Oregon's silica rule covers construction work.
  All occupational exposures to respirable crystalline silica in general industry and construction, except where exposure stays below 25 micrograms per cubic meter as an 8-hour average under any foreseeable conditions.
  Source: OAR 437-002-1053.

An employer follows Table 1 or measures the exposure.
  For each employee on a Table 1 task, the engineering controls, work practices and respiratory protection the table specifies, unless the employer assesses and limits exposure under the exposure-assessment paragraph.
  Source: 29 CFR 1926.1153(c)(1).

An employer with silica exposure keeps a written exposure control plan.
  A description of the exposing tasks, the controls and respiratory protection for each, the housekeeping measures and the access restrictions.
  Source: 29 CFR 1926.1153(g)(1).

A helper is an independent contractor only on the statutory tests.
  Free from direction and control over means and manner, customarily engaged in an independently established business (any three of five listed marks), licensed where the work requires it, and responsible for other licenses.
  Source: ORS 670.600(2) and (3).
```

## 36.16 Selling block, or selling the method

**Simple**

Block is heavy and low in price, so it does not travel far. One concrete block weighs about 40 pounds and sells delivered for $2 to $3. Suppliers charge extra for every mile past a short base radius, which puts a one-person yard's market at pickup distance. Selling a structural block to a stranger also turns one lab test into a test of every batch. Selling the method instead leaves the buyer as the maker of their own block. That method is the mix, the press settings and the cure, written down and licensed. In 1991 a federal appeals court held that the words in a how-to book are not a product, after a mushroom guide was sued.

**Applied**

1. Before any sale, compute the delivered cost per unit. Count materials, labor, the press and molds spread over their output, curing-yard time, pallets and hauling.
2. Sell at pickup, or within the radius where truck and fuel still leave a margin.
3. Represent no unit as structural unless every production lot is tested to the masonry-unit standard. Keep the reports by lot.
4. Quote a testing lab for a per-lot sample set before pricing structural block at all.
5. Test pavers and garden-wall units to their own standards, and claim no load-bearing use.
6. Write any sold mix design as a licensed document stating the ratio, press pressure, moisture target, cure, and the deviations that void it.
7. Keep the house's alternative-materials approval, test reports and conditions as an evidence package for a later builder.
8. Register the business as a seller of materials. Treat the home-occupation caps as the yard's limit.
9. Ask the county and DEQ whether a yard at your volume needs an air-quality permit.
10. Buy product liability before the first unit leaves the property.

**Professional**

CountBricks' and BuilderMuse's 2026 price compilations put a standard 8-by-8-by-16 unit at $1.60 to $2.98 delivered. Suppliers price delivery as a flat or low rate inside a base radius of 15 to 25 miles and $0.50 to $3 a mile beyond it, with some quoting $10 to $25 a mile (HomeGuide, Concrete Captain and Heavy Haulers). Freight is a large share of a unit's price at that weight and price. With neither the plant's volume discount nor its fleet, a one-person yard's radius is shorter still.

Grokipedia's page, drawing on Open Source Ecology's cost data, puts finished stabilized earth block at $2.95 to $3.45 a unit, parity with concrete block rather than a pass-through of its lower material cost. AECT's purchase page lists no price and refers buyers to its regional producers (Colorado Earth, Anteros Earth Block, Parabellum Constructors), so a wholesale figure comes from a direct quote. For the materials-and-labor cost band, the house's own test program and the alternative-materials path, the owner-made blocks chapter is the source. Business cost per unit adds the amortized press and molds, the curing-yard time, packaging and delivery to that band, once the mix and press are final.

Compliance with the masonry-unit specification is claimed lot by lot, and its test method samples each lot, six units per sample in the common reading, three for dimensions, absorption and density and three for compression. Each sample set costs a laboratory's per-sample price, obtained by quote.

ICC-ES evaluation reports are the nationally reusable listing a manufacturer buys for a product sold into many jurisdictions. ICC-ES quotes fees per application after a statement of work, its Evaluation Committee hears acceptance criteria three times a year, and the time depends on the product. It is the cost of entering the block business rather than an add-on to the house's one approval.

Approval granted for this house under the alternative-materials rule binds no other official. Only the statewide route, an Alternate Method Ruling from the Building Codes Division, binds every Oregon building department within its scope (OAR 918-008-0110). Short of it, the house's test reports, the official's written conditions and the inspection records are persuasive evidence a later builder can present under their own official's authority to accept certified reports.

Pavers and segmental retaining-wall units are specified by ASTM C936 and ASTM C1372 rather than the loadbearing-unit specification. Such a line therefore leaves the structural-masonry regime and keeps ordinary product liability for property damage, with its own testing and, for retaining walls above a jurisdiction's height trigger, its own engineering review. Bringing such a line to market is a separate mold investment plus that testing, priced only by quote.

Watershed Materials of Napa began developing a low-cement structural unit in 2011 on a $740,000 National Science Foundation grant for a machine to mass-produce structural masonry. It claimed up to 50 percent less cement and 65 percent less embodied energy than conventional block, and its company domain is parked for sale in 2026. Product businesses in alternative masonry are grant-scale capital and a decade of machine engineering, with that company's current status unconfirmed.

AECT sells portable presses and bundles a week of on-site training, and British suppliers (Earth Blocks, the Cob Specialist) hire them out. Pressing as a service, in which the press travels to the client's soil and no block is shipped, would avoid the freight problem and leave the client as the maker of the units. That service is an untested business rather than a precedent.

Selling the documented mix and process as a licensed recipe, with no physical unit sold, shifts the testing burden to the buyer who makes the block and narrows the liability. In Winter v. G.P. Putnam's Sons (938 F.2d 1033, 9th Cir. 1991) the Ninth Circuit held that the ideas and expression in a book are not a product for strict products liability, distinguishing a navigational chart, while leaving negligence theories open. One circuit's rule binds only its own courts, so the license's disclaimers, indemnity and choice-of-law clauses are the attorney's draft.

That same license document also carries the deviation clause. In product-liability law the misuse defense is strongest where the instructions are specific enough that a deviation can be proved. It is stronger again where the foreseeable deviations are named and warned against: a wrong stabilizer ratio, a lower compaction pressure, a short cure or a substituted material (Fox and Farley, In Compliance magazine).

A yard that only sells block and never lays it needs no CCB license at any volume, and ordinary business registration, the corporate activity tax above its threshold and any county business license remain. At some production volume a press and mixer may need an air-quality permit, which DEQ's Air Quality program sets. On resource land the home-occupation caps apply to the yard as they do to a workshop.

**Code**

```
Selling a finished material without installing it needs no contractor license.
  A person who furnishes materials, supplies, equipment or finished product and does not fabricate them into, or consume them in, the work of a contractor is exempt from licensure.
  Source: ORS 701.010(3).
```

## 36.17 Sales tax across state and national lines

**Simple**

Oregon has no sales tax, so a workshop taught here collects none. Selling a plan file or a recorded course online moves the question to the buyer's state. Most states tax a downloaded file or a recording as a digital good and leave a live class alone as a service. Each state's rules reach a seller only once sales into that state pass a threshold the state sets. Marketplaces that collect the tax themselves remove the burden for sales made through them, and for nothing sold elsewhere. One sale of a file to a buyer in the European Union makes the seller liable for that country's VAT.

**Applied**

1. Track sales by buyer's state from the first sale.
2. For any state approaching $100,000 in a year, check its tax on a PDF plan, a recorded course and a live call separately.
3. Repeat the check each year, since both lists change.
4. Ask a platform in writing whether it remits sales tax for your products before choosing it.
5. Treat every sale outside a facilitating platform as your own tax obligation. Your own site, a Stripe checkout and an invoiced call all count.
6. Once sales reach several states, track nexus and taxability with TaxJar, Stripe Tax or Avalara.
7. Before the first digital sale to an EU buyer, register in the non-Union One Stop Shop or sell through a merchant-of-record platform.
8. Screen international buyers against the OFAC sanctions list, as any US seller must.

**Professional**

Since the Supreme Court's Wayfair decision (South Dakota v. Wayfair, 585 U.S. 162, 2018), a state may tax a remote seller on economic presence alone, and the states converged on a revenue threshold. Avalara's 2026 state guide counts 41 states at the common sales threshold, 14 of them with a 200-transaction alternative. Alabama and Mississippi sit at $250,000, and California, New York and Texas at $500,000. Transaction tests are being dropped (Illinois on January 1, 2026, Utah on July 1, 2025). Nexus is measured per state, so it turns on sales into one state and never on national revenue.

Digital-goods taxability is the second layer, and it is not uniform. More than 30 states tax digital products and more than 20 tax software as a service. Maine added digital audiovisual and audio works on January 1, 2026, and some states exempt educational content or have no digital-goods category. One state can therefore treat a PDF plan, a recorded course and a live call three ways (Avalara, the Sales Tax Institute, TaxCloud). Streaming with live interaction keeps a class in the service category, by Avalara's, TaxJar's and Thinkific's compliance guides.

Marketplace-facilitator laws move collection to the platform for the sales it facilitates. Etsy calculates, collects and remits in the 45 sales-tax states plus the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico. Teachable's support article, read in July 2026, describes the same role for course sales through it. Gumroad's and Podia's positions are unconfirmed and are asked before any commitment.

For a seller outside the European Union with no EU establishment, the VAT due on a digital product is the customer's country's, collected through the non-Union One Stop Shop with one registration in one member state and quarterly returns. The Union's EUR 10,000 threshold is a place-of-supply rule for suppliers established inside the Union (the European Commission's OSS guidance).

Britain and Australia run separate regimes with their own registration rules, Australia's at A$75,000 of GST turnover (Avalara's global guide), and a platform acting as merchant of record absorbs the registrations. Residential plans and a mix design are published civilian information outside the Export Administration Regulations' controlled technology (the Bureau of Industry and Security's EAR pages, UC Santa Barbara's export-control FAQ). Sanctions screening against the OFAC list applies to any international seller.

## 36.18 Expectations, the long build and the brand after the house

**Simple**

Most channels never pay their owner, and the ones that do took years. Kirsten Dirksen's faircompanies, which has filmed small homes since 2007, went five years before its videos earned money. Whatever the channel earns, a documented build still produces a searchable record, a credible name and an audience that trusts a citation. Pace comes from the build, because a stalled pour is a stalled channel. Burnout is the usual reason a channel stops. When the house is finished, the channel ends, turns to teaching, or follows the people to the next project. Named for the people, a brand carries its audience along, as Odd Life Crafting's did from a container house to a sailboat.

**Applied**

1. Publish on a cadence the build can keep in its slowest month. Promise no fixed weekly schedule.
2. Keep the website and the mailing list as the home base. Export the list monthly.
3. Write the brand's name and description around the people and the method, never the house.
4. Decide before completion whether the channel closes, teaches, or follows a next documented project.
5. Post a moderation policy. Answer every footing, beam or mix question about a specific house with "hire a local professional".
6. Publish a dated correction on the page of any video or post found wrong. Keep a corrections log.
7. Keep a text-and-photo site beside the video channel as the searchable record.

**Professional**

In this niche the one dated income timeline is faircompanies' own, by its bio page. Kelly Hart and Owen Geiger's Earthbag Building Blog has run for more than a decade as a text-and-photo archive. It stays searchable and linkable per detail, which an unmaintained video channel does not. At permies.com's natural-building forums, builders compare methods and voice regret in public. One video, "Earthbag Construction Fail!" (My Little Podcast, 2017), shows a failure format with an audience of its own. Regret voiced in those forums concerns the building method chosen rather than the recording format.

Vibely's November 2021 survey of 150 creators found that 90 percent had experienced burnout and 71 percent had considered quitting, with algorithm changes the top stressor. Vibely sells a creator-community product. Tom Scott posted weekly toward a ten-year goal and stopped at it rather than build a managed team, and MatPat cited a decade of work-first priorities in stepping back. One multi-year build compounds that risk, because its delays cannot be edited out. Creators who finished, in the accounts collected by Love Thy Nerd and Study Breaks, treat consistency as more important than frequency and promise no cadence the project cannot keep. Burnout's signs and the winter lull are the body-and-mind chapter's.

Every single-house build ends on a date the channel can see coming. Its knowledge product is therefore a complete package priced once rather than a monthly drip, and the channel's brand decides what happens at completion. Odd Life Crafting, a Brazilian couple, documented the build of their shipping-container house. They then carried the same 238,000-subscriber audience into the refit of a 44-foot steel sailboat, a year and a half of sailing, and a new 43-foot aluminum build in France. Their positioning was the couple's ongoing projects and never the house.

Cob Cottage Company, started by Ianto Evans and Linda Smiley, describes itself as a loose collective and has taught for decades past its founders' day-to-day involvement. Quail Springs Permaculture kept its natural-building program running after Sasha Rabin, who led it for about a decade, moved on. Institutions survive a departure and transfer as curriculum, land and a teaching roster. Founder brands move faster, sell coaching on personal trust, and transfer with difficulty, because their asset is the audience's relationship with one person. That comparison is structural rather than priced.

Moderation policy, the redirect of every site-specific safety question to a local professional, and a dated correction on the page of any video found wrong are the channel's version of the citation discipline. That log is a record a sponsor or a complainant can be shown.
