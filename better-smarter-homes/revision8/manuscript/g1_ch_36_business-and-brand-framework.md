# Chapter 36: Business and Brand Framework

In Oregon a company that offers, advertises, bids or arranges construction for another person needs a contractor's license before any work starts, and the offer itself is the licensable act. So the land, the permits and the house stay in Jon's name under the owner-builder exemption, and Better Smarter Homes LLC owns only the intangible property: the plans, the writing, the recordings and the name. A company that owned the land, or that built to sell, would be a contractor by definition. The owner-builder exemption, the resale clocks and the words the company may not use in public are the legal chapter's. Costs are 2026 figures unless a different year is given.

## 36.1 Where the licensing line falls

**Simple**

Oregon licenses contractors by what they offer to do. The moment a business advertises, bids or arranges construction for someone else, it needs a contractor's license, before the first hour of work. You may build your own house without one, because the law exempts an owner working on their own property. That exemption covers a person, never a company. So the land, the permits and the house stay in your name. The company owns nothing physical: the plans, the recordings, the writing and the name. A company that owns land and builds on it to sell is a contractor under the statute's own definition.

**Applied**

| Lane | What it holds |
|---|---|
| The house | Land, permits, construction, occupancy, maintenance and any sale decision |
| The brand | Recordings, writing, photographs, drawings, lessons and the audience |
| The company | Website, accounts, platform income, sponsorships, products, taxes and insurance |

1. Hold the land and every permit in your own name, never the company's.
2. Keep the company to intangible property and to selling information.
3. Let the company advertise, offer, arrange or bid no construction work for anyone.
4. Take no construction deposit or progress payment through the company, under any label.
5. Treat any paid work on someone else's house as licensable the moment it is advertised, whatever its size.

[FIGURE: three-lanes, the house, the brand and the company as three columns, with what each owns and the money flows between the person and the company]

**Professional**

Licensure follows the offer, so the company's exposure begins with its website copy and its replies to comments, before any work exists. The small-job exemption disappears for anyone who advertises, so a monetized channel that also takes small paid jobs for neighbors stands worse than a silent hobbyist. An owner who builds in order to sell is inside the contractor definition, and the residential developer endorsement exists for that business, with the license, bond and insurance of a contractor. The CCB's own license-application sheet describes the covered business as one that advertises, offers, bids or arranges to do construction, the plain form of the statute's verbs. A company cannot claim the owner exemption, because the exemption names a person at work on that person's own property, and land held in the company's name puts the company inside the definition the day it builds. The two presumptions about intent and the four resale clocks are the legal chapter's.

**Code**

```
Offering to do contractor work requires a license before the work.
  A person or joint venture that undertakes, offers to undertake or submits a bid to do work as a contractor must hold a current CCB license with the endorsement for that work.
  Source: ORS 701.021(1).

An owner who builds to sell is a contractor by definition.
  A person that owns property and constructs, or for compensation arranges the construction of, one or more residential structures with the intent of selling them.
  Source: ORS 701.005(5)(b).

Advertising ends the small-job exemption.
  The under-$1,000 exemption for casual, minor or inconsequential work on one structure does not apply to a person who advertises or puts out a sign, card or device suggesting they are a contractor.
  Source: ORS 701.010(4).
```

## 36.2 What the company sells, and what it never says

**Simple**

The company sells knowledge and nothing built. It may sell a manual, a plan set, a course, a sponsorship or an hour of teaching. It may not sell construction, take a deposit toward one, inspect a house for a fee, or size a paying client's footing. That last line separates teaching from engineering, and engineering offered to others needs a license at any price. The company also claims nothing it is not: no "architect", no "engineer", no "certified", no "approved". Oregon's consumer law treats a false claim about a product or a credential as an unlawful practice.

**Applied**

The company may own:

- the brand name and marks, the website, the channel, the newsletter and the social accounts
- the rights in the manual, the courses and the templates
- the photograph and video archive, assigned in writing from the person
- the plan library, sold as reference drawings under a single-build license
- the affiliate and sponsorship contracts

The company never:

- contracts, offers, advertises, bids or arranges construction for anyone, including "we will manage your build" consulting
- takes a construction deposit or progress payment of any kind
- owns a house, a building permit or land to build on for sale
- sells a plan set described as stamped, approved, code-verified or site-adapted
- gives structural sizes, load answers or engineering advice for a specific third-party project
- performs, offers or advertises home inspection
- uses "architect", "architecture", "architectural", "engineer", "licensed" or "certified" in its name or copy
- answers a specific footing, beam or mix question in a comment thread or a paid call

Write every consulting product on the teaching side of the line:

1. Describe principles, this build's documented decisions, and plan review for general code problems.
2. Direct no labor, material or sequence on a client's active jobsite.
3. Tie no fee to the outcome of a client's build.
4. Have an Oregon attorney read the offer's wording before it goes on sale, and put any unclear case to the CCB in writing.

**Professional**

The statute's verbs are performing, offering and bidding construction work, so general teaching sits outside them and the direction of a specific paying client's jobsite sits inside "arranging". The CCB publishes no rule on consultants and answers the question case by case through its licensing line, 503-934-2227. Freestone Built's coaching product runs on the teaching side as plan review, milestones and messaging, with no subcontractor direction and no construction money.

Home inspection is its own certified category, and a fee for a written report on a house's overall condition is the trigger. Describing one's own construction on camera is outside it, and a paid walkthrough of a viewer's house is inside it.

Oregon's Unlawful Trade Practices Act reaches goods and services bought for personal, family or household purposes, which covers a plan set or a course sold to a household, and its provision for an information product is the one that governs the description of what the product is and who stands behind it. The federal counterpart is Section 5 of the FTC Act, which the disclosure rules apply.

The three reserved architecture words, the rule that a printed or spoken claim to be an engineer is itself practice, and a table of wording to use instead are in the legal chapter. The annotation to the architecture statute shows how little it takes: a firm not licensed in Oregon that indicated on its website that it practiced architecture here violated the section (Twist Architecture v. Board of Architect Examiners, 361 Or 507, 2017).

**Code**

```
Home inspection for a fee requires certification and a licensed business.
  No individual may undertake, offer to undertake or bid to do work as a home inspector unless certified by the CCB and an owner of, or employed by, a board-licensed business.
  Source: ORS 701.445(1) and (2).

The title "Oregon certified home inspector" is reserved.
  No person may use the title before receiving the agency's certification, and a business may not advertise inspection services unless properly licensed.
  Source: OAR 812-008-0030(4); OAR 812-008-0201(4).

Misdescribing a product or a credential is an unlawful trade practice.
  Representing that goods or services have sponsorship, approval, characteristics, uses or benefits they do not have, or that a person has a status, qualification or affiliation the person does not have.
  Source: ORS 646.608(1)(e).

Confusion about source, approval, certification or affiliation is separately unlawful.
  Causing a likelihood of confusion as to the source, sponsorship, approval or certification of goods or services, or as to affiliation with or certification by another.
  Source: ORS 646.608(1)(b) and (c); ORS 646.605(6)(a) and (8).
```

## 36.3 Occupancy, a later sale and the repeat build

**Simple**

Living in the finished house is what makes you an owner-builder in the law's eyes. The statute presumes that a builder who never moves in meant to sell. A second rule presumes that a person who offers two newly built houses within three years is in the contracting business. Neither sets a waiting period, and no state office promises one. A plan to build, live a while, sell and build again is therefore a sequence of separate decisions, each made when circumstances call for it and each reviewed by a lawyer first. The full record of the build raises what the house is worth at any sale and supplies the brand's material in the meantime.

**Professional**

Occupancy is a dated event in the CCB's definitions, and the move-in record is what rebuts the presumption, so the sale question and its four clocks are the legal chapter's. A scheduled portfolio supplies the evidence the second presumption names, repeated offers of newly built structures inside one short period, so the plan records no build count, no holding period and no sale target. Someone who wants the repeat build as a business takes the residential developer endorsement, with the license, bond and insurance of a contractor. The amendment to the exemption statute by Oregon Laws 2025, chapter 78, operative July 1, 2027, changes the professional employer organization terminology in a later subsection and leaves the two owner exemptions and their presumption as they read.

## 36.4 The company's paperwork and its two sets of books

**Simple**

The company is an Oregon limited liability company with its own bank account, its own card and its own ledger. Nothing the house costs ever runs through that account, and no company dollar ever buys anything that ends up in the house. A tax auditor reads the same line the licensing board does: a house built for its owner is a personal expense, and a company that paid for it would look like a developer. So every receipt is coded to the house or to the company on the day it is paid, and the owner's draw comes out on a fixed schedule. Mixed money, called commingling, is evidence against both the exemption and the business deduction.

**Applied**

1. File articles of organization with the Secretary of State ($100) and name a registered agent at a physical Oregon street address.
2. Calendar the $100 annual renewal on the company's anniversary, and the $50 assumed-business-name renewal every two years where the brand name differs from the company's.
3. Sign an operating agreement that assigns the pre-formation content to the company and states that the company holds no interest in the land or the house.
4. Open a business checking account and card in the company's name, and pay no personal or house expense from them.
5. Code every receipt to one ledger, the house cost record or the company's books, on the day of purchase.
6. Pay yourself by a scheduled owner's draw.
7. Log a helper who works on both the house and the company by the hour to each ledger, the same day.
8. Where the company uses the shop or the yard, sign a written lease between you and the company at a stated rate.
9. Keep the books yourself while the product lines fit a spreadsheet, then hire a bookkeeper at $99 to $400 a month.
10. Engage a CPA when income streams multiply, when an employee or a depreciation schedule appears, or when an IRS notice arrives.
11. Ask the construction lender at application how it documents the company's income and expenses and whether they enter the debt-to-income ratio.
12. Register for the corporate activity tax in the year commercial activity first passes $750,000.

**Professional**

A single-member company is a disregarded entity for federal tax by default, so its income lands on the owner's Schedule C and carries self-employment tax, and it can elect corporate treatment later when the profit justifies a salary. Oregon taxes the pass-through income on the personal return. The elective pass-through-entity tax, which lets a partnership or an S corporation deduct state tax at the entity level, is closed to a single-member company taxed as a proprietorship by the Department of Revenue's eligibility list, so it enters the picture only with a second member or an S-corporation election.

The hobby-loss rule is the live risk for a channel that runs losses against wage income. A deduction survives only for an activity carried on for profit, and the Treasury regulation weighs nine factors, the businesslike manner of the activity and its books first among them (26 CFR 1.183-2(b)). Affiliate, sponsorship, course, coaching and royalty income are all ordinary business income to the company, and camera equipment, a home office and depreciation follow the ordinary rules for the media business alone.

The books follow the Small Business Administration's guidance for a home-based business: one dedicated account and card, a scheduled owner's draw rather than withdrawals as the balance allows, and receipts coded at the moment of purchase, because a split reconstructed from memory is the pattern an examiner reads as commingling. For a shared asset such as the shop, a written lease between the person and the company at a stated rate is the same discipline applied to space, drafted with the attorney and the CPA together because it touches both the property and the return.

Relay's and OnPay's small-business guides put a remote bookkeeper at $99 to $400 a month or $25 to $50 an hour, with 1 to 3 percent of revenue as the rule of thumb, and they place the end of do-it-yourself bookkeeping near $250,000 of revenue or the first hire. The move to a CPA they tie to complexity, with the conversation starting between $100,000 and $250,000, and CPA-supervised bookkeeping costs 25 to 40 percent more than a bookkeeper alone (ProAxis CPA's 2026 comparison). A business with course, coaching, plan and royalty income reaches that complexity early in its revenue curve. A construction lender underwrites the person's whole return, so the company's income and losses enter the debt-to-income calculation on the lender's terms. The state's filing charges come from the Secretary of State's Business Registry fee schedule, and the registered-agent and annual-report duties from the limited liability company act.

**Code**

```
The company keeps a registered agent at a physical Oregon address.
  A registered office at a street address where the agent can be served in person, and never a mail-receiving agency, mail-forwarding business or virtual office.
  Source: ORS 63.111(1).

The annual report is due by the company's anniversary.
  Name and state of organization, registered office and agent, principal office, the managers or at least one member, and the primary business activity, current as of 30 days before the anniversary.
  Source: ORS 63.787(1) and (2).

Commercial activity above $750,000 requires registration, and tax starts above $1 million.
  Register in the year commercial activity first exceeds $750,000, and pay $250 plus 0.57 percent of taxable commercial activity over $1 million.
  Source: ORS 317A.131(1)(a); ORS 317A.125(1) and (2).

Three profitable years in five presume a profit motive.
  An activity whose gross income exceeds its deductions in three or more of five consecutive tax years is presumed engaged in for profit unless the IRS shows otherwise.
  Source: 26 U.S.C. 183(d).
```

## 36.5 Insurance for the company and the property

**Simple**

Your homeowner's or builder's-risk policy covers a house and the people who live in it. Filming for money on the site, hosting paying visitors or running a company from the property can fall outside it unless the insurer is told and agrees. The company needs its own liability policy once it has income or visitors, and a media policy for what a general policy leaves out: a claim that a video defamed someone, copied someone, or led a viewer to hurt themselves following it. A volunteer who helps the company, as against the house, has no workers' compensation available at any price, because Oregon's state fund covers volunteers only for public bodies and nonprofits.

**Applied**

1. Tell the property insurer in writing that the site will be filmed for a business and may host visitors, and file the reply.
2. Buy commercial general liability for the company before its first dollar of revenue or its first visitor.
3. Add media liability for defamation, copyright and "I followed your video" claims, as a separate policy or an endorsement.
4. Before selling any physical block, get a product-liability quote that names "manufactured concrete masonry units sold to third parties, structural use".
5. Ask for a second quote for a landscape-only line under its own product description.
6. Before the first paid workshop, price single-event general liability plus a participant-injury rider for hands-on use of a press and a saw.
7. For instruction cover, name "hands-on instruction with a hydraulic press and power tools", and ask whether a trade-instruction program fits better than an educator policy.
8. Assign unpaid help to the house side of the ledger, never to the company's.

**Professional**

Homeowner and builder's-risk forms carry business-activity exclusions, so the disclosure to the carrier is what keeps a filmed, visited site inside the policy, and the carrier's written answer is the record. Commercial general liability covers injury and property damage to people on the site who are not employees. Media liability, sold alone or as an errors-and-omissions endorsement, covers the content claims a general policy excludes.

Product liability for a small producer is written inside the general liability policy more often than as a separate line, and Insureon's and TechInsurance's 2026 cost pages put basic small-producer coverage at $500 to $1,500 a year, rising to $2,000 to $10,000 or more for a higher-risk product or larger revenue. Neither prices a maker of structural masonry units, so the premium turns on how the underwriter classes "concrete masonry units sold for structural use" against a garden-wall or paver line.

Instructor coverage is priced for lower risks than a press and a saw. ACT Insurance's workshop add-on is $125 a year over a base policy from $24.25 a month, and its example injury is a hot glue gun. Educator liability programs (Insurance Canopy, biBERK) run $108 to $203 a year for $1 million of cover, and both figures are floors for hands-on instruction with a hydraulic press. Single-event liability for a hands-on event runs $49 to $250 per event (Barter Insurance from $49, ACT Insurance, Events Guardian's 2025 cost guide), and participant-injury coverage adds $50 to $1,000 depending on what participants operate. The insurers require the signed waiver as a condition of cover and treat it as no substitute for it, and a bindable quote for a block press in a hands-on class is a question for a commercial-lines agent with the equipment named.

SAIF, the state's workers' compensation insurer, writes volunteer coverage as an opt-in endorsement for public bodies and nonprofits and offers none for the volunteers of a for-profit business, so a volunteer injured while working for the company is an ordinary negligence claim with no exclusive-remedy cap. Tool and equipment coverage, the inland marine floater, is the tools chapter's.

## 36.6 Choosing and clearing the name

**Simple**

A name is checked before anything is printed with it, because a name found taken after a year of videos means retitling the year's work. Three checks run in order, and all three are free. The federal trademark database shows whether someone already holds the name for similar goods. The state business registry shows whether an Oregon company already uses it. A handle checker shows whether the same name is open on the platforms and as a web domain. Owning the trademark does not win a handle someone else registered first. A federal application costs $350 per class of goods, and the mark registers only once the name is in use in trade.

**Applied**

1. Search the candidate name at tmsearch.uspto.gov, the USPTO's Trademark Search, with its Basic Search first.
2. Search the Oregon Secretary of State's business registry for the same name and near spellings.
3. Run the name through a cross-platform checker (Namechk, KnowEm or Namecheckr) for the domain and the handles.
4. Reject any name whose clean handle or .com is taken, and start again.
5. Register the domain and the handles the day the name clears.
6. File the federal application at $350 per class, with the goods described from the USPTO's ID Manual to avoid the $200 free-form surcharge.
7. On an intent-to-use filing, budget $150 per class for the statement of use and $125 per class for each six-month extension.
8. Prove use in commerce within six months of the notice of allowance, or extend, for at most 36 months in all.
9. Put the registration certificate, with its date and class numbers, in the binder.

**Professional**

The USPTO retired its TESS search system on November 30, 2023, and the cloud-based Trademark Search that replaced it holds the same database of more than 15 million registered, pending and abandoned federal records. It covers federal filings only, so a state registry check and, for sales abroad, a separate foreign search complete a clearance, and a state's name availability is not trademark clearance.

Two cases show the order of operations. Randy Cassingham, publisher of ThisIsTrue.com, held the trademark and still could not take the matching YouTube username, which an unrelated party had registered first (MarketingSherpa's account). Two founders writing on dev.to built a product for 14 months before learning the name was another company's registered mark, at a cost they put at $15,000, ten weeks and one lost contract, because the check came after the investment.

The fee schedule in force since January 18, 2025 ended the two-tier application system, so one base fee applies, with surcharges for an incomplete application, for a free-form goods description and for long entries. A mark filed on intent to use registers only after the statement of use proves use in commerce, so the filing date is set against the launch timeline rather than the formation date.

**Code**

```
The federal application fee is set per class.
  $350 per class for an electronic base application, with $100 per class added for an application missing required information and $200 per class for a free-form goods description.
  Source: 37 CFR 2.6(a)(1)(iii), (iv) and (v).

A statement of use is due within six months of the notice of allowance.
  One six-month extension on request, then further extensions for good cause aggregating not more than 24 months, each with a verified statement of continued intent and the fee.
  Source: 15 U.S.C. 1051(d)(1) and (2).

Use filings carry their own fees.
  $150 per class for a statement of use or an amendment to allege use, and $125 per class for each six-month extension request.
  Source: 37 CFR 2.6(a)(2)(ii), (3)(ii) and (4)(ii).
```

## 36.7 Filming, releases and the site's security

**Simple**

A camera on a construction site records people who did not come to be filmed. Every person who can be recognized signs a release. It says what will be done with the footage, on which platforms, whether it earns money and for how long. A parent signs for a child. An inspector, a utility worker or a delivery driver is asked first, and nothing an inspector says on camera is presented as an approval. Oregon's recording law adds a separate rule for sound: everyone in an in-person conversation has to be told it is being recorded. Footage goes out weeks after it was shot, with house numbers, road names and permit cards blurred. No video shows an unattended site and its tools in real time.

**Applied**

1. Get a signed release from every recognizable visitor, worker and volunteer before their footage is published.
2. Write the release to name the subject and the signer, the uses (video, photographs, which platforms, commercial use) and a duration.
3. For a minor, have a parent or legal guardian sign, with a line stating the signer's relationship and authority, and both parents where practical.
4. Keep the media release separate from any liability waiver, so a participant can decline filming and still attend.
5. Ask an inspector, utility worker or delivery driver before recording them, and accept a no.
6. Present no inspector's on-camera remark as an approval of anything.
7. Tell everyone present, in words, before recording any in-person conversation, each time.
8. Blur house numbers, road names, permit-card close-ups and geolocatable landmarks, and strip location metadata from every published photograph.
9. Publish site footage weeks behind the work, and show no security-system details, key locations or family schedules.
10. Notify neighbors, film them only with releases, and require an explicit opt-in for children and family members.
11. Keep the raw footage and the signed release behind every published clip.

**Professional**

The recording statute, its all-party notice for in-person conversations, the open-device exception and the one-party rule for phone calls are in the smart-home chapter with the camera practice the house uses. On a jobsite the other party in most conversations is a visitor, so an announcement each time replaces the posted sign. Silent video of the site is outside that statute, and the audio track is what comes off a clip before it leaves the house.

A release is a contract, and the standard templates (LegalZoom's, Wrapbook's and Jotform's forms) name the subject and the signer separately, state the exact uses and platforms and whether the use is commercial, give a duration, and carry a signature block. A minor cannot consent, so a parent or legal guardian signs with a stated relationship and authority, and both parents where practical. A liability waiver for physical risk is a different consent, and keeping the two documents apart lets a participant refuse the camera and keep their place.

Delay-posting changes when a clip is published and not how often, so the publishing schedule stays whatever the plan sets, and no engagement cost attaches to the delay itself. A video of a stocked site shows anyone watching what is there and when nobody is, which is the security reason for the delay.

The complaint channel to plan for is a licensing board or an employer, as well as a court. Arizona home inspector Cy Porter posted videos of defects found in new homes, and the builder Taylor Morrison filed a complaint with the state's Board of Technical Registration seeking discipline and deletion of the videos (2024, reported by 12News and KSDK). The board found no verifiable violation and issued a letter of concern over one clip that paired gas-leak footage with unrelated audio. The raw footage and the release behind each clip are the answer to such a complaint.

## 36.8 Copyright, registration and the licenses

**Simple**

Everything the company makes is copyrighted the moment it is saved, but the remedies that make a lawsuit affordable are not automatic. A work registered before anyone copies it, or within three months of publication, earns set damages per work and attorney fees against the copier. After both windows the case shrinks to the losses you can prove, which for one plan set may be one sale. The drawings and the design of the house are protected separately, and standard features and anything the code dictates are free to everyone. A published mix recipe cannot be a trade secret, and a patent on it would have to clear the prior art already filed on block presses and stabilizer mixes.

**Applied**

1. Register Stone and Wire and the plan library within three months of first publication, and before any pre-publication sharing where you can.
2. Use the $45 single-author application where one person wrote the work, and the $65 standard application otherwise.
3. Register unpublished drafts and detail sheets in a group application at $85 when several are ready at once.
4. Assign every pre-formation photograph, video and text from yourself to the company in a written, dated assignment.
5. License the plan library under the written single-build license: one dwelling, no resale or redistribution, modification rights stated.
6. Give each educational document and code repository a deliberate license, open or proprietary, and never let a repository's default license cover a paid asset.
7. Keep worldwide and all-language rights out of any boilerplate grant-of-rights clause, so translation rights can be licensed separately later.
8. Before paying for a patent search, write down which refinements, if any, will stay unpublished, and guard only those with a nondisclosure agreement.

**Professional**

Statutory damages run from $750 to $30,000 per work infringed, and up to $150,000 where the infringement is willful (17 U.S.C. 504(c)). With attorney fees they are what makes a suit over one copied plan set worth filing, which is why registration timing is a launch item.

Plans carry two protections, one for the drawing as a graphic work and one for the built design as a work of architecture, and both stop at individual standard features. The library's value therefore sits in the documented system, the block, the wall section and the details this house alone has, and the generic sheets a buyer could draw from any code commentary add nothing a court would protect.

A self-published author keeps every subsidiary right, translation rights included. A translation license grants a foreign publisher one language and territory for a term of three to seven years against an advance and royalties (Jane Friedman's foreign-rights guide and the Alliance of Independent Authors' guidance), and nonfiction draws foreign offers at lower domestic sales than fiction (Jane Friedman). Nothing in United States registration has to be structured to keep those rights, and the one drafting point is a grant-of-rights clause in a later contract that quietly covers all languages.

Trade secrecy ends at publication, so a mix design taught in a course or printed in a manual is not a secret, and secrecy remains only for a refinement deliberately kept out of print and guarded with agreements (the USPTO's trade-secret policy page). A patent trades disclosure for 20 years of exclusion and needs novelty against prior art, which already includes dry-cast block machines (United States patents 8,844,228 and 9,636,842) and a fly-ash and sodium-silicate stabilizer dose (8,519,016). The application takes two to three years and attorney fees, and enforcement depends on detecting and litigating infringement. Copyright in the specific text, drawings and photographs, with the single-use license, is the protection that remains.

**Code**

```
Statutory damages and attorney fees depend on registration timing.
  Neither is available for infringement that begins before registration, unless a published work is registered within three months of its first publication.
  Source: 17 U.S.C. 412.

Registration comes before any infringement suit.
  No civil action for infringement of a United States work until registration or preregistration is made, or until registration is refused and the Register is served with the complaint.
  Source: 17 U.S.C. 411(a).

Plans carry two protections, with standard features excluded.
  Technical drawings and architectural plans as pictorial or graphic works, and the design of the building as an architectural work, which excludes individual standard features.
  Source: 17 U.S.C. 101; 17 U.S.C. 102(a)(5) and (8).

Electronic registration fees are fixed by rule.
  $45 for a single author, same claimant, one work, not for hire, $65 for any other electronic application, and $85 for a group of unpublished works.
  Source: 37 CFR 201.3(c)(1) and (c)(11).
```

## 36.9 Scrapers, copies and takedowns

**Simple**

Two kinds of copying reach a public build channel. One is a person who reposts a video or resells a plan set, and the other is a machine that reads the whole site to train a model. Against the person, federal law gives a fixed notice that any platform must act on, built from six set elements. Against the machine, United States law gives no right to opt out yet, so the site declares its refusal where crawlers look and lets its hosting company block the crawlers that ignore it. Europe's rules bind the model makers, which is why the declaration is worth making from Oregon. Abroad, the copyright exists in 182 countries automatically, and enforcing it in any one of them takes a lawyer licensed there.

**Applied**

1. Add robots.txt disallow lines for the AI training crawlers (GPTBot, Google-Extended, CCBot and ClaudeBot among them), and review the list quarterly.
2. Publish a /.well-known/tdmrep.json file, or send the tdm-reservation header, declaring text-and-data-mining rights reserved, with a tdm-policy link.
3. Where the site runs behind Cloudflare, turn on its AI crawler blocking and managed robots.txt, which the free plan includes.
4. Check YouTube Studio's current settings for any third-party AI training option, and set it for each channel.
5. For a reposted video, file YouTube's copyright-complaint form with the six elements.
6. For a copied plan file on another site, send the six-element notice to that host's agent, found in the Copyright Office's DMCA agent directory.
7. When a host will not act, file a removal request with Google Search for the infringing page.
8. Send a notice only on a genuine, good-faith belief that the material infringes, never over criticism or competition.
9. If a bad-faith notice takes down your own content, file a counter-notice and expect restoration in 10 to 14 business days.
10. For infringement abroad, start with the platform takedown, and weigh a local lawyer's fee against the sale lost.

**Professional**

No United States statute requires a model developer to honor a robots.txt directive or any other signal. Whether training on copyrighted text is fair use is being decided case by case in the authors' and publishers' suits, so the declarations are voluntary infrastructure with evidentiary value rather than a legal opt-out.

The W3C's TDM Reservation Protocol (final community group report, May 10, 2024) gives three forms. A /.well-known/tdmrep.json file with location rules, tdm-reservation and tdm-policy response headers, which the report prefers, and meta elements in the page head each carry a value of 1 for reserved. The reservation is the machine-readable form Article 4(3) of the EU copyright directive (Directive (EU) 2019/790) recognizes. Article 53(1)(c) of the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, applying from August 2, 2025) requires providers of general-purpose models to put in place a policy to identify and comply with such reservations. Cloudflare's managed robots.txt and AI-crawler blocking, on its free plan since July 2025, enforce at the network edge what the file only requests.

The takedown mechanism is the platform's own interest. A host that acts on a compliant notice keeps its safe harbor, so YouTube's copyright form and a host's designated agent move faster than a letter from a lawyer, and Google Search removal takes the infringing page out of results when the host will not act. The counter-notice carries the same perjury statement and a consent to federal jurisdiction, and a knowing misrepresentation in either direction is actionable, so a notice rests on infringement and never on criticism.

The Berne Convention's 182 contracting parties (WIPO's treaty list) protect a work from the moment of fixation under national treatment, with no registration anywhere, and the convention creates no international court. An infringement in another country is litigated in that country's courts by counsel licensed there, at a cost that exceeds most single infringements, so platform takedowns and any United States-reachable point of enforcement are the first tools abroad as well.

**Code**

```
A takedown notice has six required elements.
  A signature of an authorized person, identification of the work, identification and location of the infringing material, contact information, a good-faith statement, and a statement under penalty of perjury of accuracy and authority.
  Source: 17 U.S.C. 512(c)(3)(A).

A counter-notice restores the material on a fixed clock.
  The provider replaces removed material not less than 10 nor more than 14 business days after receiving a compliant counter notification, unless the complainant has filed suit.
  Source: 17 U.S.C. 512(g)(2)(C).

A knowing false notice or counter-notice carries damages.
  Any person who knowingly and materially misrepresents that material is infringing, or was removed by mistake, is liable for the resulting damages, costs and attorney's fees.
  Source: 17 U.S.C. 512(f).
```

## 36.10 Disclosure, reviews and sponsors

**Simple**

If money, free product or a relationship stands behind a recommendation, the audience is told, in the video and in the description, in plain words. That is a federal rule, and each post is its own advertisement needing its own disclosure. A second federal rule bans fake reviews, paid-for opinions and bought followers, with a civil penalty above $50,000 per violation. For a construction channel one rule is the company's own: no sponsor's product replaces a decision the engineer or the specification already made. The documented decisions are the channel's value to its audience.

**Applied**

1. Disclose every material connection (payment, free product, discount, commission, family or employment tie) wherever part of the audience would not expect it.
2. Put the disclosure in the content itself, spoken and on screen in video and in the description, never only in a hashtag or the profile.
3. In a livestream, repeat the disclosure at intervals.
4. Use plain words, "paid link" or "I earn a commission from links in this post", instead of tags such as "#sp".
5. Recommend only products you used, and where a result is unusual, state what a typical user gets.
6. Write, buy or solicit no review or testimonial that misstates who wrote it or whether they used the product.
7. Pay for no review conditioned on its being positive or negative.
8. Disclose your relationship on any review you or a family member writes of the company's products.
9. Buy no followers, views or likes, and send no legal threat to remove a review you merely dislike.
10. Publish the sponsorship policy, which states that no sponsor product replaces a documented engineering or specification decision.
11. Decline a sponsorship that conflicts with the record before it is announced.

**Professional**

The endorsement guides treat a connection as material when it would change the weight a viewer gives the recommendation. The FTC's guidance for influencers (Disclosures 101) puts the disclosure in the content itself, in the audio and on screen for video and repeated through a livestream, because a platform's disclosure tool alone does not reach the viewer who never opens it. Each post is a separate advertisement, so a disclosure in an earlier video covers nothing in the next.

The consumer-review rule took effect October 21, 2024 as a trade regulation rule, which is what makes its violations penalty-bearing under Section 5(m)(1)(A) of the FTC Act without a prior order. An original construction channel has little to do with fake reviews until it sells a course or a plan set, and then the testimonials on the sales page, the owner's own review of a tool, and any review a family member posts fall under it.

The sponsorship policy exists because the conflict cannot be managed after the fact. Pepsi's 1989 cancellation of a $5 million Madonna sponsorship and the creator-brand splits since then (the James Charles and Morphe parting, in Phyllo's case collection) were handled after the conflict became public, so the policy is written and posted before the first sponsor signs, and a sponsor that would require a product the record rejected is declined at the proposal.

**Code**

```
A material connection behind an endorsement is disclosed.
  A connection that might materially affect the weight or credibility of the endorsement, and that the audience would not reasonably expect, is disclosed clearly and conspicuously.
  Source: 16 CFR 255.5(a).

An endorsement that is not typical discloses the generally expected result.
  Where an endorser's experience with a central attribute is not substantiated as typical, the advertisement clearly and conspicuously discloses the generally expected performance.
  Source: 16 CFR 255.2(b).

Fake reviews and bought sentiment are prohibited.
  No writing, selling or buying of a review that misrepresents the reviewer's existence or experience, and no compensation conditioned on a review's sentiment, positive or negative.
  Source: 16 CFR 465.2; 16 CFR 465.4.

Insider reviews are disclosed, and review suppression and fake influence are prohibited.
  An officer's or manager's review discloses the relationship, no groundless legal threat or false accusation to remove a review, and no selling or buying of fake followers, views or likes.
  Source: 16 CFR 465.5; 16 CFR 465.7; 16 CFR 465.8.

Each violation of a trade rule carries a civil penalty.
  Up to $53,088 per violation for penalties assessed after January 17, 2025.
  Source: 16 CFR 1.98(d) and (e); 15 U.S.C. 45(m)(1)(A).
```

## 36.11 Platforms and the revenue mechanisms

**Simple**

A channel earns nothing until it clears the platform's thresholds, which 3 channels in 100 reach, by RecurPost's count. On YouTube that means a thousand subscribers and either 4,000 hours watched in a year or ten million short-video views in 90 days. After that, advertising pays by the thousand views, at rates the platform sets and changes. Sponsors, affiliate links, memberships and products are the other mechanisms, and each costs a different amount of work for a different risk. A membership needs new content every month to justify its fee, which a single house cannot supply once it is finished. A pre-sale or a one-time product fits a build that ends.

**Applied**

| Mechanism | What it needs first | Effort | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform advertising | 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days | Low per video once eligible | Demonetization, rate changes, policy strikes |
| Sponsorship | An audience a brand wants, and a published policy | Negotiation per deal | A conflict with the documented record |
| Affiliate links | Disclosure on every post | Low | Program terms change, small amounts |
| Membership or Patreon | A monthly reason to keep paying | Continuous | Ends with the build |
| Pre-sale or crowdfunding | An existing list and priced tiers | One campaign | Delivering what was promised |
| Plans, courses, the manual | A finished product | Front-loaded | Pricing and refunds |
| Education-only consulting | The licensing line held | Hours for money | Drifting into project direction |
| Magazine articles | A finished, photographed project | Per article | Modest pay |

1. Build the website and the email list as the canonical home, with each platform as distribution.
2. Export the subscriber list and back up the content masters on a schedule.
3. Turn on 2-Step Verification, clear any Community Guidelines strike, and link an AdSense account before applying to the partner program.
4. Review YouTube's updated partner terms before January 31, 2027, when the program changes.
5. Price any paid tier against the eventual retail product, a file tier near the ebook price and a print tier near the paperback.
6. On a demonetization notice, file the appeal within 21 days, change what the notice named, and reapply after the required interval.
7. Treat a membership as a product with an end date, and plan its close or its pivot at the house's completion.

**Professional**

YouTube's partner-program page sets the two thresholds and the account conditions, and announces program changes from February 1, 2027 with updated terms to accept by January 31, 2027. In July 2026 the same page described review as about 30 days, with reapplication after 30 days or 90 after a second rejection.

Creator-tool blogs (Lenostube, Miraflow, Outlierkit) converge on $4 to $15 CPM across all content without publishing a method, and RPM, the creator's take per thousand views after the platform's share and the unmonetized views, runs well below it. Graphtreon's aggregated Patreon statistics convert 50 patrons to about $460 a month, 200 to $1,800 and 500 to $4,600. Its maker category spreads from $25 to $120 a month at a growth stage to $180 to $860 at a professional one, and it has no construction category. A membership sells continuous content, which a finite build stops supplying at occupancy, so a single-house channel either pivots at completion or sells complete products instead.

Kickstarter's own statistics page put the share of launched projects reaching their goal at 42 percent (July 2026), with wide variation by category, and most pledges come from a creator's existing audience. A pre-sale campaign for a manual or a plan library is therefore sized to the first print run, the editing and the cover, with tiers priced near the eventual retail products and launched to an existing list.

A demonetized channel appeals through YouTube's own tool, and Lenostube's guide puts the odds near 30 to 40 percent for appeals filed within 21 days. One channel owner's Medium account describes a 180,000-subscriber compilation channel suspended for reused content and a first appeal refused. Reinstatement came about 90 days later, after the owner added original commentary and cut the weakest uploads, at 44 percent of the earlier revenue three months on. Original footage of a documented build is the opposite of the reused-content profile that triggered it.

The one count of owner-built homes, Open Source Ecology's compilation of about 50,000 permitted owner-built homes in 2014 against 1.5 million housing starts, excludes unpermitted rural building and is a decade old, so it is a floor and not a market estimate.

## 36.12 The plan library

**Simple**

Oregon lets a person who is not an architect draw plans for a single-family house, so selling the drawings of this house as reference plans is lawful. Two conditions hold the product inside that permission. The plans are sold unstamped, as reference material that no one has checked against the buyer's soil, climate or local code. The buyer is told so in plain words. And the company never offers to adapt them to a buyer's site, because that is engineering done for others. The value of the library is the documented system: the block, the wall section and the details that are this house's own. Standard features and anything the code dictates are free to everyone already.

**Applied**

1. Sell each plan set under a written single-build license: one dwelling, no resale or redistribution, and the permitted modifications named.
2. State on the cover sheet that the documents are unstamped reference material, not verified against any site, soil, climate or local code.
3. State that a jurisdiction requiring a stamp will require the buyer to hire a local licensed professional.
4. State which layouts are omitted (mechanical, electrical, plumbing) and that they are the buyer's to produce.
5. Tell out-of-state buyers to check their own state's design-professional and permit rules.
6. Before any sheet derived from the engineer of record's drawings goes on sale, get the engineer's written license to reproduce it.
7. Offer three tiers, a single detail sheet, a topic bundle such as the full block-making system, and the complete reference set.
8. Price the complete set against named stock-plan sellers' current prices, read the week you price it.
9. Price a bundle at 15 to 20 percent below the sum of its sheets, and check profit per order, not order size, after each change.
10. Test a tier with a landing page and real pre-orders before drawing the rest, and treat 10 to 20 pre-sales as demand.
11. Add to every sheet that states a mix, a press setting or a cure the clause that any deviation voids the tested-performance statements.

**Professional**

The engineering act regulates services done for others, and its exemptions cover drawings, specifications and even construction supervision for a single-family dwelling and for a small building, so a reference set drawn from this house is outside registration as long as it is not adapted to a third party's site. The architecture exemption for single-family plans and the three reserved words are in the legal chapter.

The stock-plan industry supplies the packaging. Donald A. Gardner's copyright and disclaimer page and HPA Design's license page state that a stock plan carries no professional stamp, that code compliance and any required local stamp are the buyer's responsibility, and that a jurisdiction requiring a stamp means hiring a local professional, and they license one build per purchase with the modification rights defined. "Approved construction documents" is a promise no seller can make.

The sellers price by square footage and set completeness, with full sets from the low hundreds to the low thousands of dollars by house size and license scope, against $700 to $10,000 for a custom drafted plan (HomeGuide's 2026 cost guide). A single detail sheet is a product none of them lists, so the first tier is this plan's own experiment and is priced by test. EightX, a direct-to-consumer pricing consultancy, sets the bundle discount where the saving feels real to the buyer and the margin survives, puts bundle-driven order-value gains at 15 to 30 percent with mix-and-match bundles at the top, and shows in a worked example a deeper bundle cutting profit dollars per order by more than 12 points, so the measure after any price change is profit per order.

Van Westendorp's price sensitivity meter (1976) asks prospective buyers four prices, too expensive, expensive but acceptable, a bargain, and so cheap the quality is doubted, and plots the acceptable corridor, and it suits a product with no established comparable. The creator-economy practice is a landing page with a real buy or reserve button, and Matt Giaro's and LearnyBox's course-presale guides read a small run of real pre-sales as demand and a weak response as a pricing or targeting fault. Whether narrow single-sheet PDFs sell in volume in a maker niche is untested, so that tier opens as a test. The deviation clause belongs on every sheet that states a mix, a press pressure or a cure, because the sold paper describes a physical process the buyer will carry out, and the clause is the paper half of the product-liability posture.

**Code**

```
Engineering is regulated when it is done for others.
  The practice of engineering or professional engineering means the listed services when done for others.
  Source: ORS 672.005(1).

Drawings for a single-family dwelling or farm building need no engineering registration.
  Drawings, specifications or supervision of construction for a single-family residential dwelling or farm building, with its accessory buildings, by a person who is not a registered engineer.
  Source: ORS 672.060(8).

Two more exemptions cover small buildings and one's own property.
  Buildings of 4,000 square feet of ground area or less, and work by a person or firm that affects exclusively its own property or interests, unless public or employee safety is affected.
  Source: ORS 672.060(7) and (2).
```

## 36.13 The book

**Simple**

A technical manual this size is printed one copy at a time until it has proved it sells. Print-on-demand costs more per copy and nothing up front. An offset run costs a third as much per copy, paid in advance. Amazon's print service pays a share of the cover price minus the printing cost, and its catalog partner reaches bookstores and libraries at a deeper discount. Before any of that, an editor, a cover designer and a layout artist are paid by the word, the project and the page. A manual full of diagrams and code citations costs more to edit than the same number of words of story. Two magazines pay for construction stories from an owner's own build.

**Applied**

1. Launch on print-on-demand through KDP and IngramSpark, with no inventory.
2. Price the paperback at $9.99 or above on Amazon, where KDP's royalty rate is 60 percent of list price instead of 50.
3. Run KDP's printing-cost calculator for the real page count and color interior before setting the list price.
4. Set IngramSpark's wholesale discount at 55 percent with returns on where bookstore and library shelves matter, or 35 to 50 percent for online-only distribution.
5. Move a title to a short-run offset printer only once a year's steady sales pass the break-even count.
6. Budget editing per word: 3 to 4 cents for copyediting, 4 to 5 for developmental, 2 to 3 for proofreading, 2 to 2.5 for an index.
7. Budget $625 to $1,250 for the cover and $500 and up for an illustrated nonfiction interior layout.
8. Before paying for layout, test the price with a landing page and pre-orders, or a four-question price survey to the list.
9. Pitch Fine Homebuilding with an outline, a project description and photographs, at $150 a published page.
10. Pitch Mother Earth News's Country Lore at $25 to $100 an item of 100 to 300 words, for print only.
11. Keep digital, print, translation and audio rights unassigned to any single distributor.

**Professional**

KDP's royalty formula makes the printing cost the lever: it is subtracted from the royalty, rises with page count and a color interior, and sets the minimum list price, so an illustrated technical manual prices higher than a text paperback of the same length. Expanded Distribution to other retailers and libraries pays a lower rate on the same formula, and the ebook royalty is 35 percent anywhere or 70 percent less a delivery cost that averages $0.06 a unit and rises with file size. IngramSpark pays the list price less the wholesale discount, the print cost and a market access fee on wholesale-channel sales. Its March 10, 2020 price sheet put a black-and-white interior at 1.3 cents a page and premium color at 6.9 cents, and a February 1, 2026 sheet has since raised each.

PRC Book Printing's guide puts print-on-demand at $8 to $12 a copy for a 300-page 6-by-9 paperback at any quantity, against offset at $3 to $4 a copy at 500 copies and $2.20 to $2.80 at 1,000. Break-even sits near 200 to 300 copies for that book and 300 to 400 for a full-color hardcover. Offset also offers the paper stocks, bindings and color fidelity print-on-demand does not match, which matters for diagram legibility, and it takes 3 to 6 weeks from file approval against 2 to 7 business days. The point at which diagram density makes offset necessary rather than cheaper is a quality judgment priced per run.

The Editorial Freelancers Association's 2025 rate survey sets the per-word medians, and at its copyediting median every 100,000 words costs $3,000 to $4,000 before the citation checking a code-heavy manual adds, which the survey does not price. Reedsy's 2025 data put the average cover at $880 across about 9,600 projects, with illustrated or art-directed covers at $900 to $1,500 and up, and its self-publishing cost guide places half of interior-layout projects between $475 and $1,275 with complex illustrated nonfiction from $500.

Fine Homebuilding buys a submission as an outline, a project description and photographs, and Mother Earth News pays only for print and assigns features to its contributing editors with occasional freelance assignments. Grit and Off Grid Magazine take homestead and off-grid pieces without published rates, and The Freelance Writing Network's 2026 roundup lists 37 home-and-garden markets paying up to $2 a word for design features, a ceiling for that market rather than a construction rate.

O'Reilly sells one subject at two depths as two named series, Head First (from Kathy Sierra and Bert Bates's Head First Java, 2003) for the reader starting from nothing and In a Nutshell as the dense desk reference, and a buyer self-selects by series. A single manual with labeled layers for several readers has no equivalent in that catalog, so the layering is the claim its marketing makes, and the series precedent is the evidence that readers at different depths buy the same subject.

## 36.14 Courses and coaching

**Simple**

A course sells the method of this build to someone building their own, and a coaching call sells an hour of it. Both stay on the teaching side of the licensing line only while they teach principles and review plans for general problems. The moment a paid call tells a specific client what footing to pour on their specific lot, it has become engineering or construction direction. A hands-on skill such as pressing block is taught best live. Cohort courses with a live session finish at 85 to 90 percent, and self-paced video at 10 to 15 percent. Platform rent runs from $39 to $499 a month before a student enrolls, and moving platforms later costs more than any month's fee.

**Applied**

| Platform and plan | Monthly price (annual billing) | Platform fee | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teachable Starter | $39 ($29) | 7.5 percent | 5 products |
| Teachable Builder | $89 ($69) | None with Teachable Payments | 10 products |
| Teachable Growth | $189 ($139) | None with Teachable Payments | 50 products |
| Thinkific Start | $109 ($82) | None, 2.9 percent processing | 10,000 students |
| Thinkific Grow | $219 ($164) | None, 2.9 percent processing | 10,000 students |
| Kajabi Basic | $179 ($143) | 2.9 percent plus $0.30 processing | 5 products, 2,500 contacts |
| Kajabi Growth | $249 ($199) | 2.8 percent plus $0.30 processing | 50 products, 25,000 contacts |

1. Choose the platform after a trial, for the courses planned three years out, and expect never to switch.
2. Price a mini-course of under five hours at $47 to $197, and a core course of five to fifteen hours with community access at $297 to $997.
3. Run the block-pressing course as a cohort with a live session, and keep self-paced video for the theory.
4. Write every coaching offer as plan review, milestones and questions answered from this build, with no direction of a client's subcontractors and no construction money.
5. Price coaching as a monthly subscription with a minimum term, on the model of Freestone Built's $799 a month with a three-month minimum.
6. Ask the platform in writing whether it collects and remits sales tax as a marketplace facilitator before the first sale.
7. Test a coaching offer on the existing list before building its materials.

**Professional**

Freestone Built's 1-on-1 Build Coaching is the working model: a recurring subscription with a minimum term, built from plan review, a milestone roadmap with checklists and messaging through an app, with nothing that touches construction cost, subcontractor payment or financing. Tom Landis's Owner Builder Coach is a comparable single-coach service, and UBuildIt runs the same idea as a franchise with local construction consultants and prescreened subcontractors, claiming 15 to 30 percent savings against a general contractor. Neither specializes in concrete or masonry, so a block-and-concrete coaching product would be a new niche rather than a copy.

The Auroville Earth Institute is the oldest precedent for selling the method itself. Its six-day CSEB Production course, 9 AM to 6 PM, runs two days of theory on soil identification, stabilization, blockyard organization, quality control and cost analysis, then four days of hands-on pressing, press maintenance and field testing, with the fee given on application. Its curriculum already includes the economic-feasibility module a block-making business course would need.

BuddyBoss's and FreshLearn's course-pricing guides supply the price bands and the completion figures, and both find that buyers who choose on price alone refund and abandon more often, which is the case against a discount launch. The platforms changed their terms within three months of 2026 alone: Thinkific dropped its plan below $109, and Teachable removed its student caps. Kajabi's higher floor buys an integrated marketing, funnel and email suite that a single narrow course does not need. DreamGrow's and Jenna Kutcher's accounts of creators switching between Teachable, Thinkific and Kajabi describe re-uploading every video, rebuilding landing pages and automations, and a revenue dip from broken links, which is why the first choice is made for the three-year product line.

The Creator Playbook's claim that a newsletter with 500 subscribers earned $6,000 from a coaching offer while one with 4,000 earned under $1,000 a month is a self-published, uncorroborated account by a seller of newsletter advice, so it is a reason to test a coaching offer on the existing list and not a forecast. Live and recorded delivery are taxed differently in many buyers' states.

## 36.15 Workshops on the property

**Simple**

A paying visitor pressing block in your yard is a customer under the law. A customer is covered by your liability insurance and a signed waiver, never by workers' compensation. A paid helper on the company's side is an employee unless they run a real independent business. The silica rule that only advised you as a solo builder binds you in full the day an employee breathes the same dust. On farm or forest land a workshop is a home occupation, with a cap on staff and no new building allowed for it. A one-day workshop for four to eight people costs a per-event insurance premium and $500 to $1,500 of materials before anyone is paid to teach it.

**Applied**

1. Before the first workshop, confirm in writing with the county planning department that a paying-visitor home occupation is allowed, and on what terms.
2. Price single-event liability at $49 to $250 per event and a participant-injury rider at $50 to $1,000 before setting the ticket price.
3. Have every participant sign an activity-specific liability waiver, separate from the media release, before touching a press or a saw.
4. Give a safety briefing, issue a P100 respirator and eye protection, and run every cut wet before any participant handles a tool.
5. Apply the silica standard's specified controls to every workshop task, as if each participant were an employee.
6. Budget $500 to $1,500 for cement, aggregate and protective equipment for four to eight people, with the press you already own.
7. Set the ticket price against the 2022 residential cob-course rate of $100 a participant-day, which included meals and camping.
8. Pay any helper on the company's side only as an employee with workers' compensation in place, or as a contractor who meets the independent-business tests.
9. Keep volunteers off the company's side entirely.
10. Hold no workshop in a new building put up for it on farm or forest land.

**Professional**

Three relationships carry three rules. A paying participant has no employment relationship with the host, so workers' compensation never applies, and the host's duty is premises liability and negligence, met by the safety briefing, guarded equipment, protective gear and the waiver. Courts do not enforce a waiver against gross negligence or a failure to warn of a known hazard (WRS Law's waiver guidance, and the waivers that operating workshops such as AR Workshop post). A paid helper is a subject worker unless the independent-contractor tests are met, and the casual-labor exception is written for work outside the employer's trade or business, which a workshop is not, so it shelters no workshop helper. A volunteer for the company has no coverage to buy, as SAIF's rule states, which is why unpaid help stays on the house.

OSHA's authority runs through the employer-employee relationship, and its May 23, 2001 interpretation letter states that a self-employed person with no employees cannot be cited for their own exposure. One employee ends that position. The full standard then applies to that employee, by either of its two routes, the specified controls for a listed task without air sampling or an exposure assessment against the permissible limit, with the written plan either way and no reduced tier for occasional or short work. Oregon enforces its own rule, OAR 437-002-1053 to 437-002-1065, which covers construction in the same rule as general industry. Participants are outside the standard, since no employment relationship exists, and inside the host's duty of care, so the same controls apply to them on the premises-liability footing, and a waiver does not reduce the dust.

Costs come from workshop-hosting and natural-building precedents. Milieu Studio's workshop guide puts a first workshop's consumables at $500 to $1,500 and its insurance and permits at $300 to $700, and prices rented space at $50 to $500 an hour, a cost the owner's own site replaces with parking, a restroom, weather cover and staging. Cob Hill Natural Building School's 10-day course of September 2022 cost $1,000 with three plant-based meals a day and tent camping, and the Auroville Earth Institute's course runs six days. A one-day, non-residential block workshop sits below those per-day rates because it carries no lodging or meals, and the owner's real local figures for insurance, consumables and site preparation replace the proxies once a date is set.

On farm or forest land the home-occupation statute caps the on-site staff, confines the business to the dwelling or the buildings that go with the zone's permitted uses, and bars a new building put up for it, and the land chapter carries the statute, the LUBA decision on a purpose-built events building and the local size caps.

**Code**

```
Oregon's silica rule covers construction work.
  All occupational exposures to respirable crystalline silica in general industry and construction, except where exposure stays below 25 micrograms per cubic meter as an 8-hour average under any foreseeable conditions.
  Source: OAR 437-002-1053.

An employer follows Table 1 or measures the exposure.
  For each employee on a Table 1 task, the engineering controls, work practices and respiratory protection the table specifies, unless the employer assesses and limits exposure under the exposure-assessment paragraph.
  Source: 29 CFR 1926.1153(c)(1).

An employer with silica exposure keeps a written exposure control plan.
  A description of the exposing tasks, the controls and respiratory protection for each, the housekeeping measures and the access restrictions.
  Source: 29 CFR 1926.1153(g)(1).

A helper is an independent contractor only on the statutory tests.
  Free from direction and control over means and manner, customarily engaged in an independently established business (any three of five listed marks), licensed where the work requires it, and responsible for other licenses.
  Source: ORS 670.600(2) and (3).
```

## 36.16 Selling block, or selling the method

**Simple**

Block is heavy and low in price, so it does not travel far. A standard concrete block weighs about 40 pounds and sells delivered for $2 to $3. The suppliers who make it charge extra for every mile past a short base radius, which puts a one-person yard's market at pickup distance. Selling a structural block to a stranger also turns one lab test into a test of every batch, forever. Selling the method instead, the mix, the press settings and the cure, written down and licensed, leaves the buyer as the maker of their own block. A federal appeals court held in 1991 that the words in a how-to book are not a product, after a mushroom guide was sued.

**Applied**

1. Before any sale, compute the delivered cost per unit: materials and labor, the press and molds spread over their production life, curing-yard time, pallets and hauling.
2. Sell at pickup from the yard, or within the radius your own truck and fuel leave a margin at $2 to $3 a unit.
3. Represent no unit as structural unless every production lot is sampled and tested to the masonry-unit standard, with the reports kept by lot.
4. Quote a testing lab for a per-lot sample set before pricing structural block at all.
5. For pavers and garden-wall units, test to their own standards and keep every claim off load-bearing use.
6. Write any sold mix design as a licensed document: the ratio, the press pressure, the moisture target, the cure, and the deviations that void it.
7. Keep the house's own alternative-materials approval, test reports and conditions as an evidence package a later builder can show their own official.
8. Register the business as a seller of materials, and treat the home-occupation caps as the yard's limit.
9. Ask the county and DEQ whether a yard at your volume needs an air-quality permit.
10. Buy product liability before the first unit leaves the property.

**Professional**

CountBricks' and BuilderMuse's 2026 price compilations put a standard 8-by-8-by-16 unit at $1.60 to $2.98 delivered, and suppliers price delivery as a flat or low rate inside a base radius of 15 to 25 miles and $0.50 to $3 a mile, or $10 to $25 a mile, beyond it (HomeGuide, Concrete Captain, Heavy Haulers). Freight is a large share of a unit's price at that weight and price, and a one-person yard has neither the plant's volume discount nor its fleet, so its radius is shorter still. Grokipedia's page, drawing on Open Source Ecology's cost data, puts finished stabilized earth block at $2.95 to $3.45 a unit, parity with concrete block rather than a pass-through of its lower material cost. AECT's purchase page lists no price and refers buyers to its regional producers (Colorado Earth, Anteros Earth Block, Parabellum Constructors), so a wholesale figure comes from a direct quote. The materials-and-labor cost band, the house's own test program and the alternative-materials path are the owner-made blocks chapter's, and a business cost per unit adds the amortized press and molds, the curing-yard time, packaging and delivery to that band once the mix and press are final.

The masonry-unit specification is met lot by lot. The test method samples each lot, six units per sample in the common reading, three for dimensions, absorption and density and three for compression. A house-build test proves one run once to one official, and a sold structural unit must prove every lot to every buyer, indefinitely, at a laboratory's per-sample price obtained by quote. An ICC-ES evaluation report is the nationally reusable listing a manufacturer buys for a product sold into many jurisdictions: ICC-ES quotes fees per application after a statement of work, its Evaluation Committee hears acceptance criteria three times a year, and the time depends on the product, so it is the cost of entering the block business and not an add-on to the house's one approval. An approval granted for this house under the alternative-materials rule binds no other official. The statewide route, an Alternate Method Ruling from the Building Codes Division, binds every Oregon building department within its scope (OAR 918-008-0110), and short of it the house's test reports, the official's written conditions and the inspection records are persuasive evidence a later builder can present under their own official's authority to accept certified reports.

Pavers and segmental retaining-wall units are specified by ASTM C936 and ASTM C1372 rather than the loadbearing-unit specification, so a landscape line leaves the structural-masonry regime and keeps ordinary product liability for property damage, with its own testing and, for retaining walls above a jurisdiction's height trigger, its own engineering review. Bringing such a line to market is a separate mold investment plus that testing, priced only by quote. Watershed Materials of Napa began developing a low-cement structural unit in 2011 on a $740,000 National Science Foundation grant for a machine to mass-produce structural masonry, claiming up to 50 percent less cement and 65 percent less embodied energy than conventional block, and its company domain is parked for sale in 2026, so a product business in alternative masonry is grant-scale capital and a decade of machine engineering, with the company's current status unconfirmed. AECT sells portable presses and bundles a week of on-site training, and British suppliers (Earth Blocks, the Cob Specialist) hire them out. A pressing service, in which the press travels to the client's soil and no block is shipped, would avoid the freight problem and leave the client as the maker of the units, and the service itself is an untested business rather than a precedent.

Selling the documented mix and process as a licensed recipe, with no physical unit sold, shifts the testing burden to the buyer who makes the block and narrows the liability. In Winter v. G.P. Putnam's Sons (938 F.2d 1033, 9th Cir. 1991), the Ninth Circuit held that the ideas and expression in a book are not a product for strict products liability, distinguishing a navigational chart, while leaving negligence theories open. One circuit's rule binds only its own courts, so the license's disclaimers, indemnity and choice-of-law clauses are the attorney's draft. The same document carries the deviation clause. The misuse defense in product-liability law is strongest where the instructions are specific enough that a deviation can be proved, and where the foreseeable deviations, a wrong stabilizer ratio, a lower compaction pressure, a short cure or a substituted material, are named and warned against (Fox and Farley, In Compliance magazine).

The contractor-licensing statute exempts a seller of materials who does not install them, so a yard that sells block and never lays it needs no CCB license at any volume, and ordinary business registration, the corporate activity tax above its threshold and any county business license remain. A press and mixer may need an air-quality permit at some production volume, which DEQ's Air Quality program sets, and on resource land the home-occupation caps apply to the yard as they do to a workshop.

**Code**

```
Selling a finished material without installing it needs no contractor license.
  A person who furnishes materials, supplies, equipment or finished product and does not fabricate them into, or consume them in, the work of a contractor is exempt from licensure.
  Source: ORS 701.010(3).
```

## 36.17 Sales tax across state and national lines

**Simple**

Oregon has no sales tax, so a workshop taught here collects none. The question moves to the buyer's state when a plan file or a recorded course is sold online. Most states tax a downloaded file or a recorded video as a digital good and leave a live class alone as a service, so the same lesson can be taxed one way live and another way as a recording. A state's rules reach a seller only once its sales into that state pass a threshold the state sets. A marketplace that collects the tax itself removes the burden for sales made through it, and for nothing sold elsewhere. One sale of a file to a buyer in the European Union makes the seller liable for that country's VAT.

**Applied**

1. Track sales by buyer's state from the first sale.
2. For any state approaching $100,000 in a year, check whether it taxes each product type separately: a PDF plan, a recorded course, a live call.
3. Repeat the check each year, since both lists change.
4. Before choosing a platform, ask in writing whether it collects and remits sales tax as a marketplace facilitator for your products.
5. Treat every sale outside a facilitating platform (your own site, a Stripe checkout, an invoiced call) as your own tax obligation.
6. Once sales reach several states, use a sales-tax service (TaxJar, Stripe Tax or Avalara) that tracks nexus and product taxability together.
7. Before the first digital sale to a buyer in the European Union, either register in the non-Union One Stop Shop or sell through a platform that acts as merchant of record.
8. Screen international buyers against the OFAC sanctions list, as any US seller must.

**Professional**

South Dakota v. Wayfair, Inc. (585 U.S. 162, 2018) let a state tax a remote seller on economic presence alone, and the states converged on a revenue threshold. Avalara's 2026 state guide counts 41 states at $100,000 of sales, 14 of them with a 200-transaction alternative, Alabama and Mississippi at $250,000, and California, New York and Texas at $500,000, and the transaction tests are being dropped (Illinois on January 1, 2026, Utah on July 1, 2025). The threshold is per state, so nexus turns on sales into one state and never on national revenue.

Digital-goods taxability is the second layer and is not uniform. More than 30 states tax digital products and more than 20 tax software as a service, Maine added digital audiovisual and audio works on January 1, 2026, and some states exempt educational content or have no digital-goods category, so a PDF plan, a recorded course and a live call are three products that one state can treat three ways (Avalara, the Sales Tax Institute, TaxCloud). Live, instructor-led teaching, in person or streamed with interaction, is a service most states leave untaxed, and the recording of the same session is a digital good most taxing states tax (Avalara, TaxJar, Thinkific's compliance guide).

Marketplace-facilitator laws move collection to the platform for sales it facilitates. Etsy calculates, collects and remits in the 45 sales-tax states plus the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico, and Teachable's support article describes the same role for course sales through it (July 2026 reading), while Gumroad's and Podia's positions are unconfirmed and are asked before any commitment. A direct sale from the owner's own site, a Stripe-only checkout or an invoiced call is outside every facilitator's collection.

A seller outside the European Union with no EU establishment charges the customer's country's VAT on a digital product from the first sale, registers in one member state through the non-Union One Stop Shop and files quarterly, because the EUR 10,000 threshold is a place-of-supply rule for suppliers established inside the Union (the European Commission's OSS guidance). The United Kingdom and Australia run separate regimes with their own registration rules, Australia's at A$75,000 of GST turnover (Avalara's global guide), and a platform acting as merchant of record absorbs the registrations. Residential plans and a mix design are published civilian information outside the Export Administration Regulations' controlled technology (the Bureau of Industry and Security's EAR pages, UC Santa Barbara's export-control FAQ), and sanctions screening against the OFAC list applies to any international seller.

## 36.18 Expectations, the long build and the brand after the house

**Simple**

Most channels never pay their owner, and the ones that do took years. Kirsten Dirksen's faircompanies, which has filmed small homes since 2007, went five years before its videos earned money. A documented build still produces a searchable record, a credible name and an audience that trusts a citation, whatever the channel earns. The build sets the pace, because a stalled pour is a stalled channel, and burnout is the usual reason a channel stops. When the house is finished, the channel ends, turns to teaching, or follows the people to the next project. A brand named for the people carries its audience to the next project, as Odd Life Crafting's did from a container house to a sailboat.

**Applied**

1. Publish on a cadence the build can keep in its slowest month, and promise no fixed weekly schedule.
2. Keep the website and the email list as the canonical home, and export the list monthly.
3. Write the brand's name and description around the people and the method, so the channel survives the house's completion.
4. Decide before completion which of the three endings the channel takes: close, teach, or a next documented project.
5. Post a moderation policy, and answer every footing, beam or mix question about a specific house with "hire a local professional", as policy.
6. Publish a dated correction on the page of any video or post found wrong, and keep a corrections log.
7. Keep a text-and-photo site beside the video channel as the searchable record.

**Professional**

The one dated income timeline in this niche is faircompanies' own: five years of videos before the channel earned, by its bio page. Kelly Hart and Owen Geiger's Earthbag Building Blog has run for more than a decade as a text-and-photo archive that stays searchable and linkable per detail, which an unmaintained video channel does not, and the natural-building forums at permies.com are where builders compare methods and voice regret in public. The "Earthbag Construction Fail!" video (My Little Podcast, 2017) shows a failure format with an audience of its own, and the regret voiced in those forums concerns the building method chosen rather than the recording format.

Vibely's November 2021 survey of 150 creators found that 90 percent had experienced burnout and 71 percent had considered quitting, with algorithm changes the top stressor, and Vibely sells a creator-community product. Tom Scott posted weekly toward a ten-year goal and stopped at it rather than build a managed team, and MatPat cited a decade of work-first priorities in stepping back. A single multi-year build compounds the risk, because a stalled build is a stalled channel, and the creators who finished, in the accounts collected by Love Thy Nerd and Study Breaks, treat consistency as more important than frequency and promise no cadence the project cannot keep. The burnout signs and the winter lull are the body-and-mind chapter's.

A single build ends, so its knowledge product is a complete package priced once rather than a monthly drip, and the channel's brand decides what happens at completion. Odd Life Crafting, a Brazilian couple, documented the build of their shipping-container house, then carried the same 238,000-subscriber audience into the refit of a 44-foot steel sailboat, a year and a half of sailing, and a new 43-foot aluminum build in France, because their positioning was the couple's ongoing projects and never the house. The Cob Cottage Company, started by Ianto Evans and Linda Smiley, describes itself as a loose collective and has taught for decades past its founders' day-to-day involvement, and Quail Springs Permaculture kept its natural-building program running after Sasha Rabin, who led it for about a decade, moved on. An institution survives a departure and transfers as curriculum, land and a teaching roster. A founder's brand moves faster, sells coaching on personal trust, and transfers with difficulty, because its asset is the audience's relationship with one person, and the comparison is structural rather than priced.

The moderation policy, the redirect of every site-specific safety question to a local professional, and a dated correction on the page of any video found wrong are the channel's version of the citation discipline, and the corrections log is a record a sponsor or a complainant can be shown.
