## 2. The Issues, Each With Evidence And Its Fix

### Issue 1: Sentence bloat, now severe

Chapter 19 (written 2026-07-09): "First, Oregon's code structure is not the IRC's: the ORSC adopts the residential mechanical and energy chapters but not the plumbing and electrical chapters, so plumbing tests are cited to the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code (OPSC) and electrical tests to the Oregon Electrical Specialty Code (OESC), never to ORSC plumbing or electrical sections." That is one 55-word sentence, and it is typical of the batch.

Fix: the Part 1 sentence bands in the style guide (`Simple` under 14 average, `Applied` under 15, `Professional` under 22, nothing over 25 in `Simple`). The same sentence in the target voice: "Oregon splits its codes differently than the national model. Plumbing tests come from the OPSC and electrical tests from the OESC. The ORSC only covers the building, mechanical, and energy sides."

### Issue 2: The Simple layer has collapsed

Labeled block counts per chapter show the problem directly. Chapters 9 and 10 carry 7 Simple blocks each. Chapters 15 through 21 carry exactly 1 each, against 13 to 15 Applied, Professional, and Code blocks. The layer written for readers with no construction background, the layer the owner's brother reads, has nearly vanished from the second half of the book.

Fix: Revision 6 restores a Simple block to every section a non-builder might want to understand, per the expanded Simple definition in the style guide. Chapter 9 is the model for balance. The skip test is narrow: pure reference tables only.

### Issue 3: Quota-pattern layering and layer echo

In chapters 11 through 21, the Applied, Professional, and Code counts are identical in almost every chapter (13/13/13, 15/15/15, 11/11/11). Every section mechanically received all three, and in the fast final batch the three often say the same thing. Chapter 19.1 states the testing tracks in Applied, restates them in Professional, then restates them again inside the Code fence.

Fix: the choosing-layers procedure in the style guide. Write Applied first; add the others only when they contribute something new; delete echoes. Expected result: fewer, better blocks.

### Issue 4: Code blocks degraded into recap boxes in the final batch

28 fenced Code blocks across the manuscript contain no concrete citation at all. Distribution: chapter 20 has 9 of its 12, chapter 21 has 3, chapter 15 has 3, chapters 10, 13, 16, 18, and 19 have 2 each, chapters 3, 14, and 17 have 1 each. Chapters 1-9, 11, and 12 are clean. Example from 19.1: "Tests classified by who performs them: owner-performed, certified-party-required, and diagnostics (not compliance)." That is the section's own advice inside a code fence, with no source.

Fix: the three-part Code entry format now specified in the style guide (requirement, exact point, source line with book name and code number). Every entry ends with a source or leaves the block. The 28 recap boxes either gain real sources during the Revision 6 pass or their content moves into Applied/Professional where it belongs.

### Issue 5: Costs still missing

104 dollar figures in 126,562 words (8 per 10k) against Revision 2's 37 per 10k. Whole trades are discussed with no money attached. The excitement the owner wants ("slightly exciting but straightforward") is sitting unused in Revision 2's cost framing.

Fix: every section that involves buying, hiring, renting, or testing names a dollar range with the estimate year, mined from Revision 2/3 and the research files, marked as planning ranges. Volatile prices stay ranges with years, which the chapter 16 verification principle already allows.

### Issue 6: The reader disappeared

Eleven uses of "you/your" in the entire manuscript, 3 questions per 10k words. Revision 2 ran 295 and 8. The prose reads like documentation written about the house rather than to the builder of it.

Fix: imperatives already carry Applied; Revision 6 re-admits "you" and one framing question per section in Simple and Applied, per the style guide dosage rules.

### Issue 7: Personification creep in the fast batch

The 2026-07-09 chapters reintroduce mild personification the scans missed: "how the house proves itself" (ch 19), "the required-by-source matrix keeps the program honest" (ch 19), "without letting a sales counter, a shortage, a substitute product, or an old price quietly change the house" (ch 16). None are Revision-4-grade, but the trend shows the pattern returns under speed.

Fix: the lint screen plus the rewrite pass. These become "how the house is tested and proven," "the matrix keeps the program complete," "before a sales counter substitution changes the house unnoticed" or plainer.

### Issue 8: Serial-list sentences as chapter openers

Every chapter opens with the same formula: "This chapter covers X: [eight to ten noun phrases]." Chapter 16's opener chains eight abstract phrases in one sentence. Done 21 times, the formula numbs.

Fix: chapter overviews become two to four short sentences: what the chapter is, the one or two decisions it drives, and where adjacent topics live. Lists of contents belong to the table of contents.

### Issue 10: Quiet performance (found in the first calibration batch, 2026-07-10)

The first Revision 6 calibration batch fixed sentence length and citation format but retained performance in subtler forms, caught by external review (a DeepSeek pass the owner ran on chapter 1). The forms: gnomic wisdom lines ("A house is the sum of thousands of small decisions"), balanced sentence pairs and isocolon ("Internet improves the house. Internet does not run the house."; "build the shell so it can stay, and route the service systems so they can change"), chiasmus ("A cheap choice that causes hidden long-term damage is not cheap"), hedge stacks ("Informally... not every detail will literally last that long"), unanchored size words ("costs little... costs a lot"), and scene-painting vignettes that imply claims without stating them ("You can hand one chapter to a contractor and have a useful conversation"). None tripped the surface-pattern lint.

Fix: the guide's Voice Target and absolutes were rewritten 2026-07-10 to ban the function, not the pattern. Every sentence states a requirement, states a fact, gives an instruction, gives a value, or names a source. The quotable test enforces it: quotable gets rewritten until usable (an action, a value, or a constraint). Claims state property plus mechanism, never scene plus implication. Register is fixed per layer: explanation lives only in `Simple` and design-rationale notes. Guards against overcorrection: no invented numbers to satisfy the quantification rule; real trade terms (load path, permit path) are not metaphors; the register target is direct instruction, not committee legalese.

### Issue 11: The root-metaphor system underneath the prose (second external review, 2026-07-10)

The first register correction removed the visible rhetoric but not the conceptual metaphors that generate it. The second external review, run on the corrected chapter 1, identified four false root metaphors still producing personification: the book as a person or conversation partner ("carries," "answers," "has jobs," "the book says"), information as a moving physical object ("travels with it," "goes into the binder"), the reader as a guest in staged scenes ("open one chapter while working on that system"), and specifications as performances (contrastive negation against unstated defaults, permission-hedges, requirements attributed to abstract entities instead of actors). The owner's directive: the book does not do anything; it is a structured reference, and text never takes action.

Fix: the Root Metaphors section added to `writing_style_guide.md` (approved document-subject verbs, information present-or-absent, imperatives-yes-scenes-no, actor-plus-action requirements), fossil rows added to the failure tables, lint patterns added (text-as-agent verbs, unanchored comparatives, unattributed "by design"), and judgment checks 8 and 9 added. Chapter 1 rewritten under the full rule set 2026-07-10; chapters 2 and 3 still need the same excavation pass before R6-3 begins.

### Issue 12: Verb-object mismatches and compressed context (third external review, 2026-07-10)

The third review, on the root-metaphor-corrected chapter 1, rated the register roughly 95% pure and identified two remaining defect classes. First, verb-object agreement failures under conjunction: "Recorded decisions can be built, inspected, and repaired" attaches physical-work verbs to an information noun, skipping the work product entirely; the reader must silently reinterpret the subject for each verb. Second, compressed causal chains and cross-paragraph referents that assume the reader holds the writer's context. The owner also fixed the boundary for all future passes: the first-person opening, "This book is the plan," and the "thousand-year structural shell" label are the owner's own words and stay; a protected-lines list now exists in the style guide.

Fix: the Verb-Object Agreement and Reader's Context sections in the style guide, judgment checks 10-12, format repairs in chapter 1 (vertical lists where colons introduced run-on imperatives, the worked example moved out of the durability principle row, the wood-exclusion causal chain restated, the metadiscursive verb-list sentence removed), and information-only expansions (the record-transferability chain written out link by link; the structural means of the thousand-year goal restored from Revision 2).

### Issue 9: Front matter and appendices are stale Revision 5 leftovers

`g0_fm_00` (581 bytes), the table of contents, the standards index, and the glossary predate the Phase 2 rewrite. This is known, deferred closeout work, not a writing-style issue; it waits for the owner's polish declaration per the TODO.
