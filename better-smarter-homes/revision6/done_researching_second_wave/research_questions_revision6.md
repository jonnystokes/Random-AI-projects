**(b) Dishwasher circuits in Oregon: GFCI protection is NOT required in dwellings.** The redirect target, 422.5(A), is itself amended (Table 1-E, PDF page 12, /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/23oesc-table1-E.pdf): items (1) through (5) remain unchanged; item (6) becomes "Sump pumps {added: and sewage pumps}" with an added exception for a labeled single receptacle; and item (7) reads verbatim "(7) {struck: Dishwashers} {added: Not adopted by the State of Oregon.}" So Oregon's 210.8(D) points only to 422.5, and Oregon's 422.5 no longer lists dishwashers; a dwelling dishwasher branch circuit or outlet, hardwired or cord-and-plug, needs no GFCI under the 2023 OESC. No location rule catches the typical under-counter receptacle either (Table 1-E, PDF page 3): Oregon rewrote 210.8(A)(6) to "Kitchens {added: — where the receptacles are installed to serve the countertop surfaces}" and marked 210.8(A)(7) (areas with sinks and food/beverage-preparation provisions) "{added: Not adopted by the State of Oregon}"; the errata-added Exception No. 5 to 210.8(A) (tagged "Errata Jan. 1, 2025") further exempts a labeled single appliance receptacle in a dedicated space for locations (A)(2), (5), (6), (8), and (11). "Agent" in these rules means DEQ or the contract county that administers the onsite program locally; most Oregon counties are contract counties under ORS 454.725 and adopt their own fee schedules (OAR 340-071-0140(9)), so dollar amounts vary by county.

**(a) As-built plan at installation.** The rules use the term "as-built plan"; "record drawing" does not appear anywhere in division 71 or 73 (verified by search of both local files).
- OAR 340-071-0170(3)(a): to request the pre-cover inspection, the system installer must submit to the agent "a detailed and accurate as-built plan of the constructed system," plus (3)(b) a list of all materials used and (3)(c) a signed certification on a DEQ-approved form that the system was built as the permit, division 71, and division 73 require. (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/oar/oar-340-071-full.html)
- OAR 340-071-0170(1)-(2): the pre-cover inspection is mandatory before backfill unless the agent waives it; waiver is allowed for single-family-dwelling systems installed by a sewage disposal service business licensed under ORS 454.695, with photographs submitted on request.
- OAR 340-071-0400(5)(d)(A): in low-precipitation areas east of the Cascades (parcels 10 acres or larger, 20 inches annual precipitation or less), the agent may waive the pre-cover inspection if the installer submits the same as-built plan, materials list, and certification. Geographic dependency: this waiver route exists only in those areas.
- OAR 340-071-0302(5)(a): for recirculating gravel filter (RGF) systems, the designer must give the owner and agent an operation and maintenance manual at installation that includes "as-built plans with the name and contact number of the installer." This is the only rule that puts an as-built directly in the owner's hands; for other system types the as-built goes into the agent's permit file.
- OAR 340-071-0345(13): for ATT systems the designer must give the owner a comprehensive owner's manual (system specifications, installation, O&M, troubleshooting) before or at installation; the rule does not use the term as-built.
- OAR 340-073 contains no as-built, record, or maintenance obligations; it is tank and component construction standards only (340-073-0025 through 340-073-0085, all amended effective 01/01/2026). (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/oar/oar-340-073-full.html)

**(b) Certificate of Satisfactory Completion (CSC).** OAR 340-071-0175, amended effective 01/01/2026. (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/oar/oar-340-071-full.html)

- 0175(3): if the agent does not inspect within 7 days after notification of completion, or waived inspection under 340-071-0170(2) or 340-071-0400(5)(d), the CSC "will be deemed to have been issued by operation of law" and a modified Certificate is issued to the owner.

(/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/oar/oar-340-071-full.html)

- Contract content and service frequency, 0132(2): contracts must be "perpetual and continuous" until decommissioning; for newly permitted systems, inspect/adjust/service at least once every 6 months for the first two years (2)(a), then per the manufacturer's approved owner's manual and at least once every 12 months (2)(b). Provider must notify owner and agent in writing within 30 days of any problem not fixable at inspection, (2)(d).

**(d) Owner obligations at sale or transfer.** Division 71 contains no general point-of-sale septic inspection or reporting requirement (searched "sale," "transfer," "ownership," "conveyance" in the full text). What does apply:
- Service-contract continuity: because the contract must run until decommissioning (OAR 340-071-0132(2)), the obligation lands on each successive owner. The rule allows a "contract transition period of not more than 30 days" before the agent may order a compliance inspection and fee, 0132(6)(b); DEQ's O&M fact sheet states it plainly: "If you sell or buy a property, or your contract is terminated, you or the new owner have 30 days to get under contract" (https://www.oregon.gov/deq/Residential/Documents/onsiteOpMaintReq2024fs.pdf).
- Unapproved-technology systems only: before transferring ownership, the owner must notify all transferees that the technology is not approved, and the transferee must agree in writing to hold the State of Oregon harmless, OAR 340-071-0135(4)(d).
- Voluntary at-sale tool: the Existing System Evaluation Report, OAR 340-071-0155, is the inspection product typically ordered in real-estate transactions; it is not mandated by the rules. Qualified evaluators are listed in 0155(1)(a); new for 2026, a NAWT-accredited inspector must also hold a sewage disposal service license (0155(1)(a)(E); DEQ general fact sheet).
- Related but not sale-triggered: an Authorization Notice is required before placing into service, reconnecting, connecting an accessory dwelling unit to, changing the use of, or increasing flow into an existing system, OAR 340-071-0205(1). A construction permit can be transferred to a new person (name change only) before it expires, OAR 340-071-0160(6)(b). CSC conditions run with the system in use, OAR 340-071-0160(5).

**(e) New in the 2026 rules on these topics** (per rule history lines and the two DEQ fact sheets):

- Owners of contracted alternative systems no longer submit annual reports or pay the annual report evaluation fee; that duty moved solely to the maintenance provider, OAR 340-071-0132(3)(f) and (4)(a)-(b); DEQ O&M fact sheet: "No more annual reports or fees for owners." Holding-tank owners still report and pay, 0132(4)(d).

## Part 2: The statewide wildfire map — SB 762 framework, rise and repeal

- SB 762 (2021), Oregon Laws 2021 ch. 592, created the statewide map-driven framework: a statewide wildfire risk map under former ORS 477.490, mandatory State Fire Marshal defensible space standards under former ORS 476.392, and mandatory home-hardening building code standards under former ORS 455.612, which required DCBS to apply R327 to new dwellings and accessory structures "for the high wildfire hazard zone in the wildland-urban interface" identified on the statewide map (former text at https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_455.612).
- Map v1 (risk map, 5 classes) was released June 30, 2022 by ODF/OSU and withdrawn Aug 4, 2022 after roughly 750 appeals and insurance-cancellation backlash: https://www.opb.org/article/2022/08/05/oregon-wildfire-prevention-map-risk-forest-fire-insurance/
- SB 80 (2023) renamed it a "hazard" map and cut five risk classes to three hazard zones (high/moderate/low); SB 82 (2023) separately barred insurers from using any state map for rating/underwriting: https://oregonpropertyowners.org/guest-post-the-oregon-wildfire-hazard-map-is-back-and-it-cant-affect-your-insurance/
- Map v2 (final Statewide Wildfire Hazard Map) was adopted Jan 7, 2025; ODF sent certified-mail notices to owners of tax lots designated high hazard inside the WUI: https://news.oregonstate.edu/news/updated-wildfire-hazard-map-provides-landowners-policymakers-objective-data-inform-wildfire
- SB 83 (2025) repealed the entire statewide scheme: passed the Senate unanimously (April 2025) and the House 50-1 (June 2025), approved by Governor Kotek July 24, 2025, filed July 28, 2025, effective on passage July 24, 2025 (emergency clause). Enacted as Oregon Laws 2025 ch. 590: https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/lawsstatutes/2025orlaw0590.pdf ; OLIS overview: https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2025R1/Measures/Overview/SB83 . Key operative parts: Section 1 repeals ORS 477.490 (the map), ORS 455.612 and 455.614 (map-triggered home hardening), and ORS 476.390/476.394 (map-triggered defensible space); Section 8 declares every ODF order assigning property to a wildfire hazard zone "null and void"; Section 7 (codified as ORS 455.433) creates the local-adoption R327 pathway described in Part 1; Section 2 (amending ORS 476.392) converts the State Fire Marshal's role to a voluntary model defensible space code consistent with the 2024 International Wildland-Urban Interface Code §§603-604 that the state "may not require a local government to adopt"; the seller's property disclosure WUI question was struck (ORS 105.464 amendment). Law-firm summary: https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2025/07/oregon-repeals-statewide-wildfire-hazard-map-and-mitigation-requirements

## Part 3: What a builder must do at design time (July 2026) — jurisdiction-dependent

There is no statewide wildfire map and no statewide R327 mandate. Whether wildfire-hardening construction rules apply to a rural parcel depends entirely on which municipality operates the building inspection program for that parcel and whether it has adopted R327 by ordinance for an area that includes the parcel (the ordinance may define the area by map or by written description — BCD guidance, https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Documents/23orsc-r327-localadoption.pdf).

Steps:
1. Check BCD's "Home hardening and wildfire hazard mitigation" page for the current list of adopting municipalities: https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/pages/wildfire-hazard-mitigation.aspx . As of 2026-07-09 it lists five: Ashland, Bend, Deschutes County, Grants Pass, and Sisters. Known effective dates: unincorporated Deschutes County and the City of Sisters, all new dwellings and new accessory structures permitted on or after April 1, 2026 (https://www.deschutes.org/cd/page/fire-hardening-requirements-r327); City of Bend, ordinance adopted April 15, 2026, effective May 15, 2026, new detached residential construction (https://www.bendsource.com/business/businessnews/city-of-bend-adopts-residential-wildfire-mitigation-building-code-effective-may-15/). This list will grow; do not treat it as static.
2. Ask the building department that serves the parcel (the city department if the city runs its own program, otherwise the county building department) two questions in writing: has ORSC R327 been locally adopted, and is this tax lot inside the designated area. Only the local building department can enforce R327 (delegated authority under ORS 455.148, per the BCD guidance), so the local building official is the party who determines and states parcel applicability in writing (permit pre-application response, code interpretation letter, or the plan-review record). BCD in Salem confirms which jurisdictions have adopted but does not make parcel determinations. The adopting ordinance must contain a local appeal process (R327.1.1 item 3) if the owner disputes the designation, and the building official may approve alternate materials/methods under ORSC R104.11.
3. Separately ask the county/city planning department about land-use wildfire overlays and any locally adopted defensible space code (the OSFM model code is voluntary and adopted jurisdiction by jurisdiction), since those are independent of the building code.

County/site dependency, stated plainly: identical rural parcels in different counties face different rules today — a new house outside Sisters (Deschutes County) must comply with R327 in full; a comparable parcel in most other counties currently has no wildfire-hardening construction requirement at all unless and until that jurisdiction adopts R327. For a concrete-walled house, R327's exterior-wall requirement (R327.3.3 item 1, noncombustible material) is inherently satisfied; the cost-relevant items become roofing class, ember-resistant venting, protected eaves/projections, deck surfaces, and glazing.

Requirements are statutorily pinned to R327 of the 2023 ORSC (ORS 455.433); a future ORSC edition or legislative session can change this framework, and the list of adopting jurisdictions changes over time.

## (c) Pressure-test acceptance criteria in the adopted text

Oregon replaced the model IRC criteria. The model's G2417.4.1 (1-1/2 times working pressure, 3 psig minimum) and G2417.4.2 (10-minute minimum duration) do not apply in Oregon; the model duration/purging sections G2417.4.2 through G2417.7.3.1 are deleted (26orsc-matrix.pdf entry quoted above; for the model values, see for example the model-code explainer at https://www.jaspector.com/codes/irc-2024/ch24-fuel-gas/gas-leak-test-irc-2024/). The adopted 2023 ORSC text (all quotes from https://up.codes/viewer/oregon/irc-2021/chapter/24/fuel-gas, fetched 2026-07-09):

- **G2417.4.1 Test Pressure (Oregon amendment):** "Gas piping systems under 14 inches water column (3.48 kPa) pressure shall be tested at a pressure of not less than 10 pounds per square inch (69 kPa). Test pressures shall be held for not less than 15 minutes with no perceptible drop in pressure. For welded piping and for piping carrying gas at pressures exceeding 14 inches water column (3.48 kPa) pressure, the test pressure shall be at least 60 pounds per square inch (414 kPa) for not less than 30 minutes. Exception: Testing, inspection and purging of gas piping systems performed in accordance with NFPA 58." A standard residential system (supply pressure 7 to 11 inches water column, below the 14-inch threshold) therefore tests at not less than 10 psi held not less than 15 minutes with no perceptible drop.
- **G2417.4 (406.4) Test Pressure Measurement (gauge requirements, Oregon-amended):** "Test pressure shall be measured with a manometer or with a pressure-measuring device designed and calibrated to read, record, or indicate a pressure loss caused by leakage during the pressure test period. The source of pressure shall be isolated before the pressure tests are made. Mechanical gauges used to measure test pressures shall use a 15-pounds-per-square-inch (103 kPa) gauge or a 30-pounds-per-square-inch (206 kPa) gauge for testing 14 inches water column (3.48 kPa) systems and a 100-pounds-per-square-inch (689 kPa) gauge for testing systems exceeding 14 inches water column (3.48 kPa) operating pressure. The gauges shall not exceed the manufacturer's listing and labeling." (The model rule, a gauge scale no greater than five times the test pressure, is replaced by these fixed gauge sizes.)
- **G2417.2 (406.2) Test Medium:** "The test medium shall be air, nitrogen, carbon dioxide or an inert gas. Oxygen shall not be used as a test medium."
- **G2417.1 (406.1) General:** all piping installations must be visually inspected and pressure tested before acceptance and initial operation. **G2417.3 (406.3):** pipe joints, including welds, left exposed during the test (exception for previously tested concealed end joints). **G2417.3.3/G2417.3.4:** appliances rated below the test pressure must be disconnected and outlets capped; appliances rated at or above it may be isolated by their shutoff valves.
- **G2417.1.1.1 Rough Piping Inspection and G2417.1.1.2 Final Piping Inspection (Oregon-added):** rough inspection after piping is installed and before it is covered, concealed, or connected to a fixture or appliance; final inspection after concealment and attachment of fixtures, appliances, or shutoff valves. **G2401.2.1 (Oregon-added):** "A serving gas supplier or person furnishing gas shall not turn on, or cause to be turned on, fuel gas or gas meters until the approval of the rough piping inspection is in compliance with Section G2417.1.1.1."
- **G2417.1.2 Repairs and Additions:** work done after the pressure test requires retesting the affected piping; minor repairs and additions may instead be inspected and leak-checked with noncorrosive leak-detecting fluid or other approved methods.

**Answered (2026-07-09):**

**Governing rule set.** Oregon backflow tester certification and backflow assembly test timing live in OAR chapter 333, division 61 (Oregon Health Authority drinking water rules). The division applies to public water systems: OAR 333-061-0010 (Scope) states the rules apply to all public water systems providing piped water for human consumption (https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_333-061-0010). Statutory authority shown on the rules is ORS 448.131, plus ORS 448.279 for tester certification (https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/view.action?ruleNumber=333-061-0072).

**Test timing: at installation and annually.** OAR 333-061-0070 (Cross Connection Control Requirements), section (6): the water supplier must ensure that inspections of approved air gaps and inspections and tests of approved backflow prevention assemblies protecting the public water system are conducted: at the time of installation, repair or relocation; at least once every 12 months after installation; more frequently than every 12 months for assemblies that repeatedly fail or that protect health hazard cross connections, as determined by the water supplier; after a backflow incident; and after an approved air gap is re-plumbed (https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/view.action?ruleNumber=333-061-0070). **Who must be certified.** OAR 333-061-0070(16): "Backflow prevention assemblies must be tested by Authority-certified Backflow Assembly Testers, journeyman plumbers or apprentice plumbers." Certification mechanics under the same rule: Authority-approved training course, written exam score of at least 70 percent, physical-performance exam score of at least 90 percent, $195 fee, renewal every 2 years, with yearly test gauge accuracy verification or calibration reports required at renewal (https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_333-061-0072).

OAR 333-061-0074: Cross Connection Training Programs, Course, and Instructor Requirements (https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_333-061-0074).

11. **Owner-builder trade-permit scope, reconciled.** The legal research (done_researching, chapters 1-4 file) answered the ORS 693.020 plumbing and ORS 479.540 electrical owner exemptions; the testing-chapter research asked for the same cites again. Reconcile into one statement with exact subsections, including the mechanical side (OAR 918-440), so every chapter cites the same anchors. Library: `ors/ors693.html`, `ors/ors479.html`.

**Answered (2026-07-09):**

**Reconciled statement (use this wording and these anchors in every chapter).** An Oregon owner may personally perform the plumbing, electrical, and mechanical work on the owner's own dwelling without holding any trade license, under three separate authorities: plumbing under ORS 693.020(1)(a) together with ORS 447.060(1)(a); electrical under ORS 479.540(1), which adds the conditions that the property be residential or farm property owned by the person or a member of the person's immediate family and "not intended for sale, exchange, lease or rent"; and mechanical, for which Oregon statute creates no trade license at all, so the only gate is the mechanical permit at ORSC Section R105.1, with contractor (CCB) licensing inapplicable to owners under ORS 701.010(7). None of the three waives permits or inspections: ORS 693.020(4) and ORS 447.020(1) keep plumbing permits and inspections in force, ORS 479.540(1)(a) with ORS 479.550(1) keeps electrical permits in force, and ORSC R105.1 is itself the mechanical permit requirement.

**(a) Plumbing: the license exemption is ORS 693.020(1)(a), with ORS 447.060 as the companion.** ORS 693.020 (Persons and activities not regulated under chapter), subsection (1)(a): this chapter does not apply to "A person working on a building or premises owned by the person, regardless of whether the person holds a license under this chapter, if the person complies with all the rules adopted under this chapter and ORS 447.010 to 447.156 and ORS chapter 455." (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors693.html; https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors693.html). Conditions: the person must own the building or premises (occupancy is not required and is not mentioned), and the work must comply with the plumbing rules, the plumbing permit and inspection statutes (ORS 447.010 to 447.156), and the state building code statutes (ORS chapter 455). There is no for-sale or for-rent condition on a residence in the plumbing statute. The only sale-related restriction is ORS 693.020(2), and it reaches commercial and industrial buildings only: "Subsection (1)(a) to (d) of this section does not allow a person other than a journeyman plumber or apprentice plumber to install, remodel or alter plumbing in a commercial or industrial building being constructed or offered for sale, exchange, rent or lease," where "install, remodel or alter" means work inside a wall, floor, crawl space or ceiling, or a change in the configuration of a plumbing system (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors693.html). Permits and inspections survive the exemption by the statute's own terms: ORS 693.020(4): "Except as provided in subsection (1)(d) of this section, nothing in this section exempts a person from the plumbing inspection requirements of ORS 447.010 to 447.156." A secondary repair-only exemption is ORS 693.020(1)(e) (a person who owns, leases or operates residential property may repair, or use regular employees to repair, existing plumbing; "repair" defined at (1)(e)(A) as restoring the existing system to safe and sanitary operating condition).

**The exemption partly lives in ORS 447 as well.** ORS 447.060(1)(a): "ORS 447.010 to 447.156 do not apply to a person: (a) Engaging in plumbing work when not so engaged for hire," and (1)(b) extends this to work by the person's regular employees on property the person owns, leases or operates (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors447.html; https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors447.html). The business-level license the owner is being excused from is the plumbing contractor license at ORS 447.040(1); the trade-level license is the journeyman plumber license at ORS 693.030(1). The installation itself must still conform regardless of who performs it: ORS 447.020(1) requires all installations of plumbing and drainage in buildings in this state to be made in accordance with ORS 447.010 to 447.156, 447.992 and ORS chapter 455 (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors447.html).

**(b) Electrical: the license exemption is ORS 479.540(1).** Quote: "Except as otherwise provided in this subsection, a person is not required to obtain a license to make an electrical installation on residential or farm property that is owned by the person or a member of the person's immediate family if the property is not intended for sale, exchange, lease or rent." (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors479.html; https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors479.html). Conditions: (1) residential or farm property; (2) owned by the person or a member of the person's immediate family (ownership, not occupancy; "immediate family" is used but not defined in the ORS 479.530 definitions list); (3) the property is not intended for sale, exchange, lease or rent at the time of the work. Two subsections qualify it: ORS 479.540(1)(a): the exemption "does not exempt the work performed by the person from having to comply with the requirements for such work under ORS chapter 455 or this chapter and rules adopted thereunder," which keeps permits, inspections and the electrical code in force; and ORS 479.540(1)(b): if the residence is for rent, lease, sale or exchange, the exemption shrinks to maintenance work on existing installations and does not cover "new electrical installations or substantial alterations" (with a narrow landlord carve-out for replacing an existing garbage disposal, dishwasher or electric water heater of 30 amps or less, single phase). The permit chain for the owner: ORS 479.550(1): "Except as provided in ORS 479.540, no person shall work on any new electrical installation for which a permit has not been issued," and ORS 479.560(1)(a) directs the Department of Consumer and Business Services or its designated agent to issue a permit to "Any applicant who has complied with ORS 479.510 to 479.945 and the rules issued thereunder, covering an electrical installation to be made on residential or farm property owned by the applicant or a member of the applicant's immediate family as allowed under ORS 479.540 (1)" (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors479.html). A separate permit-side exemption, ORS 479.540(15), waives the permit for maintenance-only work on existing electrical installations on residential property owned by the person or immediate family, but expressly not for new installations or substantial alterations.

**(c) Mechanical: no trade license exists; the owner takes the permit directly under the ORSC.** No Oregon statute creates a mechanical trade license parallel to ORS chapter 693/447 (plumbing) or ORS 479.630 (electrical); the mechanical side is a permit-and-inspection program only (ORS 455.058(1) lists "electrical, gas, mechanical, elevator, boiler, plumbing or other systems" as the permit programs; the statute is reprinted after Section R105.4 in the ORSC chapter 1 PDF, /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf). For a dwelling, the governing permit text is 2023 ORSC Section R105.1: "Any owner or owner's authorized agent who intends to construct, enlarge, alter, repair, move or change the occupancy of a building or structure, or to erect, install, enlarge, alter, repair, remove, convert or replace any gas or mechanical system, the installation of which is regulated by this code, or to cause any such work to be performed, shall first make application to the building official and obtain the required permit" (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf). The owner is therefore an eligible mechanical and gas permit applicant by the code's own wording. Permit-exempt mechanical work is listed in ORSC R105.2 under "Mechanical:" items 1 through 8 (portable heating, cooking, clothes-drying and ventilation appliances; portable cooling units; steam, hot- or chilled-water piping within regulated equipment; replacement of any minor part that does not alter approval or make equipment unsafe; portable evaporative coolers; self-contained refrigeration systems with 10 pounds or less of refrigerant or motors of 1 horsepower or less; portable fuel-cell appliances), and ORSC R105.2.1 allows emergency mechanical replacements and repairs with the permit application submitted within 5 working business days (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf). On the rule side, OAR chapter 918 division 440 (Mechanical Specialty Code) contains no licensing or homeowner restriction: the division adopts the Oregon Mechanical Specialty Code (OAR 918-440-0010), amends it (918-440-0012) and sets fees (918-440-0050), and its only certification rule, OAR 918-440-0015 (Refrigeration Installer Certification, brazing and welding on refrigeration piping), states "Refrigeration systems installed in dwelling units regulated under the Oregon Residential Specialty Code are exempt from this rule" (https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_918-440-0015; division index https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_chapter_918_division_440; SOS source https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/displayDivisionRules.action?selectedDivision=4168; local index /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/oar/oar-918-440.html). Business-level licensing does not reach the owner either: ORS 701.021(1) requires a CCB license only of a person who undertakes work "as a contractor," and ORS 701.010(7) exempts "A person performing work on a property that person owns or performing work as the owner's employee, whether the property is occupied by the owner or not, or a person performing work on that person's residence, whether or not that person owns the residence," except where "the work is performed in the pursuit of an independent business with the intent of offering the structure for sale before, upon or after completion" (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors701.html; https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors701.html).

**Boiler caution for hydronic systems.** A boiler or pressure vessel in a private residence is exempt from the Boiler and Pressure Vessel Law "except as to all provisions relating to construction, installation, alteration or repair and to installation permits" (ORS 480.525(3)(c)), and the boiler contractor license attaches only to "A person engaging in the business of installing, repairing or altering boilers or pressure vessels" (ORS 480.630(1)); ORS 480.525(5) further lets the department assign single-family boiler inspections to specialty-code inspectors and exempts those units from periodic inspection (ORS 480.560) and operating permits (ORS 480.585) (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors480.html). Chapters discussing hydronic heat should flag the installation permit as still required.

**The asymmetry chapters must not blur.** Only the electrical exemption carries the intent test ("not intended for sale, exchange, lease or rent," ORS 479.540(1)); the plumbing exemption has no residential for-sale condition (its sale language, ORS 693.020(2), reaches commercial and industrial buildings only); the mechanical side has no license to be exempted from. All three trades still require permits and inspections for an owner-builder's new dwelling work.

12. **Statute of repose.** ORS 12.135: the period, what starts the clock, and what it covers for an owner-built home later sold. Library: `ors/ors012.html`.

**Answered (2026-07-09):**

**Source and edition.** ORS 12.135 (Action for damages from construction, alteration or repair of real property), 2025 Edition of ORS chapter 12 (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors012.html; https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors012.html). The section was amended and renumbered by 2025 c.578 §1, so subsection numbers below differ from most secondary sources; see the renumbering warning at the end.

**Repose periods.** ORS 12.135(2): "An action against a person by a plaintiff who is not a public body, whether in contract, tort or otherwise, arising from the person having performed the construction, alteration or repair of any improvement to real property or the supervision or inspection thereof, or from the person having furnished design, planning, surveying, architectural or engineering services for the improvement, must be commenced before the earliest of: (a) The applicable period of limitation otherwise established by law; (b) Ten years after substantial completion or abandonment of the construction, alteration or repair of a small commercial structure or residential structure; or (c) Six years after substantial completion or abandonment of the construction, alteration or repair of a large commercial structure." (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors012.html). So: ten years for residential and small commercial structures, six years for large commercial structures. The structure definitions are borrowed: ORS 12.135(1)(c) gives "large commercial structure," "residential structure" and "small commercial structure" the meanings in ORS 701.005. Public-body plaintiffs get a flat ten years for any structure type under ORS 12.135(3). Homeowners associations and associations of unit owners get a special tort window under ORS 12.135(4): seven years after substantial completion or abandonment, or, if the defect is discovered more than six but less than seven years after substantial completion, one year after discovery. Actions against registered architects, landscape architects and engineers are governed by ORS 12.135(5): the earliest of two years after the injury or damage is first discovered or reasonably should have been discovered, ten years after substantial completion (small commercial or residential structure), or six years (large commercial structure).

**What starts the clock.** ORS 12.135(1)(e): "'Substantial completion' means the earliest of: (A) The date when the contractee accepts in writing the construction, alteration or repair of the improvement to real property or any designated portion thereof as having reached that state of completion when it may be used or occupied for its intended purpose or, if there is no such written acceptance, the date of acceptance of the completed construction, alteration or repair of such improvement by the contractee; (B) The date when a public body issues a certificate of occupancy for the improvement; or (C) The date when the owner occupies the improvement or uses it for its intended purpose." (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors012.html). Abandonment is fixed by ORS 12.135(6), which keys it to the date the improvement is considered abandoned under ORS 87.045. For an owner-built home there is normally no contractee acceptance, so in practice the earlier of the certificate-of-occupancy date, (1)(e)(B), and the date the owner first occupies or uses the home, (1)(e)(C), starts the ten years.

**Claims covered.** Any action "whether in contract, tort or otherwise" arising from performing the construction, alteration or repair of an improvement to real property, or the supervision or inspection of it, or furnishing design, planning, surveying, architectural or engineering services for it (ORS 12.135(2) and (3)). ORS 12.135(7)(a) extends the section to actions against manufacturers, distributors, sellers and lessors of manufactured dwellings (ORS 446.003) and prefabricated structures (ORS 455.010). The section caps but never extends the ordinary limitation periods: subsection (2)(a) makes "the applicable period of limitation otherwise established by law" the first candidate for the earliest deadline, and ORS 12.080(1) and (3) (six-year periods for contract and for injury to interests in real property) expressly except "those mentioned in ORS 12.135" from their own coverage (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors012.html).

**Application to an owner-built home later sold.** Two phases. While the owner-builder still owns and occupies the home, ORS 12.135(7)(b) removes the shield: the section "Does not apply to actions against any person in actual possession and control of the improvement, as owner, tenant or otherwise, at the time such cause of action accrues" (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors012.html). After the sale, the former owner-builder is a "person having performed the construction" who is no longer in possession and control, so a buyer's claim, in contract, tort or otherwise, arising from that construction is barred at the earliest of the ordinary limitation period, ORS 12.135(2)(a), or ten years after substantial completion, ORS 12.135(2)(b), with the ten years for an owner-built home usually running from the certificate of occupancy or first occupancy under ORS 12.135(1)(e)(B) and (C), whichever came first. Practical consequence worth stating in the manuscript: the repose clock on an owner-built home starts at completion and occupancy, not at the later sale, so a house sold nine years after its certificate of occupancy carries only about one year of residual construction-claim exposure under this section, while the seller's separate disclosure and fraud exposure is governed by other statutes, not by ORS 12.135.

**Renumbering warning (why the edition matters).** Before the 2025 amendment (2025 c.578 §1), the ten-year and six-year repose periods sat in ORS 12.135(1)(a), the definitions in ORS 12.135(4), and there was no homeowners-association tort window; the oregon.public.law snapshot, current only through the 2024 regular session, still shows that older structure (https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_12.135). All book cross-references should use the 2025 Edition numbering quoted above: (1)(e) substantial completion, (2)(b) ten-year residential repose, (3) public bodies, (4) associations, (5) design professionals, (7)(b) possession-and-control exclusion.

13. **Permit expiration and reinstatement.** The ORSC R105.5 expiration clock (180 days of inactivity), extension mechanics, and what reinstatement requires. Library: `23orsc-ch1.pdf`.

**Answered (2026-07-09):**

**Source and edition.** 2023 Oregon Residential Specialty Code (ORSC), Chapter 1, Scope and Administration, official Oregon PDF (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf). Rule-side check: OAR chapter 918 division 480, One- and Two-Family Dwelling Specialty Code (local index /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/oar/oar-918-480.html; https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_chapter_918_division_480; SOS source https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/displayDivisionRules.action?selectedDivision=4173).

**The expiration rule: yes, 180 days.** 2023 ORSC Section R105.5 (Expiration): "Every permit issued shall become invalid unless the work authorized by such permit is commenced within 180 days after its issuance or if the work authorized by such permit is suspended or abandoned for a period of 180 days after the time the work is commenced. The building official is authorized to grant, in writing, one or more extensions of time, for periods not more than 180 days each. The extension shall be requested in writing and justifiable cause demonstrated." (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf). Two distinct triggers: failure to commence within 180 days of issuance, and any 180-day suspension or abandonment after commencement. The code measures suspension of "the work," not of inspections; treating a passed inspection as proof the work has not been suspended is common local administration, not code text.

**Extension mechanics.** Under R105.5 the building official may grant "one or more" extensions, so the number is not capped at state level; each extension runs not more than 180 days; the request must be in writing with justifiable cause demonstrated, and the grant must be in writing. No state fee attaches to an extension: OAR 918-480-0020 (One- and Two-Family Dwelling Permit Fees) sets fee methodology only and says nothing about expiration, reinstatement or extension fees (https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_918-480-0020). The permit application itself has a parallel clock: ORSC R105.3.2 (Time limitation of application) deems an application abandoned 180 days after filing "unless such application has been pursued in good faith or a permit has been issued," again with written 180-day extensions for justifiable cause available from the building official (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf).

**Reinstatement: no state provision exists.** The 2023 ORSC Chapter 1 contains no reinstatement section for an expired permit; a text search of the chapter PDF finds no occurrence of reinstatement in any form (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf). Once a permit is invalid under R105.5, what happens next (reactivation for a fee, a new permit at full or reduced fee, re-review under a newer code edition) is set by each building department's local policy, so the manuscript should tell the reader to ask their jurisdiction before letting 180 days run, and should present the written extension request under R105.5 as the safe path that avoids the question entirely. Related statewide backstop worth citing: ORS 455.058, reprinted in the chapter PDF after Section R105.4, lets the department or municipality assess an investigation fee when work subject to a permit "is commenced before the permit required for the work is obtained," with an emergency-repair exception if the permit is obtained within five business days (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf).

**Oregon amendments visible in the PDF.** Section R105.5 itself carries the standard 180-day framework with no Oregon-specific insertion. The Oregon-marked changes in the permit part of the chapter are: R105.1.1 (a building permit is required to create new habitable spaces, new toilet rooms or new bathrooms); R105.2 (Oregon exempt-work list, including the 200-square-foot accessory structure exemption with a 400-square-foot exception on parcels of 2.0 acres or more); R105.2.1 and R105.2.1.1 (emergency mechanical repairs may proceed with the permit application submitted within 5 working business days; emergency temporary structural repairs require 72-hour notice and application within 5 business days); R105.3.1.1 (the flood-hazard substantial-improvement determination section "is not adopted by the State of Oregon"); and the reprint of ORS 455.058 following R105.4 (all at /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/23orsc-ch1.pdf).

**OAR 918-480 has no permit-duration provision.** The division's full rule list is 918-480-0002 (name change), 918-480-0005 (adopts the ORSC), 918-480-0010 (amendments), 918-480-0020 (permit fees), 918-480-0030 (phased project and deferred submittal fees), 918-480-0125 (uniform alternate construction standard), 918-480-0130 (conventional light frame construction), 918-480-0140 (certificates of occupancy, residential) and 918-480-0150 (low volume window label program); none addresses permit expiration, duration or reinstatement (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/oar/oar-918-480.html; https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_chapter_918_division_480). Permit duration for dwellings therefore rests entirely on ORSC R105.5 as adopted through OAR 918-480-0005. Note when citing the rule text online: the oregon.public.law snapshot of 918-480-0005 is stale (it still describes the 2021 ORSC adoption); the operative edition is the 2023 ORSC per /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/README.md, so verify rule text against the SOS OARD source when the edition matters.

## 1. Smoke alarms when a dwelling is SOLD: ORS 479.260, 479.265, 479.297

> (2) All ionization smoke alarms sold in this state shall include a 'hush' mechanism that allows a person to temporarily disengage the alarm for a period of not more than 15 minutes."

Subsection (3) exempts alarms designed for persons who are hard of hearing, alarms sold for shipment out of state, and alarms for RVs, commercial vehicles, railroad equipment, aircraft, marine vessels, or manufactured dwellings. ## 3. Carbon monoxide alarms at sale and rental

**Note on the statute map:** ORS 476.725 is the rulemaking and preemption section, not the sale trigger. The sale-transfer prohibition is ORS 105.838; the rental duties are ORS 90.316 and 90.317. All are parts of the Lofgren and Zander Memorial Act: per ORS 105.844, "ORS 90.316, 90.317, 105.836 to 105.842, 455.360 and 476.725 shall be known and may be cited as the Lofgren and Zander Memorial Act. [2009 c.591 §15]" (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors105.html).

**ORS 476.725 (State Fire Marshal CO alarm standards; statewide preemption).** Subsection (1): the State Fire Marshal shall adopt rules establishing minimum standards for CO alarms in one and two family dwellings and multifamily housing (design, inspection, testing, maintenance). Subsection (2): rules for placement and location in dwellings "that were not subject to state building code requirements for carbon monoxide alarm placement or location at the time of construction." Subsection (4): "A governmental subdivision, as defined in ORS 476.005 may not enact or enforce any local ordinance, rule or regulation regarding the design, inspection, testing, maintenance, placement or location of carbon monoxide alarms." History: [2009 c.591 §4] (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors476.html).

**ORS 105.838(1) (sale/transfer prohibition).** Quoted from /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors105.html:

> "(1) A person may not convey fee title to a one and two family dwelling or multifamily housing that contains a carbon monoxide source, or transfer possession under a land sale contract of a one and two family dwelling or multifamily housing that contains a carbon monoxide source, unless one or more properly functioning carbon monoxide alarms are installed in the dwelling or housing at locations that provide carbon monoxide detection for all sleeping areas of the dwelling or housing."

Subsection (2): installation must conform to State Fire Marshal rules and the state building code. Subsection (3): violation "does not invalidate any sale or transfer of possession." History: [2009 c.591 §2].

**"Carbon monoxide source" trigger, ORS 105.836(2).** "(a) A heater, fireplace, appliance or cooking source that uses coal, kerosene, petroleum products, wood or other fuels that emit carbon monoxide as a by-product of combustion; or (b) An attached garage with an opening that communicates directly with a living space." (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors105.html). This matters for the book: an all-electric concrete home with no attached garage opening into living space contains no statutory carbon monoxide source, so ORS 105.838 does not require a CO alarm at sale, although the state building code required CO alarms in new dwellings with a source or attached garage at construction.

**Buyer remedy, ORS 105.840.** A purchaser aggrieved by a violation of ORS 105.838 or a rule under ORS 476.725 may sue "to recover the greater of actual damages or $250 per residential unit," plus discretionary attorney fees and costs; one-year limitation from date of sale or transfer. History: [2009 c.591 §3] (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors105.html). Tampering, including removing working batteries, is prohibited by ORS 105.842 [2009 c.591 §5].

**Rental CO duties, ORS 90.317** (chapter 90 is not in the local library; verified at https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_90.317, publication current through the 2024 session): landlord must install CO alarms if the unit contains or is connected to a carbon monoxide source, provide the tenant written testing instructions, repair or replace on written notice of a deficiency other than dead batteries, and alarm maintenance is a habitability requirement under ORS 90.320. The 2025 and 2026 session amended-and-repealed tables show no change to ORS 90.316 or 90.317 (https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/lawsstatutes/2025OrLawAR.pdf and https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/lawsstatutes/2026OrLawAR.pdf, both checked 2026-07-09).

## (a) The intent-to-sell presumption today, and what CCB guidance actually exists

**The statutory rule, ORS 701.010(5)** (current text, quoted from /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors701.html; same at https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_701.010):

> "(5) An owner who contracts for work to be performed by a licensed contractor. This subsection does not apply to a person who, in the pursuit of an independent business, constructs, remodels, repairs or for compensation and with the intent to sell the structure, arranges to have constructed, remodeled or repaired a structure with the intent of offering the structure for sale before, upon or after completion. It is prima facie evidence that there was an intent of offering the structure for sale if the person who constructed, remodeled or repaired the structure or arranged to have the structure constructed, remodeled or repaired does not occupy the structure after its completion."

ORS 701.010(7) carries the parallel exemption for an owner doing the work personally, with the same carve-out for "work ... performed in the pursuit of an independent business with the intent of offering the structure for sale before, upon or after completion." Current section history ends [ ... 2011 c.174 §1; 2013 c.196 §22; 2013 c.378 §1] (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors701.html).

**Companion presumption, ORS 701.031(1).** "It is prima facie evidence that a person is doing business as a contractor if: (a) The person for that person's own use performs, employs others to perform, or for compensation and with the intent to sell the structure arranges to have performed, work described in ORS 701.005 (5); and (b) Within any 36-month period the person offers for sale two or more newly built structures on which work described in paragraph (a) of this subsection was performed. [2007 c.114 §3]" (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors701.html). This is the only sale-timing number anywhere in the statute: two or more new structures offered for sale within 36 months creates the doing-business presumption. One owner-built home sold does not trigger ORS 701.031; it can still trigger the ORS 701.010(5)/(7) occupancy presumption if the builder never occupies it.

**CCB's official interpretation lives in its definitions rules, not in a publication.** OAR 812-002-0520 (Occupancy), implementing ORS 701.010, quoted verbatim from https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/view.action?ruleNumber=812-002-0520:

> "'Occupancy' may occur at the time of but not be limited to the first occurring of any of the following events: a majority of furniture and personal belongings is moved in, utility service begins, certificate of occupancy is issued, resident prepares meals and remains overnight." (History: CCB 7-2005, f. 12-7-05, cert. ef. 1-1-06; CCB 4-1998, f. & cert. ef. 4-30-98. Statutes implemented: ORS 701.010 and 701.145.)

OAR 812-002-0680 (Speculative): "'Speculative' means in anticipation of or with the intent of selling to another entity during or after construction." (https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/view.action?ruleNumber=812-002-0680, history CCB 4-1998.)

- ORS 165.540(1)(c), in-person "conversation": may not obtain by "any device, contrivance, machine or apparatus ... if not all participants in the conversation are specifically informed that their conversation is being obtained." In-person audio recording requires that every participant be specifically informed (notice to all, not merely one party's consent).
- ORS 165.540(1)(d) and (e) extend the ban to obtaining a communication known to have been illegally recorded and to using or divulging anything obtained in violation of the section.

The dividing definition is ORS 165.535(1) (same file): "'Conversation' means the transmission between two or more persons of an oral communication which is not a telecommunication or a radio communication, and includes a communication occurring through a video conferencing program." The section regulates obtaining oral communications; it does not address silent video.

**Main exceptions relevant to jobsite filming.**

- ORS 165.540(6)(a): the (1)(c) all-party-notice rule "do[es] not apply to persons who intercept or attempt to intercept oral communications ... if the person uses an unconcealed recording device" at "(A) Public or semipublic meetings such as hearings before governmental or quasi-governmental bodies, trials, press conferences, public speeches, rallies and sporting or other events; (B) Regularly scheduled classes ...; or (C) Private meetings or conferences if all others involved knew or reasonably should have known that the recording was being made." Subsection (6)(a)(C) is the operative carve-out for open, visible camera work among people who can see they are being filmed; announced recording under (1)(c) remains the clean practice.
- ORS 165.540(5)(a), felony carve-out: (1)(c) does not apply to "[a] person who records a conversation during a felony that endangers human life."
- ORS 165.540(5)(b), law-enforcement carve-out: (1)(c) does not apply to recording "a conversation in which a law enforcement officer is a participant" if made while the officer performs official duties, "openly and in plain view of the participants," the conversation is "audible to the person by normal unaided hearing," and the person "is in a place where the person lawfully may be." Subsection (10) adds that this does not authorize criminal trespass or interfering with a peace officer.
- ORS 165.540(3): the prohibitions of (1)(a) to (c) do not apply to "subscribers or members of their family who perform the acts prohibited in subsection (1) of this section in their homes."
- ORS 165.540(4) and (7): public broadcasts, amateur and citizens-band radio, and readily accessible public-safety radio traffic are outside the ban.
- ORS 165.540(8): the use-and-divulge bans of (1)(d) and (e) do not apply to a person who did not participate in the original recording "if the conversation ... is regarding a matter of public concern."

Penalty: ORS 165.540(9): violation of subsection (1) is a Class A misdemeanor. ORS 165.543 (same file) separately makes willful interception of a wire or oral communication without any party's consent a Class A misdemeanor.

## (f) Copyright registration timing, 17 U.S.C. 412

Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/412 (accessed 2026-07-09). Section 412 bars the two premium remedies unless registration is timely: no award of statutory damages or attorney's fees (17 U.S.C. 504 and 505) shall be made for "(1) any infringement of copyright in an unpublished work commenced before the effective date of its registration; or (2) any infringement of copyright commenced after first publication of the work and before the effective date of its registration, unless such registration is made within three months after the first publication of the work."

The two preserved windows: register before the infringement begins, or, for a published work, register any time within three months after first publication (the grace period, which preserves statutory damages and fees even against infringement that began right after publication). Miss both and only actual damages, infringer profits, and injunctive relief remain for that infringement. Companion prerequisite: 17 U.S.C. 411(a), "no civil action for infringement of the copyright in any United States work shall be instituted until preregistration or registration of the copyright claim has been made" (a proper application that is refused also satisfies the prerequisite with notice to the Register): https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/411. Practical consequence for the book: register within three months of the publication date as a fixed launch-checklist item.

## (a) Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act

**Operative citation:** Magnuson-Moss Warranty - Federal Trade Commission Improvement Act, 15 U.S.C. 2301 et seq. (15 U.S.C. 2301-2312, Chapter 50 "Consumer Product Warranties"): https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/chapter-50. Section titles verified there: 2301 Definitions; 2302 Rules governing contents of warranties; 2303 Designation of written warranties; 2304 Federal minimum standards for warranties; 2308 Implied warranties; 2310 Remedies in consumer disputes.

**Requirement 1: full vs limited designation.** 15 U.S.C. 2303(a): a written warranty on a consumer product must be "clearly and conspicuously" designated either "full (statement of duration) warranty" (only if it meets the federal minimum standards of 15 U.S.C. 2304, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/2304) or "limited warranty." The designation duty applies to products actually costing the consumer more than $10 (15 U.S.C. 2303(d)). Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/2303.

**Requirement 2: written-warranty disclosure.** 15 U.S.C. 2302(a): warrantors must "fully and conspicuously disclose in simple and readily understood language the terms and conditions" of the written warranty (who is covered, what will be done on failure, exclusions, claim procedure, remedies). Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/2302. Implementing FTC rules: 16 CFR Part 701 "Disclosure of Written Consumer Product Warranty Terms and Conditions" (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/part-701) and 16 CFR Part 702 "Pre-Sale Availability of Written Warranty Terms" (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/part-702).

**Requirement 3: tie-in sales prohibition (why owner installation alone cannot void a warranty).** 15 U.S.C. 2302(c): "No warrantor of a consumer product may condition his written or implied warranty of such product on the consumer's using, in connection with such product, any article or service (other than article or service provided without charge under the terms of the warranty) which is identified by brand, trade, or corporate name," unless the FTC grants a waiver after finding the branded article is necessary for the product to function and publishing the waiver in the Federal Register. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/2302.

The FTC's interpretive rule, 16 CFR 700.10 "Prohibited tying," applies this: a warrantor may not condition continued validity of a warranty on use of authorized service or authorized parts for non-warranty maintenance, but the rule "does not preclude a warrantor from expressly excluding liability for defects or damage caused by 'unauthorized' articles or service; nor does it preclude the warrantor from denying liability where the warrantor can demonstrate that the defect or damage was so caused." In other words, denial requires causation, and the burden of demonstrating it sits with the warrantor; owner installation or third-party parts by themselves are not lawful grounds to void coverage. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/700.10.

## (a) 29 CFR 1926 Subpart P (Excavations), 1926.650-.652

**5-foot protective-system threshold.** 29 CFR 1926.652(a)(1) requires each employee in an excavation to be protected from cave-ins by an adequate protective system, with two exceptions: 1926.652(a)(1)(i), excavations "made entirely in stable rock," and 1926.652(a)(1)(ii), excavations "less than 5 feet (1.52 m) in depth and examination of the ground by a competent person provides no indication of a potential cave-in" (https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.652). The under-5-foot relief is therefore not automatic; it still requires a competent-person examination finding no cave-in potential.

**4-foot ladder/egress rule.** 29 CFR 1926.651(c)(2): "A stairway, ladder, ramp or other safe means of egress shall be located in trench excavations that are 4 feet (1.22 m) or more in depth so as to require no more than 25 feet (7.62 m) of lateral travel for employees" (https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.651).

**Competent person.** Defined at 29 CFR 1926.650(b): "one who is capable of identifying existing and predictable hazards in the surroundings, or working conditions which are unsanitary, hazardous, or dangerous to employees, and who has authorization to take prompt corrective measures to eliminate them" (https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.650). Duties in 1926.651: daily inspections of excavations, adjacent areas, and protective systems for cave-in evidence, protective-system failure, hazardous atmospheres, or other hazardous conditions, 1926.651(k)(1); removal of exposed employees until precautions are taken, 1926.651(k)(2); design of structural ramps used by employees, 1926.651(c)(1)(i); monitoring of water-removal equipment and operations, 1926.651(h)(2) (https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.651).

**20-foot registered-PE line.** Sloping/benching designed under the appendices is capped at 20 feet: Subpart P Appendix B, Table B-1 (maximum allowable slopes for excavations less than 20 feet deep: Type A 3/4:1 (53 deg), Type B 1:1 (45 deg), Type C 1-1/2:1 (34 deg)) carries the note "Sloping or benching for excavations greater than 20 feet deep shall be designed by a registered professional engineer" (https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926SubpartPAppB). Independently, any sloping/benching design outside Options (1)-(3) must be approved by a registered professional engineer, 1926.652(b)(4), and any support/shield system outside Options 1-3 likewise, 1926.652(c)(4) (https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.652).

## (b) 29 CFR 1926.602 material-handling equipment

Scope: 1926.602(a)(1) covers scrapers, loaders, crawler or wheel tractors, bulldozers, off-highway trucks, graders, agricultural and industrial tractors, and similar equipment (https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.602). ## (c) Subpart CC cranes (29 CFR 1926.1400-.1442) and the homeowner-rig threshold

Scope: Subpart CC applies to "power-operated equipment, when used in construction, that can hoist, lower and horizontally move a suspended load" (boom trucks, mobile/tower cranes, articulating knuckle-boom cranes, etc.), 29 CFR 1926.1400(a) (https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.1400). So a hired boom truck or any helper-operated crane rated over 2,000 pounds triggers the certified-operator regime.

## Oregon OSHA adoption (Division 3, OAR 437-003)

Oregon OSHA adopts the federal 1926 subparts by reference with Oregon-initiated additions; the Division 3 current-rules listing (https://osha.oregon.gov/rules/final/pages/division-3.aspx) shows: Subdivision P, federal 1926.650-.652 plus Oregon rule OAR 437-003-0096 (Underground installations), Administrative Order 4-2011, subdivision text at https://osha.oregon.gov/osharules/div3/div3p.pdf; Subdivision O (Motor Vehicles, Mechanized Equipment), federal 1926.600-.606 plus Oregon-initiated rules OAR 437-003-0085 through -0094 and related rules, AO 3-2019; Subdivision CC (Cranes and Derricks in Construction), federal 1926.1400-.1442 plus Oregon-initiated rules listed as OAR 437-003-0081 and 437-003-1423, AO 3-2021. The federal thresholds above therefore apply in Oregon, with those Oregon additions layered on.

## When the rules attach to an owner-builder

Oregon OSHA enforces the Oregon Safe Employment Act, which turns on employment: "employee" means an individual "who engages to furnish services for a remuneration, financial or otherwise, subject to the direction and control of an employer," ORS 654.005(4), and "employer" means "any person who has one or more employees," ORS 654.005(5) (https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_654.005; same text at https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors654.html). The trigger is exactly as framed: the moment an owner-builder pays helpers and directs their work, the owner is an employer and Division 3 applies to those workers. Oregon OSHA's jurisdiction page confirms who is outside: "Independent contractors, sole proprietors, and partnerships that have no workers' compensation insurance coverage and no employees" (https://osha.oregon.gov/pages/topics/jurisdiction.aspx); the owner-builder working alone, or alongside licensed independent contractors, is not personally covered.

**Casual labor.** "Place of employment" under the Act "does not include any place where the only employment involves nonsubject workers employed in or about a private home," ORS 654.005(8) (https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors654.html). Nonsubject workers include casual labor under the workers' compensation statute: employment is "casual" only where the work in any 30-day period involves a total labor cost of less than $1,000 (indexed annually each July 1), ORS 656.027(3)(b), and qualifies when the employment "is not in the course of the trade, business or profession of the employer," ORS 656.027(3)(a)(A) (https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_656.027; https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors656.html). Caveat the statute does not resolve: whether a dwelling still under construction counts as "in or about a private home" for the ORS 654.005(8) exclusion; the safe planning assumption for the book is that paid helpers put the site under Oregon OSHA.

**Volunteers.** Under the plain text of ORS 654.005(4), a true volunteer furnishes services without remuneration and is not an "employee," so a homeowner using only genuine volunteers has no employees and is not an "employer" (https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_654.005). Oregon OSHA publishes no guidance addressing volunteers on a private residence; its jurisdiction page is silent on volunteers, casual labor, and household work (https://osha.oregon.gov/pages/topics/jurisdiction.aspx). The unsettled edge is in-kind or reciprocal compensation, since the statute reads "remuneration, financial or otherwise": labor swaps, room and board, or other things of value could make a helper an employee, and no Oregon OSHA interpretation or published authority on that line was found. That sub-point is NOT-RESEARCHABLE-PRO (a question for an employment attorney or an Oregon OSHA consultation, which is free and non-enforcement: https://osha.oregon.gov/pages/topics/jurisdiction.aspx for contact routing).

## (a) Oregon CDL line for towing heavy equipment

**The 26,001-pound combination threshold.** Oregon defines "commercial motor vehicle" by weight: a vehicle or combination that "has a gross combination weight rating or gross combination weight of 26,001 pounds or more, whichever is greater, inclusive of one or more towed units, with a gross vehicle weight rating or gross vehicle weight of more than 10,000 pounds," ORS 801.208(1)(a), or a single vehicle with GVWR/GVW of 26,001 pounds or more, ORS 801.208(1)(b) (https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_801.208). Oregon DMV states the licensing consequence directly: a CDL is required to drive "a trailer, or multiple trailers, with a total gross vehicle weight rating or actual gross vehicle weight of more than 10,000 pounds when the gross combination weight rating or actual gross combination weight, whichever is greater, of all vehicles combined is 26,001 pounds or more" (Class A territory), or any single vehicle at 26,001 pounds or more (Class B) (https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/cdlget.aspx). Note the "whichever is greater" wording: actual loaded weight can cross the line even where ratings do not.

**No personal-use exemption for equipment hauling.** ORS 801.208(2) exempts only: (a) emergency fire vehicles operated by firefighters, (b) emergency vehicles operated by qualified emergency service volunteers, (c) "a motor home used to transport or house, for nonbusiness purposes, the operator or the operator's family members or personal possessions," and (d) "a recreational vehicle that is operated solely for personal use" (https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_801.208). A pickup pulling an equipment trailer is neither a motor home nor a recreational vehicle. Contrast the federal definition, where a CMV must be "used in commerce," 49 CFR 383.5 (Group A: GCWR 26,001 pounds or more with towed units in excess of 10,000 pounds) (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title49-vol5/xml/CFR-2024-title49-vol5-sec383-5.xml): Oregon's statute omits the commerce element, so an owner-builder towing their own excavator at 26,001+ pounds GCWR/GCW with a 10,001+ pound trailer needs a Class A CDL in Oregon even with no business purpose involved. The DMV page's listed exceptions match the statute (personal-use recreational vehicles, emergency fire vehicles, emergency service volunteers) and include no owner-equipment or personal-hauling exception (https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/cdlget.aspx).

**Farm exemption: real but narrow, and it does not fit construction hauling.** Oregon's farm endorsement on a regular Class C license substitutes for a CDL only for vehicles that are "controlled or operated by a farmer," "used to transport agricultural products, farm machinery or farm supplies to and from a farm," "not used in the operation of a common or contract motor carrier," and "operated within 150 miles of the farmer's farm" (Oregon DMV Farm Endorsement Application, Form 735-6776, rev. 1-25: https://www.oregon.gov/odot/forms/dmv/6776fill.pdf; Class A (Y) and Class B (Z) endorsement classes described on the form). Hauling an excavator to build a house is not transporting farm machinery to or from a farm for a farming operation, so the endorsement realistically does not apply to an owner-builder unless the person is a bona fide farmer moving farm equipment for farm purposes. There is no other personal-use carve-out on the DMV commercial licensing page (https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/driverid/cdlget.aspx).

## (b) Jobsite diesel and fuel transport thresholds

**Purely private hauling is outside the HMR entirely.** The hazardous materials regulations do not apply to "transportation of a hazardous material by an individual for non-commercial purposes in a private motor vehicle, including a leased or rented motor vehicle," 49 CFR 171.1(d)(6) (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title49-vol2/xml/CFR-2024-title49-vol2-sec171-1.xml). An owner-builder hauling fuel for their own build, not in support of any business, is in this category.

**Materials of trade (the framework once the hauling supports a business, e.g. a contractor's or self-employed builder's rig).** A "material of trade" is a hazardous material other than hazardous waste carried on a motor vehicle to protect the health and safety of the operator or passengers, to support the operation or maintenance of a vehicle, or "by a private motor carrier ... in direct support of a principal business that is other than transportation by motor vehicle," 49 CFR 171.8 (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title49-vol2/xml/CFR-2024-title49-vol2-sec171-8.xml). Quantity limits, 49 CFR 173.6(c)-(d) (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title49-vol2/xml/CFR-2024-title49-vol2-sec173-6.xml): Packing Group I, 0.5 kg (1 pound) or 0.5 L (1 pint) per package; Packing Group II and III liquids and solids (this bracket covers gasoline and diesel), 30 kg (66 pounds) or 30 L (8 gallons) per package, so standard 5-gallon cans qualify; gas cylinders up to 100 kg (220 pounds) gross; and an aggregate cap of 200 kg (440 pounds) of all materials of trade on one vehicle. Within those limits the material "is not subject to any other requirements of this subchapter" beyond 173.6 itself, meaning no shipping papers, placards, or formal hazmat training (49 CFR 173.6, same govinfo URL).

**Diesel transfer tanks and the 119-gallon line.** Diesel (flash point at or above 38 C / 100 F) may be reclassed as a combustible liquid, 49 CFR 173.150(f)(1), and "the requirements in this subchapter do not apply to a material classed as a combustible liquid in a non-bulk packaging unless the combustible liquid is a hazardous substance, a hazardous waste, or a marine pollutant," 49 CFR 173.150(f)(2) (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title49-vol2/xml/CFR-2024-title49-vol2-sec173-150.xml). Non-bulk means a maximum capacity of 450 L (119 gallons) or less as a receptacle for a liquid; greater than 450 L (119 gallons) is bulk packaging, 49 CFR 171.8 (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title49-vol2/xml/CFR-2024-title49-vol2-sec171-8.xml). So a pickup transfer tank of diesel at or under 119 gallons is effectively outside the HMR even in business use. Over 119 gallons, combustible liquids pick the rules back up in bulk form: shipping papers, package marking, identification numbers on the bulk package, and Subpart F placarding, 49 CFR 173.150(f)(3) (same 173.150 URL).

**Placard thresholds.** Each bulk packaging or transport vehicle containing any quantity of a placard-required hazardous material must be placarded, 49 CFR 172.504(a); the relief for non-bulk loads is 172.504(c): "a transport vehicle or freight container which contains less than 454 kg (1,001 pounds) aggregate gross weight of hazardous materials covered by table 2" need not be placarded, and Class 3 FLAMMABLE and COMBUSTIBLE are Table 2 materials (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title49-vol2/xml/CFR-2024-title49-vol2-sec172-504.xml). Practical stack-up for the book: fuel cans within materials-of-trade limits never approach placarding (440-pound aggregate cap); a diesel transfer tank at or under 119 gallons is non-bulk and outside the HMR; a bulk (120+ gallon) diesel tank in business use is where placards and shipping papers begin.

## What is "land surveying" requiring a licensed PLS

"Practice of land surveying" is defined at ORS 672.005(2) (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors672.html). Most of its paragraphs are qualified by "for others": (a) providing professional measurement services for others, (b) geodetic surveys for others, (f) "locating and laying out for others alignments, positions or elevations for the construction of fixed works," (g)-(i) consultation, data preparation, and photogrammetric mapping for others. But two boundary paragraphs carry no "for others" qualifier: ORS 672.005(2)(c), "establishing, reestablishing or replacing boundaries or geodetic control monuments or reference points," and ORS 672.005(2)(d), "locating, relocating, establishing, reestablishing or retracing any property lines or boundaries for any tract of land, road right-of-way or easement." Boundary work is therefore the practice of land surveying no matter whom it is done for. (Related: ORS 672.005(1)(c)-(e) make surveying for area, topography, lines, grades, elevations, and construction layout the practice of engineering "when done for others," same file.)

Licensure and penalty anchors: "No person shall practice land surveying in this state unless the person is registered and has a valid certificate to practice land surveying," ORS 672.025(1); a person may not "engage in the practice of ... land surveying ... without having a valid certificate or permit," ORS 672.045(1); violation of ORS 672.045 is a Class A misdemeanor, prosecutable within two years after discovery, ORS 672.991(1)-(2) (all in /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors672.html). Holding oneself out as a surveyor, or bidding to perform commercial or professional land surveying service, is itself practicing, ORS 672.007(2)(a)-(c) (same file).

## What an owner may lawfully do

Two exemptions in ORS 672.060 ("Registration under ORS 672.002 to 672.325 is not required for the following") cover owners, quoted verbatim from /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors672.html and confirmed current at https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_672.060:

- ORS 672.060(12): "The performance of land surveying by a person: (a) On property owned or leased by the person, or on property in which the person has an interest, estate or possessory right; and (b) That affects exclusively the property or interests of the person, unless the performance affects the health or safety of the public or an employee."
- ORS 672.060(13): "The performance of land surveying work by a landowner within the boundaries of the landowner's land or by the landowner's regular employee as part of the employee's official duties within the boundaries of the land of the employer."

## The line, stated plainly

An owner-builder may measure from existing, found monuments and stake their own construction layout: building corners, setback offsets from the monumented line, grades and elevations, all within their own parcel. That is land surveying performed by the owner, on the owner's property, affecting exclusively the owner's interests, and it is exempt from licensure under ORS 672.060(12) and (13) (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors672.html; https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_672.060).

What the owner may not do is establish, reestablish, replace, or retrace the boundary itself, or set or reset monuments or stakes purporting to mark where the property line is, ORS 672.005(2)(c)-(d) (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors672.html). By the statute's own structure, a boundary determination fixes the line against the adjoining owner as well, so it does not "affect exclusively the property or interests of the person" and falls outside the ORS 672.060(12)(b) condition; the subsection (13) exemption is likewise confined to work "within the boundaries of the landowner's land," which presupposes the boundaries rather than authorizing the owner to declare them. Boundary establishment therefore requires a registered professional land surveyor, ORS 672.025(1), and doing it unlicensed is a Class A misdemeanor, ORS 672.991(1) (same file). The owner also may not hold out as a surveyor or perform survey work for others, ORS 672.007(2) (same file).

Working formulation for the chapter: measuring FROM found monuments to place a building at the required setback is construction layout an owner may do; deciding or marking WHERE the boundary runs, including replacing a missing corner, is boundary surveying and needs a PLS. If a setback is tight enough that an inch matters, or a corner monument cannot be found, the boundary question is being answered in fact, and that is PLS work.

**Answered (2026-07-09):**

Question: does an open-loop ground-source heat pump (pump-and-reinject or pump-and-discharge) in Oregon require a water right, and what well-construction and discharge rules apply. The framework below is stated so the HVAC chapter can present the conditional design path with rule anchors.

1. Jurisdictional boundary. Groundwater pumped for its thermal characteristics from a well with a bottom-hole temperature below 250 degrees Fahrenheit is "geothermal fluid" under the groundwater code (ORS 537.515(4), local file: /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors537.html). Wells at or above 250 degrees Fahrenheit bottom-hole temperature leave the Water Rights Act entirely and are regulated as geothermal resources under ORS chapter 522 by the State Geologist (ORS 537.090, same file). Every residential open-loop GSHP is far below that line, so OWRD groundwater law governs; OWRD administers it as "low-temperature geothermal" (bottom-hole temperature under 250 degrees Fahrenheit, well under 2,000 feet deep): https://www.oregon.gov/owrd/programs/gwwl/gw/pages/lowtempgeothermal.aspx.

2. Water right or exempt use. Pumping groundwater through a heat pump and out of the well is an appropriation, so it needs a groundwater right unless it fits an ORS 537.545 exemption (local file: /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors537.html). The anchors:
- ORS 537.545(1)(f): any single industrial or commercial purpose up to 5,000 gallons per day is exempt. OAR 690-230-0130(1) applies this exemption expressly to low-temperature geothermal fluid appropriation for a single industrial or commercial use not exceeding 5,000 gpd: https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_690-230-0130. OWRD's low-temperature geothermal page states the converse rule: a project drawing more than 5,000 gpd requires an industrial/commercial water right permit, plus a low-temperature geothermal well registration with the department: https://www.oregon.gov/owrd/programs/gwwl/gw/pages/lowtempgeothermal.aspx.
- ORS 537.545(1)(d): single or group domestic purposes up to 15,000 gpd are exempt, and OAR 690-230-0130(2) recognizes a domestic-purposes exemption for low-temperature geothermal fluid where combined groundwater use stays within 15,000 gpd (URL above). Whether OWRD classifies a residence's heat-exchange water as "domestic" (15,000 gpd ceiling) or as the "single commercial/industrial" thermal use (5,000 gpd ceiling) is the pivot between those two ceilings; OWRD's public guidance uses the industrial/commercial framing, so the safe chapter statement is: clearly exempt at or under 5,000 gpd; between 5,000 and 15,000 gpd only if OWRD accepts the domestic classification for the site; above that, a permit.
- ORS 537.545(1)(e): "down-hole heat exchange purposes" are exempt. This covers systems that circulate fluid inside the well without withdrawing groundwater (a down-hole heat exchanger); it is not the open-loop path.
- Exempt does not mean unrecorded: the well constructor must file a well log plus a map of the well location, with a $300 exempt-use recording fee (ORS 537.545(6)-(7), local file). An exempt use still constitutes a right equal to a certificated right to the extent beneficial (ORS 537.545(2)).
- Stability note: ORS 537.545 was amended effective September 15, 2027 (2023 c.465 s.11; 2025 c.283 s.2); the operative-2027 text keeps (1)(d), (1)(e), and (1)(f) unchanged (verified in the same local file).

3. Well construction rules. Both the production well and any injection well are constructed under the water supply well standards: OAR 690-200 (introduction, general standards, definitions): https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_chapter_690_division_200; OAR 690-210 (construction standards): https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_chapter_690_division_210; OAR 690-220 (abandonment): https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_chapter_690_division_220. On top of those sits the low-temperature geothermal division, OAR 690-230 (Standards and Procedures for Low-Temperature Geothermal Production and Injection Wells and Effluent Disposal Systems): https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_chapter_690_division_230, with 690-230-0030 (construction of production and injection wells), 690-230-0050 (start card describing proposed use), 690-230-0060 (well log identification), 690-230-0070 (well-head protection), 690-230-0075 (disposal of fluids produced during drilling and testing), 690-230-0080/0085 (pump testing of injection wells, split at 15,000 gpd), 690-230-0090 (water temperature measurement), and 690-230-0120 (water right application processing).

4. Reinjection versus surface discharge. This is the regulatory fork the chapter should state plainly:
- Definitions: OAR 690-230-0020 defines a standard low-temperature geothermal effluent disposal system (nothing but heat added to the fluid, and the effluent either put to a secondary beneficial use or returned to the producing groundwater reservoir or another suitable reservoir) versus a nonstandard system (disposal the Director considers non-beneficial, including storm sewer, drainage hole, or direct discharge to land surface or a surface water body): https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_690-230-0020.
- Consequence: under OAR 690-230-0140, a nonstandard disposal system makes the geothermal appropriation inferior to all subsequent consumptive rights and to appropriators using standard disposal; upgrading to standard disposal restores the original priority date: https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_690-230-0140. Reinjection is therefore the favored design; surface discharge is legal but demotes the right.
- Injection approval: no water supply well may be used for injection unless OWRD approves the installation, well design, and receiving formation (OAR 690-210-0070: https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_690-210-0070, which also directs applicants to DEQ for supplementary injection-well (UIC) rules). Division 230 adds siting rules split at 15,000 gpd (OAR 690-230-0040 and 690-230-0045) and injection plans (OAR 690-230-0110 and 690-230-0115), and OWRD publishes a "Request for Approval of a Low Temperature Groundwater Injection Well" form on the low-temperature geothermal page (URL in item 1).
- Statutory backstop: ORS 537.783 requires Water Resources Commission rules governing reinjection disposal from wells under 2,000 feet and under 250 degrees Fahrenheit, including prior approval of all reinjection proposals, and subsection (3) requires a DEQ water pollution control facilities permit under ORS 468B.050 before reinjection begins, waivable by DEQ-WRC agreement when fluid is returned uncontaminated to the reservoir it came from (local file: /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors537.html).

5. Closed-loop contrast (one sentence for the chapter). A closed-loop GSHP boring appropriates no groundwater, needs no water right, and is regulated only as a boring under OAR 690-240 (Construction, Maintenance, Alteration, Conversion and Abandonment of Monitoring Wells, Geotechnical Holes and Other Holes), rules 690-240-0040 through 690-240-0049: https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_chapter_690_division_240 and OWRD's Division 240 standards document: https://www.oregon.gov/owrd/Documents/Ch%20690%20Div%20240%20with%20Table%20240-3;%2020230209.pdf. That asymmetry is why the open-loop system is the conditional design path.

6. Site- and county-dependent items to flag, not resolve, in the chapter: (a) whether daily withdrawal stays under the 5,000 gpd exemption depends on system flow rate and runtime (a continuous 4 gpm already exceeds it; an intermittent residential system may not); (b) critical groundwater areas and designated special-area standards can restrict new wells and exempt uses (OAR 690-200-0025, 690-200-0027, 690-200-0028: https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_chapter_690_division_200), and OWRD's ongoing groundwater allocation rulemaking is tightening new allocations statewide; (c) whether the receiving aquifer qualifies as "suitable" for reinjection is a case-by-case OWRD approval (OAR 690-210-0070, 690-230-0110/0115); (d) DEQ's UIC program and the ORS 537.783(3) WPCF permit govern the return well on the DEQ side; (e) any surface discharge additionally raises DEQ discharge permitting and county land-use/septic setback questions, on top of the water-right demotion in OAR 690-230-0140; (f) thermal interference protection for neighbors is bounded by ORS 537.796 and ORS 537.095 (local file); (g) recheck the 2027-operative ORS 537.545 text near publication.

Bottom line for the chapter: an open-loop GSHP at typical single-home scale can run without a water right under the ORS 537.545(1)(f) 5,000 gpd exemption (with the well log, map, and recording fee filed), but the wells must meet OAR 690-200/210 construction standards plus the OAR 690-230 low-temperature geothermal rules, the injection well needs prior OWRD approval (and DEQ involvement), and reinjection to the source aquifer is the design OWRD's rules reward; surface discharge converts the right to a demoted, nonstandard-disposal status and adds DEQ/county questions.

## The tool: exact name and location

BCD's UA trade-off tool for 2023 ORSC Chapter 11 is the "Envelope trade-off calculator," an Excel workbook linked under that name on the residential energy compliance page (https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/pages/energy-residential-compliance.aspx), direct URL https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Documents/calculator-orsc-tradeoff.xlsx (downloaded and read in full 2026-07-09). The workbook's own title block: "2023 ORSC - Envelope Trade-Off Calculator," "For demonstrating compliance with TABLE N1104.1(1) only," "Use this spreadsheet to show that the thermal envelope of the proposed building is a minimum of 0% better than the prescriptive code minimum." Seven sheets: Instructions; Table N1104.1(1) (the main worksheet); Mixed-Alt. Walls U-Factor; Windows Overall U-Factor; Skylights Overall U-Factor; Doors Overall U-Factor; plus a hidden Default U-Factors sheet feeding the drop-downs.

The same BCD page also links a separate "Measure 6 thermal performance calculator" (https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Documents/calculator-orsc-measure6.xlsx). Do not confuse the two: the trade-off calculator demonstrates 0% equivalence to the prescriptive envelope; Measure 6 is Additional Measure No. 6 of Table N1101.1(2), "High Efficiency Thermal Envelope UA," which requires the proposed UA to be 8 percent LOWER than the code UA (Table N1101.1(2) footnote c, quoted in Lane County's 2023 ORSC Residential Energy Efficiency handout, rev. 07-12-24: https://www.lanecounty.org/common/pages/GetFile.ashx?key=nr44AVlA).

## Code basis

2023 ORSC Section N1104.1 (General, Exterior Envelope Requirements): the building envelope complies with prescriptive Table N1101.1(1) or is "demonstrated using Table N1104.1(1)" (section text confirmed 2026-07-09 via the UpCodes rendering of ORSC 2023 Chapter 11: https://up.codes/s/exterior-envelope-requirements). Table N1101.1(1) footnote a, quoted in the Lane County handout (https://www.lanecounty.org/common/pages/GetFile.ashx?key=nr44AVlA): "As allowed in Section N1104.1, thermal performance of a component may be adjusted provided that overall heat loss does not exceed the total resulting from conformance to the required U-factor standards. Calculations to document equivalent heat loss shall be performed using the procedure and approved U-factors contained in Table N1104.1(1)." Table N1104.1(2) supplies default U-factors for wood-framed assemblies only; for other assemblies, "U-factors for other assemblies, which include steel framing, brick or other masonry, stucco, etc., shall be calculated using ASHRAE Handbook of Fundamentals procedures" (ORSC 2023 N1104.1 via https://up.codes/s/exterior-envelope-requirements). That last sentence is the hook for a concrete roof: its assembly U-factor is user-calculated, not taken from the wood-frame default tables.

## Required inputs and how the calculation runs

From the workbook's Instructions sheet and main "Table N1104.1(1)" sheet (https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/codes-stand/Documents/calculator-orsc-tradeoff.xlsx):

1. Standard Base Case: enter the project's actual AREAS only, flat ceiling (SF), vaulted ceiling (SF), gross wall including fenestration (SF), underfloor (SF), and slab edge (LF). The tool multiplies those areas by fixed base-case factors matching Table N1101.1(1) prescriptive equivalents: flat ceiling U-0.021 (R-49), vaulted ceiling U-0.033 (R-30), opaque wall U-0.059 (R-21 intermediate), windows U-0.27, skylights U-0.50, exterior doors U-0.20, underfloor U-0.033 (R-30), slab edge F-0.52 (R-15 x 24 in.). Base Case UA = sum of U x Area (F-factor x LF for slab edge).
2. Proposed Alternative: for each opaque component, pick the actual assembly from drop-downs backed by the hidden default U-factor list (flat ceilings R-38 to R-60; vaulted rafter/scissor-truss options plus EPS foam-core panels; a large wood-framed wall list; an Insulated Concrete Form wall row; underfloor R-21 to R-49; slab-edge/underslab options). Non-listed or multiple wall types go on the Mixed-Alt. Walls U-Factor tab: description, net area (excluding fenestration), and the calculated assembly U-factor per wall type; the tab area-weights them. Windows, skylights, and doors are entered unit-by-unit (area + U-factor from NFRC/cut sheets) on their tabs, which feed area-weighted averages back to the main sheet. Proposed UA = sum of U x Area.
3. Pass criterion: the "Percent Improved" cell computes Proposed UA / Base Case UA - 1 and must be 0.0% or lower; the PASS/FAIL cell shows green PASS only when proposed whole-envelope UA does not exceed the base-case UA (Instructions step 7).
4. Submission: printed spreadsheet plus plans, product cut sheets, and specifications to the local building official (Instructions step 8).

## How a concrete roof that misses prescriptive R-value demonstrates compliance

1. Calculate the concrete roof assembly's U-factor using ASHRAE Handbook of Fundamentals procedures (the ORSC-directed method for non-wood assemblies, https://up.codes/s/exterior-envelope-requirements), with real layer thicknesses at design time.
2. Enter the roof/ceiling area and that U-factor on the ceiling line of the Proposed Alternative. Tool limitation to plan around: the ceiling rows offer only the wood-frame and EPS-panel drop-down entries, and unlike walls there is no "Mixed-Alt" tab for ceilings, so a concrete roof U-factor is a manual, documented entry (the workbook already anticipates documented custom values, "Input Brand, Model Number and U-Factor, if applicable (provide product cut sheets)") subject to building-official acceptance with the ASHRAE calculation attached.
3. The amount by which the concrete roof's U x Area exceeds the base case (U-0.021 flat / U-0.033 vaulted times the same area) must be bought back elsewhere: walls below U-0.059 (the ICF row exists for exactly this), windows below U-0.27, underfloor below U-0.033, and/or slab edge below F-0.52, until Proposed UA is at or below Base Case UA.
4. Two geometry constraints: vaulted-ceiling area entered in the tool cannot exceed 50 percent of total conditioned floor area, "if over 50%, alternative method to this spreadsheet must be used" (Instructions step 1); the code parallel is Table N1101.1(1) footnote g, vaulted area over 50 percent of heated floor area must itself be U-0.026 or better (https://www.lanecounty.org/common/pages/GetFile.ashx?key=nr44AVlA). And per footnote k/the door tab, only one solid door up to 28 SF gets the U-0.54 allowance.
5. Not run here per the item's instruction: the actual UA numbers need real assembly dimensions; this is flagged as a design-time task. Note for that pass: if the envelope only reaches 0% parity via trade-off, Additional Measure 6 (which needs UA 8 percent below code) is off the table, so plan a different Table N1101.1(2) additional measure.
