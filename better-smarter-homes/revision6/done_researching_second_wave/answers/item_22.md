## What is "land surveying" requiring a licensed PLS

"Practice of land surveying" is defined at ORS 672.005(2) (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors672.html). Most of its paragraphs are qualified by "for others": (a) providing professional measurement services for others, (b) geodetic surveys for others, (f) "locating and laying out for others alignments, positions or elevations for the construction of fixed works," (g)-(i) consultation, data preparation, and photogrammetric mapping for others. But two boundary paragraphs carry no "for others" qualifier: ORS 672.005(2)(c), "establishing, reestablishing or replacing boundaries or geodetic control monuments or reference points," and ORS 672.005(2)(d), "locating, relocating, establishing, reestablishing or retracing any property lines or boundaries for any tract of land, road right-of-way or easement." Boundary work is therefore the practice of land surveying no matter whom it is done for. (Related: ORS 672.005(1)(c)-(e) make surveying for area, topography, lines, grades, elevations, and construction layout the practice of engineering "when done for others," same file.)

Licensure and penalty anchors: "No person shall practice land surveying in this state unless the person is registered and has a valid certificate to practice land surveying," ORS 672.025(1); a person may not "engage in the practice of ... land surveying ... without having a valid certificate or permit," ORS 672.045(1); violation of ORS 672.045 is a Class A misdemeanor, prosecutable within two years after discovery, ORS 672.991(1)-(2) (all in /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors672.html). Holding oneself out as a surveyor, or bidding to perform commercial or professional land surveying service, is itself practicing, ORS 672.007(2)(a)-(c) (same file).

## What an owner may lawfully do

Two exemptions in ORS 672.060 ("Registration under ORS 672.002 to 672.325 is not required for the following") cover owners, quoted verbatim from /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors672.html and confirmed current at https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_672.060:

- ORS 672.060(12): "The performance of land surveying by a person: (a) On property owned or leased by the person, or on property in which the person has an interest, estate or possessory right; and (b) That affects exclusively the property or interests of the person, unless the performance affects the health or safety of the public or an employee."
- ORS 672.060(13): "The performance of land surveying work by a landowner within the boundaries of the landowner's land or by the landowner's regular employee as part of the employee's official duties within the boundaries of the land of the employer."

## The line, stated plainly

An owner-builder may measure from existing, found monuments and stake their own construction layout: building corners, setback offsets from the monumented line, grades and elevations, all within their own parcel. That is land surveying performed by the owner, on the owner's property, affecting exclusively the owner's interests, and it is exempt from licensure under ORS 672.060(12) and (13) (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors672.html; https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_672.060).

What the owner may not do is establish, reestablish, replace, or retrace the boundary itself, or set or reset monuments or stakes purporting to mark where the property line is, ORS 672.005(2)(c)-(d) (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors672.html). By the statute's own structure, a boundary determination fixes the line against the adjoining owner as well, so it does not "affect exclusively the property or interests of the person" and falls outside the ORS 672.060(12)(b) condition; the subsection (13) exemption is likewise confined to work "within the boundaries of the landowner's land," which presupposes the boundaries rather than authorizing the owner to declare them. Boundary establishment therefore requires a registered professional land surveyor, ORS 672.025(1), and doing it unlicensed is a Class A misdemeanor, ORS 672.991(1) (same file). The owner also may not hold out as a surveyor or perform survey work for others, ORS 672.007(2) (same file).

Working formulation for the chapter: measuring FROM found monuments to place a building at the required setback is construction layout an owner may do; deciding or marking WHERE the boundary runs, including replacing a missing corner, is boundary surveying and needs a PLS. If a setback is tight enough that an inch matters, or a corner monument cannot be found, the boundary question is being answered in fact, and that is PLS work.
