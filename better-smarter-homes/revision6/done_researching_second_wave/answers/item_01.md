**(a) NEC 210.8(D): Oregon AMENDED it, gutting the 2023 appliance list and redirecting to 422.5.** Table 1-E, PDF page 4 (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/23oesc-table1-E.pdf), 210.8(D) entry verbatim, with {added} = underlined and {struck} = strikethrough:

> "Specific Appliances. GFCI protection {added: for specific appliances} shall be provided {added: in accordance with 422.5.} {struck: for the branch circuit or outlet supplying the following appliances rated 150 volts or less to ground and 60 amperes or less, single- or 3-phase: (1) Automotive vacuum machines (2) Drinking water coolers and bottle fill stations (3) High-pressure spray washing machines (4) Tire inflation machines (5) Vending machines (6) Sump pumps (7) Dishwashers (8) Electric ranges (9) Wall-mounted ovens (10) Counter-mounted cooking units (11) Clothes dryers (12) Microwave ovens}"

Resulting enforceable Oregon text of 210.8(D): "Specific Appliances. GFCI protection for specific appliances shall be provided in accordance with 422.5." The entire 12-item 2023 NEC appliance list, including dishwashers, electric ranges, wall-mounted ovens, counter-mounted cooking units, clothes dryers, and microwave ovens, is struck. The 210.8(D) row carries no "Errata Jan. 1, 2025" or "Amendment Jan. 1, 2026" tag, so this is an original Oct. 1, 2023 amendment, unchanged through both later revision cycles.

**(b) Dishwasher circuits in Oregon: GFCI protection is NOT required in dwellings.** The redirect target, 422.5(A), is itself amended (Table 1-E, PDF page 12, /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/23oesc-table1-E.pdf): items (1) through (5) remain unchanged; item (6) becomes "Sump pumps {added: and sewage pumps}" with an added exception for a labeled single receptacle; and item (7) reads verbatim "(7) {struck: Dishwashers} {added: Not adopted by the State of Oregon.}" So Oregon's 210.8(D) points only to 422.5, and Oregon's 422.5 no longer lists dishwashers; a dwelling dishwasher branch circuit or outlet, hardwired or cord-and-plug, needs no GFCI under the 2023 OESC. No location rule catches the typical under-counter receptacle either (Table 1-E, PDF page 3): Oregon rewrote 210.8(A)(6) to "Kitchens {added: — where the receptacles are installed to serve the countertop surfaces}" and marked 210.8(A)(7) (areas with sinks and food/beverage-preparation provisions) "{added: Not adopted by the State of Oregon}"; the errata-added Exception No. 5 to 210.8(A) (tagged "Errata Jan. 1, 2025") further exempts a labeled single appliance receptacle in a dedicated space for locations (A)(2), (5), (6), (8), and (11). Two dependencies: (1) product-specific — if a particular dishwasher's listing or installation instructions require GFCI, unamended NEC 110.3(B) still enforces it (110.3(B) does not appear in Table 1-E; only 110.3(A) is amended), so check the manual; (2) for other-than-dwelling kitchens, Table 1-E leaves 210.8(B) items (1)-(15) "unchanged," so NEC baseline receptacle GFCI triggers still apply there. Confirm any edge case with the local electrical inspector, though the amendment itself is statewide.

**(c) Article 840: no Table 1-E item touches it.** The string "840" does not appear anywhere in the 18-page table (full-text search of /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/23oesc-table1-E.pdf); the table's final and only NEC Chapter 8 entry is 800.24(A) (PDF page 18), a cable-support/workmanship amendment that also adds a plenum cable-tie listing sentence referencing 800.170. Articles 805, 810, 820, 830, and 840 are all unamended, and per the table's own rule, unlisted 2023 NEC language "has not been amended by Oregon," so 2023 NEC Article 840 applies in Oregon as published.
