## (a) The intent-to-sell presumption today, and what CCB guidance actually exists

**The statutory rule, ORS 701.010(5)** (current text, quoted from /home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors701.html; same at https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_701.010):

> "(5) An owner who contracts for work to be performed by a licensed contractor. This subsection does not apply to a person who, in the pursuit of an independent business, constructs, remodels, repairs or for compensation and with the intent to sell the structure, arranges to have constructed, remodeled or repaired a structure with the intent of offering the structure for sale before, upon or after completion. It is prima facie evidence that there was an intent of offering the structure for sale if the person who constructed, remodeled or repaired the structure or arranged to have the structure constructed, remodeled or repaired does not occupy the structure after its completion."

ORS 701.010(7) carries the parallel exemption for an owner doing the work personally, with the same carve-out for "work ... performed in the pursuit of an independent business with the intent of offering the structure for sale before, upon or after completion." Current section history ends [ ... 2011 c.174 §1; 2013 c.196 §22; 2013 c.378 §1] (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors701.html).

**Companion presumption, ORS 701.031(1).** "It is prima facie evidence that a person is doing business as a contractor if: (a) The person for that person's own use performs, employs others to perform, or for compensation and with the intent to sell the structure arranges to have performed, work described in ORS 701.005 (5); and (b) Within any 36-month period the person offers for sale two or more newly built structures on which work described in paragraph (a) of this subsection was performed. [2007 c.114 §3]" (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors701.html). This is the only sale-timing number anywhere in the statute: two or more new structures offered for sale within 36 months creates the doing-business presumption. One owner-built home sold does not trigger ORS 701.031; it can still trigger the ORS 701.010(5)/(7) occupancy presumption if the builder never occupies it.

**CCB's official interpretation lives in its definitions rules, not in a publication.** OAR 812-002-0520 (Occupancy), implementing ORS 701.010, quoted verbatim from https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/view.action?ruleNumber=812-002-0520:

> "'Occupancy' may occur at the time of but not be limited to the first occurring of any of the following events: a majority of furniture and personal belongings is moved in, utility service begins, certificate of occupancy is issued, resident prepares meals and remains overnight." (History: CCB 7-2005, f. 12-7-05, cert. ef. 1-1-06; CCB 4-1998, f. & cert. ef. 4-30-98. Statutes implemented: ORS 701.010 and 701.145.)

OAR 812-002-0680 (Speculative): "'Speculative' means in anticipation of or with the intent of selling to another entity during or after construction." (https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/view.action?ruleNumber=812-002-0680, history CCB 4-1998.)

**Search of current CCB public materials (2026-07-09): no CCB guidance document states any waiting period or sale-timing safe harbor for owner-built homes.** Pages and documents checked: the CCB "CCB License" page on who needs a license (https://www.oregon.gov/ccb/pages/ccb%20license.aspx), which addresses developers who arrange improvements "with the intent to sell the property" needing a license but says nothing about owner-builders selling; the Consumer Tools page section "Acting as your own General Contractor" (https://www.oregon.gov/ccb/pages/consumer-tools.aspx), which warns only about employer status when using unlicensed workers; and the "Information Notice to Owners About Construction Responsibilities" (form adopted 9-2016, required at permit issuance by ORS 701.325(2)-(3), https://www.oregon.gov/ccb/Documents/information%20notice%20to%20owner%20about%20construction%20responsibilities.pdf), in which the unlicensed permit applicant must certify "I own, reside in, or will reside in the completed structure" or that the work is on "property I own, a residence that I reside in, or a residence that I will reside in." None of these states a rule such as "you must occupy for one year before selling." Practical conclusion for the manuscript: attribute the occupancy presumption to ORS 701.010(5) and the two-in-36-months presumption to ORS 701.031(1)(b), and the definition of occupancy to OAR 812-002-0520; do not attribute any specific holding period to a CCB guidance document, because no current one says that.

## (b) 2025 Oregon Laws chapter 78: what it changes and when

**Identity of the act.** 2025 Oregon Laws chapter 78 is Enrolled House Bill 2800 (83rd Legislative Assembly, 2025 Regular Session), "Relating to professional employer organizations under workers' compensation law," introduced at the request of Governor Tina Kotek for the Department of Consumer and Business Services. OLIS overview: https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2025R1/Measures/Overview/HB2800; enrolled text: https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2025R1/Downloads/MeasureDocument/HB2800/Enrolled.

**What section 18 does to ORS 701.010.** The only amended language in the whole section is subsection (17). From the enrolled bill (brackets are deletions, bold in the original is new text):

> "(17) A [worker leasing company] professional employer organization or temporary service provider, [both as defined in ORS 656.850,] as defined in section 2 of this 2025 Act, that supplies personnel to a licensed contractor for the performance of work under the direction and supervision of the contractor or that has entered into a PEO relationship with the contractor."

(Section 2 of the act was codified as ORS 656.849, which is how the ORS compilation renders the cross-reference.) I diffed the current ORS 701.010 text against the operative-July-1-2027 text that the Legislative Counsel sets out in the compilation: subsection (17) is the sole difference; subsections (5), (6) and (7), including the entire intent-to-sell presumption, are restated verbatim with no change (/home/droid/theBook/revision6_code_library/ors/ors701.html, note following ORS 701.010: "The amendments to 701.010 by section 18, chapter 78, Oregon Laws 2025, become operative July 1, 2027. See section 23, chapter 78, Oregon Laws 2025.").

**Operative and effective dates.** Enrolled HB 2800 section 23(1): "Section 2 of this 2025 Act and the amendments to ORS 653.412, 656.005, 656.018, 656.403, 656.702, 656.850, 656.855, 671.525, 671.527, 671.540, 671.562, 671.565, 701.005, 701.010, 701.035, 701.098, 701.470, 737.270, 743.521 and 743.522 by sections 3 to 22 of this 2025 Act become operative on July 1, 2027." Section 24: "This 2025 Act takes effect on the 91st day after the date on which the 2025 regular session ... adjourns sine die" (https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2025R1/Downloads/MeasureDocument/HB2800/Enrolled). The 2025 regular session adjourned sine die June 27, 2025 (https://www.opb.org/article/2025/06/27/oregon-legislature-ends-2025-session/), so the act took effect September 26, 2025, with the ORS 701.010 amendment operative July 1, 2027.

**Rule TODAY versus after the operative date.** Today, and at all times through June 30, 2027, ORS 701.010(17) exempts "a worker leasing company or temporary service provider, both as defined in ORS 656.850." On and after July 1, 2027, it exempts a "professional employer organization or temporary service provider, as defined in ORS 656.849 ... or that has entered into a PEO relationship with the contractor." The owner-builder exemptions and the intent-to-sell presumption in ORS 701.010(5) and (7) are identical before and after July 1, 2027; 2025 Oregon Laws chapter 78 changes nothing about selling an owner-built home. Any manuscript language implying that the 2025 amendment alters owner-builder sale rules would be wrong; it is purely a terminology change from worker leasing companies to professional employer organizations.

**2026 session check.** The 2026 regular session amended-and-repealed table shows the only chapter 701 section touched in 2026 is ORS 701.990 (2026 c.53 §5, HB 4089); ORS 701.010 and 701.031 were not amended in 2026 (https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/lawsstatutes/2026OrLawAR.pdf, checked 2026-07-09).
