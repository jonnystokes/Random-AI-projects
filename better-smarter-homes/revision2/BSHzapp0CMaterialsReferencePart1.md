# Appendix C — Materials and Supplier Reference

*Every material specified, every supplier identified, every brand evaluated*

This appendix is the materials master list. For every material category in the design — concrete, rebar, CMU, mortar, insulation, waterproofing, framing, drywall, flooring, tile, cabinets, fixtures, hardware, paint, electrical components, plumbing components, HVAC equipment, smart home gear — this appendix lists:

- **What's specified** (the material category and the design's requirements)
- **Brand recommendations** (specific products and their pros/cons)
- **Where to source** (Oregon-area suppliers and national alternatives)
- **Pricing context** (rough cost ranges as of 2026, with the understanding that prices change)
- **Alternatives** (what to substitute if a primary choice isn't available)
- **Quality criteria** (how to evaluate products in a given category)

This is a working reference, not a procurement document. Final brand and supplier choices happen at the time of purchase based on availability, price, and the specific build's needs. The intent is to capture knowledge so the owner-builder doesn't start from zero on every category.

The materials are organized in **rough construction sequence** so that a given phase's materials can be reviewed together.

## 1 — Concrete and Masonry Materials

### 1.1 Ready-Mix Concrete

**What's specified:** Pozzolanic blend with 50–60% cementitious replacement (fly ash + silica fume), W/C ratio ≤ 0.40, calcium nitrite corrosion inhibitor, 5,000+ psi at 28 days. See Section 4 and Section 12.3 for full mix design.

**How to source:** Local ready-mix concrete suppliers within delivery radius of the building site. Oregon ready-mix industry is well-developed; most rural sites have 2–5 suppliers serving them.

**Suppliers (Oregon, by region):**
- **Knife River Corporation** — operates plants throughout Oregon, large network, strong technical support
- **Cemex** — operates plants in Portland metro and southern Oregon
- **Roseburg Forest Products / RFP Concrete** — Douglas County area
- **Northwest Aggregates** — Willamette Valley
- **Rinker Materials** — Portland area
- **Local independents** — many small operators in rural areas; often most competitive on price

**How to specify the mix:**
1. Bring the mix design (cementitious blend, W/C ratio, admixtures, target strength) to the supplier in writing
2. Get pricing per cubic yard for the design mix vs. their standard mixes
3. Ask the supplier's technical team to review and confirm they can produce it
4. Schedule trial batches (Section 12.3) — most suppliers will produce small trial batches for testing

**Pricing context (2026, rough):**
- Standard 4,000 psi residential mix: $150–$200/cubic yard delivered
- Pozzolanic high-performance mix: $200–$300/cubic yard delivered
- Calcium nitrite corrosion inhibitor: $30–$60/yard added cost
- Silica fume addition: $20–$40/yard added cost
- Concrete pumper truck: $150–$250/hour with 4-hour minimum, plus mileage

**Quality criteria:**
- Supplier maintains a quality control program (regular cylinder testing of their mixes)
- Truck drivers will document slump test on delivery (request this)
- Supplier can produce documentation of mix design, batch tickets, and certifications
- Reasonable minimum order (typically 3–5 cubic yards; below that, "short load" fees apply)

### 1.2 Cementitious Materials (for trial batches and supplemental work)

**Materials:**
- **Type I/II Portland cement** — bagged 94 lb sacks; bulk available for larger work
- **Class F fly ash** — supplemental cementitious material; bagged or bulk
- **Slag (GGBS — Ground Granulated Blast-Furnace Slag)** — alternative to fly ash; bagged
- **Silica fume** — supplied densified or undensified; specialty material

**Suppliers:**
- **Local building supply** for bagged Portland (Lowes, Home Depot, Parr Lumber, Dunn Lumber)
- **Lehigh Hanson, Holcim, Cemex** for bulk
- **Specialty admixture distributors** for silica fume (Master Builders, Sika, Euclid)

### 1.3 Concrete Admixtures

**Calcium nitrite corrosion inhibitor:**
- **Master Builders DCI** (BASF) — the original calcium nitrite inhibitor, established track record
- **Sika FerroGard 901** — alternative
- **Euclid Eucon CIA** — alternative
- Dose rate: 2–6 gallons per cubic yard depending on chloride exposure expectation

**Water-reducing plasticizers:**
- **Master Builders Glenium 7920** (high-range water reducer / superplasticizer)
- **Sika ViscoCrete** series
- **Euclid Eucon Plastol**

**Air-entraining admixtures:**
- Required for freeze-thaw exposure (not as critical for Oregon's mild climate but cheap insurance)
- Most suppliers stock equivalent products
- 4–7% target air content

**Set retarders or accelerators:**
- Used to control set time based on weather
- Typically supplier provides as needed for specific pour conditions

**Where to source:** Through the ready-mix supplier (they handle dosing). Self-sourced for trial batches via concrete supply distributors (e.g., Western Group, Concrete Supply Company in Portland).

### 1.4 Aggregate (for trial batches and supplemental work)

**Coarse aggregate:**
- 3/4 inch nominal maximum size — standard residential
- Crushed stone or rounded river gravel acceptable
- Local quarry products preferred (regional consistency)

**Fine aggregate (sand):**
- Concrete-grade sand (washed, graded per ASTM C33)
- Local quarry products

**Where to source:** Aggregate suppliers (Knife River, Cemex, local quarries — same as ready-mix suppliers in many cases).

### 1.5 Reinforcing Steel (Rebar)

**What's specified:** Grade 60 (60,000 psi yield) deformed bars in sizes per structural design — typically #4 (1/2 inch), #5 (5/8 inch), and #6 (3/4 inch). Plain black rebar (uncoated mild steel). See Section 4.

**Why plain (uncoated):** This design's strategy is to protect rebar through superior concrete (low-permeability mix + corrosion inhibitor + ample cover) rather than expensive coated rebar. Epoxy-coated rebar is a separate strategy with its own challenges (coating damage during installation creates concentrated corrosion sites).

**Suppliers:**
- **Sweet Steel** — Portland, statewide delivery, residential and commercial
- **Reinforcing Services Inc.** — Eugene, central Oregon
- **National Welding** — multiple locations
- **Cardinal Steel** — Portland
- **Boise Cascade Distribution** — broad network in the Northwest
- **Local building supply yards** — many stock common sizes

**Pricing context (2026, rough):**
- #4 rebar: $8–$15 per 20-foot stick
- #5 rebar: $12–$22 per 20-foot stick
- #6 rebar: $20–$35 per 20-foot stick
- Tying wire (16 gauge): $30–$50 per roll
- Rebar chairs (plastic): $20–$50 per box of 100 pieces

**Service:** Many rebar suppliers offer **fabrication** — they cut and bend bars to the schedule provided. Saves significant on-site labor. Worth using even for an owner-builder.

**Tools needed:**
- Rebar tying tool (manual or battery — Stanley Bostitch RC58RH or similar speed-tier)
- Rebar cutter (manual hacksaw OR electric/hydraulic for large quantities)
- Bender (manual for occasional bends)

### 1.6 Concrete Masonry Units (CMU / Cinder Block)

**What's specified:** 8-inch nominal CMU for the inner structural wythe, 4-inch nominal for the outer decorative wythe (or stone veneer). ASTM C90 standard. See Section 4, Section 5.

**Sizes (nominal, including mortar joint):**
- Standard 8x8x16 inch (the "cinder block")
- 8x8x8 inch (half block)
- Specialty corner blocks
- 4x8x16 inch for veneer wythe
- 12x8x16 inch for oversize structural walls (basement)

**Decorative options for the outer wythe:**
- Standard gray smooth-face block (least expensive)
- Split-face block (rough texture, masonry "grain")
- Ground-face / honed block (smooth, refined finish)
- Architectural block in various colors and textures
- Stone veneer (manufactured or natural)
- Brick veneer

**Suppliers:**
- **Mutual Materials** — Pacific NW's largest concrete products supplier; Portland, Eugene, Salem distribution
- **Belden Brick** — premium brick option
- **Glen-Gery / Hebron Brick** — brick alternatives
- **Local block plants** — every region has at least one local CMU plant; often best pricing for standard block
- **Pacific Stone** — natural stone veneer

**Pricing context (2026, rough):**
- Standard 8x8x16 gray block: $1.80–$2.80 per block
- Decorative split-face block: $2.50–$5.00 per block
- Architectural ground-face block: $3.50–$8.00 per block
- 4-inch decorative veneer: $1.50–$3.00 per block
- Stone veneer: $10–$25 per square foot (manufactured); $20–$50+ (natural)
- Bulk delivery: minimum order typically 1 pallet (80–120 blocks)

**Block count for this design:** 6,000–12,000 blocks across all walls and floors of a 3,000 sqft three-level home. Order in batches as walls progress, not all at once (storage and damage concerns).

**Quality criteria:**
- Uniform color (within a single batch)
- No major chips, cracks, or efflorescence
- Square corners (verify with framing square)
- Consistent dimensions (small variations are normal; large variations slow down laying)
- Stored on pallets, off the ground, covered to prevent moisture absorption before use

### 1.7 Mortar

**What's specified:** Type S mortar for structural masonry; Type N for veneer wythe (less critical structurally). Pre-blended bagged mortar mix is the standard approach for owner-builders.

**Brands:**
- **Quikrete** — most widely available, consistent quality
- **Sakrete** — alternative national brand
- **Spec Mix** — premium bagged blend, used by professional masons
- **Local masonry supply** — many regional brands

**Sizing:**
- Standard 80 lb bag of mortar mix yields ~0.6 cubic feet of mortar
- Approximately 7 bags per 100 blocks laid (variable based on joint width)
- 6,000 block job: roughly 400+ bags

**Color options:**
- Standard gray (matches most CMU)
- White (with white Portland and white sand)
- Pigmented (added oxide pigments — buff, brown, black)
- Match block color for monolithic look or contrast for emphasis

**Pricing (2026, rough):**
- Bagged mortar mix: $8–$15 per 80 lb bag

**Tools and accessories:**
- **Mortar mixer** — small portable electric ($300–$800) or gas-powered ($600–$1,500); big time-saver vs. hand-mixing
- **Mortar trough** for working out of (5-gallon bucket OK for small batches)
- **Trowels** — pointing trowel (small detail), brick trowel (laying), tuck-pointing trowel (joint work)
- **Joint tool / striking tool** — concave, V-jointer, raked joint tools — choose one and use consistently
- **Mortar board** — flat board to hold a working batch of mortar at the work area

### 1.8 Wall Ties (FRP)

**What's specified:** Fiberglass-reinforced polymer (FRP) wall ties at specified spacing (typically 16 inches vertical, 24 inches horizontal). Replaces traditional steel ties to eliminate thermal bridging. See Section 4.5, Section 5.

**Brands:**
- **Pultrall** — Quebec-based, FRP rebar and ties; available in the US
- **Owens Corning Pinkbar** — fiberglass wall reinforcement
- **Composite Reinforcing Solutions (CRS)** — California-based
- **MeadWestvaco / WireBond** — has some composite tie products
- **Heckmann Building Products** — masonry accessories, including some non-corroding ties

**Specifying:**
- Diameter: typically 1/4 to 3/8 inch
- Length: depends on cavity depth (4-inch cavity in this design = 6-inch tie minimum)
- End anchorage: hooked, pintled, or threaded

**Pricing (2026, rough):**
- FRP ties: $3–$8 per tie (significantly more than steel at ~$0.50 each)
- Cost premium: $2,000–$5,000 for an entire 3,000 sqft home — modest in context

**Procurement:** Specialty product, often best ordered direct from the manufacturer or through a masonry supply distributor with experience in this product. Lead time of 4–8 weeks possible.

### 1.9 Cavity Insulation (Polyiso)

**What's specified:** 4 inches total polyisocyanurate foam in two staggered 2-inch layers in the cavity wall. R-26 nominal. ASTM C1289. See Section 5.

**Brands:**

- **Atlas Roofing** — broad polyiso lineup
- **Carlisle SynTec** — premium insulation and roofing

**Suppliers:**
- **Building supply yards** — Parr Lumber, Dunn Lumber, Lowe's, Home Depot all stock common polyiso
- **Insulation specialty distributors** — IBP (Installed Building Products), Lansing Building Products
- **Direct from manufacturer** — for large quantities

**Pricing (2026, rough):**
- 2-inch polyiso 4x8 sheet: $40–$70 per sheet
- Per square foot: $1.25–$2.20
- For an 8-foot tall, 200-foot perimeter, 4 inches total: ~$3,500–$7,000 in cavity insulation

### 1.10 Under-Slab Insulation (EPS Type XIV)

**What's specified:** 4 inches total Type XIV EPS in two staggered 2-inch layers under the entire slab footprint. R-16. 40 psi compressive strength. ASTM C578. See Section 4.3, Section 5.

**Brands:**
- **InsulFoam** — Premier Industries' brand, common in Pacific NW
- **Atlas EPS** — broad product line
- **Plymouth Foam** — Wisconsin-based, ships nationally
- **Foam Construction** — regional Pacific NW EPS producer
- **R-Control** — building integration EPS systems

**Sizing:**
- 4x8 sheets, 2 inch thick (or 4 inch single sheets where available)
- Density: 2.85 lb/cubic foot for Type XIV
- Plain or foil-faced (foil for vapor barrier integration)

**Suppliers:**
- Same as polyiso — building supply yards, insulation distributors, direct from manufacturer

**Pricing (2026, rough):**
- 2-inch Type XIV EPS: $15–$25 per 4x8 sheet
- Per board foot: $0.40–$0.80 (Type XIV is substantially more expensive than Type II EPS at ~$0.20/bf)
- For 2,500 sqft footprint at 4 inches total: ~$3,000–$6,000

**Why Type XIV specifically:** Compressive strength (40 psi) needed for under-slab use under loads from walls and stored items. Type II (15 psi) inadequate. Type IX (25 psi) marginal for the worst-case wall loading in this design.

## 2 — Waterproofing and Drainage Materials

### 2.1 Foundation Waterproofing Membrane

**What's specified:** Continuous high-performance waterproofing on exterior of basement walls. Either liquid-applied elastomeric or peel-and-stick sheet membrane. See Section 5.

**Liquid-applied options:**
- **Tremco TREMproof 250GC** — liquid-applied, hand-trowel or spray
- **Carlisle CCW-525** — premium liquid membrane
- **W. R. Meadows MEL-ROL Liquid** — single-component urethane
- **Polyguard 650** — fluid-applied bitumen-modified

**Sheet membrane options:**
- **Carlisle CCW MiraDRI 860/861** — peel-and-stick, easier application than liquid
- **W. R. Meadows MEL-ROL** — peel-and-stick
- **Grace Bituthene** — long-established sheet membrane

**Why specify above damp-proofing:** This design's 1000-year goal demands true waterproofing, not just damp-proofing. The cost difference is small in context; the durability benefit is substantial.

**Suppliers:**
- **Tremco** — direct or through commercial roofing distributors
- **Carlisle / CCW** — through Sun Coast Roofing Supply, GAF distributors
- **W. R. Meadows** — through specialty waterproofing distributors

**Pricing (2026, rough):**
- Liquid-applied: $2–$5 per square foot installed
- Sheet membrane: $4–$8 per square foot installed
- For typical basement (200-foot perimeter, 8-foot tall): $6,000–$12,000 in waterproofing

### 2.2 Protection Board

**What's specified:** Rigid protection board over the waterproofing membrane to prevent damage during backfill. Typically 1/4-inch high-density fiberboard or 1-inch foam.

**Brands:**

- **Sealtight Protection Board** — multiple sources

### 2.3 Drainage Mat / Composite Drainage System

**What's specified:** Dimpled plastic drainage mat installed over the protection board, providing a void space for water to flow down to the perimeter drain. Allows pressure relief and drying.

**Brands:**

- **System Platon** — also dimpled HDPE
- **Carlisle CCW MiraDRAIN** — composite drainage
- **W. R. Meadows MEL-DRAIN** — composite drainage

**Pricing (2026, rough):** $1–$3 per square foot installed.

### 2.4 Perforated Drainpipe

**What's specified:** 4-inch perforated PVC or HDPE drainpipe at the base of the foundation wall, wrapped in filter fabric, surrounded by drainage gravel.

**Brands:**
- **ADS** (Advanced Drainage Systems) — common HDPE corrugated
- **NDS** — fittings and PVC drainpipe
- **Ipex** — PVC drainpipe

**Suppliers:** Building supply yards, plumbing supply houses, local irrigation suppliers.

### 2.5 Filter Fabric / Geotextile

**What's specified:** Non-woven filter fabric wrapping the drainpipe and gravel, preventing fines from clogging the drain.

**Brands:**
- **Mirafi** — industry standard
- **TYPAR** — alternative
- **HJW** — economy option

**Suppliers:** Landscape supply, drainage specialty, building supply.

### 2.6 Drainage Gravel

**What's specified:** Clean, washed 1- to 1.5-inch crushed stone (no fines) in a 12+ inch envelope around the perimeter drainpipe. Acts as a capillary break and provides flow space.

**Suppliers:** Local quarries and aggregate suppliers — same as concrete aggregate suppliers.

**Pricing:** $25–$50 per cubic yard delivered.

## 5 — Plumbing Materials

### 5.1 Copper Pipe (Type L)

**What's specified:** Type L copper for the main water trunk and supply mains throughout the house. Heavier wall than Type M; thinner than Type K. Long life, proven reliability. See Section 6.

**Sizing:**
- 2-inch main trunk
- 1-inch and 3/4-inch branches to manifolds
- Joints: lead-free solder or pressed fittings (ProPress)

**Suppliers:**
- **Plumbing supply houses** — Ferguson, Consolidated Supply, Pacific Plumbing — best pricing for contractors and account holders
- **Building supply yards** — Lowe's, Home Depot for retail; pricing higher
- **Online for specialty fittings** — Supply House, Plumbing Supply

**Pricing (2026, rough):**
- 1-inch Type L copper: $7–$12 per foot
- 3/4-inch Type L: $4–$7 per foot
- 2-inch Type L: $20–$35 per foot
- Pressed fittings: $5–$30 each (smaller to larger)
- Soldered fittings: $1–$5 each

**Pressed fittings vs. soldered:**
- **Soldered:** lower material cost, longest history, most foolproof when done right
- **Pressed (ProPress, MegaPress):** faster, no flame, can be done with water in pipe; press tool rental ~$50–100/day; press fitting cost premium
- **Recommendation:** ProPress for owner-builder convenience and speed; soldered as secondary skill

### 5.2 PEX-A Tubing

**What's specified:** PEX-A with EVOH oxygen barrier for radiant floor loops and home-run distribution to fixtures. See Section 6.

**Brands:**
- **Uponor AquaPEX** — the primary PEX-A brand (formerly Wirsbo)
- **Rehau RAUPEX** — European brand, premium quality
- **Mr PEX** — value brand
- **Viega FostaPEX** — multi-layer aluminum-PEX

**Sizing for radiant:**
- 1/2-inch most common, 5/8-inch for higher-flow loops
- Coils typically 100, 300, 500 feet
- Shorter lengths in stick form for branches

**Connection methods (PEX-A):**
- **Expansion fittings** (Uponor ProPEX, Rehau Everloc) — the gold standard for PEX-A; uses an expander tool to stretch the tube, then snap-fit a fitting that grips as it shrinks back
- **Crimp/cinch fittings** (PEX-B method) work on PEX-A but lose the self-healing benefit
- **Sharkbite push-fit** acceptable for accessible joints, not recommended for embedded radiant

**Suppliers:**
- **Plumbing supply houses** — best for full-line PEX-A
- **PEX Universe** — online specialty supplier
- **SupplyHouse.com** — online, good selection

**Pricing (2026, rough):**
- 1/2-inch PEX-A 300-foot coil: $200–$350
- Per foot: $0.65–$1.20
- Expansion tool: $300–$500 (manual) to $1,200+ (battery powered)

### 5.3 PVC Drain Pipe and Fittings
