## 1. Company background

- Founded 2009 in Ottawa by Robert Wang and other former Nortel engineers; first
  product 2010.
- Merged with Corelle Brands in 2019 to form Instant Brands (also Pyrex, Corningware).
- Filed Chapter 11 on June 12, 2023, citing interest rates and credit. The appliance
  business was bought by Centre Lane Partners and emerged in March 2024 as Instant
  Pot Brands. Products, support line, and parts have continued.
- Pressure cookers are UL and ULC listed.

## 2. Specifications common to the line (from the Pro and Duo Plus manuals)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| High pressure working band | 10.2 to 11.6 psi (70 to 90 kPa) ≈ 240 to 243°F |
| Low pressure working band | 5.8 to 7.2 psi (30 to 50 kPa) ≈ 229 to 233°F |
| Safety vent pressure | about 15.2 psi |
| Minimum liquid for pressure | 1.5 cups (6 qt); 2 cups (8 qt) |
| Max fill | 2/3 line; 1/2 for foaming foods |
| Pressure cook time range | 0 to 4 hours per run |
| Slow cook | Less 180 to 190°F, Normal 190 to 200°F, More 200 to 210°F (newer units: Low/High); 30 min to 99.5 h; manual warns runs under 3 h "will result in uncooked food" |
| Sous vide (models that have it) | 104 to 194°F (40 to 90°C); presets Chicken 140°F, Beef 130°F, Egg 145°F; 30 min to 99.5 h |
| Keep warm (Pro) | Low 144°F, High 171°F, Custom 144 to 194°F; up to 10 h |
| Steam | Low 181°F, High 212°F (non-pressure, Pro) |
| Wattage | 700 W (3 qt); 1000 W (6 qt Duo, Duo Plus, Rio); 1200 W (8 qt, Pro 6 qt, Rio Wide, Pro Max) |
| Time to pressure | about 8 to 15 min at 6 qt with 2 cups water; under 8 min in reviewer tests for Duo Plus and Pro |
| Release | manual quick release (Duo: turn valve; newer: a switch), natural release; Pro adds QuickCool ice tray; Max and Pro Plus automate release |
| Inner pot | stainless steel (most); ceramic non-stick optional on some bundles |
| Altitude adjustment | none built in (Duo, Pro); some newer units have an altitude setting in the app or menu, verify per model |

Instant Pot manuals give pressure as a band rather than a point because the heater
cycles on and off between the two thresholds, so the steam temperature drifts
between about 240 and 243°F on High.

## 4. Safety mechanisms (Instant Pot lists ten)

1. Lid lock: lid cannot be turned while the float valve is up.
2. Lid position detection: heater will not start if the lid is not in the locked
   position.
3. Automatic pressure control: heater cuts at the top of the band.
4. Automatic temperature control: heater cuts on over-temperature (also the
   "Burn"/"Food burn" warning when the base overheats with too little liquid).
5. Steam release valve: primary vent for over-pressure.
6. Anti-block shield: keeps food out of the vent pipe.
7. Leaky lid detection: will not build pressure if the ring is missing, the valve is
   set to Venting, or steam is escaping; heater shuts off.
8. Excess pressure protection: if the vent blocks, the inner pot is pushed down so
   steam escapes around the rim inside the housing.
9. Electrical fuse.
10. Thermal fuse.

The interlock that matters (lid cannot open under pressure) is a mechanical float
pin that blocks the lid's rotation. This is the mechanism that failed on the
recalled Crock-Pot Express, Ninja Foodi OP300, and other brands' units. Instant Pot's
implementation has not been recalled, though it has been named in individual
lawsuits alleging lids opened under pressure.

## 5. Recall and litigation record

| Date | Product | Units | Issue |
|---|---|---:|---|
| July 2015 | Instant Pot Smart-60 (Bluetooth) | ~1,140 | Electric shock risk from a wiring defect; 3 to 4 reports, no injuries |
| Feb to Mar 2018 | Instant Pot Gem 65 8-in-1 multicooker (not a pressure cooker; sold at Walmart) | ~104,000 | Overheating and melting of the base; 107 reports, 5 with property damage |

No Instant Pot pressure cooking lid has been recalled. Lawsuits alleging burns from
lids that opened under pressure exist against Instant Brands as they do against
every major brand; the 2023 bankruptcy complicated those claims. By contrast the
Ninja Foodi OP300 series (1.8 million units, 106 burn reports) and the Crock-Pot
Express (about 914,000 units, 99 burn injuries) were recalled for lid-opening
defects.

## 6. Known weaknesses

- **Slow cook mode runs cool.** Widely reported to be weaker than a Crock-Pot on the
  same label, and the sealed lid prevents evaporation. Users compensate with the
  More/High setting, 15 to 20% less liquid, or a vented glass lid. One user measured
  195°F after 10 hours on slow cook and 189°F after switching to High, which shows
  how loosely the labels map to temperature. The manual's caution about runs under 3
  hours confirms the slow ramp.
- **No fine pressure control.** Two levels (three on Pro Plus and Pro Max). Nothing
  below about 5.8 psi.
- **Canning program is not validated.** Instant Pot's own site limits the Max to
  water-bath canning after the USDA did not endorse it.
- **Sealing ring** absorbs odors and stretches; plan on 12 to 18 month replacement.
- **Ramp time** for 8 quart units and cold, full pots can be 20 to 40 minutes.
- **The Rio line dropped Low pressure**, so if a Low setting matters, the Duo, Duo
  Plus, Pro, Pro Plus, or Pro Max are the ones that still have it.

## 7. Where the line sits on the trait scorecard

Using the scorecard from document 01 section 15:

| Trait | Instant Pot line |
|---|---|
| High pressure 10 to 12 psi | Yes on all; 15 psi on Pro Max |
| Low pressure | Duo, Duo Plus, Pro, Pro Plus, Pro Max; not Rio |
| Very low pressure (1.5 to 5 psi) | No |
| Adjustable non-pressure temperature | Sous vide 104 to 194°F on Duo Plus, Pro, Pro Plus, Pro Max; Pro also has custom keep warm 144 to 194°F |
| Slow cook holding 185 to 200°F | Weak; the line's best-documented shortcoming |
| Ramp rate | 1000 W (6 qt) or 1200 W (8 qt, Pro); under 8 to about 15 minutes |
| Natural release | Manual on all; QuickCool assist on Pro; automated on Max and Pro Plus |
| Capacity | 3, 6, 7.5, 8 qt |
| Lid interlock and recall record | Float-pin interlock; UL/ULC; no lid recall; individual lawsuits exist |
| Inner pot | Stainless standard |
| Max pressure run | 4 hours |
| Altitude adjust | Not on Duo or Pro; check per model |

The line's strength for the old-hen job is that the sous vide mode on the Pro-class
units provides exactly the 165 to 175°F multi-hour hold the meat science favors,
while the same pot does the fast high-pressure route and the broth. Its weakness is
the slow cook mode, which does not matter if the sous vide mode is used for the slow
route instead.
