# 07. Stage 2: Cooker Selection

Owner's criteria, in their words: highest PSI option; ability to adjust pressure;
manual control over automation (preset food buttons undercook); smart timers and
keep-warm; best for the price. Standing requirements from Stage 1: plug-in electric
for safety, and (from the meat research) a low-temperature hold mode is the single
most valuable extra trait for old hens. Prices are US, September 2026, list vs
typical street; they move weekly, so treat them as bands.

Honorable mentions, not ranked:

- **Zavor LUX LCD, 6 or 8 qt** ($170 to 200): Consumer Reports' top-rated multi-cooker,
  stainless pot, Flex temperature mode 102 to 356°F. Capped at 12 psi, only two
  pressure levels. A fine alternative to the Pro if the 8 qt Pro is unavailable.
- **Cuisinart CPC-900, 6 qt** ($53 clearance at Walmart): advertises "up to 15 psi" on
  a High+ setting, but Cuisinart has discontinued it and the manual does not publish
  the psi. Cheap 15 psi if you accept no long-term parts supply and an unverified
  spec.

Excluded and why: Ninja Foodi OP300 family (1.8 million unit lid recall, 2025);
Crock-Pot Express (lid recall, 2020); Presto electric cookers (9 psi); Instant Pot
Rio (High only); Instant Pot Max (discontinued); Chef iQ (12 psi, app-centric);
Wolf Gourmet (no pressure mode); Presto Precise canner (a canner with no cook
timer program to speak of, $330).

## Notes that affect the choice

- **15 psi buys about 20% time, nothing else.** From document 02: 15 vs 12 psi is
  250 vs 244°F. Both are far above the collagen threshold. Do not pay a large premium
  for it. It is worth about the $50 to 70 gap between the Pro and the Pro Plus,
  and not the $130 gap to the Pro Max on a bad day.
- **On the preset buttons.** Every cooker listed has a manual mode where presets can
  be ignored entirely. On the Instant Pot Pro Plus it is the "Pressure Cook" program:
  touch Pressure to pick Low, High or Max, set hours and minutes, touch the venting
  field to pick Natural. The presets are still on the screen but never need touching.
- **Max level on the Instant Pots is limited to two programs** (Pressure Cook and
  Canning). That is exactly where you want it.
- **The Wi-Fi on the Pro Plus and Pro Max is optional.** Everything is on the
  touchscreen and dial. Reviewers report the app adds little.
- **Warranty is one year on every Instant Pot.** Breville is one year as well. Keep
  a spare sealing ring on hand regardless of brand.
- **Altitude.** Pro Plus, Pro Max and Breville have an altitude setting. Nesco
  handles it mechanically with the 15 psi weight. The Pro does not.
- **Two hens at once** needs the 8 qt Pro, the 8 qt Zavor, or the 9.5 qt Nesco. The
  Pro Plus and Pro Max come only in 6 qt, which takes one 5 to 6 lb bird.

## The decision in one line

Buy the Instant Pot Pro Plus 6 qt at $170 or less. If it is out of stock or over
$190 and the Pro Max is under $190, buy the Pro Max. If you want to save $60 and
give up 15 psi, buy the Pro 8 qt. Buy the Nesco only if a second bird per batch
matters more than the low-temperature hold.
